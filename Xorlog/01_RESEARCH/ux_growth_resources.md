### Recommended lean stack for Xorlog

1. **Frontend + marketing site:** Next.js (or similar) on **Cloudflare Pages** (free, commercial-use-allowed, unlimited bandwidth) — NOT Vercel Hobby, because Hobby's ToS technically bars commercial use and Xorlog will be monetized from the start.
2. **API/backend logic (light):** **Cloudflare Workers** (free tier, then $5/mo Paid once >100k req/day) — routing, auth checks, thin API layer.
3. **Heavier compute (screener recompute, backtests, DuckDB/pandas batch jobs):** a small **Railway Hobby** container (~$5-20/mo) or equivalent, since Workers' CPU-time ceiling can't run pandas/DuckDB-scale jobs. This is the one line item that isn't free even at zero users, because "compute a screener over 500+ stocks with technical/fundamental factors" needs more than 10-30ms of edge CPU.
4. **Database + Auth:** **Supabase Free** (Postgres + Auth + Storage in one, 50k MAU, matches "journal + user accounts" needs) — accept the 500MB cap and 7-day pause risk at pre-launch/zero-traffic stage (mitigate pause risk with the same cron-job.org ping keeping it "active", or move to Pro the moment there's real usage).
5. **Historical price/parquet data layer:** **Cloudflare R2** (10GB free, zero egress) holding parquet files, queried via DuckDB's httpfs/Iceberg-catalog support — avoids paying for a always-on DB just to serve bulk historical OHLC data.
6. **Scheduling:** **cron-job.org** (free, precise timing) hitting Worker/Railway endpoints for EOD/data-refresh jobs; GitHub Actions as a free backup/CI-adjacent scheduler for non-time-critical jobs (accept its drift).
7. **Email:** **Resend Free** (3k/mo, 100/day) for transactional mail only (signup, password reset, KYC-status) at launch; budget Pro ($20/mo) the moment digest/alert emails become a real feature.
8. **Analytics:** **PostHog Free** (1M events/mo) for product analytics — skip Umami unless a separate lightweight pageview tool is wanted.
9. **Domain + DNS:** Cloudflare registrar/DNS (near cost-price domains, no markup) — the one guaranteed real cost.

## 4. Broker API Table

### CRITICAL — SEBI's retail algo-trading framework (effective 1-Apr-2026) applies ACROSS ALL BROKERS, not just one

[DATA] SEBI's new algo-trading framework became fully mandatory for all stock brokers from 1-Apr-2026 (phased in from Oct-2025, with brokers missing Oct-Jan milestones already barred from onboarding new retail API clients since 5-Jan-2026). Key provisions that hit any third-party platform (like Xorlog) wanting to place orders on a user's behalf:
- Every algorithmic order must carry a unique exchange-issued **Algo-ID**; unregistered algos (including API-based strategies) are treated as algo trading and must comply.
- **External/third-party platforms must be "empanelled" and hosted within the broker's own infrastructure** to keep placing orders — a SaaS company or independent developer "cannot connect directly to exchanges" and must partner with a registered broker.
- Orders only accepted from a **registered App ID mapped to a whitelisted static IP**.
- **Daily 2FA required** — no more indefinite refresh-token sessions.
- **Max 10 orders/second** per exchange/segment.
- This specifically targets **automated/algorithmic** execution. [INFERENCE] A "manual, one-click, user-reviews-and-confirms-each-order" execution helper (closer to a smart order ticket than an autonomous strategy) sits in a greyer, likely lower-burden zone than auto-executing strategies, but the empanelment/static-IP/Algo-ID machinery is broker-enforced infrastructure, not just a policy carve-out — confirm with each broker's compliance team before building anything that places orders automatically. This reinforces the india_competitors.md finding already on file: **plan Xorlog's Phase 1 around read-only trade-import/journaling + manual order tickets, and treat true automated execution as a later, compliance-heavy phase**, regardless of which broker is chosen.

## 5. Distribution Playbook

### (a) SEO — programmatic pages for stocks/screens

[DATA] Traffic scale (Similarweb, [UNVERIFIED — Similarweb estimates, not ground truth]): Screener.in ~14.6M visits, 66.5% direct / 31.7% organic search (desktop); Tickertape.in ~2.6M visits, 61.8% direct / 35.9% organic; Trendlyne ~2.4M visits, similar direct-heavy mix. **Reading:** all three lean more on direct/brand traffic than pure organic — i.e. their SEO pages exist mainly to convert people who already searched a specific ticker ("RELIANCE share price", "TCS quarterly results"), not to build a cold-traffic funnel from generic keywords. Direct-traffic dominance also implies strong repeat/bookmarked usage — a signal that the product itself (not just SEO) drives retention.

[DATA] Screener.in's URL pattern confirms a template-driven, one-page-per-listed-company structure: `/company/<TICKER-OR-CODE>/consolidated/` (e.g. `/company/AVANTEL/consolidated/`, numeric-code variant `/company/522195/`), plus templated hub pages: `/screen/new/`, `/explore/` (screening tools), `/people/` (shareholder search), `/announcements/` (disclosures), `/ai/` (AI insights hub). This is the standard programmatic-SEO pattern (one template x N data rows = N indexed pages) — with ~2,000 NSE/BSE-listed companies, that alone is ~2,000+ near-zero-marginal-cost indexed pages, each ranking for "[COMPANY] share price/financials/ratios" queries.

**First-90-days action for Xorlog:** Stand up the same template pattern immediately — one page per NIFTY 500 stock (`/stock/<TICKER>/`) with fundamentals + screener snapshot + journal-worthy stats (e.g. "how often does this stock gap up post-earnings" — a stat only Xorlog's PIT dataset can show), one page per popular screen (`/screens/<name>/`, e.g. "high ROE low debt", "momentum breakouts"), and comparison pages (`/compare/<TICKER-A>-vs-<TICKER-B>/`). Given the firm's own survivorship-bias-free datasets (NIFTY500 PIT membership, options data, 1-min data), Xorlog can differentiate these programmatic pages with genuinely unique stats competitors don't show (e.g., historical earnings-day gap distributions) rather than just repeating the same ratios everyone already publishes — this is a defensible SEO moat, not just page-count spam.

### (c) Waitlist / beta mechanics

[DATA] Jupiter Money's early playbook (as a comparable Indian fintech): invite-only private-beta waitlist, traction built via Instagram + referral loops, referral programs rewarding users for bringing friends, community-building to drive interaction. General best-practice patterns for fintech waitlists: **queue-position rewards** (referring friends moves you up the list — visible, gamified) and **tiered access** (beta → early access → general availability, gated by referral activity or signup timing).

**First-90-days action:** Build a simple referral waitlist (queue-position-by-referral, e.g. via a tool like Prefinery/Viral Loops or a lightweight Supabase-backed custom page) launched off the LinkedIn audience + the first programmatic SEO pages. Gate the earliest cohort (first ~100-200) as high-touch beta testers who give direct feedback — this doubles as user research, not just growth theater.

### (d) Content moat — the Zerodha Varsity model

[DATA] Started 2015 as free, in-depth stock-market/trading/investing education, motivated by the founders' read that "people don't trust brokers because of lack of education." **First-90-days action:** Don't try to out-produce Varsity's decade-deep library from a standing start.
