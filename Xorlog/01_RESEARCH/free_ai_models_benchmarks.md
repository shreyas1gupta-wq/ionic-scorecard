# Free AI Models & Agent Tools — Benchmark Standings (as of 2026-07-16)

Research method: live web search/fetch only (WebSearch + WebFetch), no training-data numbers used (model knowledge cutoff Jan 2026, predates all models discussed here). Every cell below is tagged with source + as-of date. Where independent secondary sources gave conflicting numbers for the same model/benchmark (common in this space — see Reliability Notes), the conflict is stated rather than a number picked arbitrarily.

Landscape note: The public AI-benchmark-blog ecosystem in mid-2026 is dominated by SEO/aggregator sites (BenchLM, TokenMix, CodersEra, MorphLLM, PECollective, etc.) that frequently restate vendor claims or each other with drifting numbers. Primary sources used where possible: artificialanalysis.ai (AA), arena.ai (formerly lmarena.ai), and vendor blogs (anthropic.com, blog.google, x.ai, deepseek/qwen/zhipu/moonshot own pages where reachable).

## Reliability Notes

- **Benchmark-number noise is itself the biggest finding.** Multiple independent secondary "benchmark" sites reported materially different SWE-bench Verified scores for the *same* Claude Sonnet 5 model — 82.1%, 85.2%, and 92.4% — attributing them to different "releases" that do not correspond to any real distinct Anthropic launch found. This suggests SEO content-mill sites in this space are unreliable single-source citations and should not be used for capital-facing decisions without a primary (vendor or artificialanalysis.ai) cross-check.
- **Vendors selectively disclose.** Anthropic's Fable 5 launch materials gave precise SWE-bench Verified/Pro numbers but explicitly did **not** quantify GPQA Diamond — calling it merely "state of the art" — in an area where independent secondary estimates (92.6%) would actually place it *behind* Opus 4.8, GPT-5.5, and Gemini 3.1 Pro. Treat vendor benchmark pages as marketing documents that report favorable numbers and omit unfavorable ones.
- **Anthropic's own SWE-bench Pro figure for Fable 5 (80.0-80.3%) is independently disputed**: techjacksolutions.com reports it was produced with Anthropic's own scaffolding, not a neutral harness, and that independent leaderboards show a different competitive picture. This is a direct case of a vendor benchmark claim not surviving independent scrutiny.
- **Financial-domain hallucination benchmark (JurisTech, 2026-05-14, n=8 models, neutral vs. "truthful" prompts, testing whether models invent values when the correct answer isn't in the options given):** Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview High was the only model rated "Very Good" under both prompt conditions. GPT-5.5 High and GPT-5.4 High also scored well. **Claude Opus 4.7 High scored "Bad"/"Fair"**, GLM 5.1 High scored "Bad"/"Poor", and Mimo V2.5 Pro High scored "Very Bad" under both conditions — i.e., a general finding that even flagship/frontier models fabricate financial figures under adversarial incomplete-data prompts, and the free/open models (GLM) were among the weakest tested. Source: juristech.net, accessed 2026-07-16.
- **FACTS Grounding** (Google DeepMind benchmark measuring whether long-form answers stay strictly grounded in a supplied document, up to 32K tokens): general finding is that even the strongest LLMs "struggle to meet strict grounding requirements on complex, long-context prompts" and frequently hallucinate or give partially grounded answers — no single current leaderboard snapshot with per-model 2026 numbers was found in the sources fetched (flagged "not found" for specific model scores).
- **Kimi K2.6 hallucination rate reported at 39%**, down from predecessor K2.5's 65% — a large improvement release-over-release, but still a meaningfully high fabrication rate for an unsupervised research/finance use case. Source: nerdleveltech.com, accessed 2026-07-16 (methodology/test-set not detailed in the source, so treat as directional not exact).
- **Open-weight Chinese model family (DeepSeek/Qwen/Kimi/GLM) specific weakness color is thin in the sources searched** — no systematic sycophancy or stale-data study surfaced; one anecdote found: "DeepSeek V4 Flash gave a plausible-looking but wrong answer in 2 seconds on a complex debugging task" (source: dev.to/eagerspark, accessed 2026-07-16) — an n=1 anecdote, not a benchmark, flagged as weak evidence.
- **General caution for this entire research task:** because my own training cutoff (Jan 2026) predates every model in this report, and the live web in mid-2026 is saturated with SEO aggregator sites that appear to recycle and drift each other's numbers, no number here should be treated as fully independently verified. Numbers directly pulled from artificialanalysis.ai or arena.ai (both primary aggregator/leaderboard operators, not vendors) are the most trustworthy category in this file; vendor-announcement numbers are the second most trustworthy but self-interested; third-party "benchmark blog" numbers are the least trustworthy and are flagged as such throughout.
