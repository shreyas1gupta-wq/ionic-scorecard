# Long Multiyear Cycle Scan — Macro & Events Desk (Cyrus Daruwalla)
Date: 2026-07-17 | Horizon: next 1-25yr | Purpose: soft CONTEXT/PRIOR inputs for the scorer's regime layer — NOT tradeable formulas, NOT timed signals.

**Honesty gate applied throughout:** a cycle is only marked USABLE if (i) it rests on either hard data with decent sample size, or a near-deterministic mechanical process (demographics), AND (ii) we can state a specific, falsifiable implication rather than a vibe. Cycles resting on n≤3 non-independent historical episodes, disputed dating, or investor-marketing literature are marked SKIP even if directionally plausible — because a scorer that ingests them will overfit to a story, not a distribution.

Data used for light validation: `rnd/panel/macro_state.parquet` (2016-2026 monthly, 127 obs — too short to test anything above ~5yr), `Shreyas_Ionic_AMC/05_DATA_OFFICE/data/credit_spread_baa_aaa.parquet` (1919-2026, [DATA]), `india_10y_gsec_fred.parquet` (2011-2026 only, [DATA]), `india_gdp_usd_annual_fred.parquet` (1960-2026, [DATA]), `us_treasury_yields_daily.parquet` (2000-2026 only in our store — pre-2000 history below is [OPINION]/general knowledge, not verified against our own data), `usdinr_fred_daily.parquet` (1973-2026, [DATA]).

## 1. Dalio long-term debt/credit supercycle (~50-75yr)

- **Real/evidenced?** Partially. The mechanism (debt/GDP rises until debt service becomes unsustainable → deleveraging via default, inflation, or austerity) is documented across ~50 historical case studies (Dalio's own compilation: Weimar, US 1920s-40s, Japan 1990s-, GFC 2008, EU periphery 2010s). [DATA] on the *symptom*: DM government debt/GDP is at multi-decade highs — Japan ~204-230% (IMF/CEIC, 2025-26), US ~125-126%, China ~107-127% (IMF WEO April 2026 datamapper). Advanced-economy debt/GDP jumped from ~70% pre-2008 to 110%+ by 2025 (Visual Capitalist / IMF). Our own `credit_spread_baa_aaa.parquet` (1919-2026) shows the BAA-AAA spread is currently benign, i.e. no acute credit-cycle stress signal today — the debt overhang is a *stock* problem (level), not yet a *flow* problem (spread blowout).
- **Confidently usable?** NO as a timed cycle — "50-75yr periodicity" has essentially n=1-2 non-independent global episodes in modern data; it is a qualitative framework, not a statistical cycle. India has no comparable full-blown sovereign deleveraging episode in its own ~20yr clean dataset, so we cannot even calibrate "where India is in it" from our own data — only borrow the DM read.
- **1-25yr implication (if used at all):** [OPINION] DM debt overhang argues for continued *financial repression* bias (governments prefer negative real rates + nominal growth over austerity) → structurally lower DM real rates over the coming cycle than pre-2008 norms, which is a soft tailwind for capital seeking EM growth (India). This is one plausible derivative use, but it is a story, not a measured base rate.
- **VERDICT: SKIP-AS-NARRATIVE.** Keep ONE fact as a live regime input, not as "the Dalio cycle": DM public-debt/GDP level and the BAA-AAA credit spread (both already in/addable to macro_state.parquet) are legitimate, continuously-updatable data points. Do not encode "50-75yr supercycle stage" as a scorer feature.

## 2. Interest-rate supercycle (~30-40yr)

- **Real/evidenced?** YES, well-evidenced as a *realized historical fact*, not a repeating cycle. US 10Y treasury peaked ~15.8% in Sept 1981 (Volcker era) and fell in a 40-year downtrend to ~0.5% in 2020 — this is public record, essentially undisputed. The turn: yields rose sharply 2021-23 (inflation shock + Fed hiking) and have stayed materially higher since (~4-4.5% handle through 2025-26, per our `us_treasury_yields_daily.parquet`, 2000-2026 range confirms the post-2022 regime shift vs. the 2010s zero-rate era within our own data).
- **Confidently usable?** YES, but only for the REGIME CALL, not for timing the next turn. We have exactly ONE realized full down-leg (1981-2020) and are ~4-5 years into an up/higher-for-longer leg — n=1, so we cannot say "this new regime lasts X years" with any real confidence. What we CAN say with confidence: the 40-year equity/bond tailwind from ever-falling discount rates (multiple expansion via falling risk-free rate) is structurally over for the foreseeable future; valuation work should not assume a repeat of that tailwind.
- **1-25yr implication:** [INFERENCE] Indian equities and INR now face a genuinely different global discount-rate backdrop than any FM/analyst under ~45 has traded through in a "normal" year — higher-for-longer DM real rates raise India's cost of imported capital, keep FII debt flows rate-sensitive, and remove one arm of the 2009-2021 EM bull case (global carry search for yield). This is the single cleanest, most defensible "regime turned" call on this list.
- **VERDICT: USABLE AS CONTEXT** (moderate-high confidence on the regime-turn FACT; explicitly LOW confidence on any implied future path/duration — tag duration claims [OPINION] only).

## 3. Commodity supercycle (~10-20yr)

- **Real/evidenced?** Moderately. Documented capex underinvestment is real: mining/oil-gas capex peaked 2012-14, then fell through the 2016-2020 downturn and has not been rebuilt at the same intensity (multiple industry sources, e.g. capex-cycle data cited by Auspice/Crux/Janus Henderson). Copper-specific: global demand projected +40% by 2040 on electrification/grid buildout against a supply base built for a slower-growth world. BUT — most of the "2020s commodity supercycle" search results are from commodity-fund marketing content (Crux Investor, Triangle Investor, 8Figures, Wright Research) — i.e., self-interested sell-side, a known bias source. Historical full commodity supercycles (1970s, 1998-2008 up-leg, 2011-2020 down-leg) give only ~3 non-independent episodes since reliable index data began — thin for a real base rate.
- **Confidently usable?** As a DIRECTIONAL PRIOR only, moderate confidence: capex-driven supply tightness is a real, checkable fact (not narrative marketing framing); the "supercycle" label and precise multi-year magnitude claims are not.
- **1-25yr implication:** [INFERENCE] India is a large net commodity importer (oil + gold dominate the import bill); a genuine multi-year commodity upcycle is a CAD-widening / INR-depreciation / RBI-hawkish risk that compresses market multiples even while energy/metals/PSU-commodity names benefit and input-cost-sensitive names (autos, paints, tyres, FMCG) get squeezed. This maps to specific book exposure (see below), unlike most cycles on this list.
- **VERDICT: USABLE AS CONTEXT** (moderate confidence, flag small-n and vendor-bias on magnitude claims — use only the capex-underinvestment fact and the India-importer sensitivity, not "supercycle" timing).

## 4. India demographic dividend (working-age population cycle)

- **Real/evidenced?** YES — the most confidently usable item on this entire list. This is close to arithmetic, not behavior: the relevant cohorts are already born, so the projection is a demographic accounting identity, not a forecast of human choices. [DATA/well-sourced OPINION-free facts]: working-age (15-64) share ≈68% in 2025; Economic Survey 2018-19 projects the dividend peaking ~2041 (working-age 20-59 share ≈59%), with the total dependency ratio reaching its low point (~44%, some estimates lower ~31% by 2030 depending on age-band definition) somewhere in the 2030-2040 window; the working-age bulge persists out to roughly 2055 (UNFPA/Economic Survey estimates cited across Carnegie, Data For India, UNFPA sources).
- **Confidently usable?** YES, with one honest caveat flagged by Carnegie/Data For India themselves: the *dividend* (growth payoff) is conditional on job creation and productive absorption of the cohort — the demographic MATH is near-certain, the ECONOMIC PAYOFF is not automatic. We should encode the math as fact and the payoff as a conditional assumption.
- **1-25yr implication:** [DATA-backed INFERENCE] India's working-age share keeps rising/stays elevated through at least the mid-2030s-2040s, a genuine multi-decade structural tailwind for consumption growth, labor supply, and potential GDP growth — the single most India-specific, least-narrative, longest-duration usable prior in this scan. It argues for a persistent (not cyclical) bullish bias on India domestic-consumption/financialization themes over the *next 25 years specifically because that is the exact window this cycle covers* — but should NOT be used to time entries/exits, only to set the multi-year regime prior.
- **VERDICT: USABLE AS CONTEXT — HIGHEST CONFIDENCE on this list.**

## 5. Kondratiev / tech-productivity long wave (~50yr)

- **Real/evidenced?** NO, in any statistically rigorous sense. Different Kondratiev-wave scholars date the same claimed waves differently by years to decades; the periodicity is retrofitted after the fact each time (classic overfitting-to-history pattern); mainstream economics treats it as heterodox/contested, not a validated empirical regularity. n≈4-5 claimed waves since the 1780s, none independently dated, no agreed mechanism.
- **VERDICT: SKIP-AS-NARRATIVE.** This is the clearest "story, not signal" item in the whole scan — do not let it anywhere near the scorer, even as a soft prior.

## 5b. AI / electrification capex cycle (~10-15yr)

- **Real/evidenced?** YES as a CURRENT, observable, data-backed capex phenomenon (hyperscaler capex prints, grid/power buildout, India's own data-center and renewable capacity pipeline, order books already tracked by the industrials desk) — but it is a THEME, not a cycle: n=1 (no prior AI-capex cycle exists to compare against or to estimate a repeat periodicity from), and it is already covered by fundamental/sector research (`analyst-industrials-rohan-deshmukh`, sector_context.parquet) rather than being new information for a macro regime layer.
- **VERDICT: SKIP-AS-CYCLE-FOR-THIS-SCORER** (real, but wrong shelf — it belongs in sector/fundamental coverage, not the regime-layer prior list; including it here would double-count an exposure the analyst desk already prices).

## 6. USD / reserve-currency cycle (~15-20yr DXY)

- **Real/evidenced?** Partially. DXY has had genuine multi-year swings since the 1973 float (1980s peak → 1985 Plaza Accord decline → mid/late-90s dollar strength → 2002-2011 weakness → 2011-2020ish strength → 2020-22 spike → since easing) but each turn was driven by a specific policy event (Plaza Accord, GFC, QE, 2022 Fed hiking cycle) rather than an inherent 15-20yr periodicity — this is pattern-matching across ~2-3 non-independent episodes, not a validated cycle.
- **Confidently usable?** NOT as a "cycle to time." The DXY LEVEL itself, however, is already a live monitored variable in `macro_state.parquet` (dxy column, 2016-2026) and is exactly the kind of thing the desk already tracks weekly (strong-dollar periods → FII/EM outflow pressure, INR weakness — well-established real-time relationship, not a long cycle).
- **VERDICT: SKIP-AS-NARRATIVE for the "15-20yr cycle" framing.** Do not invent a new cycle feature here — the desk already has the live regime variable (DXY level/trend) doing this job in real time; adding a "cycle stage" on top would be double-counting a story on top of a fact we already track properly.

## 7. Geopolitical / deglobalization / China+1 (~10-20yr)

- **Real/evidenced?** YES, reasonably well — though it is a structural ONE-DIRECTIONAL SHIFT, not an oscillating "cycle" (the label is a slight misnomer). Evidence base: US-China tariff/export-control trajectory since 2018 (bipartisan, both administrations), FDI diversion data toward India/Vietnam/Mexico, India's PLI scheme results, and repeated "China+1" commentary in electronics/manufacturing earnings calls (tracked live by `analyst-it-karan-malhotra` / `analyst-industrials-rohan-deshmukh`).
- **Confidently usable?** YES, moderate-high confidence on DIRECTION (continued diversification pressure, bipartisan US-China friction structurally intact) over 10-20yr; LOW confidence on PACE/MAGNITUDE (reversible if US-China relations materially de-escalate; India's execution/infra bottlenecks could cap how much share it actually captures vs. Vietnam/Mexico).
- **1-25yr implication:** [INFERENCE] Structural, multi-year tailwind for India manufacturing/PLI-beneficiary sectors and capex-cycle names, and a slow, non-linear positive for India's weight in global equity allocation — but should be sized as a directional tilt, not a timed cycle turn.
- **VERDICT: USABLE AS CONTEXT** (moderate-high confidence on direction, explicitly low confidence on magnitude/pace — flag that it is a structural shift, not an oscillating cycle, so "turning points" language does not apply).

## SHORTLIST — confidently usable as context/prior (regime layer)

1. **India demographic dividend** — working-age share/dependency-ratio trough falls in the 2030-2040 window, bulge persists to ~2055; near-arithmetic certainty on the math, conditional on job absorption for the growth payoff. HIGHEST confidence item on this scan.
2. **Interest-rate regime turn (post-2022)** — the 1981-2020 secular falling-rate tailwind for equity/duration multiples is over; higher-for-longer DM real rates are the new backdrop. High confidence on the fact of the turn; explicitly no confidence on how long the new regime lasts (n=1).
3. **China+1 / deglobalization structural shift** — bipartisan US-China friction + FDI diversion is a real, data-backed, multi-year tailwind for India manufacturing/PLI names; moderate-high confidence on direction, low confidence on pace/magnitude. Structural shift, not an oscillating cycle — don't look for "turning points."
4. **Commodity capex-underinvestment / India-importer sensitivity** — real capex shortfall since 2014 is a checkable fact; India's CAD/INR/multiple sensitivity to a genuine commodity upleg is a specific, mappable book exposure. Moderate confidence; treat magnitude claims from "supercycle" literature as vendor-biased, use only the capex-shortfall + import-sensitivity logic.
