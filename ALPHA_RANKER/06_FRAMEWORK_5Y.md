# 06 — Framework: 5-YEAR lens (discretionary / growth-valuation-quality-moat heavy)

**Character:** slowest-changing, narrative- and judgment-heavy. The engine here is *compounding* — earnings growth × moat durability × reinvestment runway × management integrity × price paid. Price action matters **only for the entry point**. Updated **semi-annually → annually**. Devil's-advocate pass **mandatory**; forensic gate heavy.

**Universe:** NIFTY 750.

## Factor library (these are judgment-scored with quant support, not cutoffs)
### Growth & Runway (dominant)
- Earnings-compounding trajectory over a full cycle; sustainable growth rate = ROIC × reinvestment rate.
- **Reinvestment runway:** ROIC vs WACC spread AND the size of the opportunity to redeploy capital at that spread (a wide spread with nowhere to reinvest ≈ a dividend stock, not a compounder).
- TAM growth, penetration/formalization runway, optionality (adjacent markets, pricing power).

### Moat & Industry structure (dominant)
- Moat *type* and *direction* (widening/stable/eroding): brand, network, switching cost, cost advantage, regulatory/licenses, distribution.
- Industry structure: consolidation vs fragmentation, pricing power, entry barriers, competitive intensity.
- **Disruption risk (critical):** technology/regulatory obsolescence. This is the axis that turns "quality" into a trap (AI vs IT-services, EV vs ICE ancillaries, D2C vs distribution moats). Scored explicitly and can veto.

### Management & Promoter (dominant, mostly downside-protective)
- Capital-allocation track record (past ROIC on incremental capex/M&A, buyback/dividend discipline).
- Integrity & governance (promoter conduct, related-party history, minority-shareholder treatment), skin-in-the-game.
- Guidance promise-vs-delivery over years (`08` concall rubric, multi-year).

### Quality & Balance-sheet resilience (high)
- Through-cycle ROCE/ROE consistency, margin stability, FCF generation.
- Balance-sheet resilience: net-debt/EBITDA through downturns, refinancing/contingent-liability risk, ability to survive a 2008/2020-type stress (feed `stress-replay`).

### Valuation entry (high — but quality can be paid up)
- Entry multiple vs own long history and vs the growth+quality it deserves; margin of safety.
- Reverse-DCF sanity: what growth is the current price implying, and is that beatable? (Not a point-estimate DCF — an implied-expectations check.)
- Quality-vs-value stance (brief Q8): a truly durable compounder can be bought at a fair price rather than a cheap one, but **overpaying caps forward returns** — so valuation is a *return-magnitude* dampener, not a binary gate.

### Forensic (heavy gate)
- Full `08` overlay, multi-year lens (10y cash-flow reality, capex-vs-depreciation, goodwill/impairment for serial acquirers). Over 5 years, forensic problems almost always surface — so this gate is heavier here than at 1M.

## Scoring specifics (5Y)
- Weight prior: Growth 0.30, Value 0.25, Quality 0.25, Momentum 0.05, Catalyst 0.05, Forensic 0.10 (gate). Sentiment ~0.
- **Regime dependence is low** for the *business* factors (a compounder rides through cycles) but **entry valuation is regime-sensitive** — in expensive markets demand more margin of safety; in cheap markets accept more. The **structural-disruption axis is regime-exempt** (it bites regardless).
- Calibrate score→P on **5-year forward returns** (fewer independent samples → wider uncertainty bands, lean more on cross-sectional and analog evidence; be honest about small-n).
- **Cross-horizon tax:** a large-negative 1M score subtracts a small amount (entry-timing penalty, `02` Step 9).

## Thesis paragraph style (5Y)
Discretionary and narrative: the compounding engine (what grows and why it's durable), the moat and its direction, management/capital-allocation read, the price paid vs value, and the **thesis-break triggers** (what would make you sell: moat erosion evidence, capital-allocation betrayal, governance break, structural disruption confirmation). One rich paragraph.

## Exit / review discipline (5Y)
- Re-score semi-annual/annual; the bar to *sell* is high and specific — thesis-break triggers written at entry, not price-driven.
- Sell on: moat-erosion evidence, sustained ROIC decline below cost of capital, capital-allocation betrayal, governance/forensic break, or the disruption thesis confirming. NOT on drawdowns alone.
