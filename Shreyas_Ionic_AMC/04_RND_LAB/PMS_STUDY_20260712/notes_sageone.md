# SageOne Investment Managers (Samit Vartak) — Buy-Side Study Notes
Researched 2026-07-11/12. Web-only research (public letters, factsheets, interviews). Unbiased — includes criticism/failure modes.

- SageOne Investment Advisors/Managers LLP, founded 2012, Pune-based, Founder & CIO **Samit Vartak** (CFA, MBA Olin/Wash U, engineering SPCE Mumbai Univ; ex-Gap Inc corporate strategy, PwC Consulting, Deloitte & E&Y valuation advisory in the US 1997-2006).

Mid & smallcap, market-cap RANK 101st-600th (roughly INR 10,000-85,000 cr mcap). 12-20 stock mandate; 18 held now, top-10 = 65% of book. AUM ~INR 4,290 cr (PMS only) / ~7,300 cr firm-wide incl. ## 1. Philosophy — actual documented approach (not marketing)
Self-described discipline (his words, Safal Niveshak 2016 + factsheets): "**SAGE**" = Sustainable, Accountable, Growth-oriented, Efficient. In practice it is a **sector-first, bottom-up, quantamental growth-quality-at-reasonable-price** process:

> "When you look at a business and if you get a feeling 'I wish I owned this business', that's the kind of business we are looking for... If a business is inferior, then the price of the stock does not matter and it would not interest us."

Two explicitly stated pillars:
1. **Business quality first, valuation discipline second** (a sequencing choice he says he changed early in his career — see §4 mistakes). Business analysis must convince him the company deserves to be owned regardless of price before valuation is even considered.
2. **Risk management via business quality + reasonable diversification, NOT via extreme diversification or buying statistically-cheap sub-par businesses.** ("I believe in risk management not by seeking extreme diversification or buying sub-par businesses at low multiples, but by building a reasonably diversified portfolio of high quality businesses having long term competitive advantages in attractive and high growth industries.")

He explicitly rejects blind transplant of US-style "wide moat, hold forever" investing onto India: India's regulatory/competitive backdrop (FDI policy, tax structure, IP regime) moves too fast for multi-decade moat assumptions; portfolio must be watched continuously and exited fast when the environment changes (cites cotton-seed regulatory change / Kaveri Seeds).

Objective stated in the current factsheet: "generate meaningful alpha (5-7% at gross level)" vs BSE500 TRI, via "fundamental bottom-up research... aided by plant visits, channel checks & multiple stakeholder interactions" — i.e., the stated edge source is explicitly PRIMARY/QUALITATIVE research, not a quant screen. The quant screen (see §2) is a funnel to get to a "fishing pond," not the final selection tool.

## 2. The stated selection funnel (most mechanically detailed source: Safal Niveshak/Value Investing Almanack interview, March 2016)
Step-by-step, in his own words:
1. **Universe**: top ~1,600 Indian companies by market cap.
2. **Sector screen** (his emphasis: "focusing on the right sectors is half the battle won" — likens it to picking the right country to find a marathon champion): for every sector, evaluate over multiple down-cycles (not just recent history):
   - sustainable profitability (ROE/ROCE)
   - volatility in margins
   - leverage
   - topline growth
   - cash-generating history
   → shortlist "top sectors."
3. **Company screen within top sectors** → ~300 companies.
4. **Governance/quality elimination pass**: remove companies with a history of bad corporate governance, loose/questionable accounting policy, or inefficient capital allocation → "fishing pond" of **~150 companies**.
5. **Growth filter** on the fishing pond: select companies with credible, analyzable potential to grow **topline >20% (ideally >25%) with sustainable net margins over a 3-5 year horizon.** Explicit logic: India's nominal GDP growth is ~14%, so 20-25%+ growth is only credible if there's an identifiable **share-gain enabler** — capturing share from unorganized players, PSU competitors, other organized domestic competitors, or export share gain from overseas competitors. They "consciously look for such enablers of growth," not just macro tailwind names.
6. **Valuation** — NOT a fixed PE/PEG cutoff (he explicitly says his 1999-2001 blow-up came from PE/PEG-driven investing — see §4). Instead a duration-of-competitive-advantage / residual-growth framework:
   - Growth = Reinvestment rate × ROE (standard sustainable-growth-rate identity).
   - The "fair" forward PE is almost entirely a function of **how many years** the high growth/high ROE combination can be sustained (the Competitive-Advantage-Period), not the growth rate or ROE alone. His worked numerical example: same company (25% growth, 50% ROE) is worth ~12x fwd PE if growth decays to 10% after year 2, vs ~32x fwd PE if 25% growth is sustained to year 20 — over 2.5x valuation spread for the same near-term numbers, purely from a duration assumption.
   - Theoretical extreme he states: if ROCE > WACC forever with continued growth, fair PE tends to infinity; if ROCE < WACC, fair value tends to zero — i.e., "no PE is inherently high or low," it's a function of ROCE-vs-WACC spread and its duration.
   - Explicit margin-of-safety technique: **deliberately shorten your own assumed duration** (e.g., if you believe 25% growth can run 7 years, model only 4 years) and check if the current price is still attractive under the haircut assumption.
7. **Portfolio construction / position sizing** (from current factsheets, which likely reflect the mature/current version of the discipline, not necessarily 2016):
   - SCP: 12-20 holdings mandate (18 actual), **single-stock exposure range 3%-9%**, top-10 holdings = 65% of book.
   - **Sector cap: max 25% of portfolio in any one sector** (stated identically across SCP, Small&Micro, and Diversified products).
   - **Business-group cap: max 20% exposure to any single business/promoter group** (i.e., a cross-stock concentration limit distinct from the sector cap — catches conglomerate-family risk, e.g. multiple listed entities of one promoter).
   - Current portfolio fundamentals disclosed (SCP, Feb 2026): forward PE (FY27/28) 25x/20.2x, ROCE 22%, Debt/Equity 0.1x, earnings growth (today→FY28 CAGR) median 25% / weighted-avg 29% — i.e., the stated 20-25%+ growth and 20%+ ROCE screens are visibly still being enforced in the live book.
8. **Market-cap bands are PRODUCT-SPECIFIC, not a single firm-wide rule**: SCP = rank 101-600; Small&Micro = rank 400-1200; Large&Midcap/Diversified = large-cap-tilted (63% large, 34% mid). This is a meaningful nuance — "SageOne's rule" is not one market-cap band, it's a family of bands sold as different products.
9. **Cash is NOT fixed** — flexed based on his own tracking of sector-level valuation multiples and margin-sustainability. Concrete historical example: pre-GFC he computed a combined downside risk of ~55% (40% from mean-reverting PE multiples + 24% from mean-reverting net margins, both at "all-time highs" pre-2008) and raised cash accordingly — this cushioned (but did not eliminate) the 2008 drawdown for his personal portfolio (he was not yet managing external money in 2008).
10. **Holding period**: stated investment horizon per position is 3-5 years, but he says "shorter" holding periods are appropriate for Indian companies vs. what a US quality investor would use, requiring continuous fundamental tracking. Third-party summary (PMS AIF World, pre-existing knowledge) puts realized portfolio turnover at 80-90% of names changing within 5 years — i.e., despite "long-term" framing, realized churn is high, consistent with the "exit fast when environment changes" discipline dominating over "buy and forget."

## 3. Exit / sell discipline (explicit hierarchy, his words)
He states three reasons for exits, in descending order of how "brutal"/non-negotiable they are:
1. **Business-environment deterioration** — regulatory change (e.g. cotton-seed regulation → Kaveri Seeds) or rising competitive intensity that makes the 20% growth hurdle no longer achievable. Described as the most common and least flexible trigger ("brutal in the first two aspects").
2. **Capital-allocation red flags** — bad management decisions on capital allocation generally, or — specifically for financial-sector holdings — **relaxed lending standards**. Concrete example: **J&K Bank** exited "at the first signs of its lending standards deteriorating" — cited as a exit that "worked out well."
3. **Valuation running beyond comfort** — the softest trigger, applied with more flexibility/patience than (1) and (2). Concrete counter-example, self-admitted mistake: **La Opala** — exited too early purely on valuation discomfort, banked a 5x gain, but the stock then ran another 5x after they sold. Explicit lesson: the valuation-based exit leg is the one most prone to giving up further compounding.

## 4. Self-admitted mistakes / behavioral failure modes (from his own mouth — important for unbiased assessment)
- **Origin story (2001 personal wipeout)**: In 1999 (dot-com peak) he "followed analyst recommendations, looked at simple valuation metrics such as PE ratio/PEG ratio, believed in forecasted numbers of analysts, and invested in companies where buy recommendations were highest." Used derivatives/margin to try to recover losses as the market fell. "By 2001 my entire portfolio was wiped off." He took a career break, did the CFA, and rebuilt his approach around "protecting capital was the primary goal, return on capital secondary." **This is the direct ancestor of SageOne's current no-PE/PEG-shortcut, business-first, no-leverage discipline** — i.e., today's discipline is explicitly a reaction to a documented personal blow-up, not an abstract ideal.
- **Disposition-effect / anchoring bias (explicitly unresolved, his words)**: "Holding something in your portfolio is as good as entering that stock at current market price... I have held on to positions even if I would not be comfortable buying at current market price. You may justify it by giving false comfort of having bought at a much lower price, but it's a behavioural mistake... I can't say that I have mastered them 100%."
- **Confirmation bias on bad news**: "The most common [mistake] is in the event of any bad news... coming with regards to a portfolio company that you have held for some time and have developed connect with. The natural tendency is to find arguments against the bad news and try and shove it under the carpet. You try talking to the management and typically they are the worst people to talk to in such events because they will give you great comfort in their business as always." → Structural mitigant: talk to **competitors and bearish analysts**, not co-investors with a vested long interest; internal partners act as devil's advocate.
- **Valuation-anchored premature exit**: La Opala (see §3) — sold a name up 5x purely on valuation nervousness; it then ran another 5x.
- **Style-timing admission (2014-2016 period)**: "Last couple of years have been really tough, since nothing really changed in India on the ground but the expectations from the new government went through the roof. In fact, the valuations rose when earnings were coming down in reality with global environment worsening." — A direct, self-acknowledged admission that the quality/growth style was squeezed by a multiple-expansion regime disconnected from fundamentals (2014-16 Modi-election euphoria period), forcing a defensive cash posture. This is a genuine "style got out of sync with the market" data point, not survivorship-biased marketing.
- **2008 GFC**: "One can't escape the carnage if you are a long-only investor in such periods" — explicit acknowledgment that the strategy has NO structural hedge against a systemic downturn; only partial cushioning via tactical cash raised from valuation/margin mean-reversion analysis (see §2.9). This was managing only his own money at the time, not client capital.

## 5. Third-party / outside criticism (not from SageOne itself — the important "unbiased" half)
- **IME Capital (imecapital.in) on SageOne Small & Micro Cap Portfolio**: explicitly states the fund "runs a significantly higher-risk portfolio even relative to other small-cap strategies," has "underperformed peers & is amongst the worst performing schemes in this category in the last 5-year trailing returns" (benchmarked against Nifty Smallcap 250, with a stated **-3.7% alpha** on that comparison — note this conflicts with the +7.7pp since-inception alpha SageOne itself reports vs BSE500; different benchmarks, different windows, both are simultaneously "true" and it's a good example of benchmark-shopping risk in self-reported PMS factsheets). IME's explicit house call: "**do not recommend the strategy at this stage**" (as of their review date) despite an "Upgrade" directional/strategic rating — i.e., a real analyst shop is on record NOT recommending one of SageOne's live products, which is a meaningfully unbiased data point to weigh.
- **IME Capital on the firm generally**: flags co-founder departures (Kuntal Shah, Manish Jain) as a documented leadership-continuity fact.
- **Consistency**: per available return-series commentary, the flagship Mid & Small Cap product has "outperformed and underperformed the benchmark roughly in half the years" — i.e., there is no claim (from any source, including SageOne's own factsheet, which shows 76% "outperforming periods" on a rolling basis, presumably 3-yr rolling, not annual) that the strategy beats its benchmark in most individual YEARS; the edge is a long-horizon compounding claim, with real annual-level noise/dispersion.
- **PMS Bazaar** (June 2022 blog, "Continuous Drawdown for the third month, PMS Performance Dips In June 2022") references the broad 2022 PMS-industry drawdown; SageOne funds are covered by this dataset but we could not isolate SageOne-specific 2022 drawdown magnitude from what's freely accessible (may be paywalled) — flagged as an open item if we want the exact number.
- **Trailing negative alpha on the newest product**: SageOne's own PMS AIF World data for the Large & Midcap/"Diversified" Portfolio shows **1-yr alpha of -1.23%** (Beta 1.09) — i.e., SageOne's own self-reported numbers show a currently-live product underperforming risk-adjusted over the trailing year, at the same time their older flagship reports a strong since-inception number. Good illustration that "since-inception CAGR" headlines can coexist with real recent underperformance on the newer, less-mature product.
- **Capacity acknowledgment**: SCP was **closed to new fundraising for a full year, late 2017 through 2018** — a tacit admission that the 101-600 market-cap-rank "sweet spot" has real absorptive-capacity limits, i.e., their own edge is not infinitely scalable, which matters if we ever wanted to size a similar factor.

## 7. Codability — mapping to our data (PIT quarterly fundamentals, daily OHLCV, NIFTY500 PIT universe)

### Directly codable (high confidence)
- **Growth screen**: trailing sales/PAT/EPS growth > 20% (or >25%) — use PIT quarterly fundamentals with `available_date`, NOT quarter-end (landmine #3 in our CLAUDE.md). Codable now.
- **Profitability screen**: ROE and ROCE > 20% — codable from annual fundamentals; ROCE needs EBIT + capital employed (debt+equity-cash), check field completeness in `india_fundamentals_mc/Train.parquet` (avoid the corrupt `annual_report` column per landmine #5).
- **Leverage discipline**: low Debt/Equity (current live SCP book runs 0.1x) — codable if balance-sheet debt field present.
- **Position/sector/market-cap-band limits**: max 25% sector, market-cap RANK band (101-600 or 400-1200 depending which product we're emulating) — mechanically enforceable in a backtest using month-end market cap from the NIFTY500 PIT universe file.
- **Holding-period/turnover cap**: codable as a straightforward backtest rule.
- **Crude valuation-vs-growth proxy**: PEG-style filter (trailing PE ÷ trailing growth) as an approximation of his duration-of-growth logic — NOT the same as his actual forward-duration judgment, but a defensible codable proxy. Could also proxy "growth persistence/quality" via stability of trailing YoY quarterly growth over 8-12 quarters (low stdev = more likely durable), as a rough stand-in for his qualitative confidence in Competitive-Advantage-Period length.
- **Lagging exit proxy for "growth hurdle breached"**: trailing 4-quarter sales/EPS growth falling below a threshold (e.g. <10-15%) for 2 consecutive quarters — a noisy, backward-looking approximation of his qualitative "business environment deteriorating" trigger.

### Partially codable / needs new data we may already have or could build cheaply
- **"Business group" 20% cap** — requires a promoter/group mapping table (e.g. Tata/Adani/Birla family tagging across tickers). Not in our current datasets; would need manual or scraped BSE-group tagging.
- **Governance/accounting-forensics elimination pass (300→150 names)** — his single most emphasized qualitative filter. We could partially proxy with PIT-derivable red flags (cash-flow-from-ops vs PAT divergence, auditor changes if disclosed, promoter pledge % if available) but related-party-transaction disclosures and forensic detail are NOT in our fundamentals dataset today — this would be a real data-acquisition project, not a quick add.

### NOT codable with what we have (his stated real edge)
- **Sector-level multi-cycle screening** (his own multi-decade tracking of margin/ROE/leverage by sector through full down-cycles) — no equivalent structured, long-history sector dataset on our side.
- **Forward-growth-duration conviction** (the actual core of his valuation framework — how many years of 20%+ growth is credible) — this is explicitly a forward qualitative judgment informed by channel checks/plant visits/management meetings we do not have access to; we only have trailing data.
- **Management-quality / capital-allocation-skill judgment, competitor & bearish-analyst devil's-advocate calls** — entirely qualitative/primary-research based, unavailable to us.
- **Regulatory-change and competitive-intensity monitoring** (his #1 stated exit trigger) — requires news/text monitoring we don't systematically run; not derivable from price/fundamental data.

### Bottom line
A codable "SageOne-style" screen (growth>20-25%, ROE/ROCE>20%, low leverage, market-cap rank band, sector/position caps, PEG-style valuation filter, growth-persistence proxy, lagging growth-deterioration exit) would capture roughly the mechanical skeleton of his process but explicitly NOT the parts he and outside reviewers both flag as doing the real work: the governance/forensic elimination pass, the forward-duration judgment behind valuation, and continuous regulatory/competitive monitoring behind his exit discipline. Any backtest of a "SageOne clone" should be labeled as testing the quant skeleton only, with a large expected gap vs. his live results attributable to the qualitative layer we can't replicate — and per §5, even SageOne's OWN live products show real dispersion (negative trailing alpha on the newest vehicle, worst-in-category standing on the small/microcap vehicle per an independent reviewer), so we should not assume the qualitative layer is a guaranteed source of edge either.
