## 1. Documented philosophy (their own words, verbatim where found)
- Core belief: **"Good companies aren't always great investments"**, especially when cycles dominate outcomes.
- **"Macros matter, but mining them as an investment edge is nearly impossible."** — i.e., they explicitly disclaim macro-timing as their edge, despite running a cycle-aware framework (an interesting internal tension — their edge is claimed to be reading cycles in valuation/earnings/sector/style, NOT forecasting GDP/rates/FX).
- **"Share price growth is more a function of earnings growth"** — but immediately qualified: earnings growth is "only one variable"; valuation re-rating/de-rating is the other, and they cite a Nifty 20-year return decomposition (in an ET op-ed, "Valuations matter, but stories sell better" — blog teaser only, full ET article not accessible, paywalled/not indexed) breaking returns into earnings growth vs. multiple re-rating.
- Risk definition: risk = **permanent loss of capital + opportunity cost**, explicitly NOT short-term volatility/standard-deviation. (Classic quality/value-investor framing, though notably their own factsheet still publishes Beta/Alpha/Std Dev as if volatility mattered for marketing.)
- Perception-vs-reality / cycle model (from philosophy page): "nothing of consequence goes up or down in a straight line — they operate in cycles." Illustrated pattern: (1) discovery phase — a high-quality company's stock re-rates faster than fundamentals; (2) market "over-pays" for future growth; (3) a long stagnation phase where earnings keep compounding but the stock goes nowhere for potentially a decade+ (this is their explicit **quality-stagnation trap** thesis — i.e., they publicly warn against exactly the failure mode we were asked to check for; whether they themselves avoid it is unverified).
- Four cycle dimensions they claim to track: **market-cap cycles, sector cycles, investment-style cycles (growth vs value, aggressive vs defensive), and individual-stock cycles.**
- Portfolio is **"non-model"**: clients are not fully invested from day 1; onboarding is itself a timing/allocation decision, not pure stock-picking.
- 2025 "five ideas for the year" (from a Moneycontrol piece via TradingView, Jigar Mistry, paywalled — got summary only):
  1. Earnings slowdown "may not be as temporary as we think"; expects surprises (positive, implied) in consumption, large private banks, healthcare.
  2. "Flows can't drive fundamentals... flows are a dependent variable, not an independent variable" — explicit rejection of flow-based/liquidity narratives as a reason to buy.
  3. Small/micro-cap free-float market cap is ~1/3 of large-cap, yet MF AUM allocated to each is "almost similar" — a structural flow imbalance he says creates a "virtuous cycle... until self-organized criticality takes over" (i.e., a bubble-and-reversal dynamic in small/microcaps); his call was **large caps outperform small/microcaps over the next year**.
  4. "Start thinking of businesses where implied valuation is so high that even if earnings growth continues, the stock price may not react positively for an extended period" — i.e., explicit valuation-ceiling awareness (ties back to the stagnation-trap thesis).
  5. **"Aligning portfolio beta can be a more effective strategy than making large cash calls"** — i.e., they prefer adjusting portfolio beta/composition (their Core/Satellite dial) over holding outright cash, as the tool for risk-on/risk-off positioning.

## 4. Failure modes / criticisms / bad periods (unbiased — look for the bad, not just the good)
- One independent aggregator explicitly stated: **"performance consistency has been mixed, with the fund having outperformed & underperformed the benchmark roughly in half the years"** (paraphrased from search-engine synthesis of a review page — could not pin to one single named reviewer/URL on direct fetch, treat as directionally credible but re-verify the exact source before quoting to Principal).
- Structural risk flagged by an independent reviewer (imecapital.in) even in an otherwise favorable write-up: **"Success hinges on accurately gauging underlying market dynamics"** — i.e., the entire edge is a bet on the PMs correctly calling cycle turns (market-cap, sector, style). When the call is wrong, this is explicitly a source of underperformance, not just tracking error.
- Firm is young (launched 2016) — one full-cycle historical drawdown/crisis period (COVID Mar-2020) plus the 2022 rate-hike/Russia-Ukraine drawdown is about the extent of the stress-tested history; no track record through a full decade-scale "quality stagnation" period of the kind their OWN philosophy warns about. In other words: **they have publicly diagnosed the quality-trap/multi-year-stagnation failure mode as a risk for OTHERS' style, but their own strategy has not yet lived through a decade to prove they avoid it themselves.**
- The tactical market-cap-band shifts (60% smallcap in 2020-21 → 50% large-cap in 2022) mean the strategy carries real **smallcap-concentration / illiquidity risk in "aggressive stance" periods** — consistent with the CLAUDE.md house landmine on circuit/volume fills and thin-liquidity slippage for momentum/cyclical books generally; no specific Buoyant drawdown number for a smallcap-heavy quarter was found in public sources (their factsheets would have monthly NAV data needed to compute this directly — recommend pulling the actual factsheet PDF/XLS from buoyantcap.com/our-factsheets/ for a rigorous max-drawdown calc rather than relying on secondary aggregator claims).
- No standardized/audited long-run drawdown, Sharpe, Sortino, or worst-12-month numbers were surfaced in ANY of the sources fetched (pmsbazaar, pmsaifworld, lamfindia, imecapital) beyond the single 1-year Beta/Std Dev triplet above. This is itself a finding: **public PMS aggregators for Buoyant do not disclose drawdown risk metrics**, which is a real transparency gap relative to what a rigorous quant due-diligence process would want.
- AUM figure discrepancy (₹12,068 Cr vs ₹7,442 Cr across near-contemporaneous sources) is itself a small governance/data-hygiene flag for third-party aggregators, though may simply be PMS-vs-PMS+AIF scope difference — not necessarily Buoyant's fault.

## 5. Codability assessment — what could be built with PIT fundamentals + OHLCV + NIFTY500 universe, and what we lack
### Plausibly codable (partial replication)
- **Concentrated N-stock portfolio (18-30 names)**: trivial position-count constraint.
- **"Core" quality tilt**: approximate with PIT ROE/ROCE (e.g., top-quartile/decile trailing 3-5yr average), earnings stability (low std-dev of YoY EPS growth), and possibly a dividend-payout screen — all computable from our quarterly PIT fundamentals + TTM growth fields.
- **"Industry leader not challenger"**: roughly proxy via market-cap rank within sector/sub-sector (top 1-2 by market cap) — codable if we have a sector classification map for NIFTY500.
- **Satellite cyclical sleeve by sector list**: metals, CVs, NBFCs are explicit named sectors — codable as a sector-tag filter, though the ENTRY TIMING into/out of these cyclicals ("turnaround" identification) is the qualitative part we cannot replicate.
- **Relative valuation vs own history** (their "leaders that look expensive but offer asymmetric upside"): codable as PE/PB percentile vs the stock's own 5-10yr trailing distribution, using PIT daily OHLCV + PIT EPS/BVPS — a legitimate value-vs-history factor.
- **A crude systematic proxy for their "aggressive vs defensive stance"**: e.g., NIFTY trailing-PE percentile, credit-spread proxy, or market breadth as a regime signal to tilt a mechanical Core/Satellite weight — but note this would be OUR OWN invented proxy standing in for their qualitative, IC-unanimous, cross-cycle judgment call; it is NOT their documented rule and should never be labeled as "replicating Buoyant" without that caveat.

### NOT codable / data we lack
- **The actual valuation paradigm** referenced in the "Valuations matter, but stories sell better" piece was never surfaced (paywalled ET article) — we do not have their real quantitative valuation rule, only marketing-page gestures at it. Any "Buoyant-style valuation screen" we build would be OUR OWN invention, not a replication.
- **No disclosed numeric growth/ROE/PE thresholds anywhere** — unlike some other PMS managers (e.g., who publicly state "ROE>15%, growth>20%"), Buoyant has NOT published a specific screen. Codability here means we would be reverse-engineering a plausible rule from returns/holdings data (position-level factsheet history), not implementing their stated rule.
- **Cycle-call judgment** (their actual claimed edge: reading market-cap/sector/style/stock cycles) is fundamentally a qualitative, cross-asset, macro-aware, IC-debated process — not a mechanical screen, and explicitly NOT reducible to a backtest signal by their own framing ("mining macro as an edge is nearly impossible" — meaning THEY don't think it's systematizable either).
- **On-the-ground research inputs**: management meetings, conference calls, dealer/channel checks (implied by "detailed research with strong on-the-ground checks" per one bio) — outside our PIT fundamentals + OHLCV dataset entirely.
- **Turnaround/special-situation identification** for the Satellite sleeve requires event-level catalysts (management change, debt restructuring, capacity commissioning) not present in standard quarterly fundamentals.
- **Sector cycle timing** (e.g., knowing metals or NBFC or CV cycle stage) requires commodity-price/credit-cycle/rate-cycle context beyond company-level PIT fundamentals — would need external macro series we may or may not have in DATA_CATALOG.

### Bottom line for R&D Lab
Buoyant is fundamentally a **discretionary, judgment-driven, cycle-timing overlay on top of a quality/value stock-selection philosophy**, run by a 3-person unanimous committee with 20+ year sector expertise each. Very little of their actual edge (the cycle-call, the undisclosed valuation paradigm, the qualitative Core/Satellite dial) is publicly codified into numeric rules. What IS codable (quality tilt via ROE/ROCE, sector-cyclical tagging, valuation-vs-own-history) would only replicate the STATIC building blocks of their framework, not the dynamic regime-timing that they claim is the actual differentiator. Any backtest claiming to "replicate Buoyant" should be labeled as "Buoyant-inspired quality+cyclical-value factor construction" — NOT a replication of their actual (largely undisclosed) process.
