## 2. PHILOSOPHY — IN THEIR OWN WORDS

Core question they say guides every decision: **"Is this a company we would like to own as long-term
business owners?"**

Stated goal (repeated verbatim across multiple letters): **~15%+ IRR post-fees, rolling 5 years, beating
BSE500 TRI by ~1.5% per annum post-fees** (also phrased once as "beating BSE500 by 3% per annum" gross
— the two numbers appear together in the Apr-2025 letter: "beating the BSE500 by 3% per annum (BSE
500TRI by ~1.5% per annum)" — read as pre/post some fee or index-choice distinction, not fully reconciled
in the text). Rolling 5-year TWRR is explicitly named their "preferred time horizon" / decision-making
context: "What gets measured gets done."

Formal definition of "Quality" (this is their central screening lens, quoted verbatim, appears near-identically
in both the Oct-2024 and Apr-2025 letters):
> "Our definition of 'Quality' is a business model that can deliver ~18%+ sustainable ROE with moderate
> leverage, promoter that thinks long term, prioritizes resilience over speed and aligns interests with
> minority shareholders." (Oct-2024)
> "Our definition of 'Quality' is investing with an 'ownership mindset': ~18%+ sustainable ROE (16%+ for
> Banks), promoter that thinks long term, prioritizes resilience over speed, and operates with a 'win-win'
> mindset with its ecosystem of environment, customers and minority shareholders." (Apr-2025)

Three-pillar framework repeated across secondary sources and consistent with letter language:
1. **Broadly predictable growth** — large addressable market, structural industry tailwinds, long earnings
   visibility.
2. **Improving competitive edge** — favourable industry structure, niche/sector leadership, expanding
   moat, ability to deploy incremental capital at ROE >15% (letters use ">15%" for the general floor,
   "~18%+" for their actual "Quality" bar).
3. **Disciplined management** — focused business model, prudent leverage, disciplined capital allocation,
   management that "continuously learns and adapts."

Their own tagline for risk posture: **"resilience over speed"** and **"stamina over speed"** — explicit,
repeated admission that this style **will underperform in raging/momentum-driven bull markets** because
quality/resilience is a drag on speed when "risks are an afterthought." This is their own stated failure mode,
not an outside critique (see §5).

Explicit self-description of valuation method (NOT a DCF model in the formal sense):
> "We don't make detailed DCF models. However, we use DCF to arrive at a range of valuation multiples
> basis broad assumptions on growth, longevity of growth and structural ROIC of the business. We use
> 12-12.5% discount rates assuming long term 10 Yr G Sec in India is 7.5% and 4.5% as risk premium for
> Equities." (Apr-2023 letter, footnote)

Trailing PE is explicitly said to be **useful only for "Linearity" bucket names** (mature, linear-growth
compounders like HDFC Bank — "unlikely to grow 25%," good NPA track record so PE is meaningful) and
**explicitly misleading for "Longevity"/"Asymmetric" bucket names** (early life-cycle, exponential-optionality
names like Neogen Chemicals, Shaily, Yasho — where large capex means D&A/interest hit earnings upfront
before utilization catches up, so trailing PE understates true earning power). For those, their process is:
run 5-year-forward earnings scenarios, assign an exit multiple, discount back to present.

Reverse-DCF is explicitly used as an EXIT discipline (see RACL and Ratnamani examples below) — i.e., "what
growth rate does the current price require for X years, and is that credible?"

## 3. PORTFOLIO CONSTRUCTION — MECHANICAL RULES (verbatim/near-verbatim, with sourcing)

### 3a. Concentration & sizing
- "We need ~15-20 well researched ideas from 5000 listed companies." (Oct-2024 letter, primary)
- Secondary sources (IME Capital, PMS AIF World reviews) converge on **15-20 holdings, position sizes
  3-15%**.
- Apr-2023 letter, primary, gives an explicit **4-phase position-sizing ladder tied to a company's stage of
  competitive evolution** (this is their most granular disclosed mechanical rule):
  - **Phase 1** — "First big break by a marquee customer" → **peak weight 3%**
  - **Phase 2** — "Customers scale up, moat emerging" → **peak weight 5%**
  - **Phase 3** — "Discipline, leadership depth, de-risking, widening moat visible" → **peak weight 8%**
  - **Phase 4** — "Flywheel starts spinning" → **peak weight 10-15%**
  - As of that letter: "13 of the Top 15 are Phase 3 or Phase 4 companies... Top 15 positions are ~90% of
    the portfolio."
- Overweight-trim discipline: "we have trimmed some very overweight positions in select cases, (>10% in
  an account)" (Oct-2024 letter) — i.e. ~10% appears to be a soft practical ceiling even though the Phase-4
  "peak weight" band is stated as 10-15%. ICICI Bank was disclosed at 11.2% of AUM as the largest position
  in the Apr-2023 letter's top-15 table.
- They explicitly hold >5% of the free float in several small/microcap names (RACL Geartech cited as an
  example, ~₹1,050cr market cap, zero MF/FII shareholding at the time) — a structural illiquidity choice,
  enabled precisely because they run separately-managed PMS accounts, not a pooled vehicle (see §3d).

### 3b. Hard-ish screening bands (Apr-2023 letter, "Margin of safety and resilience" section — the single
most quantitatively explicit paragraph found in any letter):
- **"Margin of safety: <12% of the portfolio where the TTM PE ratio > 40, that too where PAT growth of
  20%+ is expected for long periods of time."** — i.e. high-multiple names are capped at 12% of portfolio
  weight AND are only permitted at all if forward PAT growth is expected to be 20%+ for a long runway.
- **"All positions in companies where ROE or equivalent >15%."** — universal floor.
- **"Resilience: <15% of portfolio (non-financials) with Debt/EBITDA >2.5."**
- **"Moats intact: no company where competitive position is structurally challenged."**
- Sector-specific ROE targets for financials sub-segments (Apr-2023 letter, "Are we overexposed to
  Financials?" answer): Banks "mid-high teen growth, 15-17% ROE"; Health Insurance "late teen growth,
  18-22% ROE"; Life Insurance "late teen growth, long-tail-risk"; Asset Management "low teen growth,
  30%+ ROE, low pricing power." (Apr-2025 letter updates the general Bank ROE bar slightly to "16%+ for
  Banks" as part of the core "Quality" definition footnote.)
- Valuation-error tolerance band, explicitly stated as an accepted, sized risk rather than a hard stop:
  "the risk of over-paying by 10-15% can be managed by stretching time and position sizing." (Apr-2025)

### 3c. Three-bucket portfolio classification — NOTE: two different bucket schemes appear across sources,
apparently used for different purposes/schemes. Treat as related-but-not-identical:
- **Valuation-methodology buckets** (primary, Oct-2024 letter, "What is Solidarity portfolio's PE?" section):
  **Linearity / Longevity / Asymmetric upside**. PE is meaningful only for Linearity names.
- **Capital-allocation-by-conviction buckets, Prudence scheme** (secondary, IME Capital/PMS-AIF-World
  reviews, not verified against a primary doc): **Clear Leaders (40-50% alloc, ~15-18% IRR target) /
  Emerging Leaders (40-50% alloc, 18%+ IRR target) / Special Situations (~10% alloc, ~25% IRR target)**.
- **Capital-allocation buckets, Emerging Leaders scheme** (secondary, IME Capital review): **Growth with
  Stability (35-50%) / Growth with Longevity (30-40%) / High Potential Upside (20-30%)**; ~80% of that
  scheme's allocation is to companies **under ₹2,500cr market cap** — this is the clearest disclosed
  market-cap band found.
- "Renters" exception bucket (Apr-2025 letter, primary, explicit and important): they will occasionally buy
  sub-18%-ROE ("not great businesses") names that are very cheap and could evolve into compounders —
  done "sparingly," only when (a) upside/downside is strongly favourable, (b) they're aligned with promoter
  thinking, (c) there's a visible value-unlocking trigger, and (d) no better opportunities exist in the core
  ("owner") bucket. Polyplex (a cyclical packaging-films name bought in Q4FY25 as a "Special Situations"
  initial position) is their own cited example of this exception path.

### 3d. Exit / sell discipline — explicitly judgment-based, NOT a mechanical stop-loss or trailing-multiple
trigger. Direct quotes:
- "We will sell/reduce exposure if the position is significantly over-weight (minimize regret) and if we need
  cash to fund even better IRR ideas." (Oct-2024)
- "We will exit when we encounter euphoria and if we can allocate to better opportunities." (Apr-2025)
- "If companies are not evolving in line with our expectations, we will exit." (Apr-2023, churn section)
- "Any process for 'selling right' requires a lot of judgement: it depends on the position size, the strength
  of the narrative, the past track record of the management team, the width of the moat in the business,
  prevailing valuations, alternate opportunities competing for allocations etc." (Oct-2024) — i.e. they
  explicitly DISCLAIM a mechanical sell rule.
- Concrete exit case studies (both from Apr-2023 letter, primary — genuinely useful as worked examples):
  - **Ratnamani Metals**, exited at **35x TTM PE**. Reverse-DCF: "needs to grow profits at ~16-18% CAGR
    for two decades to justify its price" (assumptions: 12% cost of equity, 6% terminal growth, 16% ROE)
    vs. actual realized decade profit growth of only 11% (non-linear, flat 2013-18). Good business (~16%
    ROE avg decade), exited purely on valuation/reverse-DCF grounds — explicit acknowledgment "we may
    be exiting too early."
  - **Privi Specialty Chemicals**, scaled out fully by Feb-2023 after: (a) a poor FY23 (Ukraine war demand
    hit, shipping costs, delayed plant start), (b) **management stopped communicating for an extended
    period in 2022** during the poor results (a qualitative governance/communication red flag — explicitly
    called out as the "unexpected surprise" that tipped the decision), (c) **Debt/EBITDA >5** from a capex
    program that nearly doubled fixed assets FY21-23, and (d) an explicit disagreement with management's
    capital-allocation philosophy (high leverage to avoid dilution) even while conceding the business
    "could double Earnings in the next 3-4 years."
  - **STAR Health**, exited (Apr-2025 letter): bought for #1 market share in Retail Health/best cost
    structure, but they **underestimated a structural value-chain weakness** — intermediaries wouldn't
    accept claims-linked commissions, and unregulated hospitals over-treat, both squeezing loss ratios;
    management responded with a "sub-par" pivot into Group Health (a commoditized, lowest-bidder-wins
    segment they view as structurally unprofitable). This is a clean **quality-trap / moat-was-thinner-than-
    modeled** case study — dominant share did not equal a durable moat once value-chain economics were
    tested. Genuinely useful adverse evidence (see §5).
  - **Shaily Engineering & Bharti Airtel**, TRIMMED (not exited) in Q4FY25: Shaily trimmed "primarily...risk
    management on the back of extreme valuations (40X+ EBITDA)" — i.e. a valuation-triggered trim of a
    long-held (since 2018) winner, not a thesis-break exit.

### 3e. Churn / turnover
- "Churn by a PMS creates tax incidence... The impact... would be small in a PMS with **<25% churn**."
  (Apr-2023) — read as their own implicit ceiling/typical range for annual turnover, not a hard rule.
- Rationale for occasional churn despite general buy-and-hold bias: rolling-5-year measurement horizon,
  concentrated 15-20 names ("every idea must count"), and PMS structural flexibility to reallocate.
- Explicit self-admitted mistake pattern feeding the FY23 underperformance (Apr-2023 letter, "Learnings"
  section — genuinely candid self-criticism, worth flagging as real evidence of process discipline even in a
  bad year):
  1. **"We wrongly assumed in 2021 that lower interest rates for longer justified higher entry
     multiples"** — overpaid on entries specifically in the Mar-Oct 2021 window; the 10Y G-Sec stayed
     <6.5% vs. their long-term 7.5% assumption during Feb2020-2022, and a 1% move in the discount rate
     alone re-rates fair value for a 15-18%-growth/16-17%-ROE business by ~15-20%.
  2. **Covid one-off earnings distortion** — some FY21/22 earnings were pandemic windfalls (e.g. Divi's
     Labs' Molnupiravir sales) that inflated "true earnings power," requiring later recalibration once the
     one-offs rolled off.
  3. Combined effect: "the impact of these errors was felt in FY23 as high entry valuations coincided with
     disappointment on earnings growth (for example Shaily Engineering)."

### 3f. Vehicle-structure rationale (Apr-2023 letter — relevant context, not a stock-selection rule but a
process/liquidity-management point): they explicitly argue PMS (individual SMA accounts) beats pooled
MF/AIF for a small/micro-cap-tilted strategy because **pooled-vehicle redemptions force pro-rata selling of
the most liquid/best names first, leaving remaining unitholders overweight the worst-performing illiquid
tail** — a real structural argument for why their small-cap sleeve is safer as a PMS than as a fund. Relevant
to us only as a reminder that any smallcap/quality strategy we backtest must model exit liquidity honestly
(consistent with our own COST_STANDARDS/lookahead-audit concerns).

## 5. FAILURE MODES, CRITICISMS, STYLE-TRAP EVIDENCE (unbiased — the point of this section)

1. **Self-disclosed FY23 blow-up + admitted causal chain**: -8.9% vs NIFTY500 -2.3% (alpha -6.6%), explicitly
   attributed by Solidarity itself to (a) an interest-rate-regime misjudgment in 2021 that led to overpaying
   for quality names, (b) COVID one-off earnings inflating apparent quality/growth in some names (Divi's
   Labs cited by name), and (c) at least one name (Shaily Engineering) where high entry valuation collided
   with an actual growth disappointment. This is about as candid a self-admission of a valuation/process
   mistake as you'll find in a PMS letter — genuinely useful, not spin.
2. **Currently-live 2yr/3yr underperformance** (as of the most recent letter obtained, 31-Mar-2025 data):
   3-year alpha **-6.0%**, 2-year alpha **-4.8%** vs BSE500TRI. The 5-year and since-inception numbers are
   still roughly flat-to-positive, but the medium-term trend is negative and self-disclosed. Whether this is
   "quality lagging a momentum/froth market" (their own explanation) or a genuine loss of edge is exactly
   the kind of thing that needs independent verification, not manager narrative.
3. **Explicit, repeated self-description of the core style risk**: "Quality" companies (their definition:
   resilience-over-speed, ~18%+ ROE, moderate leverage) **will structurally underperform in raging bull /
   momentum-driven markets "when risks are an afterthought."** This is a textbook quality-style-trap
   admission, stated by the manager himself across at least two letters (Oct-2024, Apr-2025), not
   something we had to infer.
4. **Quality-trap / thesis-break case study (STAR Health, exited 2025)**: dominant market share (~32% of
   Retail Health) did NOT protect against a structurally weak position in the value chain (intermediary
   incentive misalignment, unregulated hospital over-treatment) — management's response was to chase a
   commoditized, low-margin adjacency (Group Health). A real example of "moat looked wide on a
   market-share metric but was thin on unit economics," relevant to anyone screening on market-share/ROE
   alone.
5. **Governance/communication red flag caught late, not early (Privi Specialty Chemicals)**: management
   "stopped communicating for a long period" during a bad quarter, compounded by rising leverage
   (Debt/EBITDA >5) from an aggressive capex program — a case where a qualitative/soft signal (IR
   responsiveness) was the actual trigger, not any quantitative metric crossing a threshold. This is
   important for codability: their exit process leans on qualitative signals (management tone/candour on
   calls, "we have not significantly over paid on entry," "if companies are not evolving in line with our
   expectations") that a pure-fundamentals screen cannot see.
6. **Illiquidity / concentration risk, self-acknowledged**: they explicitly "embrace some illiquidity... to fish
   where larger firms cannot," own >5% of free float in multiple sub-₹2,000cr names, and concede "the
   downside of this approach is that illiquidity results in much higher stock price volatility, which in the
   short term is indistinguishable from risk" (Apr-2025). Third-party reviews (IME Capital) flag the same
   risk independently: "small-cap inherent volatility," fund "relatively new... establishing its track record"
   (Emerging Leaders), and "team depth" risk — the senior team apart from Manish Gupta has "no prior
   fund management experience before joining Solidarity," creating single-key-person dependency (IME
   Capital's own words, an independent critical assessment, not Solidarity's).
7. **Thematic/sector concentration risk, self-disclosed with a table**: as of 31-Mar-2025, ~46% of the
   portfolio by weight sits in Manufacturing-for-the-world names with material export/US-sales exposure
   (Yasho, Kama/SRF, Shaily, RACL, Neogen, Garware, Shivalik Bimetal, Sansera, Pix Transmission, Polyplex)
   — a single-theme geopolitical/tariff bet that they defend (long unwind time for reshoring, structural
   competitiveness) but is a real concentration exposure a reader should weigh, not something obscured.
8. **Valuation-error tolerance is explicit and non-trivial**: they openly accept up to "10-15%" overpayment
   risk on entry as "manageable... by stretching time and position sizing" — i.e. no hard valuation discipline
   stop, a deliberate policy choice that cuts both ways (avoids selling winners early, but was the stated
   proximate cause of the FY23 losses).
9. **What we could NOT find**: no independent forensic/critical journalism (Moneycontrol/ET named-author
   interviews with pointed questions) surfaced in search; the only critical material found was (a)
   Solidarity's own letters (candid but self-narrated) and (b) IME Capital's paid-review-style commentary
   (moderately independent — flags real risks like key-person dependency and small-cap volatility, but is
   not adversarial journalism). Treat the picture here as "manager is unusually candid in his own letters,"
   not "extensively externally audited."

## 6. CODABILITY — WHAT WE CAN vs CANNOT BUILD WITH PIT FUNDAMENTALS + DAILY OHLCV + NIFTY500 UNIVERSE

### Directly codable (quantitative thresholds stated by the manager, map cleanly onto our data)
- **ROE floor** — "all positions ROE (or equivalent) >15%," "Quality" bar "~18%+ sustainable ROE (16%+ for
  Banks)." Codable from annual/TTM ROE in PIT fundamentals. Their "sustainable"/"structural" qualifier
  (steady-state, not headline-TTM) is harder — would need a smoothing/normalization proxy (e.g. multi-year
  average ROE, or ROE ex-one-offs) rather than raw single-period ROE.
- **PAT/earnings growth thresholds** — ">15% aggregate long-term earnings growth," "20%+ PAT growth
  expected for long periods" (their gate for holding a >40x-PE name). Codable as trailing 3-5yr PAT CAGR
  from PIT quarterly data as a backward-looking proxy; their actual criterion is forward-looking (an
  expectation), which we cannot replicate directly — could only proxy via trailing-growth persistence /
  extrapolation, with the acknowledged risk that's exactly the kind of extrapolation error that burned them
  in 2021.
- **Valuation bands** — "<12% of portfolio at TTM PE>40," reverse-DCF exit discipline (implied growth rate
  to justify price, vs. a credibility ceiling). Fully codable: TTM PE from price (daily OHLCV) ÷ TTM EPS (PIT
  fundamentals); reverse-DCF given assumed discount rate (they disclose 12-12.5%) and terminal growth
  (they disclose 6%) is pure arithmetic — this is actually a promising systematic screen to build (flag names
  whose price implies unrealistic multi-decade growth, exactly as they did with Ratnamani).
- **Market-cap bands** — "80% of Emerging Leaders <₹2,500cr market cap." Fully codable (price × shares
  outstanding vs. NIFTY500 PIT universe).
- **Concentration/turnover discipline** — 15-20 names, <25% annual churn, no single position >10-15%.
  These are portfolio-construction parameters we can freely impose in a systematic version; they don't
  depend on qualitative inputs we lack.
- **Debt/EBITDA <2.5 for ≥85% of non-financial book** — codable IF our PIT fundamentals dataset carries
  balance-sheet debt and an EBITDA-equivalent line; per our own data-quality notes, our standard PIT
  fundamentals emphasize sales/NP/EPS/OPM and annual ROE/ROCE — **OPM is a margin proxy for EBITDA
  margin but not the same as EBITDA in Rupees, and standalone Debt figures may not be present at PIT
  quality** — needs a DATA_CATALOG check before assuming this is buildable; flagged as a probable gap.
- **Sector-specific ROE bars** (Banks 15-17%, Health Insurance 18-22%, Asset Mgmt 30%+) — codable if we
  have sector/industry tags on the NIFTY500 universe, which we should.

### Partially codable / needs a proxy, not a direct implementation
- **Cyclicality avoidance ("deep cyclicals")** — no explicit numeric rule given, but proxyable via
  earnings-volatility (std dev of YoY EPS growth) or sector tags as a screen-out filter.
- **"Phase 1-4" position-sizing ladder** — the weight caps (3/5/8/10-15%) are mechanical and codable, but
  the PHASE CLASSIFICATION itself ("first big break by a marquee customer," "moat emerging," "leadership
  depth visible") is qualitative/narrative and depends on management commentary, customer-concentration
  data, and analyst judgment we don't systematically have. We could build a rough proxy (e.g. rank by
  combination of revenue scale, ROE trend, ROE stability, and market-cap growth) but it would not
  faithfully reproduce their actual classification.
- **"Linearity vs Longevity vs Asymmetric" bucket** (which determines whether PE or reverse-DCF applies)
  — proxyable via a heuristic (e.g., mature large-cap + low earnings volatility = Linearity; small-cap + high
  revenue growth + recent capex ramp = Longevity/Asymmetric) but this is an approximation of a judgment
  call, not a hard rule.
- **Euphoria-based exit trigger** — no explicit numeric threshold given ("we will exit when we encounter
  euphoria"); could proxy via trailing-PE z-score vs. own history/sector, or via the reverse-DCF implied-
  growth-vs-realistic-ceiling test, but this is our proxy, not their stated rule.

### NOT codable with our current data — this is genuinely their edge, and the gap is real
- **Management-quality/promoter assessment** ("intelligent fanatic," "thinks like an owner," "prioritizes
  resilience over speed," "candour on concalls," alignment via low managerial salaries) — this is explicitly
  built from **concall transcripts, direct management meetings, and multi-year relationship-based judgment**
  ("One truly understands promoters when one has owned a company and interacted with them for some
  time"). We have none of this systematically.
- **Governance/communication red flags as an exit trigger** (Privi: "management stopped communicating
  for a long period") — requires monitoring IR responsiveness / concall frequency / management tone,
  which is not in our PIT fundamentals or OHLCV data at all. This is a genuine, stated gap.
  Forensic-accounting checks generally (promoter pledging, related-party transactions, auditor changes) —
  not systematically available at PIT quality in our current datasets per our own DATA_QUALITY_RULES.
- **Channel checks / industry expert calls** (e.g. their RACL thesis leans on management concall quotes
  about customer perception of India as a sourcing base, competitor-exit data like Continental/Volkswagen
  plant closures, ZF sourcing plans) — qualitative, non-fundamental, non-price data entirely outside our
  universe.
- **Forward-looking scenario/DCF judgment on "growth longevity"** — the actual skill (distinguishing a
  name like Neogen/Shaily, where trailing multiples "mislead," from a value trap) is a business-model/
  TAM judgment call; we can build the arithmetic shell (reverse-DCF, scenario discounting) but not the
  judgment of which assumptions are credible.

### Overall codability verdict
Their SCREENING SHELL (ROE floor, growth-expectation gate for high-PE names, valuation/reverse-DCF
arithmetic, market-cap bands, concentration/turnover limits, sector-specific ROE bars) is substantially
buildable with PIT quarterly fundamentals + daily OHLCV + a NIFTY500 PIT universe, and would make a
reasonable systematic "Quality-at-a-reasonable-price with a reverse-DCF valuation gate" strategy. What we
cannot replicate is the qualitative overlay that Solidarity itself says is where the real work happens: promoter
judgment, concall/management-communication monitoring, channel checks, and the "phase" classification
of competitive-moat evolution. Any systematic version we build should be understood as a mechanical
skeleton of their process, not a replication of their actual edge — and per their own letters, the mechanical
skeleton alone (chasing headline ROE/growth without the promoter-quality overlay) is exactly the kind of
"quality at any price / momentum-chasing on quality factors" trap that produces the sharpest drawdowns
(their own FY23 mistake was arguably a failure of valuation discipline even WITH the qualitative overlay
intact — a purely mechanical version could plausibly do worse, or better, depending on how the reverse-DCF
gate is calibrated; this needs to actually be backtested, not assumed).
