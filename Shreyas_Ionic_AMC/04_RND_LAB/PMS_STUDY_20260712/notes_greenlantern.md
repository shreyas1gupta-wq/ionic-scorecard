## 1. PHILOSOPHY (as stated in their own primary docs — Disclosure Document + Nov2025/June2026 Presentation + philosophy webpage)

Core framing, verbatim/paraphrased from primary sources:
- Buffett epigraph opens their pitch deck: *"The three most important words in investing are: margin of safety."* Presented as the organizing principle of "everything we do."
- **"Deep Research & Flexibility"** — the two pillars, per multiple sources including their own philosophy page.
- **BMV framework** (their own philosophy webpage, `greenlanterncapital.in/philosophy/`): every position must clear **Business** (growth runway + competitive moat), **Management** (proven capital allocation track record), **Valuation** (margin of safety + asymmetric risk-reward) — a fairly standard quality-growth-at-reasonable-price (GARP/QARP) checklist, not novel.
- **5-step process** (per philosophy page): (1) Opportunity Identification via macro/sector trend tracking, (2) Deep Research into value chain/competitive moats/financial strength, (3) BMV Evaluation gate, (4) Portfolio Construction (high-conviction + diversification), (5) Active Monitoring (continuous valuation + fundamentals re-check).
- **"Test match" mindset** — "time in the market, not timing the market"; long holding periods (3-5 yr stated horizon on every product), explicitly avoiding tactical trading EXCEPT for cash calls (see below — this is an internal tension in their own marketing).
- **Four risk categories they say they manage against**: Valuation Risk, Earnings Risk, Balance Sheet Risk, Over-ownership Risk (the last presumably = crowding/liquidity risk from too many funds owning the same small-cap name — never explicitly defined in any source we found, its exact operational meaning is not disclosed).
- **DOs/DON'Ts slide** (presentation pg.4): DO = margin of safety, disciplined approach, rigorous due diligence, long-term focus, "skin in the game" (partners' own capital in the funds — claimed, not independently verified), transparency. DON'T = chasing expensive valuations, speculation, compromising on quality, "investing for the sake of investing," benchmark hugging.
- Investment principles explicitly stated: absolute-return mindset, asymmetric risk-return focus, preference for **undiscovered or underperformed opportunities** (i.e., not the current market darlings — a contrarian/early-cycle tilt), and "**disciplined and process-driven approach to selling**" (exit discipline is claimed but never quantified anywhere we could find).
- Buy-and-hold stated explicitly in the SEBI Disclosure Document itself (not just marketing): *"The Portfolio Manager would like to adopt a buy-and-hold approach... and would attempt to participate through the entire cycle of value unlocking, which can happen over a more extended period."*
- Active monitoring is explicitly **management-meeting-driven**: *"Investments will be actively monitored with periodic meetings with different levels of management, internal and external constituents of the companies, and competing businesses to continuously assess the validity of the investment hypothesis."* This is a qualitative, primary-research-driven monitoring loop — not a quant re-screen.

**Net read: this is a fairly conventional Indian small/midcap "quality growth + margin of safety + business-owner mindset" PMS, distinguished mainly by (a) explicit, unusually candid disclosure of hard portfolio-construction bands (see §2) in their public disclosure document — many PMS managers don't publish these — and (b) a stated willingness to swing cash allocation aggressively (8% to 53% observed across snapshots) as a top-down risk lever, which sits in tension with their own "time in the market not timing the market" tagline.**

## 4. FAILURE MODES / CRITICISMS / RISK FACTORS — unbiased, including what favorable marketing sources downplay

1. **Documented negative 1-year stretch (SEBI table, as of 31-Mar-2026): Growth Fund -1.37% vs BSE500TRI -3.12%.** Beat the benchmark on a relative basis but lost money in absolute terms for a full trailing year — a genuine bad period, not hypothetical. Directly contradicts any pitch that implies "always positive" or "never loses."
2. **Smallcap concentration / illiquidity risk is real and self-acknowledged.** Growth Fund realized small-cap allocation has ranged from 26% to 56% of the portfolio across different snapshots (56% as of the mid-2026 presentation). The SEBI Disclosure Document's own risk-factors section explicitly warns: "highly concentrated Portfolios with lesser number of stocks will be more volatile," and "non-diversified portfolios may be subject to higher volatility" (repeated verbatim in the presentation's own disclaimer page). A 20-30 stock, majority-smallcap book is inherently exposed to exactly this.
3. **FY25/FY26 category-wide smallcap correction was severe and GLC was not immune, only relatively better.** Per PMS Bazaar's own FY25 category report, Small & Midcap PMS category average cratered from +50.57% (FY24) to +11.07% (FY25); smallcap-only category from +49.41% to +8.12%. GLC's Growth Fund at +24.62% FY25 clearly outperformed the category average — a real, credible resilience data point — but this was still a massive deceleration from the prior year's boom, and the SEBI table shows the correction bit further into calendar 2026 (the -1.37% 1yr-to-March-2026 figure). This is the textbook "quality-smallcap style trap": even good stock-picking cannot fully insulate a smallcap-heavy book from a systematic multiple-compression regime.
4. **Aggressive, discretionary cash-timing sits in tension with the stated "time in the market, not timing the market" philosophy.** Observed cash levels for the Growth Fund have ranged from ~8% to ~53% of AUM across different disclosed snapshots. This is explicitly sanctioned by their own process ("cash to be a default position... maintain a higher allocation to cash... in response to adverse market conditions") — i.e., they DO time the market via cash calls, which is a discretionary macro bet layered on top of the bottom-up stock-picking, and if that market-timing call is wrong, it either creates a large cash drag in a rally (opportunity cost) or fails to protect capital in a selloff (if de-risked too late). No historical track record of the accuracy of these specific cash calls was found in any public source — this is exactly the kind of claim that needs internal backtesting/verification, not manager self-report, before it can be trusted.
5. **Capacity / scale risk given rapid AUM growth into an illiquid segment.** Discretionary AUM grew from ₹20 cr (17 clients, FY21) to ~₹1,219 cr (1,112 clients, FY26) — over 60x in five years — while the flagship fund's stated mandate is predominantly small/midcap (a segment with materially worse liquidity/impact-cost characteristics than large-cap). The impressive early-years CAGR (especially the 49.42% 4-year and 37%+ 3-year figures) was very plausibly built on a much smaller capital base able to move in and out of thinly-traded names without material market impact; there is no public evidence (and it would be very unusual for a PMS to publish this) on whether the SAME alpha-per-unit-of-capital persists at ~₹1,700+ cr scale in the same universe. This is a generic, well-known PMS/AIF "success kills the edge" capacity risk, not something GLC alone faces, but their AUM trajectory make them a candidate case, and it is consistent with the return deceleration visible from FY24 (~50%+ category-wide) to FY25/26 (single digits to low teens for the category, -1.37% to +8.56% range for GLC specifically depending on exact date).
6. **No independently verifiable stock-level track record before AUM scale-up.** Nilesh Doshi's cited "5-10x" stock picks (2009-13) are PERSONAL/employer track record predating the firm (founded ~2018-2020), not an audited PMS performance record — a very common (and generally accepted) practice in PMS marketing bios, but it should not be conflated with GLC's own regulator-filed performance, which only starts effectively at inception (Dec-2017 for Growth Fund).
7. **Every quantitative screening threshold is undisclosed — meaning the actual "edge," if any, is 100% embedded in discretionary judgment (BMV framework, management meetings, macro calls), not in any published rule.** This makes the manager's process fundamentally NOT auditable or replicable from outside data; any claimed "process discipline" is a black box to an external analyst, verified only by the track record itself (see §3) — and track records for an 8-year-old, fast-scaling small/midcap PMS are exactly the kind of sample that is prone to survivorship/point-in-time CAGR-window artifacts (see §3's "49.42% 4-year CAGR" flag).
8. **Internal document inconsistencies found during this research** (minor but relevant to any adversarial due-diligence): (a) Alpha Fund inception date stated as "Feb-2020" on multiple third-party trackers vs. the presentation deck's own Alpha Fund performance-chart header reading "Since Inception (Dec 2017)" (almost certainly a copy-paste error, but such an error appearing in an investor-facing, "Private & Confidential" pitch deck is itself a minor process/QC flag); (b) presentation deck cover page shows both "November 2025" and "June 2026" without clarifying which the enclosed data is as-of; (c) one third-party site (altportfunds) mislabels a REALIZED sector-allocation snapshot as "Sector Caps," which is not supported by anything in the primary SEBI Disclosure Document (no sector-cap rule exists there) — analysts should not repeat that "cap" framing without correcting it.
9. **No SEBI disciplinary history, litigation, or audit findings** — genuinely clean regulatory record per the Disclosure Document's own Part-1 mandatory disclosures (Nil across all 6 sub-items: penalties, direction/nature, economic-offence penalties, pending litigation incl. criminal, systems/operations deficiencies flagged by SEBI, enquiry/adjudication proceedings). This is a positive, not a criticism, but worth stating plainly since we were asked to be unbiased in both directions.

**Overall failure-mode read: this is NOT a story of a manager with a documented multi-year drawdown disaster or a proven quality-trap (e.g., no evidence found of a multi-year stagnant "quality at any price" period the way some large-cap-quality PMS managers have shown post-2021). The more relevant, better-evidenced risk for GLC specifically is (a) the generic smallcap-PMS "boom-then-correction" pattern that hit the WHOLE category in FY25/26 (GLC navigated it better than peers but was not immune — one negative trailing-year print exists), and (b) capacity/scale risk from very fast AUM growth into an illiquid segment, which is a forward-looking risk rather than a proven historical failure.**

## 5. CODABILITY — what we can implement with PIT quarterly fundamentals (sales/NP/EPS/OPM, ROE/ROCE annual) + daily OHLCV + NIFTY500 PIT universe, and what we structurally cannot

### CODABLE (portfolio-construction layer — these are genuinely mechanical and match our data):
- **Market-cap banding as an eligibility filter**: Growth Fund's 0-35% Large / 25-100% Small+Mid; Alpha Fund's 0-60% Large / 20-40% Mid / 0-30% Small. We have NIFTY500 PIT membership + can classify Large/Mid/Small via free-float market cap rank (AMFI methodology: top 100 = large, 101-250 = mid, 251+ = small) each rebalance date — directly codable as a portfolio-construction constraint.
- **Stock count constraint**: 20-30 holdings — trivially codable as top-N selection.
- **Benchmark**: BSE 500 TRI — we'd substitute NIFTY 500 TRI (near-identical universe/methodology) since that's what we can source reliably (factor-indices skill already pulls official NIFTY factor/broad indices).
- **Low-turnover / buy-and-hold discipline**: their disclosed churn ratio (0.07-0.89 monthly-basis, effectively very low turnover) is directly codable as a rebalance-frequency and minimum-holding-period constraint in any backtest — e.g., quarterly rebalance with a "don't sell unless X" persistence rule, rather than monthly re-optimization.
- **"Preference for growth stocks with reasonable ROE/ROCE"**: codable as a quantitative screen using PIT annual ROE/ROCE plus TTM sales/PAT growth from `datasets/earnings_pit/unified_quarterly_pit.parquet` — BUT we would have to INVENT the exact threshold (e.g., ROE > 15%, sales growth > 12% TTM) ourselves, since GLC has never disclosed a numeric floor anywhere we found. Any backtest of "GLC's rule" is therefore actually a backtest of OUR guess at their rule, not a true replication — this must be labeled honestly in any writeup/IC memo.
- **Cash-as-hedge / tactical de-risking**: crudely proxyable using OHLCV-only regime filters (e.g., NIFTY500 200-DMA trend state, breadth, or a valuation z-score if we compute aggregate index PE from constituent PIT fundamentals) to mechanically raise/lower an equity-exposure dial — but this would be OUR mechanical proxy for what GLC actually does via discretionary macro judgment (explicitly stated as "combining top-down and bottom-up... capitalizing on tactical opportunities" — a qualitative call), not a replication of their real signal.
- **Sector exposure monitoring**: even though no hard sector cap is disclosed as policy, we could impose a reasonable cap (e.g., no sector >25-30%) purely as a risk-management overlay of our own design, consistent with generically prudent portfolio construction, not because GLC states this as a rule.

### NOT CODABLE from our data (needs inputs we structurally lack):
- **"Deep research" business-quality / moat / pricing-power judgment** — this is the actual stock-selection engine (BMV framework's "Business" and part of "Valuation" legs) and is fundamentally qualitative: reading annual reports, assessing competitive dynamics, judging "hunger for growth" of management. We can proxy pieces of it (margin stability, capital efficiency trend, low leverage) from PIT fundamentals, but true moat/quality assessment is not extractable from numbers alone.
- **Management-quality assessment via management meetings** — explicitly stated in their SEBI Disclosure Document as core to ongoing monitoring ("periodic meetings with different levels of management, internal and external constituents of the companies, and competing businesses"). We have zero access to management-meeting content, channel checks, or competitor interviews — this is genuinely primary-research-only information no PIT/OHLCV dataset can supply.
- **Intrinsic-value-based "Margin of Safety"** — their own definition is explicitly "the difference between the purchase price and intrinsic value," which implies a DCF/owner-earnings-style forward valuation, not a simple trailing PE/PB screen. We could build a crude reverse-DCF or PE-vs-own-history z-score as an approximation, but it would not replicate their actual (discretionary, forward-looking) intrinsic-value judgment.
- **Macro/business-cycle top-down view** driving sector tilts and cash calls — genuinely discretionary macro judgment layered on top of the bottom-up process; not derivable from per-stock PIT fundamentals or OHLCV alone (would need a separate macro dataset, and even then would be OUR macro model, not theirs).
- **Forensic/governance red-flag screening** (related-party transactions, promoter share-pledging, auditor resignations, qualified audit opinions) — not systematically available in our current fundamentals dataset (and per the project's known landmine, `india_fundamentals_mc/Train.parquet`'s `annual_report` column is corrupt at source). GLC's process implies at least informal governance vetting ("ethical management," "no associate/related-party investment" as a formal policy) that we cannot mechanically replicate without a dedicated forensics data source.
- **"Undiscovered or underperformed opportunities" / early-cycle contrarian entry timing** — requires a subjective read of "being early" relative to consensus, which is not a standard factor we can compute; the closest proxy (low analyst coverage, low institutional ownership, or a technical basing pattern) is a stretch and would be our own invention, not theirs.

### Bottom-line codability verdict
We can faithfully replicate GLC's **portfolio construction shell** (market-cap bands, stock count, low turnover, benchmark) with our existing data, and can build a **plausible-but-invented GARP/quality screen** (ROE/ROCE + growth + leverage filters) to approximate their stock-selection layer — but any such backtest is a test of OUR hypothesis about what a "quality growth at a margin of safety" process might deliver in the NIFTY500 universe, not a genuine replication of Green Lantern Capital's actual (undisclosed, judgment-heavy, management-meeting-informed) selection process. This should be framed to IC/CIO as **"GARP-in-smallcap-with-low-turnover" as an independently-designed hypothesis inspired by GLC's stated portfolio-construction parameters**, never as "backtesting Green Lantern's strategy."
