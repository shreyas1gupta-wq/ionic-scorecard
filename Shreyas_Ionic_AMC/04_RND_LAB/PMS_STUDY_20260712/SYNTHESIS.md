# PMS/AIF Manager Study — Synthesis
**10 managers studied, all extract claims independently verified (verdict: SUPPORTED, no material fabrications found).** Managers: SageOne (Vartak), Buoyant (Mistry/Khivasara/Berawala), Carnelian (Khemani), Aequitas (Bhaiya, d. 31-Dec-2025), Green Lantern (Doshi/Bhardwaj/Gokhale), Bandhan Smallcap MF (Gunwani), Abakkus (Singhania), ValueQuest (Dharamshi), Solidarity Advisors (Gupta), Marcellus (Mukherjea).

---

## 1. CONVERGENT RULES (highest prior — shared by 3+ independent managers)

| Rule | Who (count) | Precise form |
|---|---|---|
| **ROE/ROCE floor, ~15-20%+** | 7/10: SageOne (>20%), Carnelian (Contra ROE>15%, MRFG high-ROE test), Aequitas (>market), Abakkus ("15:15:15" ROE>15%, min ROCE 14-15%), ValueQuest (>=20%), Solidarity (>15% floor, 18%+ "quality" bar, 16% for banks), Marcellus (ROCE>cost of capital, 10yrs running) | Nearly universal; the single most convergent screen in the whole study. |
| **Growth threshold ~10-25% CAGR** | 6/10: SageOne (>20-25%), Carnelian (Shift >20%, Contra 12-15%+), Abakkus (>15%, up to 20-25% at richer PE), ValueQuest, Marcellus (revenue growth>=10%, 10yrs running), Bandhan/Gunwani ("double in 3-4yrs" implies ~20-25% CAGR) | Threshold scales with valuation tolerance (higher PE paid only for higher growth). |
| **Concentration: 10-30 stocks, position caps 3-15%, sector caps 25-30%** | 8/10: SageOne (18-20 holdings, 3-9% position, 25% sector, 20% group), Buoyant (18-25), Carnelian (20-25, ~10% position cap), Aequitas (10-30, 15% position, 30% sector), Green Lantern (20-30), ValueQuest (8-12, most concentrated), Solidarity (ladder to max 10-15%), Marcellus Little Champs (~15-25) | No manager in the sample runs a 50+ name diversified book. |
| **Low leverage / low-debt preference** | 5/10: SageOne (no excess leverage), Carnelian (D/E<1.5 Contra, D/E 0.0 vs bmk 0.6 Shift), Aequitas (low/negligible debt, net-cash names), Solidarity (Debt/EBITDA<2.5x capped at <15% of book), Marcellus (ROCE test implicitly penalizes leverage) | Universal aversion to financing-driven ROE. |
| **Growth-adjusted valuation (PEG-style), NOT static low-PE** | 6/10: SageOne (Competitive-Advantage-Period duration model, explicit rejection of fixed PE/PEG), Carnelian ("value cannot grow faster than earnings," PEG 1.1 vs bmk 1.5), Abakkus (15:15:15 explicitly loosened — 40x PE acceptable for 20-25% growth), Bandhan/Gunwani (prefers 18-23x fwd-PE band, avoids >30-40x for capital goods), Solidarity (PE<40x cap for most of book, waived only with PAT growth>=20% and capped at <12% of AUM), ValueQuest (ROE vs P/E-vs-P/B heuristic) | Convergent rejection of "cheap absolute PE" as sufficient; growth must justify the multiple. |
| **Explicit 3-5yr investment horizon** | 6/10: SageOne, Carnelian (all 4 strategies), Buoyant (3+yr), ValueQuest (rolling 3-5yr), Green Lantern ("buy-and-hold... entire cycle"), Solidarity | Stated horizon, though *realized* turnover diverges sharply (see §3). |
| **Governance/forensic negative screen precedes the quant screen** | 6/10: SageOne (300→150 governance elimination step), Carnelian (CLEAR framework — cash-flow/liability/earnings-quality/asset-quality/related-party), Aequitas (avoid poor governance, low promoter holding, high FII/DII), Solidarity (moat + no "structurally challenged" names), ValueQuest, Green Lantern (red-flag exclusions) | Almost every manager treats this as a gate, not a factor. |
| **Valuation-only exits are explicitly distrusted / de-prioritized** | 3/10 explicit: SageOne (valuation is the *most flexible*, most-often-wrong trigger — La Opala sold at 5x, ran another 5x), ValueQuest ("thesis breaks," not price discomfort), SageOne's stated hierarchy ranks environment-deterioration > capital-allocation red flags > valuation | Smaller N but important: the managers who are most explicit about their exit logic *rank valuation last*, not first. |

**Read-through for us:** an ROE/ROCE floor + growth-adjusted valuation + tight concentration + governance gate is close to a house-style consensus across a genuinely disparate sample (deep-value, GARP, and quality-forever camps all converge here). This is the highest-confidence skeleton for any codable strategy (§4).

## 2. DIVERGENT BETS (where great managers disagree)

Three camps, same universe, different bets on what compounds:

- **GARP / growth-quality-with-fast-exit** — SageOne, Carnelian Shift, ValueQuest. Bet: pay up for growth, but treat the growth assumption as *perishable* and cut fast when it breaks.
- **Deep-value / contrarian-cyclical** — Carnelian Contra (post Feb-2025 rename from dividend-yield "YnG"), Buoyant Satellite sleeve (metals/CV/NBFC turnarounds, "leaders that look expensive but offer asymmetric upside"). Bet: cycle-timing at the sector/stock level is the edge, not stock-picking quality.
- **Quality-forever, buy-and-hold-at-any-multiple** — Marcellus CCP, Green Lantern (low-churn ratio 0.02-0.07 monthly). Bet: a 10-year ROCE>cost-of-capital + growth>=10% track record justifies holding through valuation discomfort indefinitely — no mechanical deceleration or valuation exit.

**Evidence, quality-forever camp — THE MARCELLUS QUALITY-TRAP CASE STUDY (the Principal's explicit anti-bias check):**
Marcellus CCP since-inception CAGR (to May-2026) = **11.58%** vs Nifty50 TRI **12.14%** — i.e. **negative alpha since inception**, on a strategy built entirely on a 10-year ROCE/growth quality screen with no disclosed valuation- or deceleration-triggered exit rule. Trailing 1yr **-4.05%** vs Nifty +12.14% category (alpha **-6.93%**, beta 1.07). FY24 alone: CCP +24% net vs Nifty50 TRI +30% (6pp shortfall in a strong market). This is a live, multi-year (2021-2025→2026) underperformance stretch consistent with classic quality-trap behavior: high starting multiples, growth decelerating from its 2019-21 pace (17.8% EPS CAGR FY19-24 vs the >=10% floor, i.e. comfortably past-screen but decelerating off its peak), and the manager holding rather than trimming. Little Champs (Marcellus's second product) shows the same pattern: 1yr -6.77%, 5yr CAGR -0.74% vs bmk +11.0%/-0.07% respectively — two-for-two negative-alpha products from the same house philosophy.

**Evidence, GARP-with-fast-exit camp:** SageOne SCP since-inception (Apr-2012 to Jan-2026, ~13.8yrs, overlapping most of Marcellus's live period): **25.1% CAGR** vs BSE500 TRI 14.3% (cumulative 2125.7% vs 575.5%), i.e. the single best-verified, longest-track-record number in the entire study, built on the *opposite* discipline — explicit growth-deceleration triggers and admitted mistakes on the valuation-exit side (sold too early), not on holding too long. ValueQuest Platinum (17.71% 10yr / 17.93% SI) shows a similar shape.

**Evidence, deep-value/cyclical camp:** Buoyant since-inception 20.64% CAGR vs BSE500 14.0% (+6.6pp alpha) is strong, but the manager's *own* stated philosophy explicitly names the Marcellus-style quality-trap risk ("high-quality 'discovery' stocks can re-rate too far and stagnate for a decade-plus even as earnings keep compounding") — i.e. Buoyant is aware of the trap in others' books but has not itself lived through a full decade-scale cycle (founded 2016) to prove its own cyclical-timing edge is durable rather than lucky. Carnelian Contra/Shift numbers are strong but younger still (Contra since Jan-2022).

**Net:** this is not a clean "growth beats quality" verdict — it is evidence that **a mechanical deceleration/valuation exit trigger, not the initial screen, is what separated the outcomes** across managers who otherwise share nearly identical ROE/growth entry criteria (see §1). Marcellus's initial screen (ROCE>cost of capital, growth>=10%, 10yr) is not obviously worse than SageOne's; the operating difference is what happens after growth decelerates.

## 3. THE DECELERATION OBSERVATION (Principal's trap: high-PE + decelerating growth)

| Manager | Deceleration/high-PE behavior | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **SageOne** | Exit hierarchy explicit and RANKED: (1) business-environment deterioration impairing the 20% growth hurdle = **least flexible, fastest-acted trigger** (e.g. exited Kaveri Seeds on regulatory change, J&K Bank on lending-standard deterioration); (2) capital-allocation red flags; (3) valuation "beyond comfort" = **most flexible/patient, and the trigger Vartak admits he gets wrong** (La Opala sold at 5x gain, ran another 5x post-exit) | 25.1% SI CAGR — best-verified number in the study. Deceleration-exit discipline, not valuation-exit discipline, appears to be the operative edge. |
| **ValueQuest** | Exits on "thesis breaks... capital has a clearly better home"; explicitly frames drawdowns as "price of admission," not a sell trigger | 17.9% SI CAGR, consistent alpha across 1/2/3/5/10yr windows. |
| **Marcellus (CCP)** | **No disclosed mechanical deceleration or valuation exit rule** in any source found — philosophy is buy-10yr-ROCE/growth-track-record-and-hold. Live case: has been holding through a 4+yr stretch (since ~2021) as growth normalized off its FY19-24 17.8% EPS CAGR peak and multiples stayed rich | Negative alpha since inception (11.58% vs 12.14% Nifty50 TRI); this is the textbook realization of the exact trap the Principal flagged. |
| **Carnelian** | Shift/Compounder target growth continuation but the widely-repeated "20% underperformance vs benchmark triggers mandatory IC reassessment within 7 days" rule could **NOT be confirmed** in Carnelian's own primary SEBI Disclosure Document (found only on third-party aggregator pages, absent from two Carnelian-controlled sources) — genuine uncertainty on whether a real mechanical trigger exists | FY22-23 (a rate-hike/de-rating year) saw a genuine stall across strategies (Compounder -1.12%, Shift +2.45%, near-flat) — a shared house-wide quality/growth-style squeeze consistent with the deceleration-without-exit pattern, though recovered strongly the following year. |
| **Aequitas** | Sidesteps single-name deceleration timing entirely via a **top-down** cut: slashed small-cap exposure from ~63% (May-2024) to ~14% (Dec-2024), reportedly to ~80-93% cash + Gold/Silver ETFs, ahead of the 2025 small-cap correction | Vindicated in this instance, but a single-cycle result from one now-deceased manager's personal judgment — not a repeatable mechanical rule, and explicitly flagged by IME Capital as non-transferable to new management. |
| **Buoyant** | Names the trap in its own philosophy document (quality stocks can "re-rate too far and stagnate... even as earnings keep compounding") but discloses no numeric deceleration trigger — its Core/Satellite *stance* shift (30/70 aggressive ↔ 70/30 defensive) is the closest mechanism, driven by unanimous-IC cycle judgment, not a quantitative growth-deceleration rule | Firm too young (since 2016) to have been tested through a full quality-trap cycle itself. |

**Who "averages down" into decelerating-growth-at-high-PE?** None of the ten explicitly disclose this as a house rule. Marcellus's revealed multi-year behavior (holding at negative alpha since ~2021, with disclosed factor exposure unchanged) is the closest empirical analogue to "not cutting," even without an explicit averaging-down rule. **Verdict for our design:** the deceleration-exit trigger is the single highest-value, most differentiating rule to encode mechanically — it is precisely the rule the best-performing manager (SageOne) has and the worst-performing quality manager in this sample (Marcellus) lacks.

## 4. CODABLE STRATEGY CANDIDATES (5-8 rule-sets, PIT fundamentals + daily OHLCV + NIFTY500 PIT universe)

Ranked by (a) evidence strength, (b) codability, (c) differentiation from existing sleeves (S1-F short-vol, B1b flow, quality-momentum composites already tested).

**#1 — Anti-Marcellus-Trap: Quality-floor + mandatory deceleration exit**
- Entry: ROCE>15% sustained 8+ trailing quarters (Marcellus's own twin filter) + revenue growth CAGR>=10%.
- Exit (the rule Marcellus lacks): trailing-4Q revenue growth decelerates below ~8% for 2 consecutive quarters, **AND/OR** trailing PE sits in the top decile of the stock's own 5-10yr distribution while growth has decelerated 2 consecutive quarters (joint high-valuation + decelerating-growth condition — directly operationalizes the Principal's trap observation).
- Expected churn: low-medium (fires only on the joint condition; base book is naturally low-turnover).
- Distills: Marcellus (entry screen) + SageOne (exit-trigger logic, §1/§3).
- Technical overlay: PE-percentile-vs-own-history + sector-relative-strength as a combined "expensive AND losing momentum" confirmation signal.
- Evidence: highest — this is a direct, verified causal contrast (SageOne 25.1% CAGR with the trigger vs Marcellus negative alpha without it) rather than an inference. Codability: high (all inputs are PIT fundamentals + OHLCV). Differentiation: **high** — no existing sleeve tests a deceleration-triggered exit paired with a quality entry.

**#2 — SageOne GARP funnel with market-cap-rank band**
- Entry: sector-level screen (ROE/ROCE, margin stability, growth, cash generation) → stock-level trailing 8-12Q sales/PAT growth >20% (PIT `available_date`, never quarter-end) + ROE/ROCE>20% + low D/E + market-cap RANK band (e.g. 101st-600th to emulate SCP) + growth-persistence proxy (low stdev of trailing YoY growth as a stand-in for CAP-duration conviction).
- Exit: trailing-4Q growth falls below ~15% for 2 consecutive quarters (codifies SageOne's #1 trigger); valuation alone is explicitly NOT a sell signal (avoids his own admitted mistake).
- Position sizing 3-9%, sector cap 25%, single-group cap 20%.
- Expected churn: high (SageOne's own realized turnover ~80-90% of names/5yr).
- Distills: SageOne (primary), Carnelian Shift / ValueQuest (secondary, similar growth+ROE bar).
- Technical overlay: 200dma trend filter / RS-rank screen to avoid buying fundamentally-still-OK names whose price has already broken down (bridges to our existing tech-scan Minervini trend-template infrastructure).
- Evidence: strongest single track record in the study (25.1% CAGR/13.8yrs). Codability: high. Differentiation: medium — meaningful overlap with quality-momentum composites already tested; the market-cap-rank-band + deceleration-exit specifics are the incremental piece.

**#3 — Core-Satellite sector-rotation (Buoyant-inspired, static substitute)**
- Core sleeve: top-quartile ROE + low earnings-growth volatility + dividend payer + within-sector market-cap-rank leadership.
- Satellite sleeve: named cyclical sectors (metals, CVs, NBFCs) + PE/PB percentile-vs-own-history in the bottom decile (statistical proxy for "turnaround/asymmetric" — cannot replicate the real regime-timing judgment, which Buoyant itself says isn't systematizable).
- Static 50/50 blend (cannot time the 30/70↔70/30 stance shift — that is the one part of the process explicitly disclosed as non-mechanical).
- Expected churn: low (Core), medium (Satellite).
- Distills: Buoyant.
- Technical overlay: sector-relative-strength / sector-200dma-slope regime flag as a crude, backtestable substitute for the discretionary cycle call.
- Evidence: good (20.64% SI CAGR, +6.6pp alpha) but process only partially replicable. Codability: medium (static-only version). Differentiation: **high** — we have no sector-rotation sleeve today.

**#4 — PEG-style growth-adjusted valuation screen (Carnelian/Abakkus/Gunwani-style)**
- Entry: PE / trailing-growth-CAGR (PEG proxy) < ~1.5, ROE>15%, D/E<1 (matches Carnelian Shift's disclosed factor tilt: growth 15-22%, ROE ~19%, PEG ~1.1, beta ~0.9).
- Exit: PEG richens above ~2.5 while growth simultaneously decelerates (joint condition — avoids SageOne's admitted mistake of a pure valuation-only exit).
- Expected churn: medium.
- Distills: Carnelian Shift, Abakkus ("15:15:15", PE up to 40x acceptable only at 20-25% growth), Bandhan/Gunwani (18-23x preferred fwd-PE band, avoid >30-40x).
- Technical overlay: sector-relative PEG rank + 12-1 month relative-return momentum confirmation, to avoid cheap-for-a-reason value traps.
- Evidence: medium-strong (Carnelian's raw numbers are the best in the study, though young track record: Shift SI +31.65% but only since Oct-2020). Codability: high. Differentiation: medium.

**#5 — Forensic-lite quality screen with position ladder (Solidarity-style)**
- Entry: ROE>15% (18%+ non-financials / 16%+ financials) + moat proxy (5yr stable-or-rising gross margin, low margin volatility) + PE<40x UNLESS PAT growth>=20% (capped at <12% of book) + Debt/EBITDA<2.5x for <=15% of book.
- Position ladder: initial 3% → 5% → 8% → 10-15% as conviction "confirms" (proxy: subsequent quarter's growth/ROE holds above entry threshold).
- Expected churn: low-medium.
- Distills: Solidarity Advisors (primary), SageOne (secondary, similar ROE bar).
- Technical overlay: post-earnings-announcement-drift (PEAD) quality of reaction as a timing trigger for ladder step-ups — links directly to our existing PEAD_EARNINGS_TRAIL research.
- Evidence: solid but modest realized alpha (SI +1.3pp over benchmark as of Apr-2025, though the 5yr window is admittedly COVID-base-inflated per the manager's own letter). Codability: medium (the ladder-confirmation proxy is a design choice, not something we can verify was Solidarity's actual intent). Differentiation: medium.

**#6 — ValueQuest concentrated ROE funnel**
- Entry: 400-500 universe → 150-200 (sector/quality screen) → 30-50 (deeper screen) → 8-12 final names, ROE>=20%, cross-check vs P/B.
- Exit: ROE drops out of top-quartile for 2 consecutive quarters, or growth decelerates below universe median (thesis-break proxy).
- Expected churn: low.
- Distills: ValueQuest, Green Lantern (buy-and-hold framing).
- Technical overlay: 52-week-high proximity as an "still in favor" gate before adding fresh capital.
- Evidence: strong, consistent alpha across every trailing window (1/2/3/5/10yr) — genuinely impressive breadth of confirmation. Codability: medium (8-12 name concentration means small-N idiosyncratic variance in any backtest). Differentiation: low-medium — likely overlaps heavily with quality-momentum composites already tested.

**#7 — CAUTION FLAG, do not prioritize: Smallcap GARP rank-band (SageOne Small&Micro / Bandhan Gunwani-style)**
- Same mechanics as #2 but applied to a 400th-1200th market-cap-rank band.
- Evidence is genuinely **mixed-to-bad**: SageOne's own Small & Micro Cap product is independently reviewed (IME Capital) as "-3.7% 5yr alpha vs Nifty Smallcap250" and "amongst the worst performing schemes in this category," with an explicit "do not recommend" house call. Bandhan/Gunwani (a mutual fund, different structure/liquidity/redemption mechanics) shows better numbers (31-37% 3yr) but is not directly comparable.
- If built at all, MUST carry the circuit/thin-volume slippage overlay (`lib/execution_realism.py`, landmine #7b) — smallcap execution realism, not the factor screen, is likely the actual determinant of whether this replicates SageOne's own worst product or Bandhan's better one.

## 5. WHAT WE CANNOT COPY (honest list — the non-codable edge)

- **Primary qualitative research**: plant visits, channel checks, competitor/analyst interviews — SageOne's explicitly stated edge source, including the discipline of talking to *negative-view* competitors/analysts (not management) during deteriorating events to fight confirmation bias. No PIT dataset substitutes for this.
- **Forensic accounting beyond simple ratios**: Carnelian's full CLEAR framework (true-vs-reported debt, contingent liabilities, expense-capitalization policy, related-party "connecting the dots") needs footnote/XBRL-level annual-report data we don't have — and our own `india_fundamentals_mc/Train.parquet` `annual_report` column is corrupt at source (existing landmine #5).
- **Promoter/management judgment**: integrity, skin-in-the-game, succession risk, "management in a hurry to create value" red flags (Carnelian, Aequitas) — inherently qualitative, no PIT proxy.
- **Buyback/creeping-acquisition and institutional-ownership signals**: Aequitas's pre-purchase screen (majority of holdings did a buyback/creeping-acquisition in the prior 12 months) and multiple managers' FII/DII-ownership screens need SAST/shareholding-pattern filings that still 403 for us (existing data gap, per CLAUDE.md).
- **Competitive-Advantage-Period duration judgment**: SageOne's actual valuation core — forecasting *how long* 20%+ growth persists — is a qualitative forecast, not a number we can derive from trailing data; our best available proxy (growth-persistence stdev) is a crude stand-in the source material itself flags as incomplete.
- **Discretionary market/sector/style/stock-cycle timing**: Buoyant's stated actual edge, which the manager itself says "mining macro as an investment edge is nearly impossible" to systematize — we should not claim to replicate what the source claims cannot be replicated even by them.
- **Discretionary top-down cash-timing overlay**: Aequitas's 2024 pivot to ~90% cash ahead of the 2025 smallcap correction was a personal, non-mechanical judgment call by a now-deceased founder; assuming we could have timed this in a backtest would itself be lookahead.
- **Real-time regulatory/competitive-intensity monitoring**: SageOne's #1-ranked exit trigger (business-environment deterioration) needs news/text feeds and judgment, not PIT fundamentals.
- **IC unanimous-vote qualitative debate process**: Buoyant's three-co-founder governance mechanism (each owns sectors tracked 20+ years) cannot be systematized as a rule.
- **Full governance-forensics elimination pass**: the depth at which SageOne narrows 300→150 names on governance/accounting judgment exceeds anything a crude cash-flow-vs-PAT divergence proxy can replicate.

**Net honest framing:** a codable "PMS-inspired skeleton" (§4) can replicate the *mechanical* funnel — growth + ROE/ROCE + leverage + valuation-adjusted-for-growth + concentration + a deceleration exit — but explicitly misses the qualitative-diligence and cycle-timing layers that several managers (and outside reviewers) flag as doing the real work. The Marcellus case study (§2/§3) is the clearest evidence in this whole batch that the *exit rule*, not the entry screen, is where the codable and non-codable worlds diverge most consequentially — which is exactly why strategy #1 above is ranked highest.
