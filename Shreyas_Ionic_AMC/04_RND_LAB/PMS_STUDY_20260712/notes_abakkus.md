## 1. Documented philosophy (not marketing fluff)

**Core stance: "growth in value" — reject the value-vs-growth dichotomy.** Singhania's own framing: value and growth investing "always converge." The practical translation is GARP (growth at a reasonable price), not deep-value and not quality-momentum.

**The single most distinctive, well-documented philosophical position: the 2019 "Bubble in Quality?" thesis.** Abakkus published/publicized a study of 27 high-P/E "quality" Indian stocks (Nov-2019, covered by Business Standard and Singhania's own X/Twitter thread via @AbakkusInvest), arguing:
- A majority of expensive "quality" names had NOT delivered growth that justified their P/E.
- Headline comparison used: Asian Paints vs. Sherwin-Williams (global paints leader) — Sherwin-Williams grew faster, yet Asian Paints traded at a ~300% valuation premium to it, with "absolutely no fundamental justification" absent a step-change in growth expectations.
- This is a genuinely contrarian, falsifiable call (made in 2019, well before the 2021-2025 period in which several of these "bubble quality" names — Asian Paints, HUL-adjacent FMCG/quality compounders — did in fact de-rate/stagnate). It is the strongest evidence that Abakkus is NOT a quality-momentum shop; it is explicitly anti-quality-premium.

**Practical consequence of the anti-quality-premium stance: prefer the 2nd/3rd-ranked player in a sector, at a meaningful valuation discount to the (expensive) sector leader**, rather than paying up for the "best in class" name. This is repeated verbatim across multiple fund descriptions (All Cap Growth, Emerging Opportunities).

**Bottom-up, fundamentals-first, buy-and-hold, not "share trading."** Explicit language: invest in a company's stock "as if investing in the business like a partner." Emphasis on reading P&L, balance sheet, cash-flow statement, and valuation — "keep things as straightforward as possible... focus on the fundamentals and numbers."

**Patience as an active discipline, not a passive default.** Quote: "Most multibaggers happen when you forget the investment." The MEETS framework (below) exists explicitly as a periodic-reassessment checklist so that patience doesn't curdle into complacency — i.e., patience + a formal re-underwriting discipline, not just "buy and forget blindly."

**Cash-flow-over-reported-profit bias.** Explicit examples given in interviews (ValueResearchOnline "7 strategies to spot multibaggers" piece):
- Tata Communications: generated Rs 1,500-2,200 cr/yr operating cash profit despite reported losses; thesis = look through GAAP losses to cash generation → delivered ~5x.
- Tanla Platforms: four consecutive loss-making quarters were driven by depreciation/write-off accounting, while EBITDA stayed strong; distinguishing "one-time non-cash charge" from "real operating weakness" was the edge.

**Re-rating catalysts they explicitly look for (qualitative, event-driven, not mechanical screens):**
- Management change as a re-rating trigger (Tube Investments: new leadership → revenue tripled in 3 years → 10x return in 5 years).
- Consistent earnings-growth compounding driving P/E expansion, not just EPS growth (Polycab: revenue +17-18%/yr, profit +~29%/yr, P/E re-rated 13x→56x, ~10x total return).
- Corporate demergers unlocking a hidden, faster-growing segment (AGI Greenpac: sanitaryware-arm demerger → segment profit 10x → market cap Rs 494cr→Rs 5,900cr).
- Macro/market-wide discipline point: Singhania notes the BSE Smallcap Index itself compounded ~27%/yr over the prior decade in one telling — i.e., part of the "multibagger" outcome is just disciplined broad small-cap exposure + staying invested, not stock-picking wizardry. Worth noting for our own honesty: some of Abakkus's own "multibagger" framing may be beta (small-cap regime tailwind), not pure alpha — exactly the kind of claim our attribution-analyst persona would want to decompose.

**Exit discipline framed as harder than entry discipline.** Direct quote (paraphrased from ValueResearchOnline): "exits often demand more discipline than entries." Worked example given: a paper-industry stock bought at Rs 30-40, ran to Rs 100 with the thesis pointing toward Rs 300-400, but they exited early when management started making capital-allocation decisions (buying assets that "didn't make sense") that reduced return ratios — i.e., MEETS's "Management" leg tripped before price fully played out, and they took the (correct, per their own telling) discipline of walking away from further upside.

**Client-expectation-setting discipline:** publicly sets long-run equity return expectations at "12-14% CAGR," tied to what the Indian economy/earnings can structurally deliver — used as a discipline against clients (and perhaps the PM team itself) extrapolating a hot recent run.

Sources: valueresearchonline.com/stories/224367 (stock-selection & exit); valueresearchonline.com/stories/223709 (7 strategies); outlookbusiness.com "why select quality stocks may be in a bubble"; business-standard.com/article/opinion/a-bubble-in-quality-119110500042_1.html; x.com/SunilBSinghania/status/1190930397984706561; pmsaifworld.com/portfolio/abakkus-all-cap-growth-portfolio/.

## 2. Every specific mechanical rule found (verbatim/near-verbatim, with source)

### 2.1 The "15:15:15" discipline (the single most codable rule)
"Investments are made in businesses meeting at least two of the following criteria: ROE > 15%, earnings growth > 15%, P/E ratio < 15x." Multiple independent sources converge on this exact wording/threshold set (IME Capital review of Emerging Opportunities; ALTPORT review). It is explicitly a **2-of-3 scoring framework, not an AND-gate** — confirmed by Singhania's own statement elsewhere that he is willing to pay "40 P/E" for a company growing 20-25% (i.e., PE<15 leg can be dropped if ROE/growth legs are strong enough — so codifying this as a strict AND-filter would MISREPRESENT the actual rule; it must be coded as ≥2-of-3, and even then treat as a soft screen since anecdotal PM discretion clearly overrides it in named cases).
Source: imecapital.in/pms-scheme/abakkus-emerging-opportunities-pms; altportfunds.com/abakkus-emerging-opportunities-fund-review-2025-returns-strategy; valueresearchonline.com/stories/224367 (the "40 P/E for 20-25% growth" quote).

### 2.2 ROCE/ROE capital-efficiency floor
"Companies are filtered by their ability to consistently generate a minimum ROCE of 14-15%." Appears as a standalone screening criterion distinct from (though overlapping with) the 15:15:15 ROE leg — note ROCE ≠ ROE (ROCE is EBIT/capital-employed, pre-leverage; ROE is post-leverage, equity-only). Source material is not fully consistent on which metric (ROE vs ROCE) is the "real" screen — likely both are used loosely/interchangeably in press paraphrase; treat the 14-15% floor as directionally reliable but the exact metric identity as needing primary-source (factsheet) confirmation.
Source: valueresearchonline.com/stories/224367.

### 2.3 MEETS framework (5-pillar checklist, used at BOTH entry and periodic exit-review)
- **M**anagement — quality/track record, capital-allocation prudence, capex vs ROE impact, "decisional errors" evaluation.
- **E**arnings — quality of earnings, actual-vs-expected, cyclical-vs-structural; explicit target: "companies capable of doubling profits within 4 years OR halving EV/EBITDA within 4 years."
- **E**vents/Trends — market-event reactions, disruptive-theme monitoring.
- **T**iming — price-vs-intrinsic-value discounting, mean-reversion tracking.
- **S**tructural — competitive moat, size-of-opportunity, consistency of profit growth.
Re-applied periodically as a reassessment/exit checklist (not just an entry gate) — this dual-use (entry AND exit) is itself a specific, stated mechanical process point.
Source: pmsaifworld.com/portfolio/abakkus-all-cap-growth-portfolio (via WebFetch digest); valueresearchonline.com/stories/224367.

Note: one secondary source (altportfunds.com Emerging Opportunities review) glosses MEETS differently as "Moat, Earnings visibility, Efficiency, Trustworthy management, Steady cash flows" — this conflicts with the primary M-E-E-T-S expansion above (Management/Earnings/Events/Timing/Structural) used elsewhere and by the firm's own PPT-derived language. Treat the Management/Earnings/Events/Timing/Structural version as more authoritative (it recurs across more independent sources and matches the "Events or Trends" wording on the firm's own investment-profile PPT), and the "Moat/Efficiency/Trustworthy/Steady cash flows" gloss as a reviewer's own paraphrase-drift.

### 2.4 "4 Cs" avoidance rules (negative/exclusionary discipline)
Avoid: **C**hasing momentum; **C**hurning unnecessarily; **C**opying/mimicking other portfolios; **C**redit risk (over-leveraged / fractured balance sheets).
Source: pmsaifworld.com/portfolio/abakkus-all-cap-growth-portfolio.

### 2.5 Position and sector limits (explicitly disclosed, Emerging Opportunities Fund)
- Single-stock exposure restricted to **less than 10%** at time of investment.
- Sector exposure restricted to **not more than 30%** at time of investment.
- Portfolio typically **30-40 stocks**, diversified across "over a dozen sectors," no single sector dominant.
Source: pmsaifworld/altportfunds Emerging Opportunities portfolio pages (converged across ≥2 independent aggregator sources).

### 2.6 Market-cap allocation bands (differ by product — NOT a single firm-wide rule)
- **Diversified Alpha Fund (Cat III AIF):** fixed sleeve structure — 40% large-cap / 30% mid-cap / 20% small-cap / 10% micro-cap, with **10 stocks per bucket** (≈40 names total) — a genuinely mechanical, pre-defined-weight construction rule, unusual in its rigidity for an Indian PMS/AIF.
- **All Cap Growth PMS:** stated target ~40% large-cap / ~60% mid+small — BUT an independent May-2026 snapshot shows actual allocation at 61.47% large-cap / 10.55% mid / 20.98% small / 7% cash — i.e., the "target" is a stated intent, not an enforced band; real-world drift toward large-cap has been substantial and is the single biggest documented criticism of this product (see §4).
- **Emerging Opportunities Fund:** ~90% mid+small cap; target mid-cap company-size band of **Rs 5,000-35,000 crore** market cap (from the All Cap fund's own stated mid-cap definition, reused across products).
Sources: pmsaifworld.com (All Cap Growth + Emerging Opportunities pages); holisticinvestment.in/abakkus-all-cap-pms-review (actual composition + criticism).

### 2.7 Holding period / turnover discipline
Stated target **3-5 year holding period**; "buy & hold... not like a share trader"; low-churn philosophy is repeated across every product description. No numeric turnover-ratio ceiling was found in any public source (would require the SEBI disclosure document's turnover-ratio table, which we could not machine-read this pass — see §6 gaps).

### 2.8 Sector-relative valuation rule
"Typically pursues 2nd or 3rd player within the sector but at significant discount to the leader" — repeated near-verbatim across All Cap and Emerging Opportunities descriptions; this is the mechanical expression of the anti-quality-premium philosophy in §1.

### 2.9 Fee structure (portfolio-construction-adjacent, not a stock rule, but material to net-alpha claims)
- Fixed option: **2.5% p.a.** management fee (primary/default offering per most-recent review).
- Variable option: **1.75% AMC + 15% profit-share above a 9% hurdle**.
- Exit load: **1.5% in year 1**, nil thereafter.
Source: holisticinvestment.in/abakkus-all-cap-pms-review.

### 2.10 Client return-expectation anchor
Publicly communicates **12-14% CAGR** as the realistic long-run equity return expectation (not a stock-selection rule, but a stated firm discipline against return-extrapolation).
Source: valueresearchonline.com/stories/224367.

## 4. Failure modes / criticisms / bad periods (unbiased — style traps documented where found)

### 4.1 "All Cap" mandate has drifted to a large-cap-heavy, flexicap-like portfolio — the single sharpest documented criticism
An independent review (holisticinvestment.in, detailed "zero-based thinking" analysis) found that as of May-2026 the All Cap Growth PMS held **61.47% in large caps** (vs a stated target of ~40%), with top holdings (HDFC Bank, SBI, Axis Bank, L&T, Sun Pharma, Max Financial) closely mirroring what "any well-managed flexi-cap mutual fund" already holds. Direct quote: "When 61.5% of the all-cap mandate sits in large caps, you are not running a genuinely differentiated strategy—you are running a portfolio that, at its core, looks very much like any well-managed flexi-cap fund, just at roughly two to three times the cost." The review's practical conclusion: an investor already holding a diversified/flexicap mutual fund likely already owns 5-7 of Abakkus's top-10 names, for ~2-3x the fee.

### 4.2 Fee drag materially compresses realized (net) alpha, especially in weak years
5-year **net** alpha over BSE500 TRI is 2.83%/yr, but **gross** alpha (before the 2.5% fee) is ~5.33%/yr — i.e., the fee consumes **nearly half** the manager's actual outperformance. In the shorter 2-year window this is worse: net alpha 2.64% vs gross ~5.14% (fee ate ~49%). The reviewer's assessment: "In years of thin alpha (2022, parts of 2025), the fee consumed a disproportionate share of returns." This is a real, quantified, non-cosmetic failure mode — the manager's skill is real (consistent gross outperformance) but the product's fee structure makes the client's realized edge much thinner and more fragile than the headline CAGR suggests.

### 4.3 Calendar-year cyclicality — a genuine down year (2022: -1.37%) and a weak-alpha year (2025: +11.14%, only "2-3 points above index")
2022 was a straightforward loss year for the All Cap strategy (-1.37%), and 2025's +11.14% was assessed by the same reviewer as barely ahead of the benchmark once fees are considered — i.e., alpha is NOT delivered smoothly every year; there are periods where the fee is paid for benchmark-like or worse performance.

### 4.4 Mid/small-cap sleeve exhibits sharp short-term volatility clustering, consistent with a genuine style-specific risk (not just noise)
The flagship Emerging Opportunities Fund (≈90% mid+small cap) shows a 6-month return of **-0.9%** against a benchmark of **+5.9%** even within a snapshot (30-Jun-2025) where the 1-year (+11.2%) and 3-year (+32.1%) numbers were strongly positive — i.e., a sharp intra-period reversal. A separate (later, undated) snapshot shows the same fund's 1-year return at **-2.4%**. This is textbook mid/small-cap PMS risk: strong multi-year CAGR built on top of occasionally violent short-run drawdowns/volatility — directly analogous to our own house landmine on thin-liquidity slippage and circuit-lock risk (COST_STANDARDS §Dynamic slippage, `lib/execution_realism.py`) for anyone trying to systematize this style.

### 4.5 Capacity/crowding risk as AUM has scaled
One reviewer flags that a "Rs 1,606 crore small-cap sleeve in a single strategy creates real execution constraints" — i.e., the very AUM growth that Abakkus's brand/track-record has generated works against the "2nd/3rd player, undiscovered name" edge that the philosophy depends on. This is a standard alpha-decay-via-scale concern, self-evident but rarely quantified publicly by managers themselves; worth flagging for our own crowding-check discipline if we ever consider following Abakkus 13F-style disclosures for idea generation.

### 4.6 Track record is short relative to the confidence with which multi-year CAGR figures are quoted
Firm founded 2018; flagship PMS/AIF vintages from Oct-2020 (All Cap) — under 6 years of live numbers as of this study. One independent rating (IME Capital) explicitly notes several fund vintages in its dataset show "-" (not-available) for 1Y/3Y/5Y windows depending on inception date, and does NOT provide any explicit stress-test of the philosophy against a genuine bear market — the only real crisis inside the live-PMS window is the 2022 calendar-year drawdown (-1.37%, mild) and whatever the 2024-2025 mid/small-cap correction did to the Emerging Opportunities sleeve (documented in §4.4 as volatile, but not framed by any source as a "crisis test"). Singhania's OWN longer track record (pre-Abakkus, at Reliance/Nippon MF, through 2008 and 2018 drawdowns) is frequently cited in his bio/credibility narrative but is NOT the same legal entity/vehicle as Abakkus PMS, and none of our sources quantified that earlier record independently in this pass.

### 4.7 The philosophical strength (anti-quality-premium) is also its own style-trap risk, and Abakkus's own public communications are asymmetric about it
The 2019 "Bubble in Quality?" call is Abakkus's best-documented, most falsifiable, most contrarian public thesis, and — per generally available market knowledge of how expensive-quality names like Asian Paints performed 2021-2025 — it appears to have been broadly vindicated. BUT the mirror-image risk of a GARP/2nd-3rd-player/mid-small-cap-tilted process is the classic **value/laggard trap**: buying the cheaper #2/#3 player because the market is *right* that it deserves a discount (weaker moat, worse capital allocation, structurally lower growth) rather than *wrong* (mispriced). **We found no public Abakkus source that names a specific stock where this laggard-trap actually bit them** — every worked example in their own communications (the paper-company exit, Tube Investments, Polycab, AGI Greenpac, Tata Communications, Tanla) is a WIN or a well-timed exit, never a clean loss with a name attached. This is a real evidentiary gap: Abakkus's public-facing case studies are survivorship-biased toward good outcomes, which is normal PR behavior but means our "failure mode" picture for the STOCK-SELECTION side of their process (as opposed to the fee/AUM/mandate-drift side, which IS documented by third parties) is thin. Treat the absence of a documented named failure as a gap in available evidence, not as proof that no such failures exist.

### 4.8 No regulatory/compliance red flags found — but this was a shallow pass
No SEBI enforcement action, investor-grievance escalation, or governance controversy involving Abakkus itself surfaced in this search pass. We did NOT directly query SEBI's SCORES portal or do a deep news-archive sweep — this is an "unverified absence," not a "confirmed clean record." If this manager is ever used as a benchmark/comparator in a Principal-facing deliverable, a direct SEBI SCORES check should be done first.

## 5. Codability assessment (detailed — for Quant Head / Data Officer to action)

### Directly codable on our stack (PIT quarterly fundamentals + daily OHLCV + NIFTY500 PIT universe)
1. **15:15:15 screen (≥2 of 3):** ROE>15% (annual/TTM, PIT), TTM earnings growth >15% (rolling 4Q sum vs prior 4Q sum, PIT `available_date`-respecting), PE<15x (daily close ÷ TTM EPS). Fully codable. IMPORTANT: code as a ≥2-of-3 OR-scoring rule, never a strict AND-filter — Singhania's own quoted willingness to pay 40x PE for 20-25% growers proves the PE leg is droppable, and coding it as a hard AND-gate would misrepresent the manager's actual documented rule.
2. **Market-cap allocation bands / position-sizing / sector-cap constraints** (40/30/20/10 sleeve; <10% single stock; <30% sector; 30-40 name portfolios): these are portfolio-CONSTRUCTION rules (sizing/constraints), not selection signals — 100% mechanically codable in any backtest engine, no data gap.
3. **Holding-period / low-churn discipline** (3-5yr target, buy-and-hold): codable as a minimum-holding-period / rebalance-frequency constraint on the backtest engine — trivial, no data gap.
4. **"Double profits in 4 years" (backward form only):** codable as a trailing rolling-CAGR screen on PIT net profit (~18.9%/yr sustained over 16 quarters). NOTE: the manager's actual stated criterion is FORWARD-looking ("capable of doubling... within 4 years"), which is a forecast/expectation, not a historical screen — we can only mechanically proxy it with the backward-looking version (screen for demonstrated 4-year trailing profit-doubling as a quality-momentum-of-earnings signal), which is a genuinely different (weaker, look-back-only) rule than what Abakkus claims to apply prospectively.

### Partially codable — needs a Data Officer schema check before claiming codability
5. **ROCE ≥14-15% floor:** needs confirmation that `india_fundamentals_mc/Train.parquet` (or another PIT source) carries EBIT and Capital-Employed (or debt+equity-cash) fields distinct from ROE: if only ROE is available, we can only proxy ROCE with ROE, which is a materially different (leverage-blind vs leverage-sensitive) metric and should be labeled as a proxy, not the real rule, in any writeup.
6. **Cash-flow-over-reported-profit screens** (Tata Communications-style: OCF strongly positive despite reported net loss): codable IF our PIT fundamentals carry an operating-cash-flow line item distinct from net profit; needs schema check.
7. **One-time-write-off vs core-weakness distinction** (Tanla-style: EBITDA strong despite reported net loss from depreciation/exceptional items): codable IF PIT data carries a clean "exceptional items" or depreciation/D&A breakout separate from EBITDA; needs schema check — if the dataset only has consolidated PAT/EPS without an exceptional-items flag, this is NOT codable without adding a new field/source.
8. **Sector-relative "2nd/3rd player at a discount to the leader" rule:** needs (a) a sector/industry classification for the NIFTY500 PIT universe (check `NIFTY500_TICKER_2005_2025_Final.xlsx` or elsewhere for a clean sector tag), and (b) a "rank within sector by mcap/revenue + relative PE-to-leader" computation. If sector tags exist, this is codable as a relative-valuation screen using existing daily OHLCV (market cap) + PIT fundamentals (PE, growth). If no clean sector taxonomy exists in our stack, this needs a Data Officer gap-fill (ideally free, e.g., NSE sector classification) before it's codable — flag to Kavya Reddy.

### NOT codable with our current data — genuine gaps vs what Abakkus actually uses
9. **MEETS "Management" pillar** (capital-allocation-quality judgment, "decisional errors" evaluation, management-meeting-derived conviction) — qualitative, judgment-based; not present in PIT fundamentals. Could proxy narrowly with mechanical red-flags IF available in our data (promoter pledge % changes, auditor qualification flags, sudden M&A/capex spend spikes relative to operating cash flow = a "diworsification" proxy) — but these would be OUR proxy construction, not a direct replication of Abakkus's process, and would need explicit schema verification (do we have promoter-pledge or auditor-flag fields at all? Unknown — needs Data Officer check).
10. **MEETS "Events/Trends" pillar** (market-event reactions, disruptive-theme monitoring) and the event-driven re-rating catalysts they explicitly cite (management changes, demergers) — these require a structured corporate-actions/announcements feed (demerger filings, MD/CEO-change exchange filings) which we do NOT currently have as a clean PIT dataset. Acquiring one would be a NEW DATA SOURCE requiring explicit CEO+CIO joint approval per D-025/data-source gating — not to be auto-fetched.
11. **Forward earnings-growth / analyst-consensus inputs** (needed for the true forward-looking form of "double profits in 4 years" and "halve EV/EBITDA in 4 years") — we have no forward-estimate dataset; only backward-looking PIT actuals. This is a structural gap, not a schema-check gap: even with a schema fix we cannot manufacture forward analyst estimates from historical actuals without building our own forecasting model (which is a different, much bigger research program, not a "replicate Abakkus" exercise).
12. **Qualitative governance/promoter-quality assessment and channel checks** — genuinely un-codable; this is Abakkus's stated edge-differentiator and is explicitly the kind of "meet management, form a conviction view" input that no PIT fundamentals dataset can replicate. Any systematic replication of Abakkus's process will necessarily be a "GARP-screen-plus-portfolio-construction-rules" APPROXIMATION that captures the codable 30-40% of their stated process (the 15:15:15 screen + position/sector/holding-period rules) while leaving out the qualitative 60-70% (management judgment, event-driven catalysts, channel checks) that they themselves describe as central to how specific winners (Tube Investments, AGI Greenpac, Tata Communications) were actually identified and timed.

### Net codability verdict
We can build a defensible, honestly-labeled **"Abakkus-style GARP screen"**: NIFTY500 universe → PIT fundamentals filter (≥2-of-3 on ROE>15/growth>15/PE<15, using ROE as ROCE proxy pending schema check) → market-cap-band portfolio construction (mirroring the 40/30/20/10 or all-cap target, per product) → position/sector caps (10%/30%) → 3-5yr minimum holding period rebalance rule → daily OHLCV for pricing/PE. This would NOT replicate Abakkus's actual alpha (their edge is disproportionately in the qualitative/event-driven/management-judgment layer we cannot code), but it is a legitimate, honestly-scoped backtest of "the mechanical skeleton of their stated process" — useful as a factor-replication exercise (similar in spirit to our existing NIFTY factor-index replication work), NOT as a claim that we can reproduce their actual track record. Any writeup of results from such a backtest MUST carry this caveat explicitly, per our own D-028 lookahead/honesty standards.
