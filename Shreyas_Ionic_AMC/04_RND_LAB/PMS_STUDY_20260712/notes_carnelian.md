## 2. PHILOSOPHY (as documented, not marketing copy)

Stated house style: **"Quality Growth at a Reasonable Price" (QGARP)**. Four-part investment framework disclosed identically across Compounder/Bespoke/Shift decks and reiterated in the Shift Strategy presentation (Feb-2026, pages 5-12):

1. **Quality** — split into *Quality Business* (profitable, free-cash-flow generative, incremental return on capital, large opportunity/market size, sustainable moat, "environment & socially conscious") and *Quality Management* (integrity, "passionate & driven," efficient capital allocator, skin in the game, strong risk-management culture, strong governance). Their own tagline: **"Margin of safety not only lies in the price, but also in the quality of business and management."**
2. **Growth** — split into two "baskets": **Magic** (accelerated earnings growth + valuation re-rating, triggered by a structural catalyst: management/CEO change, industry-structure change, new growth catalyst, product innovation, or completion of a capex phase) and **Compounder** (stable earnings growth via large/growing opportunity size, sustainable moat, robust FCF generation, "decades of proven leadership without foreseeable disruption risk").
3. **Forensic Analysis — "CLEAR"** (their proprietary risk/red-flag framework, described identically across all strategy docs and the SEBI Disclosure Document itself references it): **C**ash-flow analysis (earnings conversion; reported vs. stable/sustainable cash flows), **L**iability analysis (true debt vs. reported debt, contingent liabilities and their likely future-earnings impact, payables vs. acceptances), **E**arnings-quality analysis (true economic profit vs. reported profit, discretionary vs. non-discretionary profit, expense-capitalization policy), **A**sset-quality analysis (core vs. non-core assets, intangible-creation policy), **R**elated-party & governance issues (related-party transactions, complex JV/subsidiary structures, off-balance-sheet arrangements — "connecting the dots"). Their framing: **"No matter what your investing style is, forensic analysis can help avoid some of the pitfalls inherent in human misjudgment."**
4. **Valuation** — multi-faceted (P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA, DCF), explicit governing quote (paraphrase of a Buffett-adjacent idea, attributed in their own deck as house philosophy, not sourced to Buffett explicitly): **"Value of an asset cannot, over the long term, grow faster than its earnings."** Objective stated as "reasonable valuation relative to growth," not cheapest-possible or momentum-agnostic.

A separate "MRFG" acronym is used specifically to underwrite names in the **Compounder** basket: **M**oat (sustainable competitive advantage + large opportunity size), **R**OE (high/durable return on equity), **F**CF (free cash flow generation), **G**rowth, and **G**overnance — sources differ slightly on whether the second G is "Growth & Governance" as one item or two; multiple secondary sources converge on "Moat, high ROE, Free Cash Flows, Growth & Governance profile" as the expansion. A separate **"CONNECT"** framework is referenced in secondary marketing summaries as covering non-financial/qualitative factors (leadership, governance, culture) but no primary-source page defining CONNECT's full letter-by-letter expansion could be located — flagged as **unverified/likely marketing shorthand**, possibly a rebrand or subset of the same management-quality checks already covered under "Quality."

**Risk is explicitly classified into three types by source**, not by a single VaR/beta number (Shift presentation, p.11): **Type A — Capital-loss risk** (inferior management quality, disruption-prone businesses) — this is what CLEAR is designed to screen out *before* investment. **Type B — Volatility risk** (temporary market dislocation, macro factors, geo-political/political issues, liquidity) — accepted as a cost of being invested, not eliminated. **Type C — Opportunity-cost risk** (lack of knowledge, human biases) — addressed via process discipline. Their framing: **"We believe knowing the source of risk is the key to risk management."**

An explicit **red-flags list** they screen out (Shift deck, p.12, epigraph "Invert, always invert — Carl Jacobi"): aggressive accounting practices, high financial leverage, low tax incidence (as a proxy for earnings-quality manipulation), management with no skin in the game / misaligned objectives, poor governance track record, or management "in a hurry to create value."

**A quantified factor lens — "Faster, Stronger, Cheaper"** is disclosed with actual portfolio-vs-benchmark numbers in the Feb-2026 Shift deck (p.27, source: Bloomberg consensus, FY25-27E): *Faster* = Revenue growth CAGR 15.2% (Shift) vs 11.1% (BSE500); Earnings growth CAGR 22.5% vs 13.8%. *Stronger* = ROE (FY27E) 18.8% vs 14.6%; D/E 0.0 vs 0.6 (near-zero leverage in the Shift book vs. benchmark). *Cheaper* = PEG ratio (FY27E) 1.1 vs 1.5; Portfolio Beta 0.9 vs 1.0. This is the clearest quantitative statement of their actual factor tilts found anywhere in the research — growth + quality (ROE, low leverage) tilt with a *relative* (not absolute) valuation discipline (PEG below benchmark's PEG, not below 1.0 in absolute terms).

**A material philosophy pivot is documented**: the Contra Portfolio Strategy was **"Carnelian YnG (Yield and Growth) Strategy" until 5-Feb-2025**. Per the Disclosure Document's own note (p.15-16, verbatim): *"Earlier, the strategy targeted investment opportunities in companies offering reasonable dividend yields, moderate earnings growth, potential re-rating catalysts, inflation hedges, and a margin of safety in valuations. The objective of the strategy is now evolved to create a portfolio consisting of companies with moderate earnings growth, reasonable valuations with potential re-rating driven by improvement in business cycle and improvement in temporary headwinds..."* — i.e., **the dividend-yield mandate was formally dropped** and replaced with a cyclical/contrarian-dislocation mandate. This is a real, disclosed strategy redefinition, not just marketing repositioning — worth flagging for anyone tracking the "YnG" name in older third-party writeups (pre-2025 descriptions with 5-7% yield targets are now stale/superseded).

## 5. FAILURE MODES / CRITICISMS — UNBIASED ASSESSMENT

This is the section where the research process ran into a real limitation: **every third-party source found (PMS AIF World, PMS Bazaar, Kalviro Ventures, IME Capital, Altport) is a distribution/referral-fee-funded marketing channel for the PMS itself**, and none contained an independent, adversarial critique. One fetch (Kalviro Ventures review) was explicit about its own limits when asked directly: *"This appears to be promotional content published by Kalviro Ventures (a distributor)... True critical analysis would include independent performance audits and acknowledged periods of underperformance."* No SEBI enforcement action, no negative press investigation, and no critical fund-of-funds/RIA teardown of Carnelian specifically was located in this research pass. That absence is itself a data point (small-ish, ~7-year-old boutique, likely below the AUM/visibility threshold that attracts investigative financial journalism), not evidence of a clean record — treat the below as the best documented signal available, not a complete picture.

**The one genuinely documented "bad period," from the audited SEBI Disclosure Document itself (Table A above):**
- **FY2022-23 (Apr-2022 to Mar-2023), Compounder Strategy: -1.12% absolute return, underperforming its own S&P BSE 500 TRI benchmark's +2.00% by ~3.1 percentage points.** This is a real, negative, audited number, not a marketing gloss — it sits right in the middle of two very strong years (+16.28% FY21-22, +54.49% FY23-24), so it reads as a genuine stall rather than a structural break. FY2022-23 was broadly a year of rate-hike-driven de-rating for quality/growth names in India (global tightening cycle, FII outflows, value/PSU/cyclical rotation) — i.e., this is consistent with the textbook QGARP/"quality at a reasonable price" style trap: a manager whose framework (CLEAR forensic + growth + moat) structurally underweights the low-quality-momentum and deep-cyclical/PSU names that led the market in exactly that window.
- **Shift Strategy in the same FY2022-23 window** only delivered **+2.45%** (vs its own benchmark's **-2.26%** — so it did beat its benchmark, but the *absolute* return of a mid/small-cap thematic strategy was near-flat) — consistent with the broadly rough FY22-23 for mid/smallcaps generally, and sitting between a blockbuster FY21-22 (+45.69%) and FY23-24 (+73.53%). The pattern across strategies (strong-weak-strong) suggests a single shared macro factor (the FY22-23 India mid/smallcap/quality-growth drawdown year) rather than manager-specific stock-picking failure — but it is real evidence that the house style is **not immune to regime risk**, and that a QGARP investor holding through FY22-23 would have experienced a materially flat-to-negative year across the flagship strategies while cash/value/PSU-tilted portfolios did better.
- **Contra/YnG Strategy's first (partial) reporting period, FY2021-22 (Jan-Mar 2022 only, its inception quarter): +2.68% vs benchmark +3.77%** — a modest underperformance, though on a very short/partial base and therefore low-confidence as a signal.

**Structural/style risks acknowledged (by Carnelian's own risk taxonomy and by third-party reviewers, in roughly descending order of how explicitly they're stated):**
- **Concentration risk is real and disclosed, not hypothetical:** 20-25 stock portfolios mean single-name mistakes matter disproportionately; one third-party review (Kalviro) states this plainly: "holds only 20-25 stocks, which increases stock-specific risk despite potential for alpha generation."
- **Fully-invested-equity exposure to market corrections:** despite the up-to-50%-cash discretion disclosed in the SEBI document, third-party characterization is that "all Carnelian strategies are fully invested equity portfolios and remain exposed to market corrections" — i.e., in practice the cash buffer is not used as an active market-timing tool (consistent with most Indian long-only PMS mandates, and consistent with the "we don't time markets, real money is made by remaining invested for long" framing Khemani has given in press interviews, e.g. Business Standard, Jul-2021).
- **Mid/small-cap illiquidity/volatility risk is explicitly acknowledged for the Shift Strategy** — both by Carnelian's own risk taxonomy (Type B "volatility risk" includes "liquidity" as a named sub-factor) and by third-party review ("additional volatility due to its mid- and small-cap focus"). Given the observed >50% small-cap weight snapshot (§3 item 10), this is not a minor footnote — a genuine smallcap-drawdown regime (2018, 2022, or any future one) would likely hit this strategy harder and faster than its large-cap-anchored siblings, and small/micro-cap names in a concentrated ~25-stock book carry real exit-liquidity risk in a fast sell-off (the classic PMS smallcap-illiquidity-drawdown failure mode flagged generically in the assignment brief).
- **Scalability/capacity concern, raised even by a favorable third-party review:** "As assets grow, maintaining agility in smaller companies may become more challenging" — i.e., the firm's own AUM growth (from a standing start in 2019 to ~USD1.6bn, largely via the smallcap-tilted Shift strategy's strong headline returns) works against the very smallcap agility that generated those returns, a classic PMS "growing out of the strategy's capacity" risk.
- **Track record still relatively young / team-pedigree-vs-PM-track-record gap noted independently:** IME Capital's own (otherwise favorable) rating page states: *"As a PMS platform, the translation of this institutional equities pedigree into a long-term fund management track record continues to be established"* — a polite way of saying Khemani's 17 years running institutional equities sales/research at Edelweiss is not the same skill as running money, and the actual PM track record is only ~6-7 years old (since May-2019) as of this research date.
- **No documented instance found of the more severe "quality-trap stagnation" failure mode** that afflicts some peers (e.g., a multi-year absolute-flat-or-negative period, or a > -20% peak-to-trough drawdown episode) — but this may reflect the shortness of the track record (no 2018-type or 2020-COVID-crash-full-cycle test in the audited 3-year performance table, since the earliest data starts FY21-22) rather than genuine all-weather robustness. The Disclosure Document's 3-year performance table is, by SEBI format rule, only ever a rolling 3-year window — it does NOT include the strategy's true inception-to-date drawdown history in one place, so a full max-drawdown figure was not locatable in this research pass for any strategy.

## 6. CODABILITY — WHAT WE CAN vs CANNOT BUILD WITH PIT FUNDAMENTALS + DAILY OHLCV + NIFTY500 UNIVERSE

### Codable now (directly, with data plausibly in our stack)
- **Growth-CAGR screens:** Shift's ">20% CAGR earnings growth, 3-5yr" and Contra's "12-15%+ earnings CAGR" — computable from PIT quarterly fundamentals (TTM growth, or trailing-N-year CAGR from `unified_quarterly_pit.parquet`-style data), respecting the `available_date` lookahead discipline already in our landmines list.
- **ROE/ROCE threshold screens** (Contra's "ROE >15%"; MRFG's "high ROE" for Compounder basket) — computable from PIT annual/TTM fundamentals, same caveat.
- **Leverage screen** (Contra's "D/E <1.5"; Shift's observed near-zero D/E vs benchmark 0.6) — computable IF balance-sheet debt & equity line items are present in our fundamentals dataset at sufficient granularity; needs a data-availability check against `india_fundamentals_mc/Train.parquet` schema before assuming this is ready to go.
- **Relative valuation / PEG-style screens** ("value cannot grow faster than earnings," PEG ~1.1-1.5 range, PE vs growth) — computable from daily OHLCV-derived trailing/forward PE (forward requires a growth estimate proxy, which we'd have to derive from trailing PIT growth as an analyst-estimate substitute since we lack sell-side consensus data) and PIT growth figures.
- **Portfolio beta target (~0.9-1.0)** — directly computable via rolling regression of stock/portfolio daily returns against NIFTY500 or BSE500 daily returns from OHLCV.
- **Position-size cap (~10% at entry, per the unverified-but-plausible secondary source)** — a pure portfolio-construction rule, trivially codable as a backtest constraint with zero data dependency.
- **Market-cap band tilts** (Shift's mid/small tilt, Contra's large-cap bias, Compounder's cited 50:30:20) — codable via market-cap classification (price × shares outstanding, or a market-cap field if present) cross-referenced against the `NIFTY500_TICKER_2005_2025_Final.xlsx` PIT membership snapshots for survivorship-correct universe construction.
- **Relative-underperformance-triggers-review rule** (the unverified ">20% underperformance vs benchmark → review/exit" rule) — mechanically trivial to code as a rolling relative-return stop from daily OHLCV + index data IF we choose to adopt it as a working hypothesis; the caveat is only about whether Carnelian actually follows this rule, not about our ability to code a version of it.

### Not codable / data we lack
- **The full CLEAR forensic framework** (true debt vs reported debt, contingent-liability impact, discretionary vs non-discretionary profit split, expense-capitalization policy, intangible-creation policy, off-balance-sheet arrangements, related-party "connecting the dots") — this requires footnote-level annual-report/XBRL parsing well beyond summary PIT fundamentals, and our own landmines list already flags that `india_fundamentals_mc/Train.parquet`'s `annual_report` column is corrupt at source — i.e., even if we wanted to attempt a crude forensic proxy, our best available raw-filing column is unusable.
- **Qualitative management-quality overlay** ("integrity," "passionate & driven," "skin in the game," "impeccable management capabilities," succession-risk assessment) — no PIT proxy exists in our data; at best, promoter-holding-change/pledge data (if we have it) could serve as a very crude governance-adjacent proxy, but it is not what Carnelian means by this criterion.
- **"Magic" basket catalyst identification** (management/CEO change, industry-structure change, new growth catalyst, product innovation, capex-phase completion) — largely event-driven/qualitative; a capex-completion proxy (deceleration in capex/PP&E growth following a multi-year capex ramp) is partially derivable from PIT fundamentals but would be a rough proxy at best, not a faithful replication.
- **Dividend-yield targeting** (pre-2025 YnG-era 5-7% target, current Contra 3-4% figure of uncertain currency post-redefinition) — codable only if we have reliable per-share dividend/payout PIT data; not confirmed present in our current dataset inventory — flag as a data-availability check needed before attempting this leg.
- **Cash-flow-yield targeting** (pre-2025 YnG's "10-11%") — needs operating-cash-flow-minus-capex (free cash flow) figures at PIT granularity; uncertain whether our fundamentals dataset carries full cash-flow-statement line items (vs. just P&L/some balance-sheet fields) — another data-availability check needed.
- **Sector-level thematic tilting** (Shift's ~2/3 manufacturing / ~1/3 tech split) — codable only if we have a reliable sector/industry classification mapped onto the NIFTY500 PIT universe; needs confirmation this mapping exists in our data.
- **IC-approval workflow / discretionary reassessment outcomes** — this is a governance/process step, not a data-driven rule, and is inherently not codable regardless of data availability.
- **"Niche capabilities," "large opportunity size," "proven track record"** — narrative/qualitative judgments with no direct numeric proxy in fundamentals or price data.

### Bottom line on codability
