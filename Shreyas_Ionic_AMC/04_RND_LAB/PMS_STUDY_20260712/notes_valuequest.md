## 3. Stated philosophy (verbatim/paraphrased claims)

- Tagline: **"#InvestorFirst"**; house maxim: *"Never buy on impulse, hold in hope, and sell in panic"* (stated as what NOT to do).
- Three-pillar research ethos: **"Wisdom to see, Fortitude to hold, and Courage to capitalize."**
- Explicitly **absolute-return / cash-flow oriented**, not benchmark-relative — i.e., they frame their own mandate as NOT index-hugging (this matters for interpreting drawdown years below: they will say big underperformance in a flat-benchmark year is "acceptable" under their own framework, which a skeptic should treat as a convenient post-hoc framing).
- Investment horizon: **rolling 3-5 years** (core Growth/Platinum); Agile scheme explicitly shorter, rolling 1-3 years.
- Explicitly **sector- and market-cap-agnostic** ("unbiased approach... freedom to follow the finest opportunities across market caps") — this is itself a claim to interrogate given the realized portfolio is currently ~54% small-cap by weight (see §6).
- Four "megatrends" (a.k.a. "gigatrends") identified for the next 5-10 years, as of most recent public commentary: **Energy Transition, Manufacturing, Financialisation, Consumption J-Curve**. Energy transition (specifically power transmission/grid infra ahead of renewable buildout) cited by Dharamshi as his single highest-conviction theme currently, with a dedicated 2-year, 147-source research effort ("GigaTrend" report, ~97 infographics, 6 renewable-tech sub-verticals, 7 global concept sections) publicly published as marketing/thought-leadership collateral.
- Framework for entering a megatrend: identify beneficiary sub-sectors, then assign "probability of success" to companies within — but Dharamshi explicitly warns **against treating the broad trend itself as an investable thesis**; the money is made in identifying the specific early-mover with limited competition inside the trend, not by buying the theme broadly.
- Proprietary **"S.C.A.L.E." framework** (their acronym, used across PMS + AIF marketing):
  - **S**calable — large addressable market, multiple growth engines/vectors.
  - **C**ompetitive advantage — moats, governance quality, balance-sheet integrity.
  - **A**daptive — agility to technological/structural shifts.
  - **L**ong runway — macro/demographic tailwinds behind the sector.
  - **E**xecution — management with proven capital-allocation track record.
- Related/possibly newer-vintage acronym for the AIF: **"GRIP" = Growth at Reasonable Inflexion Point** style — i.e., buy businesses at the inflection where they go from "average to great," participating in the re-rating as quality of the business improves (echoes a "growth at inflection" variant of GARP rather than static GARP).
- Research methodology described as **"proprietary research, forensic accounting, channel checks, and scuttlebutt"**, with an explicit stated preference for **"depth and differentiated insight... over broad coverage or checklist-driven desk research"** — i.e., they self-describe as deliberately NOT a quant/checklist shop. This is directly relevant to our codability question: they are telling us, in their own words, that a large share of their process is NOT reducible to a screen.
- Four-step research process (as summarized by aggregator sites, wording likely paraphrased not verbatim from VQ): (1) connect thematic/top-down dots → (2) filter for "hygiene factors" (quality/balance-sheet gates) → (3) 360-degree investigation (channel checks, forensic accounting, scuttlebutt, management meetings) → (4) analyze risk-reward asymmetry before sizing.
- Six "growth enablement" lenses applied to a company once past initial screens: governance, organizational structure, technology infrastructure, employee capabilities, company culture, strategic partnerships — again qualitative/management-quality factors, not financial-statement ratios.
- Stock-level criteria (qualitative, from site + secondary summaries):
  - Opportunity size — high earnings growth, sustainability, predictability of that growth.
  - Competitive advantages — brand, network effects, switching costs, IP.
  - Scalability/sustainability — adjacency expansion, technology adoption.
  - Management traits — "scrupulous, competent, capital-efficient" (governance-first language, consistent with SEBI-registered India PMS culture post the several 2018-2020 corporate-governance blowups in Indian small/midcaps).
  - Asymmetric risk-reward — explicit language of "variant perception" (i.e., positions where their view differs meaningfully from consensus) and "operating leverage" (preference for businesses where margin expansion amplifies EPS growth beyond revenue growth — a specific, somewhat codable lean, see §5).
- One specific valuation-methodology quote attributed to Dharamshi (re: HDFC Bank-type compounders): for a business generating **"20%+ ROE on a book growing 20%+,"** he says he values it **on a P/E basis rather than Price-to-Book**, treating it as a "consumer franchise" if growth/ROE combination is sustainable and predictable. This is the single most concrete, quotable valuation-methodology statement found — and it IS a stated numeric threshold (ROE ≥ ~20%, book growth ≥ ~20%) even if only illustrated via one bank example rather than stated as a universal screening rule.
- Portfolio construction / research-funnel language, consistent across the firm's own site and third-party summaries (numbers vary slightly by source/date, likely reflecting periodic revision of stated funnel — both versions logged):
  - Version A (valuequest.in): Universe ~400-500 cos (quality-filtered) → Focus ~150-200 cos (growth-filtered) → Core ~30-50 cos (valuation-filtered) → **Final portfolio 8-12 stocks**.
  - Version B (PMS AIF World / other aggregator paraphrase): Universe 500-600 cos (balance-sheet/mgmt/positioning) → Focus List 150-200 cos (growth drivers/market-share gains/improving P&L) → Core Research 30-50 cos (favourable risk-reward + margin of safety) → same 8-12 stock end-portfolio.
  - Net read: a roughly **500→150→40→10** stock funnel, i.e., extreme concentration by the end (order-of-magnitude 98%+ elimination rate from starting universe). This funnel shape itself IS codable as a scaffold (see §5) even though the actual filtering logic each stage uses is qualitative.

## 5. Codability assessment (against our PIT fundamentals + daily OHLCV + NIFTY500 universe stack)

**What we CAN approximate mechanically:**
- The funnel SHAPE (broad universe → quality gate → growth gate → valuation/risk-reward gate → concentrated final list) is a scaffold we already use in factor/quality screens — we could build a "ValueQuest-style funnel" as a proxy, but every gate's actual threshold is undocumented, so we'd be inventing the cutoffs, not replicating theirs.
- Operating-leverage preference: codable via PIT quarterly OPM (delta) vs. sales growth — flag names where EPS/PAT growth YoY consistently exceeds sales growth YoY (margin expansion signature). This is the most concrete, replicable piece of their stated stock-selection logic.
- The illustrative ROE-driven "value like a franchise" rule (ROE ≥ ~20%, growth ≥ ~20%) is directly codable from annual ROE/ROCE + TTM growth data — but it was given as ONE example (banks), not stated as their universal buy rule, so encoding it as a universal filter would be an assumption we're making on their behalf, not a documented VQ rule.
- Concentration (8-12 stocks) and multi-year holding horizon (3-5 yrs core, 1-3 yrs satellite) are directly encodable as portfolio-construction PARAMETERS for a "high-conviction concentrated quality-growth" backtest archetype inspired by VQ, even though we cannot replicate their actual stock picks.
- Megatrend/sector-tailwind framing (Energy Transition, Manufacturing, Financialisation, Consumption J-Curve) could inform a sector-tilt filter using NIFTY500 sector classifications + a momentum/growth overlay, as a loose thematic proxy — not a rule extraction.

**What we CANNOT codify — data/process we lack:**
- Forensic accounting (they explicitly name this as core to process) — requires qualitative judgment on related-party transactions, auditor changes, promoter pledge patterns, cash-flow-vs-accrual divergence beyond simple ratios; we have some PIT fundamentals but not the granular forensic-accounting checklist (footnote-level analysis) they claim to run.
- Channel checks and scuttlebutt (management meetings, dealer/distributor/customer conversations) — zero equivalent in our data stack; this is explicitly named as a differentiator vs. "checklist-driven desk research," meaning VQ itself claims this is where their edge (if any) lives, which is precisely the part we cannot replicate.
- Management-quality/governance scoring ("scrupulous, competent, capital-efficient") — no data source for this beyond crude proxies (promoter pledge %, related-party-transaction disclosures if available in fundamentals data), nothing close to their qualitative assessment.
- "Variant perception" (positions where their view diverges from consensus) is by definition not extractable from public financial data — it requires knowing the consensus view and their differentiated read, i.e., analyst judgment, not a data field.
- No numeric position/sector/cap caps were ever published, so even the "codable" funnel-shape replication above would require us to invent thresholds rather than encode theirs — any backtest claiming to "replicate ValueQuest's rules" would actually be testing OUR guessed proxy rules, and must be labeled as such, not as a replication.
- Bottom line: this is a **discretionary, concentrated, forensic/channel-check-driven quality-growth shop** with almost no publicly stated numeric screening rule beyond position count (8-12) and horizon (3-5yr / 1-3yr). Any codification attempt is a loose thematic proxy, not a rule extraction, and should be labeled "VQ-inspired concentrated quality-growth archetype," not "ValueQuest replication."

## 7. Failure modes, criticisms, style traps (unbiased — this is the section most relevant to the "quality-trap" brief)

- **This is NOT a low-volatility "quality compounder" style at all — it is a high-conviction, high-beta, small/mid-cap-tilted concentrated growth style that swings hard both ways.** Independent reviewer (Holistic Investment) explicitly: *"This strategy does not protect capital during corrections — it amplifies the move."* This directly contradicts a naive assumption that "quality/growth PMS = defensive"; ValueQuest's realized style is closer to concentrated small-cap momentum-growth than to a Nilesh Shah/quality-at-any-price stagnation trap. The relevant failure mode here is **concentration + small-cap-beta risk**, not quality-stagnation.
- **Realized portfolio market-cap mix (Platinum, per one snapshot):** Large-cap 21.0%, Mid-cap 22.3%, **Small-cap 53.7%**, cash 2.9%. This directly contradicts the firm's own "unbiased across market caps" framing — the actual book is currently more than half small-cap. Top-5 holdings = 43.4% of portfolio; top-5 sectors = 91.9% of portfolio (Capital Goods 23.9%, Pharma/Biotech 17.4%, Financial Services 14.8%). This is EXTREME concentration by both stock-count and sector-weight standards — a genuine, documented style-trap risk: a single sector drawdown (e.g. a Capital Goods capex-cycle reversal) could hit ~24% of the book at once.
- **Pattern of cyclical boom-bust years, not smooth compounding:** calendar-year swings from −7.92% (2021) to +65.6% (2024) to +53.3% (2025) to −4.65% trailing-1Y (as of the May-2026 cut) — i.e., "similar drawdown years in 2018, 2021, and 2022, each followed by recovery" per the independent reviewer, but also periods of straight underperformance vs. benchmark roughly HALF the calendar years on record (per IME Capital's "performance consistency mixed" language) — this is a real, source-documented criticism, not spin: the long-run CAGR is real, but the ride is lumpy and there is no guarantee the next drawdown resolves the same way as the last four.
- **Small-cap capacity/liquidity constraint flagged explicitly by an independent reviewer:** growing AUM (₹3,167 cr in Platinum alone) concentrated in small-caps faces "structural" liquidity limitations — i.e., a capacity/impact-cost ceiling risk as the strategy scales, which is exactly the kind of ADV/impact issue our own capacity-check framework would want to test if we ever tried to shadow this style.
- **Two-scheme product range only, historically** (Growth + Platinum; Agile is new, 2021 vintage) — one reviewer explicitly notes this as thin variety vs. competitor PMS houses with broader/longer multi-strategy shelves, and flags "13 years experience" as newer than some rival houses.
- **No stated forensic/quality kill-switch evidence found publicly** — i.e., we found no case study or public disclosure of ValueQuest catching (or missing) a governance blow-up in a held name; this is a gap in OUR research, not necessarily in their process — we simply could not find primary-source evidence either way and did not have access to a paywalled long-form interview archive to dig further.
- **Self-serving framing risk:** the firm's own "absolute-return, not benchmark-relative" positioning is worth flagging as a rhetorical hedge — it lets them wave off a year like the current one (Platinum −4.65% vs. Nifty50 also negative but by less, −4.04%) as "the price of admission for equity investing" (Dharamshi's own phrase for drawdowns) rather than as underperformance. An unbiased read: the phrase is defensible risk-philosophy in isolation, but it is also exactly the kind of language a struggling concentrated manager would use in a soft year — cannot be adjudicated without the raw monthly return series, which we did not have access to here.
- **Style-trap conclusion:** the documented risk here is the CONCENTRATED SMALL/MID-CAP GROWTH drawdown trap (sharp multi-year busts, sector-cluster risk, capacity ceiling), not the "quality-at-any-price stagnation" trap associated with some large-cap-quality PMS peers. Anyone benchmarking ValueQuest for our own factor-sleeve design should model it as a high-beta satellite/alpha sleeve, not as a low-vol quality anchor.
