## 1. Documented philosophy (own words, not paraphrase)
Aequitas Investment Consultancy Pvt Ltd (AICPL), founded 2012/13 by Siddhartha Bhaiya (ex-Head of PMS division, Reliance/Nippon Mutual Fund). Boutique, UHNI-only PMS/AIF/FPI manager, AUM ~₹7,700-7,900 cr (Nov 2025 / Mar 2026 decks).

Self-described **"Multibagger Approach" = VALUE + CONTRARIAN + GROWTH confluence** (their own deck, verbatim):
- **VALUE**: "The valuation has to be reasonable... there has to be potential re-rating. A combination of EPS growth and P/E re-rating leads to multibagger returns."
- **CONTRARIAN**: "does not mean doing the opposite of others, it rather means doing things differently. Buying in popular names will not provide multibagger returns."
- **GROWTH**: "The company has to be a growth company with above average growth potential for the next 3-5 years. Markets reward a higher P/E multiple for growth companies."

Bhaiya's own quotable framing (multiple interviews): *"It is not just value or growth or a contrarian approach, it's a confluence of all three."* Look for industry leaders, best players in out-of-favour sectors, highest market share/moat, corporate governance. *"Market cap to sales is one of the best ratios"* — deliberately avoids segmenting by market-cap bucket (small/mid/large) because *"capitalization is a stupid matrix"* — instead sizes conviction by earnings power, i.e. "big companies trading at small valuations," not literally small companies. His own quote on style: *"I want to see blood on the Street because that's when you make a multibagger"* — explicit crash-buying / distressed-sentiment contrarianism (executed in March 2020 COVID crash). Capital preservation is explicitly ranked ABOVE return-seeking: *"Errors of commission hurt more than errors of omission"* (false-positive buys are costlier than missed opportunities — an asymmetric, bias-toward-inaction discipline). Also: *"If you minimize the downside, you automatically increase your probability of going right."*

Bhaiya's last public market call (Dec 2025, per The Ken) was a bearish valuation warning: *"We are in a bubble of epic proportions... Nifty PE is more than 40"* (ex certain PSU names), mid/smallcap PEs "in excess of 50," and a memorable line contrasting SIP inflows against promoter selling: *"It is not SIP. It is SWT — systematic wealth transfer from India's middle class to the rich."* His last public investor guidance: *"Don't listen to narratives. Go back and look at your portfolio. What is the PE multiple of stocks in your portfolio?"*

Sources: [pmsaifworld](https://www.pmsaifworld.com/portfolio/aequitas-india-opportunities-pms/), [pmsbazaar](https://pmsbazaar.com/FundManager/Aequitas-Investment-Consultancy-Pvt-Ltd/Siddhartha-Bhaiya), [Aequitas blog — 21-year journey](https://www.aequitasindia.in/blog/unveiling-the-path-to-multi-baggers-wisdom-from-siddhartha-bhaiyas-21-year-investment-journey/), [The Ken profile](https://the-ken.com/story/siddhartha-bhaiyas-aequitas-is-the-fund-for-the-ultra-rich-that-can-win-by-not-playing/), [scanx.trade obit feature](https://scanx.trade/stock-market-news/stocks/aequitas-managing-director-siddhartha-bhaiya-passes-away-following-cardiac-arrest/28893411), own Nov-2025/Mar-2026 investor decks.

## 2. EVERY specific mechanical rule found (with source)

### 2a. From the formal SEBI Disclosure Document (dated 1-Dec-2025, the legally binding one — most authoritative source; converted from `Aequitas-PMS-Disclosure-Document.pdf`)
- **Minimum number of stocks: 10. Maximum number of stocks: 30.**
- **No individual stock >15% of the portfolio at time of investment.**
- **No individual sector >30% of the overall portfolio at time of investment.**
- Equity allocation range: **0% to 100%** (i.e., legally free to go to all-cash — this is the enabling clause behind the 2024 cash call, see §4).
- Bottom-up stock selection; portfolio = "highest conviction ideas."
- Objective: pick companies with **"return ratios higher than the market"** and **"valuations lower than market valuations"** (i.e., ROE/ROCE-above-index, PE-below-index screen — directionally quantifiable, though no numeric threshold is codified in the legal doc itself, unlike the marketing deck's explicit <20x).
- Portfolios "expected to be fairly concentrated with an average of 15-20 stocks."
- Investment horizon: "generally 3 to 5 years."
- Benchmark: Nifty 50 TRI (chosen for broad-market representativeness).
- Permitted tactical/defensive sleeve: Gold ETFs, Silver ETFs, money-market/debt/liquid funds — explicitly for "liquidity management, defensive/tactical positioning, or pending deployment."
- Zero related-party/associate-company investment.
- **Audit observations of the previous 3 years: NIL. Penalties/pending litigation/regulatory findings: NONE disclosed** — clean compliance record.
- Fee structure: management fee **cap 2% of AUM**; performance fee with **high-water mark**, charged annually post first-year completion (marketing deck states 2% + 10% profit share w/ HWM specifically).
- **Minimum investment: ₹25 crore (PMS) / USD 3 million (international)** per the Nov-2025/Mar-2026 investor decks — note this is far above the SEBI PMS floor of ₹50 lakh and above the ₹10cr/₹3cr figures an older pmsaifworld snapshot cited; the minimum ticket appears to have been raised over time (or pmsaifworld's figure is stale/wrong — flag for verification, don't treat both as simultaneously true).

### 2b. From the investor decks (Nov-2025 and Mar-2026, text identical between the two — i.e., survived the leadership transition unchanged)
- **Portfolio construction**: 15-20 (deck) vs 10-30 (legal doc) stocks, balanced across sectors, each with an identifiable "catalyst for stock re-rating."
- **Explicit numeric risk-management thresholds** (their own "Risk Managers" slide):
  - *Valuation risk control*: **"The average P/E for the fund is likely to be less than 20x."**
  - *Earnings risk control*: use earnings "adjusted for cyclicality" — assess normalized/trough earnings, not peak-cycle earnings, before valuing a cyclical.
  - *Balance-sheet risk control*: target **zero to negligible debt** and strong operating cash flow; explicitly avoid "overleveraged balance sheet... ignored during good times in favor of the cyclic high and unsustainable earnings."
- **Selection-criteria checklist** (their own "Selection Criteria" slide, six named factors): (1) Industry leaders w/ sustainable competitive advantage/moat; (2) Low/negligible debt, some net-cash positive; (3) Management quality — dividend record + corporate governance + long track record; (4) **"Majority of our companies have done buyback / creeping acquisition in the 12 months prior to our initial acquisition"** — a specific, dated, and CODABLE pre-entry signal (promoter buyback/creeping-acquisition filings are public); (5) **Portfolio P/E < market P/E, adjusted for cyclicality**; (6) Consistent, strong cash-flow generation.
- **Explicit exclusion list ("We Avoid")**: impulsive decisions, being influenced by financial-news channels, acting on market moves, chasing hot stocks, trading/short-term positions, **derivatives** (categorically excluded from the vehicle).
- **Explicit red-flag list**: low promoter holding, poor corporate governance, high institutional ownership (!— i.e., they treat heavy FII/DII ownership as a negative screen, consistent with the contrarian/undiscovered-name thesis), leverage, "fad/hot" sectors, expensive valuations, **IPOs**, **QIPs/secondary equity sales** (all four of the last three are explicit no-go categories for entry).
- **Research cadence, explicitly time-boxed**: *Daily* — news, corporate announcements, insider buying/selling, commodity prices. *Quarterly* — "running queries," channel checks, management meetings. Screener inputs: annual report, quarterly results, industry/company news, management interviews, press releases, conference calls/analyst meets, peer analysis.
- **Ongoing-research checklist**: globally dominant businesses with strong moats; sales growth & PAT growth; valuation dislocation via EV/Sales, EV/EBITDA, P/E; debt; dividend track record; quality of ownership; long-term track record.
- **Explicit market-cap-agnosticism argument** ("Multibaggers Know No Size" slide) with two supporting exhibits: (a) large caps that became near-worthless — RCom (-100%), Reliance Power (-74%), DLF (-56%), Jet Airways (delisted, -100%), Yes Bank (-95%), Suzlon (-84%), Unitech (-99%); (b) small caps that became huge multibaggers since 2002 — Kotak (245x), IPCA (175x), Sun Pharma (129x/162x dual entries shown), Axis (130x), ICICI (89x), HDFC (53x), Airtel (unspecified). Their own house multibagger book (return buckets, own deck): >100x Avanti Feeds; >50x Apar Industries, Sanghvi Movers; >20x Jindal Stainless (JSL), Gujarat Ambuja Exports (GAEL), HEG, Finolex Cables, TIIL; >15x Maithan Alloys, Nilkamal, Power Mech Projects; >10x HIL, Garware, CCL Products, Cosmo First; >5x "15+ stocks."
- **Holding-period discipline** (from the 21-year-journey blog interview): average holding period **>5 years**; **top 7 of the current top-10 holdings held >8 years**; explicitly describes a staged-conviction-build approach — "invest regularly in a company monthly for 3-5 years," gradually extending hold duration to 2/3/5-yr tranches; named example Apar Industries took "4-5 years to even start paying off" before becoming a 36x.

### 2c. Fee/vehicle mechanics
- 2% p.a. management fee on daily-average AUM, billed monthly.
- 10% profit share, subject to high-water mark, billed annually.
- ₹25 cr PMS / $3mn international minimum ticket (per latest deck).

## 4. Failure modes, bad periods, criticisms — UNBIASED pass

1. **Key-person risk crystallized (Dec-2025)**: see §0. This is not a hypothetical style-trap; it is a live, current, material risk that IME Capital and (implicitly) the press treat as the #1 risk factor right now. Historical returns are Bhaiya's; forward returns are an unproven successor team's (albeit with 3+ years of Daftari continuity as day-to-day FM).
2. **Style-trap candidate — "quality/contrarian-at-any-price" market timing risk cuts both ways**: the fund's single biggest post-2024 decision was a top-down valuation call — cutting small-cap holdings from ~63% (May-2024) to ~14% (Dec-2024), reportedly later reaching ~80-93% cash and then rotating a large chunk into Gold/Silver ETFs (per The Ken and per the businesstoday portfolio snapshot showing ~90.85% cash with a rump 5-stock equity sleeve). **This is explicitly NOT pure bottom-up stock-picking — it is a discretionary top-down market-timing overlay layered on top of the bottom-up multibagger process, legally enabled by the 0-100% equity mandate.** If the market call is wrong or mistimed, this is a source of large tracking error and potential severe underperformance in a period where smallcaps keep rallying while the fund sits in cash/gold. The Dec-2023 "bubble" call and the 2024 cash-raise were vindicated by the 2025 smallcap correction (per The Ken's retrospective framing) — but this is precisely the kind of survivorship-flattering narrative that needs adversarial scrutiny: a manager who correctly times ONE cycle gets lionized; the multi-cycle base rate for such top-down timing calls is generally poor, and Bhaiya himself is no longer around to make the next one.
3. **Illiquidity / small-cap-microcap concentration risk** — explicitly disclosed in the manager's own SEBI document ("low liquidity," "trading volumes, settlement periods and transfer procedures may restrict the liquidity of the Investments," "risk arising out of non-diversification" from concentrated sector/stock bets). A 15-20 stock, small/micro-cap-tilted, up-to-15%-per-stock/up-to-30%-per-sector portfolio is structurally exposed to exit-liquidity risk in a drawdown — exactly the kind of risk this firm's own `07_RISK_OFFICE/LOOKAHEAD_CONTROLS.md` and `lib/execution_realism.py` slippage/circuit-lock landmines are designed to catch if this style were ever replicated mechanically.
4. **Low churn / long holding periods (>5yr avg, top-7-of-10 >8yr) is a double-edged discipline**: it avoids overtrading and tax drag, but it also means the portfolio can sit through multi-year periods of a thesis simply not working (Apar Industries cited as "took 4-5 years to even start paying off") — a real "stuck value/growth thesis" stagnation risk, survivorship-narrated here only because it eventually worked (36x); the firm does not appear to publicly document the names that did NOT pay off after a 4-5 year wait (no visible "graveyard" disclosure of killed/exited long-held losers).
5. **Benchmark choice (Nifty 50, not a small/microcap index) flatters trailing "alpha"** — see §3 adversarial flag #3. This is a documentable, structural criticism of how returns are marketed, independent of stock-picking skill.
6. **No independently-audited risk metrics found**: no Sharpe ratio, standard deviation, or max-drawdown figure was located on any portal (pmsaifworld, businesstoday, IME Capital) or in the SEBI disclosure document (which is not required to disclose these). All qualitative risk narrative, no quantitative risk-adjusted-return disclosure — a gap versus more transparent PMS peers who do publish std-dev/Sharpe on aggregator sites.
7. **"Asia Hedge Funds" league-table rankings are a category mismatch** worth flagging as potential marketing over-reach (see §3 flag #4) — a long-only, no-derivatives, buy-and-hold equity PMS being ranked in a "hedge fund" table is unusual and the ranking provider/methodology is not named in the deck.
8. **2022-23 relative resilience is real but modest in absolute terms**: discretionary PMS delivered +19.82% in a flat-to-down Nifty year (-0.60%) — genuinely good relative performance, but the manager's own most dramatic edge shows up in the big beta years (FY23-24 smallcap rally), which is the standard "looks great when the factor tailwind blows" pattern that needs decomposition, not the flatter years that better isolate stock-selection skill.
9. **Advisory-service (non-discretionary) product showed much higher dispersion than the discretionary product** in FY22-23 (6.41% vs discretionary 19.82%, both same Nifty benchmark) — suggests the "bespoke, non-model portfolio" claim can cut against clients when the manager doesn't have full discretion/timing control, i.e., the same philosophy executed with client-driven timing frictions produces materially worse results — an execution-realism lesson directly relevant to how this firm should model any "signal + human discretion" hybrid strategy.

## 5. Codability assessment — what we CAN vs CANNOT build with PIT fundamentals + daily OHLCV + NIFTY500 universe

### CAN be coded (mechanical, PIT-fundamentals + OHLCV only)
| Rule | Codability |
|---|---|
| Min 10 / max 30 stocks; 15-20 target | Trivial — portfolio construction constraint, no data dependency |
| No stock >15% at entry; no sector >30% at entry | Trivial — position-sizing/rebalancing logic on our existing weights engine |
| Portfolio-average P/E < 20x (and < market P/E) | **Codable** — quarterly/TTM EPS from PIT fundamentals + daily close gives trailing P/E; compare to NIFTY500 (or Nifty50) median/mean P/E each rebalance date |
| "Return ratios higher than market" (ROE/ROCE screen) | **Codable** — annual ROE/ROCE from PIT fundamentals vs NIFTY500 cross-sectional median, same-period comparison (must be careful about annual-report lag / `available_date`, per our landmine #3) |
| Sales growth & PAT growth ("above-average growth," "EPS growth + re-rating") | **Codable** — TTM YoY growth from PIT quarterly fundamentals; can build a growth screen (e.g., top-quartile sales/PAT growth vs NIFTY500) |
| Low/negligible debt (balance-sheet risk control) | **Codable** if debt/equity or net-debt/EBITDA is in our PIT fundamentals set (need to confirm field coverage — check `india_fundamentals_mc/Train.parquet` schema) |
| Cash-flow consistency screen | **Partially codable** — needs operating cash flow line item, PIT, multi-year; check field availability |
| Market-cap-to-sales ratio screen | **Codable** — market cap (daily) / TTM sales (PIT) |
| EV/Sales, EV/EBITDA valuation-dislocation screens | **Codable** if EBITDA + net debt fields exist PIT; EV = mcap + debt − cash |
| Long holding period / low churn (>5yr avg hold, monthly-tranche entry) | **Codable as a backtest RULE** (e.g., min holding period before exit, staged entry) — but this is a portfolio-construction/backtest-mechanics choice we impose, not something we can "detect" was Bhaiya's true intent stock-by-stock |
| "Avoid IPOs / QIPs/secondary sales within window" | **Codable** — flag recent-IPO names (e.g., <12-24 months listed) and exclude; QIP/secondary-sale flagging needs a corporate-actions feed (check if we have one) |
| Institutional-ownership red flag ("high institutional ownership" = avoid) | **Partially codable** — needs FII/DII/institutional shareholding-pattern data (quarterly shareholding pattern filings); flagged in our CLAUDE.md as a data source that has been 403'ing (FII/DII "still 403 → home network/VPN") — currently a gap, not fully in hand |
| Promoter buyback / creeping-acquisition in prior 12 months (their #4 selection criterion) | **Partially codable** — needs buyback announcement data + SAST creeping-acquisition disclosure (promoter shareholding delta QoQ from shareholding-pattern filings); shareholding-pattern deltas may be derivable from the same institutional-ownership data source noted above once acquired |
| Sector-cyclicality-adjusted earnings ("earnings at bottom of cycle") | **Hard to fully codify** — requires a definition of "cycle trough" per sector; could proxy with multi-year normalized/average EPS instead of TTM, but this is a judgment call not a hard rule |

### CANNOT be coded with our current data (needs data we lack)
- **Management-meeting / channel-check qualitative research** (their explicit "quarterly: channel checks, management meets" and "management interviews" screener input) — no dataset substitute; this is genuinely unstructured qualitative diligence.
- **Corporate-governance / promoter-quality judgment** ("efficient and ethical management," "sound long-term performance record," "disciplined personality" style commentary) — partially proxyable via governance-score datasets (which we don't currently have) or via promoter-pledge/related-party-transaction red flags if we had that feed; currently not codable beyond crude proxies.
- **"Moat"/competitive-advantage and "industry leader" qualitative calls** — market-share data by sector is not in our PIT fundamentals set; could crudely proxy with relative revenue rank within a sector bucket, but this is a stretch, not a direct replication.
- **Discretionary top-down cash/gold market-timing overlay** (the single highest-impact decision in the 2023-2025 window) — this was explicitly Bhaiya's personal macro judgment call ("bubble of epic proportions," index PE >40 commentary), not a rule expressed in numbers we could mechanically trigger without building our own regime/valuation-timing model — and even then we would be reverse-engineering a justification for a discretionary call after the fact, not replicating a documented rule.
- **Insider buying/selling + commodity-price daily monitoring** — insider-trading disclosure data (SAST) is a feed we do not currently have integrated; commodity prices are gettable but not currently wired into this manager's specific use-case (sector cyclicality timing).
- **Analyst calls / conference-call transcript reads** — not in scope of PIT fundamentals + OHLCV.

### Net assessment
Aequitas's **entry-screen mechanics (valuation, growth, quality-of-balance-sheet, position/sector limits)** are substantially codable with what this firm already has (PIT fundamentals + NIFTY500 universe + OHLCV), assuming field coverage for debt/cash-flow/EBITDA is confirmed in `india_fundamentals_mc/Train.parquet` or equivalent. The **hardest-to-replicate and arguably highest-alpha-contributing element (the discretionary top-down cash/gold timing call, and the qualitative management/channel-check diligence)** is NOT codable from our data and would need to be either (a) excluded from any mechanical replication (accept we're building "Aequitas-screen-only," always-invested, no market-timing version) or (b) separately modeled as an independent regime/valuation-timing overlay with its own validation, clearly labeled as "inspired by" rather than "replicating" Bhaiya's process. Any backtest claiming to replicate Aequitas's full historical return MUST NOT assume we can time the 2024 cash call — doing so would be textbook lookahead (T-taxonomy per our `LOOKAHEAD_CONTROLS.md`) since that call was Bhaiya's personal, non-mechanical, non-repeatable market judgment.
