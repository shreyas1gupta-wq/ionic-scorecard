Memo 1 wins on depth; Memo 2 wins on catching things that would otherwise quietly pass.
- **Lens 3 (technical/timing):** Memo 1 is clearly more complete here — six distinct, mostly actionable points (stage tagging, ATR proxy, stop/R-multiple mislabeling, exit-timing lag, RED-state vagueness, liquidity×entry interaction) against Memo 2's three-to-four (eligible-set thinness, daily/weekly code-asymmetry, manual chart review of losing trades, generic entry-lag-into-bull-leg observation). Overall: not a case of one memo being thin. Both are substantive, specific, and mostly non-redundant with each other. Memo 1 is the more voluminous and execution-ready document (especially for the DESK-100 build/BT-11 spec). Memo 2 is more economical but independently surfaces at least two things — the cross-book correlation gap and the kill-#4 circularity — that are arguably more consequential for an actual IC capital decision than most of Memo 1's Lens-3 diagnostics, which are explicitly framed as cheap non-gating refinements.

## 3. Which one would I act on before an IC decision

I would pick **Memo 1** as the working document — it is the more complete build/validation playbook (explicit Gate-4 sign/no-sign criteria, concrete cheap diagnostics, worked DSR reasoning) and would drive a materially better BT-11 spec on its own.

But I would **not** take it to IC without patching in two things from Memo 2 first, because their absence in Memo 1 is a real gap, not a stylistic omission:
1. A cross-book correlation test — Track-2 vs the **short-vol book's own return series**, jointly in the 2018/2020/2022 slices — since Memo 1's own required orthogonality check only covers Track-2 vs S-06/S-07 (within-book), leaving the actual "only non-short-vol driver in the firm" claim untested.
2. An explicit check on whether kill-criterion-#4 (regime gate cuts DD in 2018/2020/2022) is circular — i.e., whether P5's threshold was set using knowledge of those same crisis outcomes.

Without those two, I'd be taking a diversification thesis and a crisis-DD validation to IC that could both be quietly self-referential.
