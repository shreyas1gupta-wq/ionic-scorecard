**(a) The honest trial count is not 4.** §9 opens the ledger at "≥4 prior configurations." That is not honest for Bailey-LdP. The prototype went +21%→+11.6% by sweeping "tighter stop/regime" — that alone is many undocumented cells. **(b) A Sharpe near ~0.5 may not clear DSR>0.95 once N and skew are honest.** The prototype Sharpe was 0.43 full / 0.60 OOS. DSR deflates SR by the expected max-Sharpe-under-null across N trials (grows ~√(2 ln N)) and *penalises negative skew* in the denominator. Momentum is **negatively skewed** (leadership crashes) — that pushes DSR *down*. A ~0.5 annualized Sharpe, negative skew, and N in the dozens is a genuine coin-flip against the 0.95 bar even with 17y of weekly returns. **This is more likely to kill the idea than the liquidity gate the spec names as #1.** It is not a reason to kill pre-build — but the team should expect it, and should NOT respond by quietly shrinking N to rescue DSR. If DSR fails honestly, that is the answer.

A regime-gated long-only small-cap book holds ~12–30 names that are **all long momentum simultaneously**. In a 2014 (+76%) or 2021 (+62%) bull run, those are ~one bet, not 20–30. The "≥30 trades/param" minimum is nearly vacuous here: 30 trades inside two bull episodes ≈ 2 independent draws. Consequences:
- **Weekly-portfolio Sharpe will look better than reality** — the √N variance cut from co-moving positions is exactly the artifact IC-1 flagged. **Certify PER-TRADE**, and additionally report a **block-bootstrap / regime-block** Sharpe where the block = one GREEN episode, not one week.
- **Run the incremental-vs-base shuffle BEFORE certifying** (my standing rule): what remains after stripping small-cap-beta and momentum-regime beta? If the edge collapses to "was long small-caps during two bull runs," this is beta, not alpha, and belongs sized as a convex diversifier, not a certified edge — which is, to Devika's credit, exactly how the spec proposes to *defend* it (diversification + episodic convexity, not headline CAGR). Stats must confirm that framing, not the CAGR framing.

- **Kill #2 metric is mis-specified.** It compares full-period **CAGR to N500 buy-&-hold**. A regime-gated strategy sits in cash by design → lower CAGR is *expected and correct*, not a failure. The honest kill is **risk-adjusted (Sharpe/Calmar) vs the right benchmark**, and the right benchmark is **Nifty Smallcap-250 B&H** (the book fishes small-caps) plus the **naive always-on RS-momentum** variant the spec already includes (isolates the gate's contribution). Recommend rewording Kill #2 to risk-adjusted terms before the run so a correct-cash result is not mechanically scored as a kill.
- **I will sign Gate-4 iff:** honest N logged and DSR>0.95 survives it; PBO<25%; P5 (regime breadth) shows a *plateau* not a spike; per-trade / regime-block Sharpe (not just weekly-equity) clears the bar; net-positive at 2× costs; no catastrophic slice in {2018,2020,2022}; and the incremental-vs-beta shuffle leaves something after small-cap+momentum beta.
- **I will NOT sign** on a weekly-equity Sharpe alone, on DSR computed with N=4, or on a single flat-but-correct-cash 2025 OOS being read as either confirmation or refutation — a flat OOS in a non-momentum-regime year is *uninformative*, and must be labelled uninformative, not passed.

None triggerable pre-build, but pre-register the thresholds: Sharpe>4 (won't happen here — the *opposite* risk), win-rate>75% with W/L<0.5 (momentum is low-win/high-payoff — watch for the inverse: a high W/L masking a lottery of two years), P&L concentration (**expected and central here** — measure Herfindahl of P&L across GREEN episodes; if >~0.5 of P&L is in 2014+2021, that IS the result, disclose it), R²>0.98 on the equity line (a too-smooth equity curve would signal the fake-low-variance booking bug — the exit-period booking should prevent it, verify), ADV violations at fills.

### Lens 3

**Idea:** T2-MOM-01 — Track-2 small-cap leadership momentum, regime-gated
**Reviewed against:** `04_RND_LAB/ideas/20260703_track2_engine_spec.md` (2026-07-03, Devika Menon)
**Scope of this lens:** is "leadership momentum" a real trend-template setup, does the entry/exit logic deserve the name, what does stage analysis add, and where is the timing risk in the engine as spec'd — not a P&L or stats verdict (that's Quant/Overfit's lens).

---

**[DATA]** §2.1 of the spec reproduces my 8-criterion trend template verbatim and correctly: close>150/200MA, 150>200, 200MA rising ≥22 sessions, 50>150>200, close>50MA, ≥30% above 52w-low, within 25% of 52w-high, RS percentile ≥70. All-criteria, no partial pass — that's the right discipline, and I'll say plainly: most in-house adaptations I've seen elsewhere water this down to a 5-of-8 or "mostly" pass. This spec didn't. Credit where due.

**[OPINION]** But criterion 9 — VCP volume contraction through the base, the actual pivot-identification mechanism — is explicitly deferred to v2 (§2.4), and v1 substitutes a single flag: entry-day or prior-day volume ≥1.5× 50d average. That is not a substitute, it's a different signal. The trend template's 8 criteria tell you a stock is a *qualified leader in an uptrend*. They do not tell you *where in that uptrend you are* or *where the risk is defined*. VCP does both — it's the difference between "this stock is strong" (true of a name up 40% in the last month) and "this stock just built a low-risk base with a knowable pivot and a knowable stop." Without it, the leader-board in v1 will rank-order names purely on 12-1/6m momentum among trend-template passers, and that will mix two very different animals:
- genuine pivot breakouts (tight base, volume dry-up, then expansion) — the setups I'd actually buy
- names in the 4th or 5th leg of a Stage-2 advance, already extended, where the "breakout volume" is often the volume of the move that's about to fail

Both pass all 8 criteria identically. The engine as spec'd cannot tell them apart in v1. That's my single biggest technical objection, and it's cheap to partially fix — see §4.

**[INFERENCE]** The 8 criteria are, collectively, a Stage-2 filter (Weinstein). Good — that's the correct stage for momentum longs. But the spec has no explicit stage classification layer distinguishing:
- **early Stage 2** (recent Stage-1→2 breakout, first leg, tightest bases, best risk/reward), from
- **late Stage 2** (multiple legs in, wide/sloppy consolidations, base count 3+, classic O'Neil "late-stage failure" risk), from
- **Stage 2→3 transition** (climax volume, first sharp break of the 50DMA on volume — early distribution).

12-1 momentum is backward-looking by construction; it will score a name identically whether it's on breakout #1 (buy it) or breakout #4 with climactic action three weeks old (avoid it — this is precisely where O'Neil's "last stage failure" pattern lives, and where 12-1 momentum scores are often at their *highest* right before the top, because trailing return is maximized right before reversal). The regime gate (§4 of the spec) protects the book at the *market* level; nothing in v1 protects it at the *individual-name stage* level. This is a real gap between "passes my template" and "is a timing-safe entry."

**Recommendation (no-cost, diagnostic-only, does not touch the ≤5 free-parameter budget):** in BT-11, tag every entry with a cheap stage proxy — e.g. base count since the last 52w-low-to-high cycle, or simply "sessions since the name first qualified for the trend template" (a fresh qualifier vs. a 40-week incumbent). Report win rate / R-multiple split by this tag in the GATE-11 pack. This costs nothing to compute from data already on hand and will tell CIO/Quant whether the edge concentrates in early-stage entries (as the Minervini/O'Neil literature would predict) or is being carried by momentum's blunter backward-looking score regardless of stage. If it's the latter, that's useful to know before sizing.

**[DATA]** §3: initial stop = "below the pivot / last contraction low," with the 15% trailing stop as the fallback once in profit. Position sizing (§3, RISK_LIMITS 1% rule) is `shares = (0.01 × equity) / stop_distance_per_share`.

**[OPINION]** This is the part of the spec I'd push back on hardest. Without a VCP detector, there is no "last contraction low" to measure from — v1 has no base structure to speak of, only a moving-average/52-week filter. So in practice the *initial* stop for a v1 trade is not a measured pivot-low, it's the 15% trailing stop applied from day one, i.e., an account-management heuristic wearing a technical-stop label. That has a direct, non-cosmetic consequence: the 1%-risk sizing formula is only as honest as its stop_distance input. If stop_distance is really "15% of entry price" for every name regardless of that name's actual volatility or base tightness, then:
- position size becomes a function of entry price only, not of the stock's real risk (a low-ATR consolidator and a high-ATR small-cap coming off a wide, sloppy base get sized by the *same* percentage rule even though their true 1R is very different)
- the R-multiple reported in trade-level output (if any) is not measuring "risk actually taken vs. actual stop discipline," it's measuring a fixed 15% band — so the whole R-multiple framing the spec's language borrows from my checklist doesn't mean what it normally means until there's a real pivot to measure from

This doesn't kill v1 — a fixed-percent stop is a legitimate, simple risk control and I'd rather see it than nothing. But I'd relabel it precisely in the engine's own docs (§3 currently blurs "initial stop" and "trailing stop" together) so nobody at GATE-11 or IC quotes an "R-multiple" that's actually just "15%-fixed-stop-multiple." Cosmetic now, misleading later if the numbers get repeated without the caveat.

**[OPINION]** Full VCP detection (contraction counting, T1>T2>T3 measurement) is genuinely a v2-scope build — I'm not asking DESK-100 to pull that into SIG-11. But a *volatility-contraction proxy* is one line of already-available data: 10-day ATR% vs. its own 50-day ATR% at the time of the breakout-volume flag. A name whose ATR has been contracting into the breakout-volume day is doing a real (if simplified) VCP; a name whose ATR is already elevated on the flag day is chasing an extended move. This is cheap (no new data dependency, computable from the same OHLCV panel DATA-11 already builds), doesn't touch the parameter budget (it's a diagnostic flag, not a gate — same treatment as the stage tag in §2), and would let GATE-11 report edge split by "contraction vs. no contraction at entry." If the CIO/Quant side wants to eventually gate on it, that's a P6-candidate discussion for v1.1, not something I'm asking to freeze into v1 now.

**[OPINION]** §3: decision on last session of the week, fill next-session open. In my discipline, a breakout is bought on the pivot day or within 1-2 sessions of it, with volume confirmation same-day — the whole point is buying *strength as it happens*, not strength that happened up to 4 trading days ago. A name that triggers the breakout-volume flag on, say, Tuesday isn't bought until the following Monday's open under this cadence — up to 4-5 sessions of lag. In small-caps, that's frequently the difference between buying the pivot and buying the extension day after the move already ran. This is a structural, not incidental, mismatch between weekly batch rebalancing and pivot-based entry timing.

I understand *why* weekly was chosen (§3: cost/turnover control for a small book, comparability to the prototype) and I'm not proposing daily rebalancing — that's a cost/turnover call for Devika/Quant, not mine to overrule. But I want it on the record: the backtest's entry-timing realism should reflect this lag honestly (fill at next-open after a *stale* signal, not next-open after a same-day signal), and if BT-11's fills are modeled as if the flag and the fill are adjacent, the backtest will overstate entry quality relative to what a real weekly-cadence trader would get. Worth a specific check in fill-audit /order-plan terms before this goes to paper.

**[DATA]** §4: GREEN/YELLOW/RED state machine on index direction (>50d & >200d MA) plus breadth (% of universe passing trend template ≥ threshold).

**[OPINION]** This is sound Weinstein-school design and it's the piece of this engine I have the least to add to — breadth-of-names-in-Stage-2 is exactly the right market-level complement to my name-level template, and gating new entries (not existing positions) on it is the correct mechanical translation of "don't fight the tape." My only comment: the RED trigger (direction-down OR breadth ≤ lo-threshold) manages entries but the spec's exit trigger for regime-driven risk-off is "scale down / no new buys, tighten to exits" — soft language. If RED coincides with a real market Stage-3→4 transition, I'd want the tightened-exit rule spelled out as concretely as the entry rules are (e.g., trail tightens to 50DMA break firm, not just "tighten"), so this doesn't become a discretionary call under stress. Flag for GATE-11, not a kill item.

**[DATA]** §3 exit triggers include: falls out of trend-template AND out of top-N for 2 consecutive rebalances.

**[OPINION]** At weekly cadence, "2 consecutive rebalances" is ~2 weeks / ~10 sessions before a name that's lost its leadership status is force-exited on the quality/time stop. Small-cap tops can round-trip a meaningful chunk of gains in that window — this is where I'd normally look for early distribution (heavy-volume down days clustering, a sharp break of the 50DMA on expanding volume) rather than waiting for the full template to fail twice. This is a genuine, if secondary, timing risk in the exit design; I'd note it as a candidate refinement rather than a blocker, since the hard stop and trailing 15% stop are still doing the primary loss-control work independent of this rule.

**Timing verdict: ACT to build and cheap-test as spec'd, WAIT-FOR before treating any resulting P&L as an honest read on "leadership momentum, Minervini-style."** The template implementation itself (§2.1) is correct and I have no changes to it. But the entry/exit machinery built around it is momentum-with-a-trend-filter, not momentum-with-pivot-discipline — the two diagnostic tags I've proposed (stage/incumbency, ATR-contraction proxy) cost nothing to add at BT-11 and would tell the desk, honestly, whether the eventual number is coming from what I'd recognize as real setups or from riding already-extended leaders that happen to still clear 8 static thresholds. Either answer is useful; not asking the question isn't.

**Invalidation of my own view:** if the stage/contraction diagnostic (once built) shows no meaningful edge difference between early- and late-stage entries, that would mean stage timing isn't adding value in this specific universe/regime-gated context — a real, well-documented possibility in small-caps where regime + relative-strength alone can dominate (the academic 12-1 literature doesn't condition on stage at all and still finds a premium). I'd take that result at face value rather than defend the framework past what the data shows.

**Where I agree with the spec's own risk framing:** kill criterion #2 (liquidity gate erasing the edge vs. B&H) is rightly flagged as the likeliest failure mode, and I'd add one interaction to watch for at COST-11/VAL-11: the names most likely to produce a genuine tight-base breakout (my "real setup" cases) are disproportionately the same thin-liquidity small-caps where the ADV participation cap bites hardest and slippage is worst *on the breakout day itself* (volume expansion days are exactly when the stock is least "our size" relative to normal turnover). If the liquidity gate and the volume-confirmation entry rule fight each other, that's worth a specific note in the GATE-11 pack rather than being absorbed silently into a single blended cost number.

**Interaction with existing sleeves and mandates.** Two things worth flagging for portfolio construction: (a) the dual-mandate framing (D-032: trading line vs investment line) — this reads as a trading-line strategy (weekly rebalance, swing horizon, hard stops) rather than investment-line, so it should compete for capital against other trading-line ideas, not against the long-horizon fundamental book; (b) if the equities/momentum book later also runs a factor-index replication sleeve (per the FIRM MAP's `factor-indices` skill), there's a real risk of double-counting momentum exposure across two "momentum" sleeves under different names — worth a cross-sleeve factor-loading check once both exist, not now.

**Allocation verdict for this stage:** PROCEED to build as scoped — this is a defensible diversifier bet with the right kill criteria already pre-registered. Do not let this get sized before (a) the correlation-to-short-vol-book number exists and (b) a target sleeve weight is proposed in the IC pack (§10 step 7), not left implicit.

**Trial-count honesty — directionally right but the ledger needs to stay literal, not rhetorical.** §9 states "≥4 prior configurations" (V1, V2, multistrat combo, always-on reference) must feed the DSR as an honest starting count, with every walk-forward grid cell added on top. Two things to watch when GATE-11 actually computes this:
1. 2. The DSR > 0.95 / PBO < 25% thresholds are stated as gates but not yet computed — this is the single most important number this cheap-test-adjacent memo cannot pre-judge. Given only ~4-6 honest trials plus a bounded grid, DSR should be achievable if the effect is real, but PBO on a 2005–2026 span with survivorship/liquidity corrections already baked in is a genuine open question — small-cap momentum has a well-documented literature (12-1 premium, Minervini/O'Neil trend-template descendants), which cuts both ways: it raises the prior probability the effect is real (this isn't a p-hacked one-off), but it also means the effect is more likely to be crowded / decaying in the exact regime-dependent way the spec itself flags (§D: "episodic convexity... not a sustained rate"). Recommend running `/crowding-check` alongside GATE-11, not after — small/mid-cap momentum in India has had real capital flow into it post-2020 (smallcap mutual fund AUM growth, momentum-factor ETF launches) that this backtest, ending 2026-01-21, may not fully reflect if the walk-forward's most recent validation years are the ones where crowding bit hardest.

**Walk-forward design is sound structurally, with one sequencing risk.** 3y train / 1y validate / 6m roll, final untouched OOS = most recent 12 months opened exactly once, is the right shape and matches RESEARCH_SOP. The risk is operational, not statistical: the final-OOS-touched-once rule is only as strong as the discipline enforcing it across a multi-step DESK-100 build (§10, 8 ordered steps) that may span multiple sessions/context resets. Recommend the run-engineering convention (§7, `results/track2_momentum/<run_id>/`) explicitly logs a boolean "final_oos_opened: true/false" in `config.json` from step 1 onward, so a resumed session can mechanically verify the seal hasn't been broken rather than trusting memory of what was already looked at. This is exactly the kind of thing the OOS-audit skill (`/oos-audit`) should check before GATE-11 closes, not something GATE-11 self-attests.

**The single biggest statistical risk is exactly what the spec names as the likeliest kill (#2): the liquidity gate erasing the edge.** This deserves elevation, not just agreement. The prototype's entire edge was generated on a close-only panel with zero volume gate — meaning every historical entry assumed a fill at a price the strategy could not actually have gotten in illiquid small-caps. A liquidity gate that cuts the tradable set down to genuinely 10%-ADV-fillable names in the smallest, most illiquid tier of a 500-name universe very plausibly removes most of the alpha, because momentum's academic edge concentration is often strongest exactly in the names retail/institutional capital can't yet access at scale — the same names a 10% ADV cap will exclude first. This isn't a reason to expect a kill, but it is a reason the pre-registered kill criterion (falls below Nifty-500 buy-and-hold post-gate) should be treated as a live 50/50, not a formality to clear on the way to a foregone PROCEED. If it survives the gate, that survival IS the finding — worth its own callout in the eventual memo rather than folded into a routine gate-pass line.

**Secondary statistical concern: the regime gate is doing double duty as both an economic mechanism and an overfit risk.** §4's regime state machine (breadth ≥ threshold, direction vs 50/200MA) is a coarse, defensible design, but kill criterion #4 (catastrophic-slice test) is testing whether the SAME regime gate that was hand-tuned (breadth threshold P5 ∈ {35,40,45}%) also happens to reduce drawdown in the exact historical crises being used to validate it. If P5's grid was chosen partly by eyeballing 2018/2020/2022 outcomes during spec design, kill criterion #4 is not an independent test of the gate — it's circular. Worth an explicit check at GATE-11: was P5's default (40%) chosen before or after looking at regime-slice drawdowns? If after, criterion #4 needs a stricter bar (e.g., gate performance on a slice NOT used to set the threshold) to mean what it claims to mean.

**Cost/promotion gate (2x stress) is well-specified and should not be softened.** 35bps one-way small-cap floor doubled to 70bps on exits, survive-2x-before-paper as a hard gate, is consistent with COST_STANDARDS. No concerns here beyond the standard fill-audit sanity check (`/fill-audit`) once BT-11/COST-11 produce actual fills, to confirm the modeled ADV-participation cap isn't silently assuming fills on circuit-locked or zero-volume days (Landmine #7b in the firm's data lessons applies directly here).

**Stats verdict:** the validity design is above the bar for a cheap-test stage — DSR/PBO thresholds, honest trials ledger, plateau rule, and 2x-cost promotion gate are all correctly specified. The two things to insist on before Gate-4 sign-off: (a) an explicit final-OOS-seal log, and (b) treating the liquidity-gate kill test as a genuine coin-flip finding worth its own writeup, not a checkbox.

**Is "leadership momentum" a real trend-template setup here, or a label on a generic momentum score?** It's a real trend-template implementation, not just a momentum score with technical branding — the 8-criterion Minervini stack (§2.1: price above both 150/200d MA, 150>200 MA, 200d MA rising, 50>150>200 stack, price above 50d MA, ≥30% off 52w low, within 25% of 52w high, RS percentile ≥70) is the canonical Stage-2 definition, applied as an ALL-must-pass filter rather than a soft score — that's the correct implementation choice; a partial-pass or weighted version would be a materially weaker (and less defensible) setup. Requiring all 8 simultaneously is a real constraint, not a token gesture — in a typical Indian small/mid-cap universe this alone will shrink the eligible set sharply, which is good discipline but means the effective universe on any given rebalance may be thin, especially outside the strongest regimes. Worth checking during SIG-11 how many names pass ALL 8 criteria in a median GREEN week vs a median YELLOW week — if it's routinely under 20-30 names, the top-N=20 leader board (P1) isn't really selecting a top slice, it's nearly the whole eligible set, and P1's grid {12,20,30} may be testing something that doesn't bind.

**Pivot/breakout logic is honestly labeled as v1-lite, and that gap matters.** The spec is explicit (§2.4) that the real Minervini criterion 9 — VCP (volatility contraction pattern: progressively tighter T1>T2>T3 contractions on declining volume, entry on pivot breakout with volume expansion) — is NOT built in v1; it's stood in for by a much cruder rule: breakout-day or prior-day volume ≥1.5x its 50-day average (§3). This is a meaningful simplification of what actually makes trend-template entries work in practice. VCP exists specifically to solve the false-breakout problem: a name can pass all 8 trend-template criteria and still be extended/exhausted rather than freshly launching from a proper base, and a simple 1.5x-volume trigger will happily buy volume spikes that are blow-off continuation moves, not fresh pivots. This is very likely a real source of entry-timing slippage relative to what a Minervini-school reviewer would consider a clean setup — not a fatal flaw for v1 (it's correctly scoped as v2 backlog), but it should be named explicitly as a known entry-quality gap in the eventual IC memo, not just a scope note. Expect the v1 backtest's per-trade edge to understate what a VCP-gated v2 could do, and conversely expect some v1 losing trades to be exactly the "bought the spike, not the base" failure mode — worth a manual chart review of a sample of losing trades once BT-11 produces a blotter, to confirm whether that failure mode is visible and how often.

**Weekly rebalance vs swing-horizon reality.** Weekly decision / next-session-open fill is a reasonable and defensible cadence for a multi-week swing horizon, and it's consistent with the legacy prototype for comparability. The technical risk here is smaller than the VCP gap: weekly rebalancing means a name can breach its stop mid-week and not be acted on until the next scheduled rebalance unless the exit logic is checked more frequently than the entry logic. The spec (§3) does list "stop hit" as a trigger separately from rebalance cadence, which correctly implies stops are monitored continuously (daily) even though new entries are weekly — that's the right asymmetry (fast exits, disciplined slower entries) and should be explicitly confirmed as implemented that way in BT-11, since it's easy to accidentally code both legs on the same weekly clock.

**Regime gate as a timing filter, not just a risk filter.** From a pure technical-timing lens, GREEN/YELLOW/RED gating on breadth + direction is the right instinct — trend-template setups fail far more often in weak-breadth tapes, which is precisely the "under-reaction reversed by regime" mechanism the spec's economic rationale claims. The RED-state behavior (no new buys, manage exits only) is correct trend-template doctrine (Weinstein Stage 4 discipline: don't fight the tape). The one timing nuance worth flagging: breadth and direction are both lagging, MA-based measures (50/200DMA, breadth vs template-pass rate) — by the time breadth crosses the GREEN threshold, a real leadership rotation may already be several weeks old, meaning entries early in a new bull leg could be systematically late relative to the true pivot. This is a known and accepted cost of trend-following (you never catch the exact bottom), not a design flaw, but it's worth being explicit in the memo that the regime gate buys drawdown protection at the cost of some entry lag — that tradeoff is exactly what kill criterion #4 is designed to validate (does the DD protection outweigh the lag cost).

**RS percentile computed correctly (PIT-universe-relative only), which avoids a common technical-scan error.** §2.3 explicitly restricts the cross-sectional percentile to the tradable PIT set on date D, not the full 2,535-symbol file — this is the right call and avoids leaking non-member relative-strength comparisons into the ranking, a mistake that's easy to make in a naive `/tech-scan`-style implementation.

**Technical verdict:** the trend-template implementation is faithful to the school it claims (full 8-criterion ALL-pass, correctly PIT-relative RS, correct stop/exit asymmetry vs entry cadence), and the regime gate is sound doctrine. The one real technical gap — the missing VCP/pivot-quality filter, stood in for by a blunt volume trigger — is honestly scoped as v2 but should be named as a specific, expected source of entry-timing noise in the v1 number, not left as a footnote. If v1 clears its kill criteria, the VCP overlay (already flagged as "the single most valuable v2 add," §2.4) should be prioritized immediately, since it targets exactly the failure mode most likely to explain any near-miss result.
