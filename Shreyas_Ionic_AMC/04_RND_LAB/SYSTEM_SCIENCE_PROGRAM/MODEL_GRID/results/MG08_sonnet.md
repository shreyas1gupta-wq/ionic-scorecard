# MG08 — Sonnet Answer

**Prompt:** A paper abstract claims a 2.1 Sharpe ML strategy on US equities, OOS 2010-2023, 940 features (price/fundamentals/news sentiment). List the 6 most likely reasons this won't survive scrutiny/replication, ranked by probability, with mechanism + single confirming check.

---

## 1. Multiple-comparisons / selection overfitting across the 940-feature, model, and hyperparameter search space
**Mechanism:** The reported Sharpe is effectively the maximum (or near-max) order statistic over however many feature subsets, model architectures, hyperparameter settings, and label/embargo choices were tried during development. Under pure noise, the expected max Sharpe over N trials grows roughly as sqrt(2·ln N), so a true edge of ~0.3-0.5 Sharpe can present as 2.1 after enough trials — especially if the "2010-2023 OOS" window was itself revisited multiple times across paper revisions (non-nested development).
**Check:** Compute the Deflated Sharpe Ratio / Probability of Backtest Overfitting (Bailey & López de Prado), using the disclosed (or reconstructed, from the described search space) trial count N. If DSR collapses toward zero, the excess Sharpe is a trial-count artifact, not signal.

## 2. Look-ahead / point-in-time violations in fundamentals and news-sentiment features
**Mechanism:** Fundamental fields are commonly pulled keyed to fiscal-period-end rather than public filing/announcement date, and restated (post-10-K/10-Q amendment) values get substituted for what was actually known at the time. News-sentiment scores are often generated by an NLP model calibrated on the full historical corpus, or timestamped by article-publish-date rather than true machine-readable availability — both leak future information into features labeled as "past."
**Check:** Spot-check a sample of feature values against SEC EDGAR actual filing dates (or vendor `available_date` flags) vs. period-end dates used, then re-run with a conservative fixed lag (e.g., +90 days fundamentals, true wire timestamp for news). If Sharpe collapses, this is the driver.

## 3. Unrealistic transaction-cost / execution assumptions given implied turnover
**Mechanism:** A 940-feature daily/weekly ML signal typically re-ranks names frequently, producing high turnover. If the backtest fills at close-to-close mid-price with flat/zero cost, a modest gross edge (often a few bps/day) survives un-decremented, while realistic one-way cost (spread + market impact, particularly once the "US equities" universe includes small/micro caps) of 10-20+ bps would consume most or all of it.
**Check:** Re-run the costed backtest with a realistic bid-ask + square-root market-impact model scaled to the strategy's measured turnover and ADV participation; compare net vs. gross Sharpe.

## 4. Survivorship bias in the equity universe construction
**Mechanism:** If the universe is built from current index membership (or a "clean" current ticker list) rather than a full point-in-time list including delisted, bankrupt, and merged names, the backtest silently removes the worst left-tail return days (delisting losses), simultaneously inflating mean return and suppressing realized volatility — both push Sharpe up.
**Check:** Confirm the universe source explicitly carries delisted tickers with delisting returns (e.g., CRSP delisting-return field) across the full 2010-2023 span; re-run including the full delisted-name basket and measure the Sharpe delta.

## 5. Cross-sectional / temporal leakage via improper feature scaling or non-purged cross-validation
**Mechanism:** Pipelines with hundreds of engineered features commonly compute cross-sectional ranks, z-scores, or PCA components over the full panel (all stocks, full history) before the train/test split, letting future distributional information (e.g., 2023 factor mean/std) bleed into features used to predict 2010 returns. Separately, multi-day forward-return labels without purge/embargo around fold boundaries let overlapping-in-time samples sit in both train and test.
**Check:** Audit whether every scaler/PCA/feature-selector was fit strictly inside each walk-forward training window with a purge+embargo gap ≥ the label horizon; if not, refit walk-forward-only and compare Sharpe.

## 6. Regime concentration — the headline Sharpe is driven by a narrow favorable sub-window, not a stationary edge
**Mechanism:** An aggregate 2010-2023 Sharpe can be dominated by a handful of outsized months (e.g., 2020 COVID dislocation/recovery, 2021 reversal episodes) where a sentiment/ML signal happened to catch a large dislocation, while the remaining sample is flat-to-negative — i.e., a regime-specific lucky draw rather than a repeatable edge, which is exactly what fails to replicate once that regime passes.
**Check:** Era-split / rolling 12-month Sharpe decomposition across 2010-2023, and recompute headline Sharpe with the single best month or year excluded — if it drops sharply (e.g., >50%), the number is regime-concentrated.
