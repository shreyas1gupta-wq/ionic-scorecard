# SHRIRAMFIN — Shriram Finance Ltd.

> [!summary]
> Shriram Finance had a strong FY26: profit crossed ₹10,000 crore for the first time, the loan book crossed ₹3 lakh crore, and Japan's MUFG Bank completed a large ₹39,618 crore investment for a 20% stake that pushed the company's capital position and credit ratings to the top of the industry (AAA across all major agencies), which should lower its borrowing costs over the next two years. At the same time, the share of loans showing early repayment stress has crept up for two straight quarters across used-vehicle, MSME and personal-loan segments, even though the company has also built up a bigger cushion against losses. The stock has already re-rated sharply over the past year on the MUFG and rating-upgrade news, so at current levels we see it as fairly valued rather than a bargain. We rate it a Hold: a genuinely stronger balance sheet, but not enough margin of safety at this price to add, and the recent rise in early-stress loans is worth watching, not ignoring.

## Recommendation — Hold

Hold, not Sell: the funding/capital/rating story is real and structural (AAA across three agencies, CRAR in the low-30s%, MUFG capital cushion removing dilution risk, diversified ECB access), and profitability (ROA 3.6%, ROE ~19%) is genuinely strong for the NBFC peer set — there is no funding freeze, promoter fraud, or regulatory suspension that would justify an outright Sell. But Sell is also not warranted purely on valuation discipline in the other direction — at ~2.3x P/B / ~24x P/E the stock is fully priced for the good news already delivered (~55% one-year re-rating), not egregiously stretched given the ROE profile. The three-consecutive-quarter rise in Stage 2/3 stress and the fading of one-off funding-cost/hedge-MTM tailwinds argue against paying up further or treating this as an automatic add at current levels. Hold captures 'own it if you have it, don't chase it here.'

## Bull case

MUFG's ₹39,618cr capital infusion (20.11% stake, completed April 2026) has structurally transformed the balance sheet — CRAR moved from ~20.7% to an estimated low-30s%, among the highest of any large Indian NBFC, funding a multi-year growth runway without dilutive equity raises. This directly earned AAA long-term ratings from CRISIL, ICRA and India Ratings within days of deal completion, with management guiding to a 50-100bps reduction in cost of funds over two years — a real, durable margin tailwind. Reported metrics back this up: FY26 PAT crossed ₹9,998cr, Q4 ROA was 3.63% and ROE 19.13%, AUM crossed ₹3.02 lakh crore (+14.9% YoY) with genuine diversification progress (CV core down to ~46% of AUM; FY27 guidance of PV>20%, Gold>30%), and net NPA actually improved to 2.33% on rising provision coverage (50.34%, up ~700bps YoY) — a lender adding to its buffer even as gross slippage ticks up. CEO succession (Parag Sharma, reappointed for 5 years with 98.75% AGM votes) has been smooth, removing a leadership overhang some peers still carry.

## Bear case

Gross Stage 3 has now risen for three consecutive quarters (4.55% Q4FY25 → 4.54% Q3FY26 → 4.58% Q4FY26) and Stage 2 alongside it (6.78% → 6.91%), taking the combined early-stress-plus-NPA pool to 11.49% of AUM — and management's own commentary shows the stress is not confined to one pocket: MSME, personal loans and construction equipment in Q3, then CV, used-asset and consumer finance lending in Q4. That is precisely the rural/first-time-buyer/used-CV population this coverage note is built to worry about, and it is broadening rather than resolving. Second, the MUFG deal is not governance-clean: a $200mn non-compete payment to the promoter Trust drew formal opposition from proxy advisors IiAS and SES on related-party-transaction grounds, and independent legal commentary argues the 20%-stake-plus-preferential-allotment structure was built to stay just under SEBI's mandatory open-offer trigger while still securing MUFG board/management influence — a resolved vote (91.94% approval) does not erase the structuring question, and it is a template other NBFC/promoter deals may now try to replicate, inviting regulatory attention. Third, and most basic: much of the 'cheaper funding, higher rating' story is already in the price — the stock is up ~55% over one year and trades at ~2.3x book against a ~19% ROE that must now be sustained through a period of rising, not falling, credit-cost pressure; part of the Q4 FY26 profit beat also reflects a large swing in derivative/hedge mark-to-market assets (₹526cr to ₹4,684cr), which is not a repeatable core-earnings item and modestly overstates the run-rate. None of this is Sell-grade on its own, but together it argues against paying up further.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At ~2.3x P/B and ~24x P/E against a ~19% reported ROE (Q4 FY26 annualized) and 3.6% ROA, the market is pricing Shriram Finance for a sustained high-teens ROE through the CV cycle, underwritten by the AAA-rating/lower-cost-of-funds tailwind and the MUFG capital cushion — a reasonable ask given genuine execution, but not a margin-of-safety price. For the multiple to expand further (rather than just hold), Gross Stage 3 and the combined Stage 2+3 pool (11.49% and rising for three straight quarters) need to turn down, not merely stabilize, and credit cost needs to hold near/below 1.7% through the MSME/PL/used-CV stress management itself has flagged. If that stress instead broadens — plausible given rural cash-flow sensitivity and a CV-replacement upcycle that industry data show as modest (MD/HD truck volumes roughly flat YoY in 2026) rather than strong — the current multiple has no cushion and would compress back toward book-value-linked NBFC peer levels (~1.5-2x P/B). Net: fair-to-mildly-rich, not cheap, not indefensibly expensive — a name to hold through the cycle rather than add to at this valuation.
