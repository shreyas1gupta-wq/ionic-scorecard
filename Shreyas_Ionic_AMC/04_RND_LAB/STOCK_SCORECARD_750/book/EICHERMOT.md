# EICHERMOT — Eicher Motors Ltd.

> [!summary]
> Eicher Motors had a record FY26 - Royal Enfield sold 1.22 million motorcycles, VECV's truck volumes rose 15%, and consolidated profit grew 17% - and June 2026's monthly numbers beat Street estimates on both the motorcycle and truck side. But the September-2025 GST change that made sub-350cc bikes cheaper and 450cc-plus bikes more expensive is quietly working against the higher-margin upgrade cycle: Himalayan 450 and Super Meteor 650 volumes actually fell in FY26, even as overall company volumes rose on cheaper entry-level bikes. Competition from Honda, the Bajaj-Triumph-KTM-Husqvarna stable, Harley-Davidson and Jawa-Yezdi-BSA keeps widening, even if Royal Enfield's overall lead is still large. At close to 37 times trailing earnings - a premium to other listed two-wheeler makers - the stock already prices in continued strong growth, and the new electric Flying Flea motorcycle is a long-term option, not yet a profit contributor. We rate this Hold: a well-run, dominant franchise, priced for a smoother growth path than its most profitable segment is currently delivering.

## Recommendation — Hold

Hold, not Sell: there is no thesis-break in the franchise - RE and VECV both beat estimates in the latest monthly print, FY26 volumes and margins were records, the balance sheet is net-cash, and ROE remains well above cost of capital, so an outright Sell call would be fighting genuinely good execution. But Sell-vs-Hold is close on valuation grounds, and we don't clear the bar for Sell because the business itself hasn't broken - only its most profitable growth lever (450-650cc premiumization) has hit a structural tax headwind that shows up in the model-wise data. Hold reflects: priced about right to somewhat rich at ~37x trailing earnings against a realistic mid-teens forward growth path, no urgent reason to exit a dominant, well-run franchise, but no margin of safety to add either.

## Bull case

Royal Enfield remains the dominant player in India's mid-size motorcycle market and has absorbed three years of new entrants (Triumph, Harley-Davidson X440, Honda CB350, Jawa-Yezdi-BSA) without losing its position; FY26 volumes hit a record 1.22 million units and June 2026 sales beat estimates on both the motorcycle and truck side. Spares and aftermarket revenue grew 18% YoY - a high-margin, recurring annuity on a large and growing installed base. VECV gives Eicher a genuine second earnings engine riding a commercial-vehicle upcycle, with volumes up 15% and EBITDA margin expanding from 8.8% to 9.7% in a single year. The balance sheet is net-cash and return ratios are well above cost of capital, funding the Flying Flea electric-motorcycle bet without diluting the core business.

## Bear case

The engine behind the last three years' re-rating - premiumization into 450cc and 650cc twins - has just run into a direct tax headwind: GST on motorcycles above 350cc was raised to 40% (from 28%) even as sub-350cc bikes were cut to 18%, and FY26 data already shows the strain, with Himalayan 450 down 11%, Super Meteor 650 down 11%, and Shotgun 650 down 10% YoY, while overall growth is being carried by the now-cheaper, lower-margin entry-level range. A widening field of well-funded competitors - the Bajaj-Triumph-KTM-Husqvarna combine, Honda, Harley-Davidson, and Jawa-Yezdi-BSA - is growing 30-125% YoY off small bases, aimed squarely at the price ladder GST just made relatively more expensive for Royal Enfield. At roughly 37x trailing earnings against consensus growth of about 15% (an implied PEG near 2.5x), the stock is priced for a cleaner growth trajectory than the current mix data supports, and sell-side consensus targets imply only modest further upside from the current price - the market itself is not underwriting much more re-rating from here.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At ~37.4x trailing consolidated EPS (price Rs 7,388 vs EPS of roughly Rs 197) against a ~31.9x peer average and a ~14.4x broad Asian-auto average, the market pays Eicher a clear premium multiple versus other listed two-wheeler makers. Reverse-engineering that multiple at a reasonable ~11-12% cost of equity implies the market needs Eicher to sustain mid-to-high-teens earnings compounding for several years - above the ~14.6-15.4% Street consensus already built in for the very next quarter - to look fairly valued on a normalized PEG basis (currently ~2.5x at consensus growth, versus ~1-1.5x that would look fair for a moat compounder at this growth rate). Because GST 2.0 is a structural (not one-quarter) headwind to precisely the 450-650cc segment that drove the re-rating, and competitive intensity is rising even if absolute share loss remains modest, we judge the current price as FAIR-TO-RICH: FY26 execution was genuinely excellent and VECV is a real second engine, but the multiple leaves little room for the premiumization mix to disappoint over the next several quarters, which is the specific risk sitting in front of the stock right now.
