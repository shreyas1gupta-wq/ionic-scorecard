## Recommendation — Sell

This is a genuine, non-trivial judgment call and I am flagging it as such. The bull case is real: Q4 FY26 shows a tangible margin inflection (OPM 17.7-18%, best in seven quarters), the DASDA anti-dumping duty is an already-landed tailwind (not a hope), the polycarbonate/downstream integration is funded and credible given management's execution history, and current balance-sheet leverage (D/E 0.28x) is not distressed. That is enough to make a mechanical 'negative 3y revenue CAGR = Sell' read too simplistic on its own -- the quant model's 3-year leg is largely capturing a commodity-cycle trough, and troughs do mean-revert. ## Detailed rationale

Deepak Nitrite is a commodity-chemicals downcycle story, and the numbers say the cycle is real: FY22 (the post-COVID phenol/acetone supercycle peak) delivered sales of Rs 6,802 crore at ~24% OPM (Q1 FY22 alone hit ~30%) and net profit of Rs 1,067 crore. By FY26, sales were Rs 7,887 crore (-4.8% YoY) but OPM had compressed to 13% for the full year and net profit fell to Rs 551 crore (-21% YoY) -- ROE and ROCE have collapsed to 9.6-9.8% and 10.5-11.4% respectively, against five-year averages of ~21.3% and ~29.3% (marketsmojo.com Q4 FY26 result analysis). A five-year EBIT CAGR of -5.15% against a +15% revenue CAGR over the same period confirms this is margin/quality erosion, not just a soft quarter. There is a genuine, board-confirmed inflection in the most recent print: Q4 FY26 (Mar-2026) OPM jumped to 17.7-18%, up ~703bps QoQ and ~319bps YoY, the best in seven quarters, on sales of Rs 2,120 crore and net profit of Rs 220 crore (+8.6% YoY), and management guided further sequential improvement into Q1 FY27 on secured feedstock, new product launches and cost optimization (scanx.trade; sahi.com Q4 FY26 coverage). But OPM has swung nearly 900bps (8.85% to 17.73%) over just seven quarters -- one strong print does not yet establish a durable floor, and independent commentary (marketsmojo) explicitly wants 2-3 consecutive quarters above 15% before calling this a genuine recovery rather than a cyclical bounce. Q1 FY27 results are not yet out (AGM record date is July 27, 2026; result date unannounced as of this review), so there is no confirming data point yet -- and pre-result analyst previews (Uniresearch, via univest.in) actually model Q1 FY27 revenue near Rs 1,648 crore, below the Rs 1,890 crore booked in Q1 FY26, even as management's own commentary points to sequential (not YoY) improvement -- a reminder that near-term numbers remain noisy.

On the policy/competitive-intensity side that drives this business (68% of FY26 revenue sits in the commodity Phenolics segment -- Rs 5,400 crore of Rs 7,887 crore, per screener.in segment data -- with the balance in Advanced Intermediates), the picture is two-sided. DGTR has already imposed anti-dumping duty on Chinese DASDA imports following a Deepak Nitrite petition (scanx.trade), a direct, already-landed tailwind to the Advanced Intermediates/fine-chemicals margin line, and a separate DGTR probe into Chinese IPA imports (filed by Deepak Fertilisers, a different company, but Deepak Phenolics is a domestic IPA producer that would co-benefit) is live. A phenol-specific DGTR anti-dumping case (AD/OI/012/2026) is also open, but notably targets Saudi Arabia, Singapore, South Africa, South Korea, Taiwan, Thailand and the US -- not China -- even as China continues adding large-scale phenol capacity (an estimated 680,000 mt/year, per S&P Global chemical-trends coverage) that keeps global spreads under structural pressure and periodically gets offered into Southeast Asia. The more consequential, less-discussed risk this review surfaced independently: Haldia Petrochemicals (HPL, IOC/Chatterjee Group-backed) has built a comparable-scale domestic phenol/acetone plant (expanded to ~345 KTPA phenol plus 185 KTPA acetone, a ~Rs 3,000-3,600 crore investment, per ICIS and Indian Chemical News coverage) explicitly to become "India's first integrated player in the phenolics chain" alongside Deepak, targeted for completion around Q1 2026. This is a new, well-capitalized domestic competitor entering Deepak's largest, most India-scaled business right as the market is asked to underwrite a multi-year margin-recovery re-rating -- a structural, not merely cyclical, threat to the ceiling on Deepak Phenolics' pricing power, and one that consensus sell-side targets (both still "Neutral") do not appear to be explicitly pricing.

The capex/leverage picture compounds the timing risk. Deepak Chem Tech (the wholly-owned subsidiary) has approved ~Rs 3,500 crore for a 300 KTA phenol / 185 KTA acetone / 100 KTA IPA capacity expansion, plus a ~Rs 5,000 crore, 165,000 MT polycarbonate project at Dahej built around an asset/technology acquisition from Trinseo's Stade, Germany facility (indianchemicalnews.com; business-standard.com) -- a genuine, funded, credibly-run growth bridge (management has a strong prior execution record with Deepak Phenolics and the DASDA plant), but one whose commercial contribution does not begin before FY28 commissioning, with FY28-29 the earliest real earnings contribution. Total capex across FY25-30 is guided in the Rs 11,000-14,000 crore range, funded partly by debt; consolidated borrowings were already Rs 1,638 crore at Mar-2026 (up from a much lower base) and peak debt is expected to reach ~Rs 7,900 crore by FY30, with negative free cash flow expected through the build phase (hdfcsec.com company update; icicisecurities IC note). Current leverage (D/E 0.28x, interest coverage 21.3x per the quant snapshot) is not a today-problem, but the trajectory is a multi-year headwind layered on top of, not instead of, the Haldia competitive risk. At a trailing P/E of ~38-43x (sources vary by date/EPS basis: screener.in shows 42.2x at Rs 1,708; a separate July-2026 dated commentary cites 38.30x with EV/EBITDA ~23x) on earnings that are still well below the company's own normalized/historical run-rate, and against a stock that an independent rating desk (MarketsMojo) itself downgraded from Hold to Sell on July 2, 2026 citing stretched valuation, this reads as a name priced for a smooth multi-year margin-normalization-plus-capex-payoff scenario with a new large domestic competitor about to test exactly that assumption.
