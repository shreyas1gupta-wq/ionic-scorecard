# PNB — Punjab National Bank

> [!summary]
> PNB's June-2026 quarter profit jump of over 200% made headlines, but it mostly reflects a weak year-ago quarter (last year's number was cut by a one-time tax charge) rather than a genuine business acceleration - underlying interest income grew only about 2%. The good news is real: bad-loan levels are at a multi-year low (gross bad loans about 2.8% of advances, net bad loans under 0.3%), the bank has set aside provisions covering over 97% of stressed loans, and capital levels are comfortable with no raise needed. The caution is that margins remain below the bank's own target, loan growth is outpacing deposit growth, a chunk of the improved credit costs is aided by recoveries from old written-off loans rather than purely cleaner new lending, and an accounting-rule change due next year will add a real, one-time cost. At the current price versus book value, the stock looks fairly priced for what it can sustainably earn - neither compellingly cheap nor expensive. We rate it Hold.

## Recommendation — Hold

Hold rather than Sell because the credit-quality core of the bank - GNPA, NNPA, PCR, restructured book, capital ratios - is the cleanest it has been in years, with no going-concern or capital-raise stress visible. Hold rather than a more constructive call (we don't issue Buy) because NIM has undershot the bank's own guidance for two consecutive quarters, FY26's organic profit growth once the Q1 tax-charge base distortion is stripped out was only in the low-teens on a quarterly run-rate basis, deposit growth is lagging loan growth, and a real, already-quantified ECL provisioning cost lands in FY28 that the current 0.8-0.9x P/B does not yet have to absorb. Net effect: a fairly valued, structurally-improved-but-not-fully-normalized PSU bank, with no clear mispricing edge to justify a directional call away from Hold.

## Bull case

Asset quality is genuinely at a structural high point - GNPA at 2.78% and NNPA at 0.28% are the cleanest PNB's book has been in years, provisioning coverage of 97%+ leaves little room for negative surprises, and the standard restructured book is a slim 1.29% of standard advances. Capital is ample (CRAR 18.13%, CET1 14.52%) with no near-term raise needed. Loan growth is running a healthy 12-13% YoY (core advances ex-IBPC even higher at 15.4%), management has retained its full-year NIM/NII guidance and expects sequential margin improvement now that deposit repricing is complete, non-interest income grew a strong 15.2% in FY26, and core operating profit (stripping out recoveries and treasury gains) grew 35.7% YoY in Q1FY27 - a sign the underlying franchise, not just one-off items, is improving. Governance is stable, with the CEO roughly two years into a three-year term and no reappointment cliff in sight.

## Bear case

The headline 214% Q1FY27 profit jump is optically dramatic but not real operating strength - it is a base effect against a Q1FY26 quarter depressed by a one-time Rs 3,324 crore tax-regime-shift charge; strip that out and NII grew just 2.1% YoY while global NIM (2.50%) sat below the bank's own 2.60-2.70% FY27 guidance for a second straight quarter. A meaningful share of the current low credit cost (0.25%) and clean asset-quality print is recovery-aided - Rs 2,789 crore recovered in Q1 alone, 1.34x fresh slippages, against a full-year recovery target of Rs 13,000 crore - rather than purely a function of a structurally lower-risk loan book; by its nature this recovery tailwind is a depleting asset as the legacy stressed pool shrinks. Deposit growth (8.5% YoY) is lagging advances growth (12.7-12.85%), pushing the credit-deposit ratio to 73.8% and constraining how much further the bank can grow loans without either paying up for deposits (pressuring NIM further) or slowing growth. Management has already flagged a real, quantified future cost: a one-time ECL provisioning hit of Rs 9,500-10,000 crore from April 2027, plus a recurring 10-12bps/quarter drag - a headwind the current cheap-looking P/B does not yet have to absorb.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At roughly 0.8-0.9x trailing book value, PNB is priced for a sustainable ROE of roughly 12-14% (Gordon-growth cross-check: (ROE-g)/(Ke-g) is approximately (13-8)/(14-8) = 0.83x, close to the observed multiple). That is plausible for a bank generating ROA around 1.0% (FY26: 1.06%) on roughly 13x leverage - but the FY26/Q1FY27 print embeds a still-elevated recovery/write-back contribution and an unsustainably low 0.25% credit cost that should normalize higher as the legacy stressed-asset recovery pool depletes and the FY28 ECL transition (Rs 9,500-10,000 crore one-time charge, plus 10-12bps/quarter recurring) lands. Strip those tailwinds out and normalized ROA is probably closer to 0.85-0.95%, sustainable ROE closer to 11-13% - still roughly in line with what the current multiple already implies. Conclusion: fairly valued, neither a value trap nor a bargain.

## Detailed rationale

PNB's June-2026 quarter (Q1 FY27, reported 18-Jul-2026) showed net profit of Rs 5,253 crore, up 214% YoY - a headline that overstates the underlying business. The comparison base, Q1 FY26 (Rs 1,675 crore), was depressed by a one-time Rs 3,324 crore charge from the bank's shift to a lower corporate tax regime; strip that distortion out and the operating picture is far more modest - net interest income grew just 2.1% YoY to Rs 10,798 crore, and global NIM of 2.50% sat below the bank's own FY27 guidance range of 2.60-2.70% for a second straight quarter (domestic NIM did improve sequentially to 2.64% from 2.47% in Q4 FY26 as deposit repricing completed by May-2026). FY26 full-year net profit told a similar story: Rs 16,904 crore, up only 1.6% YoY, even though Q2, Q3 and Q4 FY26 each grew a healthy 13-14% YoY individually on a standalone basis - the muted full-year growth number is arithmetic, not deterioration, explained by the tax-charge-depressed Q1 FY26 base.

Asset quality is genuinely at a structural high point, not just optics. GNPA has fallen steadily on a PIT basis - 3.78% (Jun-25) to 2.95% (Mar-26) to 2.78% (Jun-26) - NNPA is down to 0.28-0.29%, provision coverage ratio is a conservative 97.2%+, the slippage ratio is a low 0.68% (inside the bank's <0.9% guidance), and the standard restructured book is a slim 1.29% of standard advances. That said, recoveries remain a real and depleting driver: Q1 FY27 alone saw Rs 2,789 crore of recoveries (1.34x fresh slippages), against an FY27 total recovery target of Rs 13,000 crore, of which Rs 4,000 crore is specifically targeted from written-off/PWA accounts. Credit cost of just 0.25% (vs a <0.4% target) is partly a function of this recovery tailwind rather than purely organic low delinquency, and management itself has flagged a coming, quantified headwind: a one-time ECL provisioning impact of Rs 9,500-10,000 crore from April 2027 (FY28), plus a recurring 10-12bps/quarter drag thereafter, as the bank transitions to expected-credit-loss provisioning ahead of the mandatory switch.

Capital and funding are comfortable but not spotless. CRAR stands at 18.13% (CET1 14.52%, Tier-1 16.03%), all well above regulatory floors, with no near-term capital raise flagged. But deposit growth (8.5% YoY) is lagging advances growth (12.7-12.85% YoY), pushing the credit-deposit ratio to 73.8% and CASA ratio holds at a middling 36.7% for a PSU bank of this scale - a funding-pace constraint that management is managing by disciplined deposit pricing (34bps cost-of-deposit reduction YoY) rather than chasing bulk deposits. At roughly Rs 105/share (17-Jul-2026) against book value in the Rs 115-130/share range (sources diverge on standalone vs consolidated denominator), PNB trades at roughly 0.8-0.9x P/B - a multiple that, on a Gordon-growth cross-check against an estimated sustainable through-cycle ROE in the low-to-mid teens, looks close to fair value rather than mispriced in either direction. Governance is stable for now: MD & CEO Ashok Chandra is roughly 18 months into a three-year term (since 16-Jan-2025) with no reappointment cliff visible, and the non-executive chairman rotation (K.G. Ananthakrishnan's term concluding) is a normal governance event, not a red flag.
