# SOLARINDS — Solar Industries India Ltd.

> [!summary]
> Solar Industries India has genuinely transformed from an industrial-explosives maker into a fast-growing defence-ammunition and rocket-systems supplier -- FY26 revenue grew 30% and profit grew 35%, with defence revenue nearly doubling and now a quarter of the business. The order book of Rs 21,300 crore is real and growing, but only covers about 18 months of the company's own FY27 revenue target, meaning future growth depends on winning fresh large contracts that are not yet signed. The stock trades at roughly 90-100 times trailing earnings, more than double what fully-valued defence public-sector peers command, and the business has also seen two fatal explosions at its explosives plants in the last three years. We rate this a Sell on valuation and risk grounds, not because the underlying business is weak -- it is a strong business priced for a level of flawless, uninterrupted execution that even a very good company will struggle to sustain indefinitely.

## Recommendation — Sell

This is a valuation call, not a business-quality call -- the execution here is genuinely excellent (record FY26, defence revenue nearly doubling, a real and structurally-supported ammunition/rocket ramp, strong capital efficiency funded mostly by internal accruals). But at ~90-100x trailing earnings -- more than double even the richest defence-PSU comparables the desk tracks at 30-40x -- the price is not just crediting the signed Rs 6,084cr Pinaka contract and the Rs 21,300cr order book (only ~1.5x FY27-guided revenue, i.e. ~18 months of cover); it is crediting an unbroken chain of future, currently-unsigned mega order wins stretching many years out, with zero discount for the single-customer (MoD) concentration in the core franchise or for a business that has had two fatal plant explosions in three years. A name can be a excellent compounder and still be overpriced; the asymmetry here favours de-rating risk (an order-flow air pocket, a weak quarter, or another safety-incident headline) over further re-rating upside. Hold would be the call if the multiple were merely 'rich but earned' against a multi-year signed book; it is not -- the book depth doesn't support the multiple, so Sell.

## Bull case

Solar Industries is India's largest domestic bulk/cartridge explosives manufacturer and has executed a genuinely credible pivot into high-value defence systems: the Pinaka Enhanced-Range rocket contract (Rs 6,084cr, signed) is already in commercial dispatch, 155mm artillery-shell production is coming online into a real global ammunition shortage, and export revenue has compounded from Rs 400cr (FY15) to ~Rs 3,800cr (FY26) with footprint now across Africa, Turkey, Central Asia, Southeast Asia and Australia and guided to grow ~30% further in FY27. FY26 numbers were a genuine beat, not a promise: revenue +30%, PAT +35%, defence revenue +94%, ROE 31.7% and ROCE 36.8% funded mostly through internal accruals with only moderate debt (~Rs 1,524cr). Promoter holding is high and stable (73.15%), and this sits inside India's structural Atmanirbhar Bharat defence-manufacturing and ammunition-export tailwind -- a multi-year theme, not a one-quarter pop.

## Bear case

The strongest bear point is that the price has run well ahead of what the signed backlog can justify. At ~90-100x trailing earnings the stock trades at more than double even the richest defence-PSU comparables (HAL/BEL/BDL-type names at 30-40x), yet the Rs 21,300cr order book covers only ~18 months of the company's own FY27 revenue guidance -- growth beyond that depends on continuing to win large, currently-unsigned contracts ('advanced-stage negotiations' per management, not booked revenue), concentrated overwhelmingly in one customer (Indian MoD/Army) for the core rocket/ammunition franchise. Segment margins embedded in the backlog are not disclosed. Layered on top of that is a real, recurring safety/governance risk that a growth multiple typically ignores: two fatal explosions at Solar Industries' Nagpur-area plants in three years (2023, and again 4-Sep-2025 with 1 dead/8 injured), with opposition allegations of safety-drill lapses and a chronically understaffed explosives regulator (PESO, ~98 officers versus a stated need of ~700). The stock fell ~11% on the September 2025 incident alone despite strong underlying quarterly numbers -- proof the market does treat this as a live tail risk, just not one priced into the multiple on a good week.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

A ~90-100x trailing P/E (against ROE ~32%, ROCE ~37%) is pricing in a very long window of exceptional compounding -- materially longer and faster than the ~18 months of revenue the current signed order book (Rs 21,300cr vs FY27 guidance of Rs 14,000cr) actually covers. Even crediting management's own FY27 guidance in full (revenue +42%, defence >Rs 4,500cr), a reverse-DCF using a reasonable equity discount rate (~12-13%) and a terminal multiple in line with best-in-class Indian industrials/defence names (35-55x, itself a premium to the 30-40x the desk uses for HAL/BEL/BDL) requires the market to be underwriting on the order of a decade-plus of 25-30%+ per-share earnings growth with no interruption from order-flow lumpiness, MoD-customer concentration, or plant-safety incidents. That is a demanding, low-probability path, not a base case. My own genuine forward view (~20-22% per annum over 3-5 years) is a strong number in absolute terms but does not, by itself, clear a multiple this rich under any reasonably conservative discounting -- the stock is priced for more than the business, however good, can be relied upon to deliver. Verdict: rich/expensive, not fair.
