# DRREDDY — Dr. Reddy's Laboratories Ltd.

> [!summary]
> Dr Reddy's is living through the earnings cliff its Revlimid (cancer-drug) exclusivity loss was always going to cause -- full-year profit fell about a quarter versus last year, with almost the entire hit landing in a single quarter (Jan-Mar 2026) when the licence protection lapsed. The two businesses meant to replace that profit -- a generic weight-loss injection (semaglutide) and a new biosimilar pipeline -- have each hit a manufacturing or regulatory snag in the past five weeks: a US FDA inspection flagged seven observations (up from five nine months earlier) at the plant making the biosimilar, and a manufacturing quality issue has paused semaglutide supply until around November. The stock has already fallen on this news, but not enough, in our view, to compensate for how little visibility there now is into the next couple of quarters. We rate it Sell, while flagging that the underlying business outside the cliff (India, Europe, the new nicotine-replacement-therapy franchise) is genuinely growing and this is not a call that the company is broken.

## Recommendation — Sell

Hold would be the defensible call if the two post-Revlimid growth engines were tracking cleanly. Instead both semaglutide and the abatacept biosimilar have hit quality/compliance stumbles within the same five-week window (Bachupally 483 escalating 5->7 observations; semaglutide API halt cutting FY27 revenue assumptions roughly in half), while the earnings base has structurally reset lower (FY26 PAT -24% YoY, Q4 -86% YoY) and Q1 FY27 -- the first full clean quarter -- reports in 3 days with essentially no visibility. That combination of a resetting earnings base, unresolved execution risk in the exact replacement assets, and a multiple that is not statistically cheap (18-32x depending on the earnings base used) tips this to Sell over Hold. This is not a high-conviction structural Sell -- the base business (India, Europe, NRT) is genuinely growing -- and the call should be revisited hard on the Q1 FY27 print (22-Jul-2026) and on DRL's CAPA response to the Bachupally 483.

## Bull case

The base business outside Revlimid is genuinely broad-based: India revenue grew 16% and Europe 55% in FY26, the newly-integrated Haleon nicotine-replacement-therapy franchise (~$632mn deal, integration largely complete by May-2026) gives a real non-Rx growth leg into a North American market growing ~17% CAGR, and management guides double-digit North America growth even excluding lenalidomide. DRL was also the first company anywhere to win a national regulatory approval (Health Canada, Apr-2026 NOC) for generic injectable semaglutide, underscoring genuine formulation/regulatory capability, and the biosimilar pipeline (abatacept BLA under active FDA review since Feb-2026, more molecules behind it) targets $500-700mn of annual sales by FY29 -- a real multi-year diversification away from small-molecule genericization risk. The stock has already fallen roughly 13% from its 52-week high on the recent bad news, giving some cushion, and normalized on FY25/FY26 clean earnings the multiple (high-teens to low-20s P/E) is not extreme for an Indian pharma major with several live growth vectors.

## Bear case

The strongest bear case is that the two assets meant to fill the Revlimid hole have each failed a quality or compliance test within the same five-week window. The Bachupally biologics site -- the plant behind the abatacept biosimilar the company needs for FY28-29 growth -- was re-inspected in June-2026 and got WORSE: seven Form-483 observations versus five at the same site nine months earlier. On this desk's own historical base rate, escalating repeat-site observations convert to warning letters more than 75% of the time, and a warning letter would directly delay the one biosimilar approval management has guided investors to expect within this fiscal year. Days later, DRL disclosed a semaglutide API process failure that halted supply to Canada -- the exact market DRL had just proudly launched into as a global first -- with resupply not expected before November-2026 and FY27 semaglutide revenue assumptions already cut by roughly half at some brokerages (one estimate: $144mn to $75mn), driving 5-15% EPS cuts at ICICI Securities and Elara Capital. Layered on top, the earnings base has structurally reset: FY26 PAT fell 24% YoY and Q4 FY26 alone crashed 86% YoY as the exclusivity cliff hit, and Q1 FY27 -- the first full quarter with zero cushion -- reports in three days with essentially no analyst visibility on how clean the print will be. None of this is a stale data point or ordinary uncertainty; it is two freshly-dated regulatory/quality events stacked directly on top of an already-confirmed profit reset, in the two segments the bull case depends on.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At ~Rs 1,236 (mkt cap ~Rs 1,03,809cr) the multiple -- roughly 18-24x normalized FY25/26 PAT, higher (~32x) on the one-off-depressed FY26 trailing base -- is pricing a steady-compounder path: double-digit ex-Revlimid growth plus a clean semaglutide and biosimilar ramp over 2-3 years. The base business can plausibly clear a high-single/low-double-digit growth bar on its own (India, Europe, NRT). It cannot yet clear a 'flawless execution on semaglutide + biosimilars' bar given the two quality/compliance misses just logged in the exact assets the multiple is underwriting. The multiple is fair-to-full for what is provable today, and is not cheap enough to absorb a further 483-to-warning-letter escalation or a longer semaglutide resupply delay -- rich relative to near-term-visible cash flows, tipping the call to Sell.

## Detailed rationale

Dr Reddy's FY26 (year to Mar-2026) numbers show the Revlimid cliff arriving on schedule and landing hard in a single quarter. Full-year revenue was roughly flat-to-up (Rs 33,593cr reported, +3.2% YoY) but full-year PAT fell to Rs 4,285cr from Rs 5,655cr in FY25 (-24% YoY), and almost the entire decline is concentrated in Q4 FY26 (Jan-Mar 2026), the quarter in which DRL's limited Revlimid (lenalidomide) exclusivity expired on 31-Jan-2026: Q4 PAT crashed 86% YoY to Rs 220cr (from Rs 1,593cr), North America revenue fell 51% YoY to Rs 1,756cr, and results carried a Rs 453cr Lenalidomide shelf-stock adjustment plus CAR-T/Eftilagimod Alfa impairments. By contrast Q2 FY26 PAT was still up 14% YoY and Q3 down only 14% YoY -- the real step-down is a Q4 event, not a gradual glide. Q1 FY27 (Apr-Jun 2026), due 22-Jul-2026 (three days from today, NOT yet reported), is the first full quarter with zero exclusivity cushion and will be the cleanest read yet on the post-Revlimid run-rate.

What is supposed to fill the Revlimid hole is exactly where DRL has just stumbled twice within five weeks. First, a USFDA Pre-License Inspection at the Bachupally, Hyderabad biologics site (16-25 Jun-2026) closed with a Form-483 carrying 7 observations -- up from 5 at the same site in a Sep-2025 PAI -- directly relevant to the abatacept (DRL_AB) biosimilar BLA that has been under active FDA review since Feb-2026, the asset management is counting on for a targeted $500-700mn in annual biosimilar sales by FY29 (first US IV launch hoped-for within FY27, break-even not before FY28). On this desk's own historical base rate (OAI-to-warning-letter conversion >75%), a rising repeat-site observation count at a BIOLOGICS/PLI facility is a standing high-risk flag, not noise. Second, on 9-Jul-2026 -- after the Q4 earnings call -- DRL disclosed an out-of-spec impurity in a new, higher-yield semaglutide API process, forcing a halt of injectable semaglutide supply to India, Canada and several emerging markets, including the very Canada launch DRL had trumpeted in May-2026 as the first Health-Canada-approved generic semaglutide in a G7 market. Management targets process re-validation by end-Sep-2026 and market resupply by Nov-2026 (no patient-safety/recall issue on already-shipped batches, made on the old validated process), but FY27 semaglutide revenue assumptions have already been cut sharply by analysts (one house: $144mn to $75mn), with EPS trims of 5-15% at ICICI Securities and Elara Capital.

That multiple prices a steady-compounder path: double-digit ex-Revlimid North America growth (management guidance) plus a clean semaglutide and biosimilar ramp over 2-3 years. The base business (India +16% YoY, Europe +55% YoY in FY26, the newly-integrated Haleon NRT franchise) can plausibly clear a high-single/low-double-digit growth bar on its own.
