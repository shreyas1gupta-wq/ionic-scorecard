## Bull case

Bharti Airtel's balance-sheet and cash-generation trajectory has genuinely inflected: net debt/EBITDA has fallen to ~1.1x (annualized) from 1.47x-1.98x a year earlier, FY26 India capex (ex-passive) moderated to ~Rs 31,000 crore as the 5G build-out matures, and operating free cash flow (EBITDA less capex) exceeded Rs 41,500 crore for FY26, with FY27 capex guided flat rather than higher -- sell-side models roughly Rs 1 lakh crore of cumulative FCF across FY26-27E. ARPU premiumization is working in relative terms: Airtel's Rs 257 ARPU sits meaningfully above Jio's ~Rs 214 even with a smaller (480.9m vs 500m+) subscriber base, evidence the company is genuinely capturing higher-value customers rather than just chasing volume. Home broadband is a real, fast-growing adjacency -- revenue up 31%+ YoY, 14.2 million home customers (+1.1 million in the quarter), 45 million fibre home-passes -- diversifying away from pure mobile. Competitive structure remains supportive: Vodafone Idea's survival now runs through continued government forbearance (Rs 36,950 crore of AGR dues converted to ~49% government equity, Rs 87,695 crore frozen until FY32) rather than competitive strength, meaning Airtel and Jio effectively face a 2.5-player market aligned on the direction of future tariff repair. Airtel Africa's FY26 revenue grew 24.0% in constant currency with profit up sharply to $813m, adding a genuinely growing, geographically diversified profit pool. No balance-sheet or governance red flag surfaced in this review (bs_flag GREEN, 0 quant red flags), and ROE/ROCE of 20.5%/27.4% are strong for the sector.

Competitive structure supports the tariff-repair and pricing-discipline thesis: as of the most recent TRAI data, Jio holds ~39.25% wireless subscriber share (500m+ subscribers, the first Indian operator past that mark) and Airtel ~37.81% (480.9m), with Vodafone Idea down to ~15.61% and structurally impaired -- government converted Rs 36,950 crore of Vi's AGR dues to equity (raising its stake to ~49%), Rs 87,695 crore of AGR dues were frozen until FY32, and Vi still faces an estimated annual cash shortfall of ~Rs 20,000 crore and may miss its own Rs 50,000-55,000 crore capex guidance -- meaning Vi's survival now runs through continued government forbearance, not a normal competitive recovery.
