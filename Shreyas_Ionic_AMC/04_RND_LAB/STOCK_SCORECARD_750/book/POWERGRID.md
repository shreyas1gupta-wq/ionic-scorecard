# POWERGRID — Power Grid Corporation of India Ltd.

> [!summary]
> Power Grid is India's national power-transmission utility, rebuilding its pace of spending and asset commissioning after a flat 2020-24 stretch - it spent and commissioned more in FY26 than it originally guided, and has laid out a similar step-up plan for FY27 and FY28. Profit has not moved much yet because under its regulated-utility model, earnings only grow once new assets are formally commissioned, and that step-up is only now working through the numbers. The company has a large, multi-year pipeline of confirmed and bidding work, its collections from customers have actually improved rather than worsened, and it continues to pay a steady, well-covered dividend. The main watch item is that Power Grid is winning a smaller share of the competitively-bid transmission projects than it used to, as rivals bid more aggressively, which could mean thinner returns on future growth. At the current price the stock is not expensive relative to its own history, but it is not a bargain either - it looks like a fairly-priced, steady utility rather than a re-rating opportunity, so we rate it Hold.

## Recommendation — Hold

Hold, not Sell, because there is no deteriorating-fundamental or balance-sheet trigger: bs_flag is GREEN, receivables are improving rather than worsening, ROE is stable in the mid-teens, and the FY26-28 capitalisation ramp gives genuine (already partly executed) earnings-growth visibility after years of flat revenue. It is also not priced as a Sell: 16.6x PE is a modest premium to its own 10-year history, not a stretched multiple, and the reverse-DCF read is fair-to-mildly-cheap against the disclosed Rs 1.7 lakh crore pipeline. But this is not a compellingly undervalued re-rating story either - growth is capped by the CERC-allowed 15% transmission RoE times RAB growth on the regulated book, and the TBCB win-rate slide (32-44% vs historical 50-60%) signals rising competitive intensity on the one growth lever that isn't regulator-capped. Net: a steady regulated compounder, fairly priced for what it can deliver - Hold.

## Bull case

The capex-to-capitalisation-to-earnings chain that had been dormant since 2020 is now visibly turning: FY26 capex (Rs 39,967 cr) and capitalisation (Rs 28,206 cr) both beat guidance, transformation capacity added more than doubled YoY, and management has repeated a rising FY27/FY28 capex/capitalisation guidance (Rs 37,000 cr/Rs 30,000 cr and Rs 45,000 cr/Rs 35,000 cr) rather than walking it back. The pipeline behind that guidance is real and large - Rs 1.7 lakh crore work-in-hand plus over Rs 1.1 lakh crore under active bidding, against a management-flagged Rs 15 lakh crore-plus multi-year opportunity in renewable evacuation, hydro corridors, cross-border interconnections and emerging data-centre demand. Receivables - the classic worry in Indian power - have actually improved (down to Rs 2,905 cr from Rs 4,795 cr YoY, aided by late-payment-surcharge rules), and PGCIL's central transmission-charge pooling mechanism is structurally better secured than retail discom collections. The dividend is dependable (Rs 9/share FY26, ~3.2% yield, ~66% historical payout) given 51%+ government ownership, and at 16.6x trailing PE the stock is not paying a large premium for that visibility.

## Bear case

The single biggest structural cap on this story is regulatory: transmission RoE for the current 2024-29 CERC tariff period was cut from 15.5% to 15%, and the regulated cost-plus book (the ~19% of work-in-hand that isn't TBCB) can only ever compound at allowed-RoE-times-RAB-growth, mathematically limiting earnings growth even if every rupee of the pipeline commissions on schedule. The real growth lever, TBCB, is where the bear case bites hardest: PGCIL won just 9 of 28 FY26 TBCB awards (~32% in-year share, ~44% cumulative), down from a historical 50-60% share, as Adani Energy Solutions, HG Infra and other private players bid more aggressively - if that slide continues, either incremental order intake shrinks or PGCIL wins share back only by accepting thinner IRRs, and either way the FY28 capitalisation guidance of Rs 45,000 cr carries more execution risk than the Rs 39,967 cr FY26 print that just beat guidance. The multi-year track record on growth conversion is genuinely poor: 3-year revenue CAGR is close to zero and FY26 total income grew just 0.5% YoY despite the capex beat - the capitalisation-to-P&L lag has been long, and repeated disappointment on that front is exactly why our own quant screen scores growth_3y at just 12.87/100. A PSU that has under-delivered on growth for four straight years needs more than one strong capitalisation quarter to prove the inflection is durable.

## Detailed rationale

Power Grid Corporation is India's central transmission utility (CTU), operating a cost-plus regulated-equity model (CERC Tariff Regulations 2024-29, transmission RoE trimmed to 15% from 15.5% for this control period) layered with a competitively-bid TBCB growth engine. After a stagnant FY20-24 stretch (3-year revenue CAGR near zero, our own quant screen's growth_3y_score of just 12.87/100), the capex-to-commissioning cycle has clearly re-accelerated: FY26 capex of Rs 39,967 cr beat the revised Rs 35,000 cr guidance, and capitalisation of Rs 28,206 cr beat the Rs 25,000 cr target, with over half of that (Rs 15,291 cr) landing in Q4 alone. Physical execution backs this up: 4,765 ckm of new lines (vs 4,054 ckm FY25) and 72,055 MVA of transformation capacity added (vs 32,819 MVA FY25, more than double). Management has repeated, not walked back, FY27/FY28 capex guidance of Rs 37,000 cr / Rs 45,000 cr with capitalisation of Rs 30,000 cr / Rs 35,000 cr - a multi-year ramp with real pipeline behind it.

Total work-in-hand (incl. CWIP) stands at roughly Rs 1.7 lakh crore, of which Rs 1.37 lakh crore (81%) is TBCB and the balance is regulated cost-plus/CTU-captive scope; a further >Rs 1.1 lakh crore is in active bidding, and management flags a >Rs 15 lakh crore multi-year opportunity across renewable-evacuation corridors, the Brahmaputra hydro corridor, cross-border OSOWOG interconnections, green hydrogen and emerging data-centre-linked demand. The quality tell to watch is the TBCB win rate, which has visibly softened: PGCIL won just 9 of 28 FY26 TBCB awards (~32% in-year share, ~44% cumulative since inception) versus a historical 50-60% share, as Adani Energy Solutions, HG Infra and other private players bid more aggressively. That is where genuine competitive erosion would show up - via thinner IRRs on new TBCB wins - not in the ~81%-locked regulated core where PGCIL remains the natural-monopoly CTU. Receivables, usually the discom-risk worry in Indian power, are actually a positive and PGCIL-specific (not a generic sector read): outstanding receivables fell to Rs 2,905 cr in FY26 from Rs 4,795 cr in FY25 (aided by Late Payment Surcharge rules), with collections of Rs 40,684 cr broadly matching billings - PGCIL's central transmission-charge pooling mechanism is structurally better secured than retail discom collections.

The persistent tension is that capex/capitalisation have surged while P&L has not yet caught up: FY26 consolidated PAT was Rs 15,928 cr (+3% YoY) on total income of Rs 47,684 cr (+0.5% YoY) - essentially flat, because regulated revenue is booked only on the capitalised RAB, and RAB growth was anaemic through 2020-24. The FY26-28 capitalisation ramp (Rs 28,206 cr to Rs 30,000 cr to Rs 35,000 cr) is the mechanism by which that should finally show up in earnings; sell-side (PL Capital) models 6-8% EPS CAGR FY26-28E off a 16% ROE and a 2.8x FY28E P/B target. At CMP ~Rs 284 (17-Jul-26 close, ~Rs 2.64 lakh crore market cap), the stock trades at 16.6x trailing PE and 2.6x P/B against a 16.5% ROE and a 3.17% dividend yield (~66% historical payout) - a modest premium to its own 10-year PE median (~10.7x) but nowhere near the stretched multiples that make some PSU cousins dangerous re-rating/de-rating candidates.
