# LODHA — Lodha Developers Ltd.

> [!summary]
> Lodha Developers had a strong FY26 -- record pre-sales, rising profits, resolution of its long-running family brand dispute, and a comfortably low debt level -- and has laid out an ambitious growth plan through FY31 that includes a large new data-centre business. Execution has genuinely been good. But the stock's price already reflects most of that good news: it trades at a rich valuation for a real-estate developer, a business still concentrated in the expensive Mumbai market, where cash collections have been growing slower than headline sales. We rate it a Hold -- a quality business, but not offering value at current levels, and without a fresh red flag serious enough to call for an exit either.

## Recommendation — Hold

Hold, not Sell: there is no active fundamental red flag. The brand/trademark dispute is fully resolved (April 2025 settlement, June 2025 renaming), promoter holding is high (72.27%) and unpledged, net debt/equity (0.23x) sits comfortably below the company's own 0.5x cap, and business-development replenishment is running at 2.4x annual guidance -- the growth pipeline is genuinely being built, not depleted. A Sell call needs either deteriorating fundamentals or a valuation with a visible de-rating catalyst; here it is a good business at a full price, not a broken one. But it is also not a name to add capital to at ~34x trailing earnings / 5x book, with sell-side consensus already pricing in only single-digit further upside and the collections-vs-bookings growth gap (5% vs 16%) as an early cash-conversion caution flag. Hold captures both sides: don't chase, don't force an exit.

## Bull case

Lodha closed FY26 with its best-ever quarterly bookings, record annual pre-sales of over Rs 20,500 crore, and profit growth of 24%, while keeping debt low and adding new land and projects worth more than double its own yearly target -- a sign the company can keep growing for several years without straining its balance sheet. A years-long family dispute over the 'Lodha' brand name has been fully and formally settled, removing a governance overhang. The company is also starting to build new income streams beyond home sales, including a large data-centre park in the Mumbai region, that could diversify earnings over the next several years.

## Bear case

The business is still heavily tied to one city region -- Mumbai and its extended suburbs continue to account for the clear majority of sales, and that market is already India's least affordable relative to what buyers earn, leaving less room to absorb a slowdown or a rate shock. Cash collected from customers grew only about 5% last year even as new bookings grew 16%, a gap worth watching since collections not keeping pace with announced sales is an early cash-flow tell in real estate. Management has also flagged rising construction costs as a real risk to margins. On top of this, at roughly 34 times last year's profit and 5 times book value -- versus a sector average closer to 23 times forward earnings -- the stock already assumes many more years of uninterrupted high growth, leaving little cushion if the housing cycle in its core market cools.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At ~34x trailing PAT and ~5x book (sector average forward P/E closer to 23x), the market is pricing Lodha to deliver something close to its own guided ~20% PAT CAGR through FY31 (Rs 3,430cr to Rs 8,500cr+) with margins holding at 32-34% and no residential down-cycle along the way. Sell-side consensus already implies only 2-3% further upside, meaning this optimism is already largely in the price. The business can plausibly deliver on execution (strong BD replenishment at 2.4x guidance, disciplined leverage) but the multiple assumes a smooth multi-year compounding path in a core market (MMR) already at its affordability ceiling, with collections growth (5%) currently lagging bookings growth (16%) and management-disclosed construction-cost inflation risk to margins. Verdict: rich, not cheap -- priced for a best-case continuation, not for a normal residential cycle.

## Detailed rationale

Lodha Developers (formerly Macrotech) closed FY26 with pre-sales of Rs 20,530 cr (+16% YoY), a record Q4 of Rs 5,890 cr (+23% YoY), and PAT of Rs 3,430 cr (+24% YoY, ~20% PAT margin) [DATA: Business Standard/Angel One Q4FY26 coverage]. Business-development replenishment was exceptionally strong: 12 new projects added across MMR/Pune/Bengaluru/NCR with GDV of ~Rs 60,100 cr, or 2.4x the company's own annual BD guidance [DATA] -- the single most reassuring number in the print, since it means the multi-year pipeline is being actively re-stocked rather than run down. Balance sheet discipline is real: net debt closed FY26 at ~Rs 5,380 cr, net debt/equity of 0.23x against the company's own 0.5x ceiling (it had spiked to ~Rs 6,170 cr in Q3 on land buying before being paid back down in Q4) [DATA], and promoter holding stands at 72.27% with zero pledge [DATA: Trendlyne shareholding pattern].

This matters because MMR is, per Knight Frank, India's least affordable housing market -- homebuyers there commit ~69% of household income to EMI servicing against a price-to-income ratio of ~9-10x [DATA] -- even as MMR's own unsold inventory has tightened to ~20 months of sales, the tightest in years [DATA]. A second yellow flag: FY26 collections grew only ~5% YoY (to Rs 15,160 cr) against 16% pre-sales growth [DATA] -- collections lagging bookings by this margin is exactly the cash-conversion tell real-estate investors should watch; it can be timing (construction-linked payment plans catching up later) or early evidence that realized cash is running behind the headline sales story. Management itself flagged construction cost inflation of 3-5% in select categories with a potential ~1.7pp margin drag if sustained -- a real, company-disclosed risk to the 32-34% embedded margin guide [DATA].

The company's own long-range target -- FY31 PAT of Rs 8,500 cr+ from FY26's Rs 3,430 cr, implying ~20% PAT CAGR -- is the growth path this multiple is underwriting [DATA]. That is a demanding ask: high-teens pre-sales compounding for five straight years with no residential down-cycle, margins holding at 32-34% despite disclosed cost inflation, collections catching back up to bookings, and data-center/annuity optionality (income only starts FY29 per the powered-shell plan) executing without a stumble [INFERENCE].
