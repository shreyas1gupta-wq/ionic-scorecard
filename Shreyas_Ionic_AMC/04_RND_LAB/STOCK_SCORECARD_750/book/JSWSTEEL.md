# JSWSTEEL — JSW Steel Ltd.

> [!summary]
> JSW Steel has had a genuinely good twelve months: the multi-year Bhushan Power & Steel legal fight is finally over (Supreme Court upheld its resolution plan in March 2026), the Bhushan Power business has been folded into a 50:50 joint venture with Japan's JFE Steel that brought in fresh cash, and net debt has fallen sharply, taking net-debt-to-EBITDA down from over 3x to about 1.5x in a year. Steel volumes are growing and a safeguard duty is currently protecting Indian mills from cheap Chinese and Vietnamese imports, pushing profitability per tonne up sharply. The catch is that the stock, near its 52-week high, already prices in a lot of this improvement, plus continued strong (duty-assisted) margins and years of flawless execution on an ambitious capacity-expansion plan -- while the company's actual returns on capital remain modest for a business valued this richly. On balance, this looks like a case where the good news is already reflected in the price of a still-cyclical, capital-intensive business, more than a name to hold for further re-rating.

## Recommendation — Sell

Sell rather than Hold because the current multiple isn't simply 'fair compensation for a cyclical' -- the reverse-DCF math requires (a) persistence of currently duty-assisted, above-normal per-tonne spreads (Rs 14,990/tonne, +27% YoY) that must survive the Apr-2028 safeguard-duty sunset and any renewed Chinese export pressure, and (b) flawless multi-year execution of a capex programme scaling capacity from ~44MT to 62-78MT, all while underlying capital returns (ROE ~9-10%, ROCE ~11%) remain unremarkable for the multiple paid (~9.3x EV/EBITDA, 3.02x book). The balance-sheet deleveraging and BPSL litigation closure are genuine, durable positives -- but they are precisely the kind of well-flagged, already-reported catalysts that get priced in fast, and the stock's proximity to its 52-week high suggests they largely have been. This is a 'sell the good news on a cyclical' call, not a distress call -- there is no accounting red flag or going-concern issue, just a valuation that has run ahead of what a commodity steel business with single-digit-to-low-teens returns on capital can be expected to sustainably clear through a full cycle.

## Bull case

The bull case is real, not manufactured: a six-year legal overhang (Bhushan Power & Steel) is fully resolved in JSW's favour, the business has been de-risked via a JFE Steel joint venture that brought in fresh equity and cut net debt from Rs 79,850 crore to Rs 46,157 crore in a year, net debt/EBITDA is down to 1.46x, and Fitch has upgraded the credit outlook to BB+/Positive. Volumes are growing (Q1FY27 crude steel +3% YoY, guided +13% for FY27) on the back of BF3, Dolvi and JVML ramp-ups, India steel demand is expected to grow 7-9% in FY27, and a safeguard duty is protecting realizations from Chinese/Vietnamese dumping for roughly two more years. Capacity visibility to 62MT domestically (78MT with JV partners) by FY32 gives a genuine multi-year volume growth runway for a business that has historically been supply-constrained relative to India's steel demand growth.

## Bear case

The strongest bear point is that none of the good news changes the fact that steel is a commodity cycle business, and the current multiple is being paid on a cycle-elevated, duty-assisted margin, not a through-cycle one. EV/EBITDA of roughly 9.3x on an annualized Q1FY27 base is near the top of JSW's own historical range, and that EBITDA/tonne of Rs 14,990 is inflated by a safeguard duty that starts at 12% and steps down to 11% before fully sunsetting in April 2028 -- a known expiry date, not an indefinite moat. Management itself flagged coking coal costs running Rs 12-15/tonne higher near-term. Reported ROE (8.97% three-year average, 10.2% latest) and ROCE (~11%) are unremarkable for a stock priced at 3.02x book value, and the company is simultaneously monetizing stakes (JSW One Platforms IPO, JFE taking 50% of the BPSL JV) to fund a capex plan that could run to Rs 2.26 lakh crore over several years -- meaning a large share of future value creation may accrue to JV partners and continued heavy capex absorption rather than flowing cleanly to JSW Steel shareholders. China's steel overcapacity and export pressure remain a structural, multi-year risk that safeguard duty only partially and temporarily offsets.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At an enterprise value of roughly Rs 3,48,700 crore against annualized Q1FY27 adjusted EBITDA of ~Rs 37,500 crore (~9.3x EV/EBITDA), the market is pricing in continuation of currently elevated, safeguard-duty-assisted spreads (Rs 14,990/tonne) and near-flawless execution of a multi-year capacity build from ~44MT to 62-78MT -- a demanding combination for a commodity cyclical whose reported ROE (~9-10%) and ROCE (~11%) have not structurally re-rated to match a 9x+ EV/EBITDA, 3x price-to-book valuation. Stripped of the safeguard-duty tailwind (which begins stepping down and fully sunsets by April 2028) and reverting per-tonne margins toward a more normalized mid-cycle level, the current multiple does not look adequately cushioned. The business quality and balance-sheet trajectory have improved; the price already reflects more than that improvement.

## Detailed rationale

JSW Steel's balance sheet and legal-overhang picture has genuinely transformed over the past year. Consolidated net debt fell from Rs 79,850 crore (Q1FY26) to Rs 53,870 crore (Mar-2026) to Rs 46,157 crore (30-Jun-2026), taking net debt/EBITDA from 3.20x to 1.81x to 1.46x over the same three points, and net debt/equity to 0.42x [DATA]. Two events drove this: first, the Supreme Court on 5-Mar-2026 reversed its own May-2025 order and finally upheld JSW's Rs 19,700-crore resolution plan for Bhushan Power & Steel (BPSL), rejecting creditors' additional-payment claims and closing a legal saga that had run since 2019-20 [DATA]; second, the Rs 15,750-crore 50:50 JV of the BPSL business (JSW Sambalpur / JSW JFE Kalinga Steel Ltd) with Japan's JFE Steel was completed in two tranches through 30-Jun-2026, deconsolidating roughly Rs 21,500 crore of BPSL enterprise debt while bringing in fresh JFE equity [DATA]. Fitch responded with a BB+/Positive outlook upgrade [DATA]. This is a real structural de-risking rather than an accounting artefact -- the single biggest overhang on the stock for years is now closed.

Operationally, Q1FY27 (quarter to Jun-2026) consolidated crude steel production was 6.59 million tonnes (+3% YoY, +2% QoQ), India capacity utilisation ~94% ex-BF3 (Vijayanagar BF3 was under a capacity upgrade shutdown, restarting hot metal 23-Jun-2026) [DATA]. Consolidated revenue was Rs 47,364 crore (+9.8% YoY) and adjusted EBITDA/tonne jumped 27% YoY to Rs 14,990 (from Rs 11,783 in Q1FY26), helped by the safeguard duty wall against Chinese/Vietnamese/Nepali flat steel (12% from Apr-2025, tapering 11.5%/11% over two more years, sunsetting Apr-2028) and softer iron-ore costs [DATA]. Management guides FY27 production/sales of 29.75/28.6 million tonnes (+13%/+10%) on an expected 7-9% India steel demand growth, but also flagged coking coal costs running Rs 12-15/tonne higher near-term before easing later in the year, and a Rs 22,000-24,000 crore FY27 capex within a larger multi-year plan (Rs ~1.3 lakh crore board-approved, a further Rs 2.26 lakh crore blueprint not yet approved) to grow standalone capacity from ~44MT toward 50.3MT by FY30 and 62MT by FY32, plus another ~16MT via the JFE and new POSCO JVs [DATA]. Importantly, the FY26 headline net profit of Rs 25,508 crore (Q4 alone Rs 16,370 crore, +989% YoY) is NOT the clean run-rate -- it is inflated by a one-off BPSL-deconsolidation gain of ~Rs 17,888 crore; normalized Q4FY26 PAT was Rs 3,475 crore on adjusted EBITDA of Rs 9,713 crore (19% margin), and Q1FY27 profit-before-exceptionals-and-tax was Rs 6,160 crore (+100.5% YoY, +37.2% QoQ) [DATA]. Q1FY27 reported net profit (~Rs 4,696 crore, one source cites Rs 4,651 crore) also includes a modest one-off from the approved Rs 811-crore JSW One Platforms IPO stake sale [DATA].

On valuation: at Rs 1,233/share (17-Jul-2026), market cap is Rs 3,02,576 crore; adding net debt of Rs 46,157 crore gives an enterprise value of roughly Rs 3,48,700 crore [DATA]. Annualizing Q1FY27's Rs 9,373-crore adjusted EBITDA gives an EV/EBITDA of approximately 9.3x [INFERENCE, calculated from the DATA above] -- near the top of JSW's historical cyclical range (roughly 5-6x at trough, 8-9x at prior peaks) -- and that multiple sits on a per-tonne margin (Rs 14,990) that is itself elevated by a safeguard duty scheduled to taper out by Apr-2028, not a normalized through-cycle spread. Reported ROE is 10.2% (latest year) / 8.97% (3-year average) and ROCE ~11% [DATA, Screener.in], mediocre for a stock trading at 3.02x book value. The stock is currently ~21% above its 52-week low (Rs 1,017) and within ~7% of its 52-week high (Rs 1,328) [DATA] -- i.e., much of the good news (deleveraging, BPSL resolution, JFE cash-in, capacity roadmap) already appears to be in the price.
