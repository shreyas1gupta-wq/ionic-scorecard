# MAXHEALTH — Max Healthcare Institute Ltd.

> [!summary]
> Max Healthcare keeps delivering - revenue and profit have grown every quarter for more than five years running, its newest hospitals (Lucknow, Nagpur, Noida, Bhubaneswar) are being funded from a healthy balance sheet, and international patients plus its diagnostics (Max Lab) and home-healthcare (Max@Home) arms are growing fast. But the share price already assumes all of that goes right for many years to come - at roughly 74 times last year's profit and about 10 times book value, it is one of the most expensive hospital stocks in the country, and recent quarters show per-bed revenue growth has actually slowed to low single digits. We rate MAXHEALTH Sell: a well-run hospital business, priced well ahead of what we think it can realistically deliver over the next few years.

## Recommendation — Sell

A simple two-stage reverse-DCF (12% cost of equity, 5% terminal growth, 10-year explicit high-growth phase) shows the current ~74x P/E requires roughly 25-27% sustained annual earnings growth for a decade to be fair value today. My genuine forward 3-5y view for Max is ~17% - a strong compounder given the credible, funded bed pipeline and the international/diagnostics/home-care tailwinds, but well short of what the multiple demands, especially with margin-dilutive new capacity (Lucknow, Jaypee-Noida, Nagpur, Kalinga) each needing 3-5 years to mature and a live CGHS/institutional pricing headwind already cutting into oncology, historically its highest-margin service line. This is not a business-quality problem - execution has been excellent (22 straight quarters of YoY growth, conservative leverage) - it is a price problem: the stock offers no margin of safety against even a modest integration slip or another payer-pricing action, and Indian hospital stocks have historically de-rated hard on the first such miss. That asymmetry (limited upside if things go right, large downside if any one of several concurrent integrations disappoints) is why I mark this Sell rather than Hold.

## Bull case

22 consecutive quarters of YoY revenue and EBITDA growth is a genuinely rare execution track record in Indian hospitals; FY26 network revenue grew 16% to Rs.10,538 Cr with EBITDA up 14% and PAT up 22%. The bed-capacity plan (~10,000 beds by FY30, more than doubling the current 4,966) is funded from a conservative balance sheet - net debt/EBITDA under 1x, D/E 0.32x, interest cover ~18x - so this is growth capital the company can genuinely afford. Growth is also diversifying away from a single engine: international patients (+25% YoY revenue, ~30% ARPOB premium), Max Lab (+14% YoY) and Max@Home (+30% YoY) all give Max multiple, less-crowded levers, and its scale as India's largest listed hospital chain by market cap gives it first pick of bolt-on assets (Kalinga, Jaypee-Noida, Alexis-Nagpur all completed in the past 12 months).

## Bear case

Valuation is the central risk, not a side issue. At ~74x trailing consolidated P/E and ~10x book, against an ROE of just 15% and ROCE of 14.7%, the returns on offer are ordinary while the price is not. My reverse-DCF implies the market is pricing roughly 25-27% sustained decade-long earnings growth - well above my honest 17% forward view. Growth quality is already showing cracks: ARPOB grew just 1% YoY in Q2 FY26 and 3% YoY in Q3 FY26, after a CGHS-linked chemotherapy price revision stripped ~Rs.200 Cr of annual billing and cut oncology's inpatient revenue share from 26% to 21% - clear evidence that a large institutional payer can move pricing against Max with little warning. Simultaneously integrating Lucknow-greenfield, Jaypee-Noida, Nagpur-Alexis and Kalinga-Bhubaneswar multiplies execution risk, and immature capacity structurally dilutes network ARPOB/margins for years before turning accretive. Indian hospital multiples are already rich across the board, so Max, sitting at the very top of that range, would be hit hardest by any sector-wide re-rating.

## Detailed rationale

Max Healthcare's operating momentum is genuinely strong. FY26 network revenue was Rs.10,538 Cr (+16% YoY), network EBITDA Rs.2,638 Cr (+14% YoY, 26.2% margin) and network PAT Rs.1,631 Cr (+22% YoY) - the 22nd straight quarter of YoY growth in Q4 FY26 alone (revenue Rs.2,664 Cr +10%, EBITDA Rs.682 Cr +8% at 26.8% margin, PAT Rs.387 Cr +3%, occupancy 75%, EBITDA/bed Rs.73.4 lakh, 4,966 operational beds). On the equity-consolidated statement that actually accrues to shareholders (net of O&M/minority economics in the 'network' figures), FY26 revenue was Rs.8,373 Cr, net profit Rs.1,442 Cr, OPM 27%, ROE 15%, ROCE 14.7% - solid but not extraordinary returns for a stock priced at ~74x trailing consolidated EPS and ~10x book (CMP ~Rs.1,098 on 16-Jul-2026, market cap ~Rs.1.08-1.09 lakh Cr). Q1 FY27 (Jun-2026 quarter) results are NOT yet out as of today; the trading window has been closed since 1-Jul-2026 and results are expected in early August 2026 - the year-ago comparator is Q1 FY26 revenue Rs.2,028 Cr / PAT Rs.308 Cr.

The expansion pipeline is the crux of the forward story: management guides to ~10,000 operational beds by FY30 (more than double today's 4,966) on Rs.6,000 Cr of capex. Concrete legs include a new Lucknow greenfield hospital on Shaheed Path (Rs.1,400 Cr phase-1, ~712 beds, 36-month build) plus a Gomti Nagar (Lucknow) expansion to ~570 beds and a further 200-300; Nagpur (the acquired Alexis Hospital, rebranded Max Nagpur); Jaypee Hospital Noida (acquired Nov-2025, now ramping as Max Hospital Noida); a controlling stake acquired in Kalinga Hospital, Bhubaneswar (FY26); brownfield ramp-up at Mohali/Mumbai/Delhi (~20% capacity add) and a Gurgaon greenfield (~10% more) phasing in. The balance sheet can carry this - net debt was Rs.1,755 Cr (Q1 FY26) vs Rs.1,576 Cr a year earlier, debt/equity ~0.32x, net debt/EBITDA comfortably under 1x, interest cover ~18x - so this is an execution/integration risk, not a solvency risk. But Jaypee-Noida, Nagpur-Alexis, Kalinga-Bhubaneswar and the new Lucknow campus are each multi-year margin-drag assets before they reach network-average ARPOB/EBITDA-per-bed, and running four-five integrations concurrently raises the odds that at least one slips or dilutes margins longer than modeled.

Growth quality has softened at the margin: ARPOB grew only 1% YoY in Q2 FY26 (Rs.77,300) and 3% YoY in Q3 FY26 (Rs.77,900), partly because a CGHS-linked chemotherapy price revision cut ~Rs.200 Cr of annual billing and pulled oncology's inpatient revenue share down from 26% to 21% - a reminder that government/institutional payers can move pricing against a hospital operator with little notice. Institutional-payer revenue share ticked up as a proportion of the mix; management expects this to normalize as premium/international categories re-scale. International patients remain the bright spot (~9% of revenue, Q2 FY26 international revenue Rs.231 Cr +25% YoY, ~30% ARPOB premium, ~1.2x ALOS), and Max Lab (+14% YoY) and Max@Home (+30% YoY) are small but fast-growing adjacent annuities that shorten length-of-stay and build patient stickiness. None of this changes the core valuation arithmetic: at ~74x trailing consolidated EPS, the market is pricing in a very long runway of high compounding.
