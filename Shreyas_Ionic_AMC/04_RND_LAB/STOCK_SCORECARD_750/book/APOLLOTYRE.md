## Detailed rationale

Apollo Tyres closed FY26 (year ended 31-Mar-2026) with consolidated revenue of Rs 28,471 crore, up 9% YoY (FY25: Rs 26,123 crore), and consolidated net profit of Rs 1,372 crore, up 22% YoY (FY25: Rs 1,121 crore). Q4 FY26 alone was the standout: consolidated revenue Rs 7,336 crore (+14.2% YoY), consolidated EBITDA margin expanded to 14.6% from 13.0% a year ago, and India (standalone) operations delivered a record quarter -- revenue of roughly Rs 5,240 crore (+14.3% YoY) on "strong high-teens volume growth in both replacement and Original Equipment segments," record truck-and-bus-radial (TBR) replacement volumes tied to a freight-activity rebound, and continued premiumization. The balance sheet story is genuinely strong and not a one-off: net debt/EBITDA improved to 0.4x (from 3.2x in March 2020) and debt-equity to 0.20 (from 0.27 in FY25), with interest coverage at 10.3x (from 5.6x) -- a real multi-year deleveraging, not an accounting artifact. Europe remains in restructuring mode: the Enschede (Netherlands) plant is being closed by 30-Jun-2026, and Europe's segment EBITDA margin was 14.6%.

Earnings-quality check on the headline Q4 PAT jump (+242% YoY, Rs 631 crore vs Rs 185 crore): the quarter carried two disclosed one-off items -- a Rs 338 crore restructuring/impairment exceptional charge (linked to the European restructuring) and a Rs 574 crore one-off tax reversal from adopting the concessional tax regime. Netting just these two items (ignoring second-order tax effects on the charge itself) removes roughly Rs 236 crore of the reported PAT beat, implying an underlying/normalized Q4 PAT closer to Rs 395 crore versus Rs 185 crore a year ago -- still a genuine ~+113% YoY improvement consistent with the EBITDA margin expansion and volume growth, but well short of the +242% headline. The full-year standalone PAT of Rs 1,851.77 crore (vs Rs 629.43 crore FY25, +194%) is similarly flattered by tax items per management's own disclosure, and this is the number the market is using: Apollo's quoted trailing P/E of ~14.8x (price ~Rs 430.6, market cap ~Rs 27,351 crore as of mid-July 2026) reconciles almost exactly to that standalone PAT base, meaning the headline multiple looks cheaper than the recurring-earnings multiple actually is. On the input-cost side, natural rubber has spiked from roughly Rs 200/kg to about Rs 250/kg within a few months in 2026; management is guiding high-teens raw-material cost inflation and has taken 6-8% India price hikes so far but has stated only "half the required increase" has been implemented -- a live near-term margin-compression risk until pass-through catches up. FY27 capex guidance is Rs 3,500 crore (Rs 3,000 crore India / Rs 500 crore Europe, ~80% growth/capacity-led), signalling management confidence in the demand cycle but adding depreciation/opex ahead of volume ramp-up.

Competitively, this is not a clean tailwind story for Apollo alone: MRF still leads on scale and brand recall, but CEAT is the more visibly aggressive share-gainer via its Chennai capacity expansion and the Camso off-highway-tyre acquisition, targeting the mid-premium replacement segment and rural/semi-urban markets. Apollo's counter is its Vredestein European premium brand plus global manufacturing integration -- a genuine three-way (at least) share contest rather than a market Apollo can coast through on demand recovery alone. Q1 FY27 (Apr-Jun 2026) results have NOT yet been released as of today: Apollo's last board meeting was 14-May-2026 (FY26 audited results/final dividend), and the investor-announcements page through 16-Jul-2026 shows no Q1 FY27 board-meeting date fixed yet; the company has historically reported Q1 in the first half of August, so this is a pending catalyst, not a released one.
