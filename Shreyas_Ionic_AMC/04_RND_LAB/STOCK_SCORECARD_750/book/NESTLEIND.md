# NESTLEIND — Nestle India Ltd.

> [!summary]
> Nestle India just posted its best back-to-back quarters in years - double-digit volume growth and 26-46% profit growth over the last two prints - but a meaningful part of that is a GST rate-cut base effect that will fade through FY27, and a third of the business (milk and nutrition products) is now flat-to-shrinking, with confectionery and coffee doing the heavy lifting instead. The company remains debt-free and highly profitable, and results due July 22 are expected to stay strong, but at roughly 77-78 times earnings - well above FMCG peers - the stock already prices in growth the business has not sustained over a full three-year cycle. We rate this a Hold: a quality franchise, fully priced, with no margin of safety.

## Recommendation — Hold

This is a Hold, not a Sell, because nothing in the recent print trend supports forcing an exit: profit growth has accelerated for two straight quarters (46% then 26-27% YoY), volumes are genuinely double-digit (company's own characterization), the balance sheet carries no meaningful debt, and the next catalyst (Q1 FY27 results, due 22-Jul-2026, three days from today) is a data-pending event where sell-side consensus still expects ~28% YoY PAT growth (~Rs 830 crore) and 9-11% domestic volume growth - selling into that setup would be timing a print, not underwriting a thesis. But it is equally not a Buy-quality setup: at ~77-78x trailing earnings (matches the quant context PE of 78.43, and independent trackers put it near 77-80x as of mid-July 2026) versus Britannia (~55-57x), HUL (~33-53x) and the broader FMCG/food peer set (~52x average), the multiple already prices in sustained mid-teens-plus growth that the business has not delivered over any trailing 3-year window (revenue CAGR 3y = 11.07% per quant context) - and the most recent acceleration is flattered by a GST-driven base effect plus a heavy, margin-dilutive step-up in A&P spend to defend share against ITC (now India's #2 packaged-foods player, ahead of Britannia) and other entrants. A third of the revenue base (milk & nutrition) is now flat-to-declining. That combination - excellent business, rich price, narrowing growth engine, base-effect-inflated recent prints - is the textbook definition of Hold: own if you already do, don't chase, no margin of safety to add.

## Bull case

Two consecutive quarters of accelerating, volume-led growth (Q3 FY26 profit +46%, Q4 FY26 +26-27%, on double-digit volumes, not price) is a real inflection, aided by GST-2.0 rate cuts that moved most of the portfolio to the 5% slab and lifted affordability. The confectionery franchise is compounding at 18-22% annually and India is now Nestle's largest global KitKat market; quick-commerce distribution (e-commerce ~8% of sales, growing ~97% YoY) and a fast-growing out-of-home channel are extending premium products beyond the metros. The balance sheet is pristine - near-zero debt, 74% ROE, 93% ROCE - funding a Rs 5,000-6,500 crore multi-year capex program (including a 10th India factory) entirely from internal accruals, and easing global cocoa/coffee prices should support margins into FY27. A shareholder vote also capped the royalty payout to the Swiss parent at its current ~4.5%-of-sales rate, rejecting a proposed escalation to 5.25%.

## Bear case

The strongest bear point: the recent 'double-digit volume growth' story is inflated by a GST rate-cut base effect (effective roughly September 2025) that mechanically boosts affordability and lowers the prior-year comparison base - this laps out during FY27, and the underlying run-rate once it does is untested. Meanwhile milk & nutrition - still a third of revenue - is structurally shrinking (FY26 revenue +0.7%, volumes -2%, down from 49.6% of turnover in CY16 to 33.4% now), so growth is increasingly concentrated in confectionery and coffee, categories directly exposed to cocoa/coffee price cycles and now being defended with a >50% jump in A&P spend against an emboldened ITC (which has already overtaken Britannia in packaged foods) - that spend is a margin call option, not free growth. Sell-side previews for the imminent Q1 FY27 print already flag raw-material (milk) inflation risk and an expected sequential EBITDA margin step-down to ~22.3% from Q4's 26.3%. All of this sits under a ~77-78x trailing PE - versus ~52-57x for listed FMCG/food peers and a third-party DCF-implied fair multiple closer to 42x - pricing in growth durability the business has not proven across any full 3-year window.

## Detailed rationale

Nestle India's last two reported quarters look genuinely strong on the surface: Q3 FY26 (Dec-2025) profit rose 46% YoY to ~Rs 1,018 crore on 19% revenue growth, and Q4 FY26 (Mar-2026) profit rose 26-27% YoY to Rs 1,110-1,114 crore on 22.6% revenue growth to Rs 6,748 crore, with the company itself flagging 'double-digit volume growth' as the driver (not price) alongside a >50% jump in advertising/promotion spend. Full FY26 consolidated revenue came in around Rs 23,100-23,150 crore, up 14.6% YoY. A meaningful share of this acceleration is a GST-2.0 base effect: the rate rationalization that moved most of Nestle's portfolio to the 5% slab (UHT milk to 0%) improved affordability and is estimated to have added roughly 200-250bps to near-term category volumes - a real but partly one-off tailwind that will lap out through FY27 rather than repeat indefinitely. Underneath the headline number, the portfolio mix is shifting: milk products & nutrition, still 33.4% of FY26 turnover (down from 38.1% in FY25 and 49.6% in CY16), grew revenue just 0.7% with volumes down 2% - the weakest and now structurally shrinking leg of the business, pressured by cyclical domestic milk-procurement cost inflation. Growth is instead being carried by confectionery (KitKat/Munch/Milkybar, +33% revenue and +25% volume in FY26, growing 18-22% annually, with India now Nestle's largest global KitKat market) and by Maggi/prepared dishes plus out-of-home and quick-commerce channels (e-commerce ~8% of sales, quick commerce ~60% of that, growing ~97% YoY off a low base) - though base Maggi noodle volume growth itself is only 5-7% annually per available reporting. Input costs are a genuine swing factor: cocoa and coffee prices have eased from their 2025 peaks (Nestle SA's global CEO flagged this as a 2026 margin tailwind, strengthening into H2), which should help the beverages and confectionery categories, but domestic milk costs remain a live headwind for the nutrition segment. On royalty, Nestle India paid its Swiss parent (Societe des Produits Nestle S.A.) Rs 1,024.5 crore in FY26 (~4.5% of net sales), up ~14% YoY in line with sales growth; a proposed step-up to 5.25% by FY29 was put to shareholders and rejected at an earlier AGM, so the rate itself has not escalated - a governance non-event rather than a fresh overhang. Capex remains heavy and expansionary: Rs 2,000 crore/year run-rate, a broader Rs 5,000-6,500 crore multi-year program including a 10th India factory (Odisha, MoU with the Ministry of Food Processing Industries) and a new Munch line at Sanand (Rs 225 crore, ~8,300 tonnes added FY26) - all funded from internal accruals given near-zero debt (D/E 0.09, interest coverage 33x). The balance sheet and profitability profile (ROE 74%, ROCE 93%, GREEN bs_flag) are best-in-class for the sector; the open question is entirely about growth durability and price paid, not business quality or solvency.
