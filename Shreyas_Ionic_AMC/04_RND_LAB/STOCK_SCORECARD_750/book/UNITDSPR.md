# UNITDSPR — United Spirits Ltd.

> [!summary]
> United Spirits' premiumization story is genuinely working — its premium-and-above portfolio is now nine-tenths of sales and margins keep expanding — but the stock's roughly 55-times trailing-earnings valuation already assumes years of smooth, high-teens profit growth that this business has not actually shown; in the very year it delivered strong numbers, it also absorbed two separate state excise shocks (in Andhra Pradesh and a steep Maharashtra duty hike) that a national spirits company is structurally exposed to almost every year. The Royal Challengers Bengaluru cricket-franchise stake has now been sold for roughly Rs 16,660 crore in cash rather than held as a growth lever, so the current price has to be justified by the core liquor business alone going forward — and on that basis, we think the price is ahead of what the business can reliably deliver. Sell.

## Recommendation — Sell

Sell, not Hold, because the valuation gap is not marginal. A ~55x trailing P/E on a business that grew reported NSV 7.6-7.7% and needed a favorable Karnataka-reform narrative plus a one-time RCB windfall to sustain the bull case is pricing in a smoothness this business has never actually delivered — in the very year it posted these results, it absorbed two separate state-excise shocks. That pattern (AP now, Maharashtra now, some other state next) is close to a base-rate certainty for a national Indian spirits distributor, not a tail risk the multiple should be allowed to ignore. The RCB sale, while a well-executed disposal, removes the one clean re-rating lever (optionality on a scarce cricket-franchise asset) and converts it into a one-off cash distribution — after which the stock stands or falls purely on premiumization execution against ENA/glass cost inflation and a shrinking Popular base. Given Macquarie and UBS already carry Underperform ratings with targets 10-20% below CMP, and our own reverse-DCF cross-check implies a priced-in terminal growth rate above what the reported growth mix (price/mix-heavy, not volume-heavy) supports, the risk/reward for new money at this multiple skews to Sell. This is a Universe-mode coverage call (not a held position), based on valuation-vs-growth math and recurring regulatory-risk pattern, not a call that the franchise itself is broken.

## Bull case

Premiumization is real and structural: P&A is now 89.6% of standalone NSV (+1.1pp YoY), per-case realization has risen meaningfully as consumers trade up, and EBITDA margin expanded 60bp to 18.4% in FY26 even while absorbing state disruptions. Karnataka's shift to strength-based (AIB) excise taxation and price decontrol from April 2026 removes a longstanding structural cap in one of India's largest liquor markets, and brokerages (Motilal Oswal, Nomura, JM Financial) read it as a multi-year positive. The RCB divestiture (Rs 16,660 cr, ~35x return, CCI-cleared) is excellent capital allocation that should deliver a large special dividend while letting management focus purely on the core spirits business, and Diageo continues to back the India franchise with heavy brand investment (Rs 500cr+ annually cited) plus bolt-on craft M&A (NAO Spirits' Greater Than/Hapusa gin brands). Half the covering Street (Goldman Sachs, BofA) remains Buy-rated.

## Bear case

The valuation — ~54.7x trailing earnings, ~11.5x book — prices in a smoothness this business has never delivered. In FY26 alone it absorbed two separate state-excise shocks: Andhra Pradesh base-normalization and a 50% Maharashtra IMFL duty hike that management-adjacent estimates peg at a 6-8% EPS hit in a state that is 20-22% of sales. This is the recurring pattern in Indian spirits, not a one-off — state excise regimes change unpredictably and sometimes punitively, and USL's geographic footprint means it is structurally exposed to at least one such shock most years. A meaningful share of reported 'growth' is realization/mix-led (per-case realization up from roughly Rs 1,605 to Rs 1,918 over recent years) rather than pure volume expansion — lower-quality growth composition, especially alongside a Popular segment (9% of sales) that is outright shrinking. And the RCB sale, while good execution, removes the one asset that gave bulls a genuine 'free option' argument for the rich multiple; once the special dividend is paid, USL is judged purely on a spirits business growing NSV at 7-8% while trading near 55x earnings — a gap Macquarie and UBS (both Underperform, targets Rs 1,250-1,350, bracketing CMP from below) are already flagging.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At CMP ~Rs 1,376 (17-Jul-26), USL trades at ~54.7x trailing standalone EPS and ~11.5x book (mcap Rs 100,069 cr / FY26 standalone PAT Rs 1,830 cr). A simple perpetuity cross-check (P/E is approximately 1/(cost of equity minus terminal growth), using ~11-12% cost of equity typical for an Indian consumer name) implies the market is pricing in terminal growth near 9-10% in perpetuity — above India's long-run nominal GDP growth and well above the 7.6-7.7% reported NSV growth actually delivered this year. The business can plausibly clear high-single-to-low-double-digit revenue growth for the next few years (Karnataka reform tailwind plus intact premiumization runway) and mid-teens EPS growth via margin execution, but the multiple assigns almost no probability to the state-excise air-pockets this business has now hit twice in one fiscal year — a demonstrated pattern, not a tail scenario, on the historical run-rate of Indian state liquor policy. That gap between what is priced and what has actually been demonstrated (net of one-off items like AP re-entry and the now-completed RCB monetization) is the core of the Sell call: rich, not obviously irrational, but priced past the base rate of this business's own volatility.

## Detailed rationale

United Spirits' FY26 (year ended Mar-26) numbers confirm the premiumization engine is real: standalone NSV grew 7.6% to Rs 12,448 cr, Prestige & Above (P&A) NSV grew 8.6% to Rs 11,152 cr (11.3% ex-Maharashtra) and now makes up 89.6% of standalone sales (+1.1pp YoY), while the mass-market Popular segment (9% of sales) actually saw net sales decline 0.3%. Standalone EBITDA rose 11.6% to Rs 2,296 cr with margin expanding 60bp to 18.4%, and standalone PAT grew 17.5% to Rs 1,830 cr. But dig into composition: a meaningful part of P&A 'growth' is realization/mix-led rather than pure volume expansion (analyst work around the Q3 FY26 print pegs per-case realization near Rs 1,918 versus roughly Rs 1,605 a few years back), even though actual case volumes did grow too (Q2 FY26 volumes were 16.6m cases vs 15.4m cases YoY, +7.8%). Price-led 'growth' layered on top of genuine but moderate volume growth is a lower-quality growth mix than headline NSV numbers suggest.

The bigger issue is that this growth was delivered while absorbing two separate state-excise disruptions in the same fiscal year — Andhra Pradesh (base-normalization drag flagged for Q4) and a punishing Maharashtra IMFL excise hike (duty raised from 3x to 4.5x manufacturing cost, ~50% effective jump, alongside a country-liquor duty increase; Maharashtra is 20-22% of USL sales and brokerages pegged the EPS hit at 6-8%). Ex-AP/Maharashtra, national P&A growth was a healthier 8.5% in Q4 — but the fact that USL needs an 'ex-bad-state' adjustment most years is itself the tell: state excise shocks are a recurring structural feature of the Indian spirits business, not a one-off. Karnataka's move to alcohol-in-beverage (AIB)-based excise and price decontrol from April 2026 is a genuine multi-year positive for one of India's largest markets, but it phases in over 3-4 years, not immediately. Management has also flagged ENA and glass cost inflation as near-term gross-margin risks.

On top of the operating business, USL completed the 100% divestiture of Royal Challengers Sports (RCB) for Rs 16,660 cr (~$1.78bn) to a consortium of Aditya Birla Group, Times of India Group, Bolt Ventures and Blackstone — announced 24-Mar-26, CCI-cleared 30-Jun-26, with BCCI ratification the remaining formal step. This is excellent capital allocation (reported as roughly a 35x return on Diageo's original investment) and is expected to fund a large special dividend (Nomura estimated up to Rs 196/share on a full payout, ~Rs 98/share if half is retained) — a genuine near-term cash event. But it also removes the 'hidden asset' optionality argument that was part of the long-running bull case: once distributed, USL reverts to being valued purely as a spirits operating business. At CMP Rs 1,375.8 (17-Jul-26), that business trades at ~54.7x trailing standalone EPS and ~11.5x book (mcap Rs 100,069 cr vs Rs 1,830 cr FY26 PAT) — rich even by India FMCG staples standards, and Street itself is split: Goldman Sachs (TP Rs 1,575) and BofA (TP Rs 1,630) stay Buy, while Macquarie (TP Rs 1,250) and UBS (TP Rs 1,350) are Underperform, bracketing the current price almost symmetrically.
