## Detailed rationale

Anand Rathi Wealth is a genuinely high-quality, asset-light HNI wealth-distribution franchise, and the Q1 FY27 print (quarter ended 30 June 2026) confirms the franchise is still compounding on its own merits, not just on market beta. AUM crossed Rs 1,06,300 crore for the first time, up 21% YoY, on net inflows of Rs 2,743 crore in the quarter alone -- the 19th consecutive quarter of this kind of print, per management's own framing. The distribution engine behind it looks structurally sound rather than headcount-driven: RM count grew to 417 from 382 a year ago, but AUM-per-RM rose in parallel to Rs 249 crore from Rs 224 crore, and 151 RMs now manage over Rs 200 crore each (79% of total AUM, up from 129 RMs/76% a year ago) -- productivity is scaling faster than headcount, which is the opposite of a franchise buying growth with hiring. Stickiness is real, not narrative: client attrition ran at just 0.09%, and 'regret' RM attrition (RMs above Rs 40 crore AUM leaving) was zero in Q1 FY27 versus two departures a year earlier. Return on capital is elite for the business (3-year ROE ~42%, FY26 annualized ROE as high as ~46.7% on some cuts versus the quant snapshot's 39.7%; D/E of 0.08 confirms this is earned on almost no leverage), which is exactly what you'd expect from a fee/commission distribution model with negligible balance-sheet intensity.

The business-model risk the charter flags is real and current, not historical: 57% of FY26 turnover came from the sale of structured products (market-linked debentures), with the remaining 43% from mutual-fund distribution/brokerage. Budget 2023 already stripped MLDs of LTCG tax treatment (all gains now taxed as STCG at slab rate), and the firm absorbed that shock and kept growing -- a genuine resilience data point -- but a revenue base that is still majority-weighted to one product line, whose economics are set by tax policy and SEBI's NCS/structured-product framework (both of which have moved against the product once already this decade), is a structural concentration risk that doesn't go away just because it was survived once. The other new data point worth flagging is margin: Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin compressed sharply to ~33.7% from ~46.6% a year ago (~1,289 bps), which management attributed to accelerated RM hiring and growth investment rather than yield erosion -- plausible, but it is the first visible crack in the operating-leverage story that has powered PAT growth ahead of revenue growth in recent years, and it needs 2-3 more quarters to read as noise versus trend. On governance: recent SEBI penalties (Rs 10 lakh for cybersecurity/compliance lapses, Rs 5 lakh for reconciliation/margin-reporting violations, an EY forensic audit into a Rs 13 crore fraud) all sit against Anand Rathi Share & Stock Brokers Ltd (ARSSBL), the group's separate broking entity -- not against the listed wealth-management entity (ANANDRATHI) under review here. That is a real distinction, not a technicality, but the shared brand is worth a watch-item given how reputationally intertwined HNI-trust businesses are with their group name.

The valuation is where the real tension sits. At a market cap of roughly Rs 34,600-34,900 crore against trailing consolidated PAT of approximately Rs 400-410 crore (FY26 adjusted PAT of Rs 386-396 crore, rolled forward with Q1 FY27's Rs 116 crore adjusted print replacing the year-ago quarter), the stock trades at roughly 85-90x trailing earnings -- consistent with the quant snapshot's 87.3x. Even granting the elite ROE, the negligible capital intensity that lets almost all earnings be reinvested, and a real multi-year India-HNI-wealth TAM tailwind (industry AUM projected to grow from ~USD 1.1 trillion in FY24 to ~USD 2.3 trillion by FY29, ~16% CAGR), the current price is not underwriting 'the company hits its own 20-25% AUM growth guidance' -- it is underwriting that growth, PLUS margin re-expansion back toward historical levels, PLUS zero multiple compression, simultaneously and for the better part of a decade.
