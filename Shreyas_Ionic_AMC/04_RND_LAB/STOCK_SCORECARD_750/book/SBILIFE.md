# SBILIFE — SBI Life Insurance Company Ltd.

> [!summary]
> SBI Life is a high-quality life insurer with India's widest bancassurance reach, and it got a genuine regulatory tailwind this month when IRDAI declined to force banks to sell rival insurers' products through their branches - protecting the SBI branch network that drives 60% of its business. Full-year FY26 numbers look healthy (embedded value up 15%, new business value up 12%, industry-best persistency and mis-selling metrics), but the underlying quarterly trend has cooled: growth and margins both softened in the March 2026 quarter, and sell-side estimates point to a further margin dip in the June 2026 quarter (results due 24-Jul-2026, not yet out). The stock also trades at the richest earnings multiple among its listed peers and near its 52-week high. We rate it a Hold - a genuinely good business that is fully priced, with growth momentum that needs to find a new gear rather than a reason to sell.

## Recommendation — Hold

Hold, not Sell, because nothing here is a business-quality breakdown: persistency is improving, mis-selling is industry-low, EV/RoEV compounding is intact, and the regulatory news flow (open-architecture stance) actively protects the distribution moat rather than threatening it - there is no asset-quality or governance impairment thesis to justify a Sell. But Hold, not a stronger call, because the valuation (richest P/E among peers, P/EV ~2.3x, near 52-week high) already prices in the good news, while the visible quarterly trend (Q4 FY26 APE +5.5% YoY only, margin -225bps YoY, VNB down YoY, and a sell-side Q1 FY27 margin estimate of ~26% vs 28.35% in Q4) shows momentum has cooled, not accelerated. A name where the franchise is sound but the price has already spent the good news and the near-term print is expected to be soft is a Hold, not a reason to add or to exit.

## Bull case

The distribution moat just got a regulatory upgrade: IRDAI's opposition to mandatory bancassurance open-architecture (17-Jul-2026) protects SBI Life's exclusive hold on the SBI branch network behind 60% of its APE. Franchise-quality metrics are best-in-class - 13th-month persistency at 87.9% (+53bps YoY) and a ~0.02% mis-selling complaint ratio, among the lowest in the industry. FY26 value creation was solid: EV +15% YoY to Rs.80,790 cr, RoEV 19.7%, VNB +12% to Rs.6,670 cr, margin held at 27.5% (upper half of guidance) despite the GST-ITC hit. Product mix is de-risking away from ULIP dependence - ULIP APE share down to ~60% from 64%, protection APE +10% YoY (individual protection +24%).

## Bear case

ULIP is still ~60% of APE, so real sensitivity to equity-market cycles remains - a sharp drawdown would hit both new ULIP sales and the Rs.4.87 lakh cr AUM base simultaneously. The full-year growth numbers bury a clear Q4 FY26 deceleration: standalone APE grew only 5.5% YoY (vs 13.4% full-year), VNB margin compressed ~225bps YoY to 28.35%, VNB itself fell YoY, and net profit dipped 1.1% YoY. The GST-ITC loss and the (still-running) surrender-value-regulation drag are structural margin headwinds that management is offsetting through a product-mix/pricing treadmill, not a genuine tailwind - and the sell-side's own pre-print estimate has Q1 FY27 margin slipping further to ~26%. Meanwhile the stock's P/E (~76-79x) is the richest among the three large listed private life insurers, sits near its 52-week high, and already prices a smooth continuation of low-teens growth - leaving no margin of safety if the expected margin softness is confirmed when Q1 FY27 results are announced (24-Jul-2026, not yet reported).

## Detailed rationale

SBI Life's franchise quality is genuinely strong and got an incremental regulatory tailwind this month: on 17-Jul-2026, IRDAI formally opposed mandating 'open architecture' in bancassurance (which would force banks to sell multiple insurers' products through their branches). That protects SBI Life's exclusive access to State Bank of India's branch network, which contributed 60% of FY26 APE (individual APE Rs.14,120 cr, +11% YoY) - a real distribution moat, not a hypothetical one. Asset-quality-of-franchise proxies for a life insurer are persistency and mis-selling, and both are clean: 13th-month persistency improved to 87.9% (+53bps YoY), and the company's mis-selling complaint ratio of ~0.02% is among the lowest in the industry. FY26 headline metrics: Embedded Value Rs.80,790 cr (+15% YoY), RoEV 19.7%, Value of New Business (VNB) Rs.6,670 cr (+12% YoY), VNB margin 27.5% - held at the upper half of the 26-28% guided band despite absorbing the GST-linked input-tax-credit (ITC) loss (management pegs the underlying margin ex-GST at ~29%). Product mix is de-risking gradually: ULIP fell to ~60% of APE from 64% a year earlier, while protection APE grew 10% (individual protection APE +24%, sum assured +62%) and non-par/par gained share.

The forensic flag is that full-year optics mask a clear Q4 FY26 (Jan-Mar 2026) deceleration: standalone quarterly APE grew just 5.5% YoY (Rs.5,750 cr) versus 13.4% for the full year; VNB margin compressed ~225bps YoY to 28.35% (from 30.6% in Q4 FY25); VNB itself fell YoY (Rs.1,630 cr vs Rs.1,670 cr); and net profit slipped 1.1% YoY to Rs.804.6 cr. Sell-side (Emkay, pre-print) estimates Q1 FY27 VNB margin at ~26%, down further from Q4 FY26's 28.35% - a genuine sequential compression from the GST-ITC loss and the (now-absorbed, still-running) surrender-value regulation drag (SBI Life had guided ~50-60bps of margin impact from the Oct-2024 surrender-value norms), only partially offset by the mix-shift toward protection/non-par. Management's own FY27 guidance is ~14% APE growth with margin range-bound 26-28% (internal aspiration >27%) - a deliberate 'hold the line' framing, not an acceleration story.
