# SBIN — State Bank of India

> [!summary]
> SBI's FY26 numbers show a bank in genuinely strong health wearing a deceptively soft top-line growth number. GNPA/NNPA (1.49%/0.39%), SMA (7bps of loans) and provisioning coverage (74-92%) are all at or near multi-decade bests, and CRAR (15.40%, CET-1 12.29%) sits comfortably above the regulatory floor with no capital-raise pressure. The 5.0% one-year revenue growth against a 13.6% three-year CAGR is a real deceleration, not a base effect -- domestic NIM compressed 18bp for the year (21bp in Q4 alone) as the repo-linked loan book repriced down faster than deposit costs, even as loan volumes grew a strong 16.87% YoY; standalone profit still grew 12.88% to a record Rs 80,032 crore, partly aided by a falling credit cost (0.37%) and a lower Q4 tax rate that have limited further room to help. ## Recommendation — Hold

This is an existing, large holding and nothing in the fundamentals argues for exiting it. Asset quality and capital are genuinely clean -- GNPA/NNPA at multi-decade lows, SMA book near-zero, provisioning conservative beyond what's required, CRAR 3-4 points above the regulatory floor -- so the cheap multiple is not a credit-risk discount in disguise. The real story is a cyclical NIM compression that management guides to stabilize (not worsen) into FY27, plus subsidiaries that had a softer year than the standalone bank. Combined with a live SOTP catalyst (the SBI Funds Management listing this week), the balance of evidence favors continuing to hold a core PSU banking anchor position rather than trimming it.

## Bull case

The bull case: asset quality is at or near multi-decade bests and getting cleaner, not just looking clean -- GNPA down to 1.49%, NNPA to 0.39%, slippage ratio just 0.54%, and an SMA book of only 7bps of loans that says the forward pipeline is clean too, backed by conservative (not window-dressed) coverage of 74-92% plus a further Rs 29,713 crore non-NPA buffer. Capital is comfortably ahead of the regulatory floor (CRAR 15.40%, CET-1 12.29%) with broad-based loan growth (+16.87% YoY) across agri, SME, corporate and retail, so there's no capital-raise or growth-funding constraint. And the valuation gap looks like a genuine opportunity: about 1.6x book and under 12x earnings against a standalone ROE north of 18% is a real discount to what the return profile would justify, reinforced right now by a concrete, dated catalyst -- SBI Funds Management's IPO (allotment 17 July, listing 21 July 2026) pricing India's largest AMC at up to Rs 1.17 lakh crore, which should sharpen the market's sum-of-the-parts read on the parent.

## Bear case

The bear case: NIM compression is real and ongoing, not priced-in noise -- domestic NIM down 18bp for FY26 and 21bp YoY in Q4 alone, in a competitive deposit market, and management's own FY27 guidance is for NIM to merely stabilize 'around 3%,' not recover. Revenue growth has decelerated for three straight years (roughly 25% to 11.6% to 5.0%), and FY26's still-strong 12.88% standalone profit growth leaned partly on a falling credit cost (0.37%, likely near a cyclical floor) and a lower Q4 tax rate (23% versus 26% a year earlier) -- tailwinds that cushioned this year's EPS but have limited room to repeat, while consolidated profit growth (+7.6%) already lagged the standalone bank because subsidiaries had a softer year. Funding the loan-deposit growth gap (16.9% versus 11.0%) required a sharp increase in borrowings, and the structural PSU/agency-cost discount plus periodic government stake-sale supply (57.4% to about 55.5% over the past year, likely continuing toward the minimum-public-shareholding deadline) can keep capping the re-rating even while the underlying numbers stay clean.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

[INFERENCE/OPINION] A cash-flow reverse-DCF doesn't fit a deposit-funded lender any better here than for any other bank -- free cash flow is structurally consumed by balance-sheet growth -- so the cleaner lens is the ROE-implied justified P/B. At about 1.6x book against an 18.57% standalone FY26 ROE (15.4% consolidated), a simple justified-multiple check -- (ROE minus growth) over (cost of equity minus growth), e.g. 16% ROE, 13% cost of equity, 10% terminal growth -- implies a 'fair' multiple closer to 2.0x book. The market's implicit assumption embedded in the current 1.6x is roughly 'NIM stays under pressure and the PSU discount persists indefinitely' -- a defensible base case given genuine, ongoing margin compression, but probably somewhat too conservative given multi-decade-best asset quality, comfortable capital headroom, and the imminent AMC-listing SOTP catalyst that should provide a market-clearing valuation reference for a subsidiary currently buried in the consolidated numbers. Net: cheap for real, identifiable reasons (cyclical NIM plus structural PSU discount), not for a hidden solvency or credit-quality reason -- a genuine value opportunity rather than a value trap, though not one where I'd underwrite a fast re-rating absent a clearer NIM inflection.

## Detailed rationale

SBI's balance sheet is in genuinely good health, not superficially good health. Whole-bank advances grew 16.87% YoY in FY26 to about Rs 48.8-49.3 lakh crore, broad-based across SME (+21.0% to Rs 6.12 lakh crore), agri (+19.7% to Rs 4.17 lakh crore), corporate (+14.8% to Rs 14.25 lakh crore) and home loans (the largest single book at Rs 9.44 lakh crore, +13.7%), against deposit growth of 11.0% to roughly Rs 59.8 lakh crore -- pushing the credit-deposit ratio up to about 82% and requiring a sharp step-up in borrowings (+27% YoY per the balance sheet) to fund the gap. That funding-mix shift is worth watching over the next few years but isn't stretched yet for a bank with SBI's CASA franchise. Asset quality is the standout, not a caveat: GNPA improved to 1.49% from 1.82% and NNPA to 0.39% from 0.47% YoY, both multi-decade lows, fresh slippage ratio was just 0.54%, and -- the more forward-looking number -- the SMA (special-mention-account) book sits at only 7bps of loans, among the lowest in the industry, so the clean headline NPA ratio isn't masking a building pipeline. Coverage is conservative rather than aggressive: PCR of 74.4% (92.0% including technical write-offs), plus a separately disclosed Rs 29,713 crore of additional non-NPA provisions equal to roughly 158% of net NPAs. Capital is not a constraint: CRAR of 15.40% with CET-1 at 12.29% (March 2026) sits comfortably above the regulatory floor (roughly 11.5-12% all-in for a bank carrying SBI's D-SIB surcharge) -- the government's stake easing from 57.4% to about 55.5% over the past year reads as routine minimum-public-shareholding compliance, not a distress capital raise.

The growth deceleration the quant layer flagged -- 13.6% 3-year revenue CAGR versus just 5.0% in the latest year -- is real, and it is a margin story, not a one-off or an easy-base-effect story. Revenue (interest plus other income) growth has decelerated for three straight years -- roughly +25% in FY24, +11.6% in FY25, +5.0% in FY26 -- tracking the rate cycle turning: SBI's large EBLR/repo-linked loan book repriced down faster than deposit costs could follow as rates were cut, compressing domestic NIM by 18bp for the full year to 3.03% (cost of deposits ran 5.04-5.21% through the year), with the pain concentrated in Q4 (domestic NIM down 21bp YoY to 2.93%; Q3 alone was still a healthy 3.36%) -- the stock actually sold off on results day (reports range from -4% to -7%) specifically on the NIM miss despite a record profit print. What kept the bottom line growing while the top line decelerated was the asset-quality tailwind: standalone PAT still grew 12.88% to a record Rs 80,032 crore because credit cost fell to just 0.37% and the effective tax rate ticked down (23% in Q4FY26 versus 26% a year earlier) -- real profit, but partly flattered by two levers close to their floor that can't keep improving at the same rate. Worth flagging separately: consolidated PAT (Rs 86,666 crore, +7.6%) grew slower than the standalone bank number, meaning the insurance/AMC/cards subsidiaries had a softer year and diluted the group print. Management's own FY27 guidance -- NIM 'around 3%' (stabilizing, not recovering) and credit growth of 13-15% -- is the honest read: don't expect margin compression to reverse, but don't expect it to worsen either; growth from here should track loan-book volume more than yield expansion.

At a current price near Rs 1,045, SBI trades around 11.9x trailing consolidated EPS and about 1.6x book (Rs 646 book value) against an 18.57% standalone FY26 ROE (15.4% consolidated, diluted by the lower-return subsidiary mix). A bank compounding book at mid-to-high-teens ROE would, on a simple justified-P/B basis (ROE minus growth, over cost of equity minus growth), 'deserve' something closer to 2x book at a reasonable cost of equity -- so the discount is real, and importantly it is not being driven by a hidden credit problem, per the asset-quality data above. It reads as the standard PSU/agency-cost derating plus near-term NIM caution -- and there is a live catalyst working against it right now: SBI Funds Management (SBI Mutual Fund, about Rs 12.5 lakh crore AUM, India's largest AMC) completed IPO allotment on 17 July 2026 and lists 21 July 2026, with the OFS implying a subsidiary valuation of up to Rs 1.17 lakh crore -- a market-clearing price that should sharpen sum-of-the-parts read-throughs on the parent and typically chips away at conglomerate discounts rather than widening them. Net judgment: this looks like a genuine value opportunity rather than a value trap -- cheap for identifiable cyclical (NIM) and structural (PSU discount) reasons the market can see and price, not for a credit-quality reason it is missing.
