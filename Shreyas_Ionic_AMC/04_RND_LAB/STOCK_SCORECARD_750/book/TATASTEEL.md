## Recommendation — Hold

Hold. The India franchise is genuinely firing on all cylinders -- record volumes, record 24% EBITDA margin, a funded and already-commissioned Kalinganagar ramp, and real balance-sheet deleveraging -- and this year's profit surge is corroborated across quarters rather than resting on a single exceptional item, which is a meaningfully cleaner earnings-quality picture than Tata Steel has historically delivered. But nearly all of the group's Rs 34,848 crore consolidated EBITDA currently comes from that one India segment running at a margin above its own historical average, while Europe -- the segment the market has been waiting years to see genuinely recover -- nets out to roughly breakeven, with Netherlands' improvement offset by a persistent UK loss and now threatened by a fresh Dutch regulatory closure risk at IJmuiden, plus a further delay to the UK's own EAF transition. At roughly 9x trailing EV/EBITDA and 21-22x trailing P/E, the market is not paying up for an aggressive compounding story; it reads closer to 'India's current margin holds, Europe stays a wash' -- a reasonable, not generous, assumption that leaves room for upside if Kalinganagar volumes and eventual Europe normalization both deliver, but also leaves the position exposed if India's margin mean-reverts (rising imported coking-coal costs, a still-modest safeguard duty against a genuinely oversupplied global steel market) before Europe's optionality has a chance to show up. None of this is a thesis break -- balance sheet is healthy (bs_flag GREEN, zero redflags), deleveraging is real, and growth is funded rather than promised -- so there is no case for selling an existing position. But the valuation is fair rather than cheap given how concentrated current profitability is in one cyclically-elevated segment, so this is a hold-and-monitor rather than a name to be complacent about.

## Bull case

FY26 was a genuine, broad-based operating recovery rather than a one-off-driven print: consolidated PAT of ~Rs 10,886 crore (+215.6% YoY) and EBITDA of Rs 34,848 crore were corroborated quarter-on-quarter in our internal PIT data (Q1 Rs 2,007cr, Q2 Rs 3,183cr, Q3 Rs 2,730cr, Q4 Rs 2,965cr), a cleaner earnings-quality picture than Tata Steel's history of lumpy, restructuring-charge-driven swings (as recently as Jul-Sep 2023 the company booked a Rs 6,511 crore quarterly loss on UK closure charges). India delivered record volumes (23.4 million tonnes crude steel production, 22.5 million tonnes deliveries) at a record 24% EBITDA margin, and the newly-commissioned Kalinganagar Phase-2 expansion (Rs 27,000 crore, capacity taken from 3 to 8 MTPA) provides funded, already-built volume growth that does not depend on prices staying elevated. Balance-sheet deleveraging is real, not promotional: net debt fell to Rs 80,144 crore (net debt/EBITDA 2.3x), overseas debt as a share of total debt dropped from ~50% (FY21) to 18% (FY26), and Rs 7,556 crore was prepaid from internal cash flows, materially reducing FX and refinancing risk. India also has genuine near-term pricing support from a 3-year safeguard duty on flat steel imports (through April 2028) layered on firm domestic infrastructure, housing and auto demand even as China's own steel output keeps declining. Netherlands (IJmuiden) turned solidly EBITDA-positive (EUR 267 million) for the first time in years, and valuation (~9x trailing EV/EBITDA, ROCE 17.4%) does not require aggressive assumptions to look reasonable.

## Bear case

Nearly the entire Rs 34,848 crore of consolidated FY26 EBITDA is currently India-sourced, at a 24% margin that is well above Tata Steel's own historical mid-teens average and has previously proven cyclical (the quant snapshot's own -1.6% three-year revenue CAGR is the residue of just how sharply that cycle turned in FY23-24) -- Europe nets to roughly breakeven (Netherlands' EUR 267mn EBITDA against UK's GBP -217mn loss), so the group is effectively being priced on one segment's cyclically elevated margin. On the cost side, BMI raised its 2026 metallurgical coal price forecast to $190/tonne (from $180) on strong India and China import demand -- squeezing exactly the spread India's own demand growth is helping to widen at the price end -- while India's domestic coking-coal production is falling (-10% YoY even as crude steel output rises 10% YoY), and the safeguard duty (12% tapering to 11%) is modest against a genuinely oversupplied global steel market. Europe's restructuring arc is not actually complete: UK remains structurally loss-making (GBP -217mn EBITDA, zero primary steelmaking since the 2024 blast-furnace closures) and its new Port Talbot electric-arc furnace -- meant to end that limbo -- has just been flagged for a further 6-12 month delay (already pushed past the original 2027 target) due to National Grid connection issues, extending rather than closing the UK loss period. Netherlands, the one genuine Europe bright spot, now faces a fresh threat: on 23 April 2026 Dutch regulators signaled intent to revoke IJmuiden's coke and gas plant operating permits and pull forward closure from an originally-planned 2037, after Tata Steel Netherlands paid over EUR 20 million in FY26 environmental penalties -- a real risk to the newly-profitable Netherlands business that most sell-side 'Europe is finally turning' narratives have not yet fully absorbed. FY27 capex is also stepping up 38% to Rs 20,000 crore, which will pressure free-cash-flow conversion (current fcf_yield 5.2%) even though the spend is largely legitimate India growth capex (tinplate, wire, HRPGL Tarapur, coke ovens Jamshedpur). Reported ROE of 9.9% remains unremarkable for a company posting record profits, underscoring how much capital sits tied up in the still-underperforming European asset base.

Kalinganagar's funded volume growth (3 to 8 MTPA, already commissioned) is the more durable offset, since it does not require the current margin to hold to deliver incremental EBITDA. Net judgment: fair value on a normalized view, not a bargain and not overpriced -- the multiple is neither pricing peak-cycle India margins as permanent nor pricing in meaningful Europe upside, which is the honest, cycle-adjusted read given how much of today's earnings power is concentrated in one segment currently running above its own historical norm.
