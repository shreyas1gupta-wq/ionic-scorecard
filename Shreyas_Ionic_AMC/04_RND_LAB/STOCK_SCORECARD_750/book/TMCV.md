# TMCV — Tata Motors Ltd.

> [!summary]
> TMCV (the demerged Tata Motors CV entity, listed since 12-Nov-2025) is executing well operationally -- FY26 revenue Rs 77,400 crore (+11%), EBITDA margin expanding to 13.2-13.9% (Q4), market share at 35.7% overall CV / 55.0% HCV, and Q1 FY27 volumes +27% YoY (108,488 units) -- though that print is flattered by an easy Q1 FY26 base and industry-wide FY27 growth is guided to moderate sharply to just 4-6% (ICRA)/5-6% (CRISIL) after FY26's high-base rebound. Reported valuation multiples (~45-50x trailing PE per Screener/our own snapshot) are materially overstated: FY26 PAT was depressed by ~Rs 3,700 crore of largely one-off impairment/MTM/demerger/labour-code charges, and on a clean basis the stock trades closer to 21-22x trailing earnings against a 23.8% ROE/41.5% ROCE core business -- a defensible, not stretched, multiple for the standalone franchise. The overhang is the pending EUR 3.8 billion Iveco Group acquisition (ex-defence), financed upfront by a EUR 3.875bn bridge loan against a currently net-cash balance sheet, targeted to close imminently (Q2 FY27) and explicitly guided by management as earnings-accretive only from year 2 -- implying near-term dilution risk from both interest cost and a possible equity raise. Call: Hold, with this genuinely a closer judgment call than a routine Hold given the imminent, leveraged transformation of the balance sheet and investment thesis.

## Recommendation — Hold

Hold, not Sell, and directionally aligned with the quant model's own Hold/Hold verdict, though we would flag this as a closer call than the quant score alone suggests. The case against selling: the India CV franchise itself is not expensive on a clean basis (~21-22x adjusted trailing earnings against 23.8% ROE / 41.5% ROCE, once ~Rs 3,700 crore of FY26 one-off impairment/MTM/demerger/labour-code charges are stripped out of reported PAT), market share is at or near record highs across HCV/ILMCV, volumes are still growing well ahead of a moderating industry (though flattered by an easy Q1 FY26 base), margins are expanding (10-12% trailing EBITDA margin two years ago to 13.2-13.9% now), the balance sheet today is genuinely clean (net cash, D/E 0.44x), and no governance or accounting red flag exists in the core standalone business (bs_flag GREEN, 0 redflags in our screen). The case against treating this as a comfortable, add-more Hold: the company is roughly one to two quarters from closing a EUR 3.8 billion, debt-funded acquisition of a business nearly triple its own current revenue base, financed initially by a bridge loan against a company that currently has no meaningful leverage to spare relative to that deal size, with management's own guidance implying EPS dilution in year 1 post-close and a genuine (if disputed) prospect of a further equity raise. That is a real, imminent, binary-ish change to the risk profile of this specific holding -- not a chart wobble, not a data-staleness artifact -- and it is the reason we are flagging this one for the Principal's own read rather than calling it a clean Hold. Net: hold the existing position through the Iveco close (expected Q2 FY27, i.e., very near-term), do not add fresh capital ahead of confirmed deal terms/financing mix, and treat either a materially larger-than-flagged equity dilution or a post-close consolidated leverage print meaningfully worse than management's 4-year deleveraging guide as the trigger to revisit toward Sell.

## Bull case

The core India CV franchise is compounding on every axis that matters: FY26 standalone revenue grew 11% to Rs 77,400 crore with EBITDA up 22% to Rs 10,200 crore (margin +120bps to 13.2%), and Q4 FY26 alone showed EBITDA margin expanding to 13.9% (+130bps) and PAT up 70% YoY -- genuine, multi-quarter operating leverage, not a one-off print. Market share sits at or near record highs -- 35.7% overall domestic CV, a commanding 55.0% in HCV and 39.5% in ILMCV for FY26 -- with Ashok Leyland a clear but distant #2 in M&HCV and VECV-Eicher a distant #3, and Q1 FY27 (Apr-Jun 2026) volumes accelerated further to 1,08,488 units (+27% YoY), broad-based across SCV (+36%), HCV (+22%) and buses (+23%). EV/electrified CV volumes grew 4.4x YoY in Q1 FY27 off a small base, with 3,400+ new eCV orders (including ~500 buses) booked in June 2026 and a share of the government's PM-eBus Sewa pipeline (10,000 e-buses targeted nationally, ~6,500 already contracted) -- a real, if still modest, structural tailwind. The balance sheet today is clean -- Rs 13,700 crore net cash (consolidated), D/E of 0.44x, interest coverage 8.7x -- and free cash flow grew to Rs 9,200 crore in FY26 (+Rs 2,200 crore YoY). Critically, the headline ~45-50x trailing PE (Screener, our own quant snapshot) is not the real multiple: FY26 PAT was suppressed by ~Rs 3,700 crore of largely non-recurring items (Rs 2,355 crore investment impairment, ~Rs 2,000 crore Tata Capital MTM loss, Rs 962 crore demerger costs, Rs 603 crore labour-code provision, Rs 82 crore deal costs), and on a clean/adjusted basis (PBT-before-exceptionals of Rs 8,700 crore, +46% YoY) the stock trades at roughly 21-22x earnings against a 23.8% ROE / 41.5% ROCE business -- a reasonable, not rich, multiple for the market leader in a structurally growing category. CLSA (Outperform, target near Rs 527) has also raised its long-run India CV market-share assumption for the company to 40% and projects a combined post-Iveco RoCE of 30-35% with FCF of 7-9% of revenue, framing the acquisition as synergy-positive rather than value-destructive.

## Bear case

The dominant risk to this holding is the pending EUR 3.8 billion (~$4.3-4.4bn) all-cash acquisition of Iveco Group, financed upfront by a EUR 3.875 billion syndicated bridge loan (Morgan Stanley/MUFG-led) against a company that currently has essentially no leverage headroom of that scale (net cash of only Rs 13,700 crore, D/E 0.44x) -- a debt-funded deal roughly 2.7-2.8x the size of the existing net-cash cushion, before Iveco's own liabilities are even consolidated. Management guides to refinancing the bridge within 12-18 months via a mix of long-term debt and equity (a ~EUR 1 billion rights issue/QIP has been reported by some sources, though management commentary elsewhere has been inconsistent on whether dilution is planned at all) plus proceeds from selling the 4.7% Tata Capital stake, with a four-year target to fully repay acquisition debt -- and explicitly frames the deal as earnings-accretive only 'from year 2' post-close, an implicit admission of near-term EPS dilution. Completion is targeted for Q2 FY27 -- imminent, inside the current-to-next quarter -- meaning this is not a distant, discountable risk but a live event that could crystallize before the next results cycle, still conditional on Iveco's own defence-business carve-out (sale to Leonardo S.p.A.) closing first. Separately, on the standalone India business, industry-level growth is guided to decelerate sharply in FY27 -- ICRA projects just 4-6% wholesale volume growth (M&HCV trucks only 1-3%), CRISIL similarly guides 5-6% -- after FY26's ~13% high-base rebound, so TMCV's own red-hot 27% Q1 FY27 growth (flattered by an easy Q1 FY26 base, itself down 9.2% YoY on delayed fleet replacement) is likely to normalize meaningfully through the year. FY26 reported earnings quality also needs care independent of the Iveco question: Rs 3,700 crore of impairment/MTM/demerger/labour-code charges depressed both standalone (Rs 3,400 crore, -23% YoY) and consolidated (~Rs 3,030 crore, -24% YoY) PAT, and while we believe these are largely one-off/non-recurring, the Tata Capital MTM loss in particular is a mark that could as easily reverse as recur depending on that stock's own path, and has not been independently tracked forward this session. EV volumes, while growing fast, remain a small share of the overall mix (~10% salience in SCV/pickups only, in specific months) and are not yet a P&L swing factor.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

Stripping out the ~Rs 3,700 crore of FY26 one-off impairment/MTM/demerger/labour-code charges gives a clean trailing PAT of roughly Rs 7,000-7,100 crore against a Rs 151,930 crore market cap -- a clean P/E of approximately 21-22x, not the ~45-50x the raw screener/quant-snapshot multiple implies. Against a 23.8% ROE / 41.5% ROCE core business (management's own narrower 'Auto ROCE' metric runs even higher, 72% in FY26), a 21-22x multiple at an 11-12% cost of equity reasonable for a large-cap Indian industrial implies the market is pricing something in the low-double-digit range of sustained earnings growth (roughly 10-14%) before fading to mid-single-digit terminal growth -- a bar the core India CV business, growing volumes well ahead of even a moderating industry (FY27 industry guide 4-6% per ICRA) and still expanding EBITDA margin (10-12% two years ago to 13.2-13.9% now), looks capable of clearing on its own, without crediting any Iveco upside. That is the reassuring half of the picture. The unresolved half is that this clean-multiple math says nothing about the Iveco transaction, because Iveco's ~EUR 22bn (combined) revenue base and its own debt/cost structure are not yet consolidated into TMCV's reported numbers -- the deal is financed upfront almost entirely by debt (EUR 3.875bn bridge) against a company with Rs 13,700 crore of net cash today, management concedes the combination is earnings-dilutive in year 1, and a possible ~EUR 1bn equity raise is still not confirmed either way. A genuine reverse-DCF on the POST-Iveco combined entity cannot be run responsibly today because the financing mix, closing date and first full consolidated quarter are all still pending -- so our judgment is necessarily two-layered: the standalone India CV business clears its implied growth bar comfortably at the clean multiple; the incremental Iveco-driven earnings/leverage/dilution path is a real, near-term swing factor this valuation exercise cannot yet price with confidence, and is the reason this name carries more two-sided risk than the clean multiple alone would suggest.

## Detailed rationale

TMCV is the renamed TML Commercial Vehicles Limited -- the entity that inherited Tata Motors' truck, bus and CV business when the group demerged effective 1 October 2025 (record date 14 October 2025; CV entity listed 12 November 2025 on NSE/BSE, opening at a 26-28% premium to discovered value). On the operating numbers this remains India's dominant CV franchise and is currently executing very well: FY26 (Apr-2025 to Mar-2026) standalone revenue was Rs 77,400 crore (+11% YoY) with EBITDA of Rs 10,200 crore at a 13.2% margin (+120bps YoY), and Q4 FY26 alone printed Rs 24,500 crore revenue (+22%), Rs 3,400 crore EBITDA (+35%) at a 13.9% margin (+130bps) and PAT of Rs 2,400 crore (+70% YoY) -- our own PIT quarterly series (datasets/earnings_pit/unified_quarterly_pit.parquet, symbol=TMCV, 7 quarters Sep-2024 to Mar-2026) independently corroborates the trend: consolidated sales of Rs 83,854 crore and net profit of Rs 3,028 crore sum almost exactly to the company's own reported consolidated FY26 revenue (Rs 83,855 crore) and PAT (Rs ~3,030 crore per the FY26 Integrated Annual Report). FY26 domestic market share closed at 35.7% overall CV, with HCV at an commanding 55.0%, ILMCV 39.5% and SCV 26.8% -- Ashok Leyland remains the clear #2 in M&HCV (SIAM Q4 FY26 zone data shows Tata leading all four zones), VECV-Eicher a distant #3 in M&HCV trucks (20,758 units, Q4 FY26), and Mahindra competes mainly in the LCV/SCV pickup segment (it briefly overtook Tata on a single-month CV share basis in October 2025 before Tata 're-took' the lead in November). Momentum has carried into FY27: Q1 FY27 (Apr-Jun 2026) wholesale volumes were 1,08,488 units, +27% YoY, broad-based across SCV cargo/pickup (+36%), HCV trucks (+22%), passenger carriers/buses (+23%) and ILMCV (+17%), with June 2026 alone up 35% YoY -- though this print is meaningfully flattered by an easy base: Q1 FY26 domestic volumes had fallen 9.2% YoY on delayed fleet replacement before GST-2.0 rate cuts and recovering infra-linked freight demand drove a double-digit H2 FY26 recovery (management had explicitly guided flat-to-slightly-negative H1 FY26 CV growth followed by double-digit H2, which is exactly what played out). EV/electric CV volumes grew 4.4x YoY off a small base in Q1 FY27, with electric SCV/pickup salience touching ~10% in May-June 2026, and the company disclosed 3,400+ new eCV order intake (including ~500 buses) in June 2026 alongside a slice of the government's PM-eBus Sewa programme (10,000 e-buses targeted across 116 cities, ~6,500 already contracted nationally as of early 2026 across Tata, Olectra, JBM and Ashok Leyland) -- a real but still-modest tailwind relative to ~428,000 units of FY26 wholesale volume. The order book itself is thin in the classic capital-goods sense (this is a build-to-wholesale OEM, not an EPC backlog business) but the largest disclosed single order -- 70,000 Yodha/Ultra T.7 units for deployment in Indonesia -- plus 5,000+ buses won from State Transport Undertakings through FY26 support the volume story. Set against this, industry-level growth is guided to moderate sharply in FY27: ICRA projects domestic CV wholesale growth of only 4-6% (M&HCV trucks 1-3%, LCVs 6-8%, buses 7-9%), CRISIL similarly expects 5-6% after FY26's ~13% high-base rebound, meaning Tata's own 27% Q1 FY27 print is running well ahead of the industry's full-year expectation and is likely to decelerate as the base normalizes through the year.

The single biggest fact overhanging this name, and the one that most changes the shape of the investment case, is the pending EUR 3.8 billion (~$4.3-4.4bn) acquisition of Iveco Group (ex-defence business) announced in July 2025 -- an all-cash voluntary tender offer at EUR 14.10/share for a European CV/bus/powertrain maker, combining to ~540,000 units/year and ~EUR 22 billion of pro-forma revenue split roughly Europe 50%/India 35%/Americas 15%. This is being financed upfront by a EUR 3.875 billion ($4.5bn) syndicated bridge loan (Morgan Stanley/MUFG-led, per Bloomberg, Sep-2025) against a TMCV balance sheet that currently carries Rs 13,700 crore of NET CASH (consolidated, FY26-end) and a D/E of just 0.44x (int. coverage 8.7x) per our snapshot -- i.e., a debt-funded deal roughly 2.7-2.8x the size of the company's current net-cash cushion, before Iveco's own balance sheet (which likely carries its own debt/pension obligations, not independently verified this session) is even consolidated. Management's stated plan is to refinance the bridge within 12-18 months via a mix of long-term debt and equity (reports diverge on the exact equity component -- some cite a contemplated ~EUR 1 billion rights issue/QIP, other management commentary has pushed back on the idea that dilution is planned), supplemented by proceeds from selling Tata Motors' 4.7% stake in Tata Capital, with a stated target of fully repaying the acquisition debt within four years. Regulatory approvals were described as 'nearing completion' as of mid-2026 (France/Spain financial-regulator sign-off the last pending items per one tracked source), completion is guided for Q2 FY27 -- i.e., imminent, inside the current-to-next quarter -- and closing remains conditional on Iveco's own defence-business carve-out (agreed sale to Italy's Leonardo S.p.A. at ~EUR 1.7bn EV) completing first. Management itself frames the deal as earnings-accretive only 'from year 2' post-close, which is a direct admission of near-term EPS dilution risk. Sell-side is broadly constructive -- CLSA has an Outperform rating (target has moved around, most recently cited near Rs 527, having been as high as Rs 673 in an earlier note) citing an India+EU CV upcycle, Iveco cost/revenue synergies and a raised long-run India CV market-share target of 40% (from 36%), and projects a combined RoCE of 30-35% with FCF of 7-9% of revenue -- but this is a promotional, deal-supportive sell-side view, not an independent stress-test of integration risk, and it is worth remembering Tata Motors Group's own multi-year history with a large, initially-leveraged cross-border CV/auto acquisition (JLR, 2008) took years to prove out before it became value-accretive.

On valuation, the headline numbers are genuinely misleading and this is a distinct, name-specific issue from the pre/post-demerger PE-blending problem already flagged on Siemens -- it is a one-off-exceptional-items problem, not a period-blending one. At Rs 416/share (17-Jul-2026, Trendlyne) and a market cap of Rs 151,930 crore, Screener's own PE of ~45.16x and our quant snapshot's PE of 50.1 both key off trailing reported PAT of only Rs 3,400 crore (standalone) / ~Rs 3,030 crore (consolidated) for FY26 -- but that PAT was hit by approximately Rs 3,700 crore of largely one-off items: a Rs 2,355 crore impairment provision on investments in subsidiary/associate companies, roughly Rs 2,000 crore of mark-to-market losses on the recently-listed Tata Capital stake, Rs 962 crore of demerger-related costs, a Rs 603 crore new-Labour-Code provision and Rs 82 crore of Iveco-deal-related acquisition costs (per company disclosures around the Q2/Q3 FY26 results). The company's own PBT-before-exceptional-items for FY26 was Rs 8,700 crore, +46% YoY -- strip the same ~Rs 3,700 crore back into PAT and clean/adjusted FY26 PAT is roughly Rs 7,000-7,100 crore, putting the clean trailing P/E at approximately 21-22x rather than the ~45-50x the raw screener/quant multiple implies. That is a materially different, and materially more defensible, valuation picture for a business generating a reported 23.8% ROE / 41.5% ROCE (management's own narrower 'Auto ROCE' metric, likely computed on a different core-capital-employed base excluding treasury investments, was disclosed even higher at 72% FY26 vs 61% FY25) -- but none of this clean-earnings math yet says anything about whether the Iveco integration, once consolidated, adds or destroys value, because Iveco's financials are not in TMCV's reported numbers as of this review.
