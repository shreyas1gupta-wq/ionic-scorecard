# HINDPETRO

> [!summary]
> HPCL posted a record FY26 profit (Rs 17,175 cr standalone PAT, up 133%), but almost all of that came from unusually strong refining margins ($8.79/bbl for the year, spiking to $14.27/bbl in the Mar-2026 quarter) rather than from its core fuel-retailing business, which is actually running at a loss right now because pump prices have stayed frozen even as crude oil spiked after the Feb-2026 Middle-East escalation. The stock looks statistically cheap (about 4.7x trailing earnings) but that multiple is flattered by a profit level unlikely to repeat if refining margins normalize or if the government continues holding pump prices flat against costlier crude. A large new refinery (Rajasthan, ramping through FY27) is the genuine long-term positive. Net: hold, don't chase the cheap-looking multiple, and watch the marketing-margin/crude-price gap closely.

## Bear case

The FY26 'record' profit is a refining-margin story, not a marketing one -- and the segment that has historically carried more of HPCL's earnings power, fuel marketing, is running structurally NEGATIVE margins right now because retail pump prices have stayed frozen for roughly four years even as crude spiked sharply after the Feb-2026 Iran-related Middle-East escalation; press reports peg combined OMC losses at up to Rs 2,400 cr/day at the peak, and HPCL's stock touched a 52-week low (Rs 316.20) in that window. Market commentary flags HPCL specifically as carrying the thinnest margins and highest leverage of the three OMCs, i.e. the most exposed to a sustained under-recovery cycle -- structurally the same setup that produced large sector-wide OMC losses in FY22. If refining cracks normalize back toward a $5-7/bbl mid-cycle level from the current $14+/bbl, or if crude stays elevated while the government (a political, not economic, call) keeps holding pump prices flat, the EPS this cheap multiple is built on will not repeat or could reverse hard. Layer on a large, not-yet-earning-full-return capex programme (Rajasthan refinery alone >Rs 72,900 cr, only ramping through FY27-28) and elevated leverage (D/E ~0.85-1.4 depending on basis/period), and the 'cheap' multiple is priced off a peak-cycle number rather than a sustainable one.

## Detailed rationale

HPCL is one of India's three PSU oil-marketing companies (OMCs), with an integrated refining (two wholly-owned refineries + JV stakes in HMEL and the under-construction HRRL/Rajasthan complex) and fuel/LPG marketing franchise. FY26 (year ended Mar-2026) was a record year on paper: standalone PAT of Rs 17,175 cr (+133% YoY) and consolidated PAT of Rs 18,047 cr (+168% YoY), on revenue of Rs 4,78,543 cr (+2.6% YoY). Q4 FY26 standalone PAT was Rs 4,901.5 cr (+46% YoY); consolidated Rs 6,065 cr (+77% YoY). The entire beat is a refining story: Gross Refining Margin rose to $8.79/bbl for FY26 (vs $5.74/bbl FY25) and spiked further to $14.27/bbl in Q4 FY26 (vs $8.44/bbl Q4 FY25) as refining cracks widened globally, aided by the highest-ever refinery throughput of 26.04 MMT (+3.0% YoY). Marketing volumes grew a modest 3.3% to 51.45 MMT, but marketing MARGINS on petrol/diesel turned negative during the year: retail pump prices have stayed frozen for roughly four years even as crude spiked sharply after the Feb-2026 Iran-related Middle-East escalation, and press reporting (Business Upturn, Goodreturns) pegs combined OMC (HPCL+BPCL+IOCL) losses at up to Rs 2,400 cr/day at the peak of that spike, with HPCL's stock falling to a 52-week low of Rs 316.20 in that window before recovering as crude eased back toward $90/bbl. Market commentary (Whalesbook) explicitly flags HPCL as carrying the thinnest margins and highest leverage of the three OMCs, making it the most exposed to a sustained under-recovery cycle -- structurally the same mechanism (frozen prices vs. rising crude) that produced the sector's large FY22 losses.

Capex: the ~Rs 72,900+ cr Rajasthan Refinery (HRRL, HPCL 74% / Rajasthan govt 26%, 9 MMTPA) is the single largest ongoing project, targeting a mid-cycle GRM of ~$20/bbl once ramped, with CDU crude-in expected around Oct-2026 and the petchem unit by Jan-2027; the Vizag Residue Upgradation Facility (RUF) was commissioned Jan-2026 and is guided to add $2.5-3/bbl at the refinery level from Q1 FY27. FY25 capex was ~Rs 14,500 cr, FY26 guided at Rs 13,000-14,000 cr, mostly project completion spend. Management has guided standalone debt-equity down from ~1.38 toward 1-1.1 over the next year and a longer-term target of 0.7 as the capex cycle tails off and internal cash generation improves (the scorecard's own D/E read of 0.85 is a more recent/different-basis snapshot -- both point the same direction: leveraged but de-levering). Dividend: Rs 19.25 final + Rs 5.00 interim declared for FY26.

On multiples: trailing P/E is ~4.7x (CSV pe_current=4.71, value_score=85.97/100 -- statistically the cheapest end of its own history and of the OMC pack), off a market cap of ~Rs 85,000 cr that is consistent between the scorecard snapshot (Rs 84,990 cr) and a live July-17-2026 quote (Rs 85,198 cr) -- no staleness concern. But that P/E is computed on an EPS numerator inflated by a cyclical GRM spike that is unlikely to persist at $14+/bbl, while the historically larger and steadier marketing segment is running at a structural loss under a politically-set price freeze. Quant composite: final_score_3y 41.2 / final_score_1y 51.0, both landing recommendation_overall = Hold, with weak growth_3y_score (11.8) and growth_1y_score (14.5) despite the headline PAT surge -- the model is not being fooled by the PAT print into calling this a grower, but the very high value_score is arguably reading a distorted (peak-cycle) trailing multiple as 'cheap.'
