# LLOYDSME

> [!summary]
> Lloyds Metals is turning India's largest single iron-ore mine into an integrated pellet-DRI-steel-and-copper business, and FY26/Q1FY27 volume and profit growth has been genuinely exceptional. But the stock already trades at a rich ~27x earnings for what remains a cyclical commodity business, funded by a large multi-year debt-heavy capex program whose biggest payoff (the integrated steel plant) is still years away. We rate it a Hold: strong operating momentum, but the valuation leaves little room for the iron-ore price cycle or execution timeline to disappoint.

## Recommendation — Hold

Quant composite lands at Hold (final_score_3y 68.6, final_score_1y 57.2, recommendation_overall Hold) -- strong quality/growth scores are offset by a rich value score (33.6) and a cyclical-commodity tag. The asymmetric-override rule bars converting a quant Hold to Sell; nothing here rises to that bar in the other direction either -- the business is executing a credible, well-financed volume-growth story on a world-class captive ore body, but the valuation (27x trailing PE, ~INR1.08 lakh cr market cap) is already pricing several more years of exceptional growth, and margins are cyclically flattered by a firm iron-ore pricing environment that will not persist indefinitely. Hold is the correct fundamental call: own-if-held, no fresh conviction to initiate at this multiple, no forced sell given genuine execution progress.

## Bull case

Surjagarh is a scarce, low-cost, captive asset (India's largest single iron-ore mine, licensed to 2057, long-run path to 55 MTPA) that insulates Lloyds from the ore-cost side of the cycle the way pure-play steelmakers are not; captive ore is exactly why Q4FY26 EBITDA margin hit 42.4% even as NMDC has been raising benchmark iron-ore prices. The value-chain move into pellets (capacity doubled to 8 MTPA) and DRI (guided +70% in FY27) captures margin that used to be exported as a raw-ore seller, and FY27 guidance (pellet +160%, DRI +70%, iron ore +18%) shows the capacity investments converting into volume on schedule. Congo copper (first full quarter of cathode output, 49% KMF stake) is a genuine new-commodity optionality that diversifies away from pure ferrous exposure, funded from a business already generating strong free cash flow (12.2x interest coverage) rather than a speculative bolt-on.

## Bear case

The bear case is valuation and cycle timing, not business quality: at ~27x trailing earnings and a value_score of 33.6, the market is already underwriting several more years of the current growth rate, yet a meaningful share of the 72%/155% (3yr/1yr) revenue CAGR is base-effect capacity ramp that mechanically slows once each new pellet/DRI tranche is fully utilized -- the FY27 guided growth rates (even at +160% pellet) are off a still-small base and will decelerate into FY28-29. Margins today are also cyclically flattered by a firm iron-ore pricing environment; a correction in ore/pellet prices would compress the 40%+ EBITDA margins investors are extrapolating. The ~INR32,000cr capex program (40:60 debt:equity) means another INR10-11k cr of debt layers on in FY27 alone before the integrated steel plant generates a rupee of revenue, so leverage and interest cost rise ahead of the payoff, and the flagship mine sits in a historically Naxal-affected belt (Gadchiroli/Chandrapur) that is a standing, if currently quiet, operational-continuity risk for a single-asset-dependent miner.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

A 27x trailing PE for a cyclical iron-ore/pellet/DRI business implies the market expects sustained 20%+ earnings growth for several more years at close to today's ~35-42% EBITDA margins. The FY27 guidance (pellet +160%, DRI +70%, iron ore +18%) can plausibly deliver that near-term, but this is a capacity-ramp curve, not a steady-state compounding rate -- it decelerates by construction once each plant tranche hits utilization, and the next big growth leg (the integrated steel plant) does not land until late in the FY24-29 capex window. Current margins also embed a favorable iron-ore pricing cycle that a reverse-DCF should not assume as permanent. Net: rich, not absurdly so given the genuine captive-asset quality and value-chain progress, but priced for near-perfect execution with limited cushion for an ore-price correction or capex/commissioning slippage. ROE is 16.8% (quant row), below the 20% bar that would support a hypergrowth multiple without dilution risk -- with ~40% of the INR32,000cr program funded by equity/internal accruals, some future dilution or accrual-funding strain is a reasonable watch item, though the company has not announced a specific equity raise as of this review.

## Detailed rationale

Lloyds Metals and Energy operates the Surjagarh mine in Gadchiroli/Chandrapur, Maharashtra -- India's largest single-location iron ore mine (lease valid to 2057, long-run capacity path to 55 MTPA, near-term dispatch target 26 MTPA in FY27 vs 21.96 MT actually produced in FY26, +120% YoY). The company is executing a multi-year transition from raw-ore seller to an integrated pellet-DRI-steel producer: pellet capacity doubled to 8 MTPA with the second plant commissioned, DRI kilns newly ramped, and a ~INR 32,000cr (INR320bn) capex program through FY24-29 (funded ~40:60 debt:equity) targets a slurry pipeline, transmission lines, beneficiation plant, and a fully integrated steel plant. Roughly INR13,500cr of that has been spent FY24-FY26, with a further INR10,000-11,000cr planned for FY27 standalone -- meaning the balance sheet re-levers materially before the integrated plant contributes revenue.
