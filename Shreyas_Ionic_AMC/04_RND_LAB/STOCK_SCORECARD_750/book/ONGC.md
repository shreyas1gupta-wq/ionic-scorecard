# ONGC — Oil & Natural Gas Corporation Ltd.

> [!summary]
> ONGC is India's largest state-run oil and gas producer, trading around 6x earnings with a dividend yield above 5%, but its underlying crude and gas output has been flat to declining for years and keeps missing the company's own production targets -- including its flagship deepwater gas project, whose ramp-up has been pushed out again. Government policy on royalty and gas pricing has swung back and forth even within the last two months, and roughly half of its overseas earnings sit in Russia, adding a geopolitical wrinkle to that portion of the business. The cheap valuation and strong, well-covered dividend make it a reasonable name to hold for income and balance-sheet safety, but there is not yet clear evidence of the production turnaround that would justify treating it as anything more than that.

## Recommendation — Hold

Not a Sell: the balance sheet is green (D/E 0.47, interest coverage ~7.9x), the dividend is large and well-covered (5.36% yield off a record FY26 payout), and the single-digit multiple (6.2x) already prices in the stagnant-growth, policy-buffeted reality -- there is no overvaluation or thesis-break to sell against a business generating this much cash and yield. Not more than Hold either: production has missed guidance for multiple consecutive quarters, standalone (core) profit actually fell 7.6% in FY26 despite a supportive gas-price and royalty-removal backdrop, and the one project that could change the growth story (KG-98/2) has slipped again. Real production-side value creation remains promised, not delivered, so this stays a Hold-for-yield name rather than a name to add to.

## Bull case

ONGC screens cheap on every conventional yardstick -- about 6.2x trailing consolidated earnings and 0.83x book -- with one of the higher dividend yields in the Nifty-100 (5.36%) after paying out a record Rs 16,669 crore (Rs 13.25/share) in FY26. The balance sheet is unambiguously green: debt/equity of 0.47 and interest coverage near 8x leave no solvency question. The bp technical-services tie-up, expanded in June 2026 from Mumbai High alone to the whole Western Offshore Basin, is a genuine structural lever -- it measurably moderated production decline in its first year and its fee structure now aligns bp's incentives with incremental output. Gas pricing policy has turned supportive (APM ceiling raised to $7/mmBtu, plus a fresh 20% premium on new-well gas), the windfall tax that dogged the sector since 2022 was abolished at end-2024, and consolidated FY26 profit hit a record Rs 49,793 crore (+30%) on strong contributions from HPCL, MRPL, OVL and OPaL.

## Bear case

The core upstream business is not growing -- standalone PAT fell 7.6% YoY in FY26 to Rs 32,894 crore even with royalty relief and higher gas prices helping, because volumes keep disappointing: crude and gas output both declined YoY and came in below guidance ONGC had already cut mid-year. The flagship KG-98/2 deepwater project, the one asset that could genuinely move production higher, is running well below design (oil down to ~28,000 bpd, gas at just ~3 of 10 mmscmd design capacity) and its ramp-up has been pushed out yet again to end-calendar-2026 -- a project that has disappointed on timeline repeatedly since first oil. Government policy is demonstrably unstable: royalty rates were cut in May 2026 and partially reversed within a month (dinging EPS ~1.4%), and city-gas distributors are actively lobbying to cap the very new-well gas pricing that has been propping up realizations. The much-touted consolidated profit growth is overwhelmingly a cyclical subsidiary refining-margin story (HPCL/MRPL), not an E&P production story, and can reverse as fast as it arrived. Roughly 55% of OVL's (overseas arm) production sits in Russia, with real dollars (~$350mn of dividends) still stuck behind sanctions-adjacent banking restrictions.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At ~6.2x trailing consolidated EPS and ~0.83x book, the market is pricing ONGC as a flat-to-slowly-declining state-owned upstream producer whose earnings swing on crude realizations and cyclical subsidiary refining margins, with policy risk (royalty, windfall tax history, new-well gas price caps under discussion) cutting both ways -- and with no currently proven production-growth engine, given standalone profit fell 7.6% in FY26 and the flagship KG-98/2 ramp has slipped again. That is a fair clearing multiple for the business as it exists today: not a value trap (the yield and balance sheet are real and safe), but not an undervalued turnaround either, since the discount is fully explained by stagnant-to-declining core volumes and a history of ad-hoc government policy interference. A re-rating toward the top of ONGC's historical ~4-9x range would require the KG-98/2 gas ramp to actually land on its newly guided calendar-2026-Q4 schedule, or subsidiary refining strength to prove durable rather than cyclical -- neither is the base case given the track record of delay.

## Detailed rationale

ONGC's earnings engine is crude/gas realization times volumes minus cost inflation, and FY26 shows that formula working against the standalone (core E&P) business even with a supportive price backdrop. Standalone PAT fell 7.6% YoY in FY26 to Rs 32,894 crore because production kept missing targets: crude output declined to 18.355 MMT (from 18.558 MMT) and gas to 19.533 BCM (from 19.654 BCM) -- both BELOW the guidance ONGC itself had already cut mid-year (from an original 21 MMT/21.5 BCM to roughly 20 MMT/20 BCM). Consolidated PAT of Rs 49,793 crore (+30% YoY, a record) looks much better, but that headline is overwhelmingly a subsidiary story -- HPCL, MRPL (PAT swinging from just Rs 56 crore in FY25 to Rs 1,925 crore in FY26 on refining margins), OVL and OPaL all improved -- not an E&P production story, and refining-margin cycles reverse as fast as they arrive.

The one asset that could genuinely move the production needle, the KG-98/2 deepwater project, keeps disappointing on timeline: oil output has actually slipped to ~28,000 bpd (from 30,000 bpd) and gas is running at just ~3 mmscmd against a 10 mmscmd design capacity, with the ramp now pushed to the end of calendar 2026 pending compressor/living-quarter installation -- the latest in a string of delays since first oil was declared. The bp technical-services partnership is a real, if more modest, structural positive: the Mumbai High pilot (Feb 2025) 'moderated decline' in its first year, and was expanded on 25 June 2026 to cover ONGC's entire Western Offshore Basin, with bp's fee shifting after two years to a share of incremental production revenue -- aligned incentives, but decline-rate management is not the same as growth.

Policy is a genuine two-way risk here, not a one-off. The windfall tax (in force since July 2022) was abolished in December 2024, removing a standing overhang. Gas pricing has been supportive: the APM ceiling was raised to $7/mmBtu and a fresh 20% premium was allowed on new-well/intervention gas from nomination fields. But royalty policy whipsawed within a single month in 2026 -- cut from 16.66% to 10% in May, then partially reversed back to 16.66% in June -- lifting ONGC's effective crude royalty from $7.7/bbl to $8.6/bbl (-1% standalone EBITDA, -1.4% EPS by analyst estimates), a live demonstration that the government can and does move the goalposts on this stock with no warning. City-gas distributors are separately lobbying to cap the 'new well gas' price, which has itself surged ~58% YoY to ~$12.91/mmBtu -- a risk to the realization upside the market may be underwriting. On the overseas book, OVL's Russia exposure (Sakhalin-1, 20% stake) was ~55% of OVL's FY25 production, with ~$350mn of dividends still stuck in Russian banks on an 'unfriendly nation' banking designation (though ~$550mn is reportedly moving and could flow by FY27) -- a geopolitical tail wholly disconnected from operations.

On valuation: at a share price of ~Rs 247 (17 Jul 2026), ONGC trades near 6.2x trailing consolidated EPS (~Rs 39.6, on Rs 49,793cr PAT and ~1,258cr shares) and ~0.83x book, with a 5.36% trailing dividend yield after a record Rs 16,669 crore (Rs 13.25/share) FY26 payout. A reverse-DCF read of that multiple: the market is pricing ONGC as exactly what it has been for years -- a flat-to-slowly-declining state upstream producer whose earnings swing on crude realizations and cyclical subsidiary refining margins, with policy risk cutting both ways and no durable production growth engine currently proven. That is a fair clearing price for the business as it stands, not a value trap and not a screaming bargain. A re-rating toward the top of ONGC's historical ~4-9x band would need the KG-98/2 gas ramp to actually land on the newly guided calendar-2026-Q4 schedule, or HPCL/MRPL refining strength to prove structural rather than cyclical -- neither is a base-case assumption today given the repeated timeline slippage on record.
