# IRFC — Indian Railway Finance Corporation Ltd.

> [!summary]
> IRFC is the Railways' financing arm and is about as safe as a lender gets, because it has historically lent to the Ministry of Railways at a government-fixed, wafer-thin markup. But Railways has stopped taking fresh loans from IRFC since FY2023-24, funding new trains and wagons straight from the Union Budget instead. So IRFC's future growth now depends entirely on a newer push into lending to power, renewable-energy, metro and port projects - areas where, for the first time, it carries real repayment risk and competes with older, more experienced government lenders. Profit still grew a healthy 7.8% in FY26 to a record Rs7,009 crore, but that came mostly from wider margins on the new book, not from the underlying business getting bigger - revenue was almost flat. At roughly 2x book value, the market is already paying for this diversification story to succeed smoothly, even though today's return on equity (about 13%) is actually lower than comparable government-owned infrastructure lenders that trade at similar or cheaper valuations. Add a steady drip of government stake sales (the latest 2% offer-for-sale was in June 2026, with more expected) and there isn't enough margin of safety left in the price for a business with a structurally capped lending spread.

## Recommendation — Sell

Sell rather than Hold because the price has already run ahead of a fundamentally slow-moving, spread-capped business: the reverse-DCF shows the stock priced at or above even the bullish diversification-success scenario, the only real cushion is a modest ~2.4% dividend yield, the legacy book (still the bulk of AUM) is in structural runoff, the growth engine carries first-time credit risk in a competitive field, and there is a recurring government-stake-sale overhang rather than a one-off. A Hold would require the current multiple to look fair or cheap against the current ROE - it does not; it looks rich even under generous assumptions.

## Bull case

IRFC remains one of the safest quasi-sovereign lenders in the country - zero NPAs since inception, AAA/Stable ratings from CRISIL, ICRA and CARE, and a lease/cost-plus structure with the Ministry of Railways that has never once seen a payment delay in nearly two decades. The diversification pivot is real and running ahead of its own guidance: FY26 disbursements of Rs35,067 crore beat the Rs30,000 crore target, sanctions of Rs72,949 crore give revenue visibility into the next 2-3 years of disbursement, and new-segment spreads (management points to a 100-250bps range depending on the deal) are genuinely additive to the historic 35-40bps railway margin - NIM already moved from roughly 1.3-1.4% to 1.50% in FY26, with management guiding ~1.65% for FY27. The dividend has grown to Rs2.10/share for FY26 (+31% YoY, a ~2.4% yield), and AUM is tracking toward a Rs5 lakh crore target by September 2026. For an investor who simply wants a safe, dividend-paying, government-linked lender with a credible - if early - growth story, the underlying business quality is genuinely not in question.

## Bear case

The strongest bear point is that the market is pricing IRFC as if the diversification story is already a proven, de-risked success, when it is neither. The legacy Railways book - still the overwhelming majority of the Rs4.85 lakh crore AUM - has had no new disbursements since FY2023-24 and is now a pure amortizing annuity that shrinks as a share of the balance sheet every year on current trend. Everything management is guiding to (double-digit FY27 growth, NIM to 1.65%, AUM to Rs5 lakh crore) depends on scaling, from a standing start, into segments (power, renewables, metro, ports) that are crowded with PFC, REC, IREDA and banks that have underwritten these risks for decades - IRFC has not, and its clean record on this specific book is barely two years old, too short to have seen a single credit cycle. At ~2.04x book for a ~13% ROE business - actually below the ROE of peer PSU infra-lenders trading at comparable multiples - the stock carries no real margin of safety if diversification disburses slower, prices in tighter (competed-away) spreads, or the new book takes its first credit hit. Layer on a Government of India that sold another 2% of the company in June 2026 (its 6th divestment of the fiscal year, with SEBI public-float norms all but guaranteeing more supply ahead) and the risk-reward skews to the downside from here, not the upside.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At CMP ~Rs88, IRFC trades at ~2.04x FY26 book value (Rs43.4/share) against a reported ROE of ~12.8-13%. Using a Gordon-growth justified-P/B = (ROE-g)/(CoE-g): a conservative cost of equity of ~11-12% (defensible given the AAA rating, sovereign-linked ownership, and historically near-zero volatility of the legacy railway cashflow) and realistic blended growth of 6-8% (legacy book flat-to-declining, new diversified book scaling off a small base) implies a fair multiple of roughly 1.2-1.6x book - the stock is rich by an estimated 30-50%. Even a bull-case scenario where the diversification push fully succeeds and blended ROE lifts to 15-16% within three years only justifies ~1.8-2.0x book - i.e. the current price already embeds the optimistic outcome, leaving no cushion for slower execution, spread compression from competition in power/renewables/metro lending, or the new book's first credit event.

## Detailed rationale

IRFC's business model is undergoing a structural pivot that the market has not fully priced through to earnings yet. Historically it was a pure on-lending/leasing vehicle to the Ministry of Railways at a fixed cost-plus spread of roughly 35-40bps over its own cost of borrowing - a near risk-free annuity but with a hard regulatory cap on ROE. FY26 confirms the pivot is now the whole story: Indian Railways has not drawn fresh disbursements through IRFC's legacy railway channel since FY2023-24 - rolling-stock and wagon capex is now funded directly via Gross Budgetary Support, which the Union Budget raised to roughly Rs2.77 lakh crore for FY27. That means essentially all of IRFC's growth now has to come from its 'IRFC 2.0' diversification into non-railway but railway-linked infrastructure - power generation, transmission, renewables, fertilizer plants, metro rail (a Rs13,527 crore metro loan sanctioned in May 2026) and ports - plus opportunistic refinancing of DFCCIL (Rs9,821 crore, ~Rs2,700 crore of savings) and HURL (Rs12,842 crore) debt. FY26 headline numbers: AUM at an all-time high of Rs4.85 lakh crore (management targets Rs5 lakh crore by September 2026), sanctions of Rs72,949 crore and disbursements of Rs35,067 crore (beating the Rs30,000 crore guidance), NIM improved to 1.50% (management guides ~1.65% for FY27) as the new book earns materially wider spreads than the railway book (industry commentary places new-segment margins in a 100-250bps range depending on the deal, versus 35-40bps on legacy). PAT came in at a record Rs7,009 crore (+7.8% YoY), but total income grew barely at all (Rs27,285 crore vs Rs27,153 crore, +0.5%) - almost all of the profit growth was margin-led, not balance-sheet-led, because the legacy book is amortizing down even as the new book is still small in absolute terms.

On the asset-quality/regulatory question that actually matters for a quasi-sovereign lessor: credit risk on the legacy Railways book is genuinely close to zero (sovereign counterparty, cost-plus lease structure, zero NPAs since inception) - that part of the old thesis is intact and isn't really in question. The live underwriting question is the NEW book: for the first time in its history IRFC is taking real counterparty credit risk (power/renewables/metro/port borrowers are not the sovereign), competing directly against PFC, REC, IREDA and PSU/private banks that have decades of infrastructure-lending experience IRFC does not. A clean NPA record so far is a fine starting data point, but this book is barely two years old - too early to say the underwriting holds through a full credit cycle. Governance-wise there is a structural, recurring overhang rather than a single binary event: the Government of India held 84.65% as of February 2026 and sold roughly 2% via a June 23-25, 2026 offer-for-sale (floor price Rs91, raising ~Rs2,300 crore for the exchequer - the government's 6th divestment of FY27 already). With promoter holding still near 82-83%, further OFS supply over the medium term is a near-certainty, not a one-off.

On valuation: at CMP ~Rs88, IRFC trades at ~2.04x FY26 book value (Rs43.4/share) and ~16.5x trailing earnings, for a reported ROE of ~12.8-13% - actually lower than peer PSU infra-lenders PFC/REC/IREDA (~15-20% ROE) who trade at similar-or-lower book multiples. Applying a simple Gordon-growth justified-P/B lens with a conservative cost of equity (~11-12%, reasonable given the AAA rating and historically near-zero volatility of the legacy cashflow) and realistic blended medium-term growth (6-8%, since the legacy book is flat-to-declining and the new book is scaling off a small base), the justified multiple works out to roughly 1.2-1.6x book - well below the current 2.04x. Even a bull-case where diversification fully succeeds and blended ROE lifts to 15-16% over three years only justifies ~1.8-2.0x - i.e. today's price already assumes the optimistic outcome, with little room for slower disbursement, competed-away spreads, or the new book's first credit event.
