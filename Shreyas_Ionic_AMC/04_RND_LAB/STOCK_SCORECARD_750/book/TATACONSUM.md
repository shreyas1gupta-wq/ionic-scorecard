# TATACONSUM — Tata Consumer Products Ltd.

> [!summary]
> Tata Consumer's FY26 numbers look strong on the surface - 15% revenue growth, margins up, profit up over 20% - but much of that momentum is coming from smaller, newer businesses (Sampann foods, Capital Foods, Organic India, and a Starbucks joint venture that is finally narrowing its losses) rather than the core tea business, where volumes grew just 4% last quarter with no real pricing power, and the UK Tetley business is shrinking. The stock trades at a rich ~70 times earnings for a business earning only about 7-7.5% on its equity, well below peers like HUL and ITC - the market is betting on margins and returns improving substantially over the next few years. We rate it a Hold: the improvement is real but not yet proven at scale, and the next quarterly result (due 24 July 2026) is an important test of whether that improvement continues.

## Recommendation — Hold

Hold, not Sell: the operating trend this cycle is genuinely improving (margin expansion delivered not just promised, Starbucks JV losses shrinking fast, growth-business mix scaling), so there is no dated catalyst or thesis break to justify exiting today - a Sell call here would be a pure valuation call, not a catalyst-driven one, and the next print (Q1 FY27, 24 July 2026) hasn't happened yet to confirm or deny the FY27 margin/volume guidance. Not a Buy/Accumulate either (vocabulary aside) because valuation offers no margin of safety - ~70x earnings against 7.3-7.5% ROE - and the two largest, most mature pieces of the portfolio (core India tea, UK Tetley) are flat-to-declining in real terms with no current pricing power.

## Bull case

The growth engine is genuinely diversifying away from commoditized tea. Salt has now posted five straight quarters of double-digit, largely volume-led growth with a premiumization angle (Lo-Sodium Rock salt). Sampann-led foods grew 69% in Q4 off a low base and is pushing into adjacent categories like protein snacking. The combined 'growth businesses' bucket - Sampann, Capital Foods, Organic India, NourishCo - is now 31% of the India business and compounding at 24%, a real structural shift in revenue mix away from legacy tea and salt. Internationally, the US Eight O'Clock coffee franchise is taking share at roughly twice the category growth rate. The long-loss-making Starbucks India JV cut its loss by 64% to Rs49.5cr on a third consecutive quarter of positive same-store sales as store count crossed 500 - early evidence the format is approaching unit-economics breakeven. Management guided 50-75bps of FY27 EBITDA margin expansion on benign tea costs, and the just-reported Q4 already delivered 111bps of YoY margin gain, which is at least directionally consistent with that guidance being met rather than just promised.

## Bear case

The valuation is the central problem and it is not a minor one. At ~70x trailing earnings and ~42x EV/EBITDA, the market is pricing Tata Consumer like a best-in-class compounder, yet ROE has sat at just 7.3-7.5% for three years running - roughly a third of HUL's and a quarter of ITC's - and consolidated operating margin has been stuck in a narrow 13-15% band for FOUR consecutive fiscal years despite two acquisitions (Capital Foods, Organic India) that management explicitly called margin-accretive and that are now fully folded in. That is the strongest bear point: the margin/ROE re-rating story has been told repeatedly and has not yet actually shown up in the consolidated numbers, only in individual segment growth rates. Layered onto that, the core India tea business - still the single largest revenue base - is growing volumes at only 4% with zero net pricing power right now (input-cost relief is being passed straight through to consumers rather than captured as margin), and the UK Tetley business is shrinking outright (-4% revenue, category volume decline, Tetley share loss, cost-of-living-driven downtrading). The two largest, most mature parts of the portfolio are flat to declining in real terms. And the cost tailwind the FY27 guidance leans on is not obviously secure: Indian tea auction prices in Jan-Feb 2026 were still running 6-9% above year-ago levels on crop-failure-driven shortages even as management calls the FY27 cost outlook 'benign' - if that reverses, the pass-through dynamic could flip into a genuine margin hit instead of the expansion currently priced into the multiple.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At ~70x trailing earnings / ~42x EV/EBITDA against a 7.3-7.5% ROE, the current multiple only clears if Tata Consumer delivers sustained double-digit revenue growth AND expands EBITDA margin from today's 14% toward its own 17%+ medium-term target (and eventually a stated 20% aspiration) AND sees a structural ROE recovery, sustained over several years across a still-fragmented portfolio (legacy tea with no pricing power, a shrinking UK business, three recently-integrated growth brands, and a loss-narrowing but still unprofitable Starbucks JV). The realized track record has not yet cleared that bar - margin has been range-bound 13-15% for four straight years and 5-year sales CAGR is just 11.8% - even though this quarter's data point (margin +111bps YoY, Starbucks loss -64%) moves in the right direction. Read: priced for a multi-year execution story that hasn't been demonstrated yet, not for what has actually been delivered over the last four years.

## Detailed rationale

Tata Consumer's Q1 FY27 print is not out yet - the board meets on 24 July 2026 to approve it, so this is a pre-print read on a strong-looking FY26 base. FY26 consolidated revenue was Rs20,280-20,290cr (+15% YoY), Q4 revenue Rs5,433.6cr (+17.9% YoY), Q4 EBITDA margin 14.57% (+111bps YoY), FY26 net profit Rs1,547cr (+20-22% YoY). Decomposed by volume vs price, the picture is more mixed than the headline: core India tea volumes grew just 4% in Q4 with tea revenue roughly flat as lower input costs were passed straight through to consumers - i.e. zero net pricing power right now. The oft-quoted '16% India volume growth' blends this large, slow-growing tea base with much smaller, faster-growing pieces: Salt +12% (5th consecutive double-digit quarter, volume-led plus premiumization via the new Lo-Sodium Rock salt), Foods +21% (Tata Sampann +69% off a small base, now entering protein snacking), and the combined 'growth businesses' bucket (Sampann + Capital Foods + Organic India + NourishCo) crossing Rs4,000cr and 31% of India revenue at +24% for FY26. The Starbucks India JV loss narrowed 64% to Rs49.5cr (from Rs135.7cr) on a third straight quarter of positive same-store sales and 502 stores across 80 cities - a real turnaround signal, though still loss-making.

Valuation is rich and, on standard quality screens, out of line with the underlying returns. The stock trades near Rs1,090-1,110 at roughly 70x trailing consolidated P/E (screener.in), ~42x EV/EBITDA, ~4.95x book - against an ROE of just 7.3-7.5% (3-year average) and ROCE ~9.2%. The problem is the realized track record doesn't yet support that bar: consolidated operating margin has been range-bound 13-15% for FOUR straight fiscal years (FY23 13%, FY24 15%, FY25 14%, FY26 14%, per screener.in) despite two acquisitions (Capital Foods, Organic India) that management repeatedly called margin-accretive and that are now fully consolidated. Five-year sales CAGR is just 11.8%.

Net: the business is moving in the right direction this cycle (Q4 margin +111bps YoY, Starbucks loss -64%, growth-business mix scaling to 31%), so there is no fresh, dated deterioration to hang a Sell call on today. But the valuation demands proof that hasn't shown up yet in the consolidated numbers - only in segment anecdotes. Q1 FY27 (24 July 2026) is the next real test: watch whether tea volumes reaccelerate now that FY27 tea-cost guidance turns benign, whether the growth-business bucket holds its 24%+ pace as it scales past Rs4,000cr, and whether Tetley UK stabilizes.
