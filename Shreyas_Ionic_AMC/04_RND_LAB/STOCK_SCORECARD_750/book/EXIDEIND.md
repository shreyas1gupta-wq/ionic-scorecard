# EXIDEIND

> [!summary]
> Exide's core lead-acid battery business is delivering (margins and standalone PAT both up YoY in Q3/Q4 FY26 despite a sulfur cost spike met with price hikes), but the stock's ~32x P/E is really a bet on the Exide Energy Solutions (EESL) Li-ion cell plant, which remains pre-revenue, has already slipped its commercialization timeline, just changed CEOs, and is visibly dragging consolidated (vs standalone) profit. Hold: fine core, expensive optionality, no fresh Sell trigger.

## Recommendation — Hold

V1 universe is Sell/Hold only. Core standalone lead-acid business is executing fine (Q3/Q4 FY26 standalone PAT/margins both up YoY, price hikes offsetting a real commodity (sulfur) cost shock) -- nothing here rises to a Sell on the core business. But the stock trades at ~32x trailing earnings on a business whose standalone growth is mid-single-digit and lumpy (Q2 FY26 revenue -18% YoY is the volume-cycle wobble this desk watches for), which only makes sense if the market is underwriting the EESL Li-ion optionality -- and EESL is still pre-revenue, has slipped its commercialization timeline once already, just changed CEOs, and is dragging consolidated profit (Q3 FY26 consol PAT -25.7% YoY vs standalone +5.2%) while funded off group cashflow. That is a legitimate reason to NOT upgrade conviction, but not (yet) a fundamental Sell trigger absent a further capex blowout, a funding/liquidity event, or a hard EESL delay confirmation. Internal quant score is independently Hold/Hold, so no asymmetric-override conflict arises -- Hold stands on both scoring and qualitative grounds.

## Bull case

Standalone FY26 execution is solid: Q4 FY26 revenue +9.4% YoY with EBITDA margin expanding to 11.65% (from 11.23% YoY) despite a ~5x YoY spike in sulfur prices (~Rs.150cr negative hit) that management offset with 5-6% price hikes; Q3 FY26 standalone PAT grew 5.2% YoY. No material exceptional item distorted Q4 FY26 (PBT before/after exceptional both Rs.420cr). Zero-debt balance sheet per the FY26 annual report, and EESL capex (~Rs.4,902cr of ~Rs.6,000cr planned) is being funded via internal accruals rather than fresh debt/equity dilution, preserving the core business's financial flexibility while the Li-ion optionality is built out.

## Bear case

The 32x trailing P/E prices in growth the standalone lead-acid business alone does not obviously support (FY26 quarterly revenue swung from -18% YoY in Q2 to +9.4% YoY in Q4 -- a volume cycle, not a smooth compounder), meaning the premium rests on EESL's Li-ion ramp, which just changed CEOs and has already slipped its commercialization target once (from FY25 to FY26-end); consolidated Q3 FY26 PAT fell 25.7% YoY even as standalone PAT rose, showing the EESL drag is real and recurring at the group level; gross margin still compressed 90bps QoQ in Q4 on commodity cost pass-through lag; and the Rs.6,000cr all-in EESL capex commitment (already Rs.4,902cr deployed) remains a pre-revenue bet with no disclosed EESL P&L guidance for when it turns accretive.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

[INFERENCE] At ~Rs.435/share (~Rs.35,800cr mcap) and ~32x trailing standalone EPS, the market is capitalizing far more growth than the core lead-acid business's demonstrated mid-single-digit, commodity-volatile, price-hike-dependent trajectory can justify on its own -- a reverse DCF at that multiple implies double-digit (~15%+) sustained earnings growth for many years, which the standalone segment (OEM+replacement demand, GST/monsoon-linked volumes) is not currently showing (Q2 FY26 -18% YoY is the tell). The gap is being bridged by the market pricing in EESL's Li-ion cell business as a call option on a multi-GWh EV/ESS market -- but EESL is pre-revenue, has slipped its own timeline once, just lost its CEO, and its ~Rs.6,000cr all-in capex (Rs.4,902cr spent) has no disclosed break-even date. There is limited margin of safety if that optionality is delayed further or the ramp underdelivers on cost/yield versus the 20-30% cost-reduction case management has cited.
