## Bull case

LGEINDIA is the highest-ROE, lowest-leverage large-cap name in Indian consumer durables (ROE ~32%, D/E ~0.06x, interest coverage ~59x), with genuine multi-category market leadership and local manufacturing scale that smaller/import-dependent competitors cannot easily replicate. Q4 FY26 delivered the company's highest-ever quarterly revenue (Rs 8,054 cr, +8.1% YoY), showing the GST-rate cut and seasonal AC demand can still drive real top-line acceleration, and 3-year revenue CAGR of ~7.4% is respectable for a mature-category durables business. As India's largest listed pure-play durables MNC subsidiary with a scarce free float, the stock also carries an index-inclusion/scarcity premium that could persist independent of near-term earnings.

## Bear case

The bear case is simply that earnings are shrinking while the multiple is not: FY26 net profit fell 24% YoY on flat revenue, and the two festive/pre-festive quarters (Q2 and Q3 FY26) saw PAT collapse 27% and 62% YoY respectively on margin compression from rupee depreciation and higher marketing spend — even the strong Q4 revenue quarter still posted a YoY profit decline. A 63.7x trailing PE on a business whose growth_1y_score is a weak 13.7/100 and value_score is 38.2/100 (both per the quant row) leaves very little room for disappointment, especially with Q1 FY27 not yet reported and competitive intensity (Samsung, Voltas-Beko, Daikin, Haier, Chinese entrants) unresolved. ## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

The balance sheet (ROE ~32%, D/E ~0.06x) is strong enough to argue for a scarcity/quality premium, but FY26's actual print — flat revenue, profit down 24%, two quarters down 27%/62% — does not clear the bar the multiple demands. ## Detailed rationale

LG Electronics India (LGEINDIA) is the India subsidiary of Korea's LG Electronics Inc., a market leader across refrigerators, washing machines, air conditioners, TVs and kitchen/home appliances, manufactured largely out of Noida and Pune. The parent monetized the India franchise via an IPO (Oct 2025, priced Rs 1,080-1,140/share, ~Rs 11,607 cr issue) that listed up ~50% on debut, briefly making LGEINDIA India's most valued consumer-durables company by market cap and pushing its own market cap above the Korean parent's. Parent retains a large majority stake post-IPO, so float is thin relative to headline market cap and a future OFS/lock-up unwind is a standing supply overhang. Fundamentals have decelerated sharply through FY26 even as the stock re-rated. FY24 revenue grew 7.5% YoY with profit +12.3%; FY25 revenue rose further to ~Rs 24,367 cr. But FY26 (year to Mar-2026) revenue was effectively flat at Rs 24,605 cr (+1.0% YoY) while net profit fell 24% YoY to Rs 1,685 cr from Rs 2,203 cr. The quarterly cadence inside FY26 was volatile and mostly ugly on the bottom line: Q2 FY26 (Sep-25) PAT -27.3% YoY to Rs 389 cr on margin pressure; Q3 FY26 (Dec-25) PAT -61.6% YoY to just Rs 89.67 cr on subdued appliances/air-solutions demand and a soft EBITDA margin of 4.8%, even though revenue was down only ~6.4% (Rs 4,114 cr vs Rs 4,396 cr); Q4 FY26 (Mar-26, the seasonally heaviest AC quarter) posted the company's highest-ever quarterly revenue at Rs 8,054 cr (+8.1% YoY), aided by GST-rate-cut pass-through and early summer pre-buying, but PAT was still down 8.2% YoY to Rs 692.73 cr — i.e., even the best quarter of the year could not restore YoY profit growth. Commentary attributes the margin compression to rupee depreciation on imported components and stepped-up marketing/premiumization spend, alongside competitive intensity from Samsung, Voltas-Beko, Daikin, Haier and value Chinese entrants in ACs and appliances.
