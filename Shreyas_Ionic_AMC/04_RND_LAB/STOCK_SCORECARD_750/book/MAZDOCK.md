# MAZDOCK — Mazagoan Dock Shipbuilders Ltd.

> [!summary]
> Mazagon Dock is India's leading warship and submarine PSU shipyard, debt-free and consistently profitable, but its own order book has shrunk 59% from its FY21 peak (Rs 49,700cr) to Rs 20,535cr as of March 2026 as the P-17A frigate program it was building has now been fully delivered -- leaving only about two years of confirmed revenue visibility. The stock's ~38-42x earnings multiple is betting on a large pending pipeline (the Rs 70,000-99,000 crore Project-75I submarine deal, a further Scorpene-class order) converting into signed contracts roughly on schedule; as of this research neither has been signed -- P-75I has cleared Finance Ministry approval but still awaits final government sign-off, months past management's own original target date, and Mazagon Dock has already lost one live defence tender (the Next-Generation Corvette) to a rival PSU shipyard. FY26 profit growth (6.8% for the year) was also partly helped by lower provisioning and a large slice of profit coming from interest income rather than core shipbuilding margin, which are not the same as sustainable operating improvement. Given the gap between a full core order book that is depleting and a valuation that already assumes the next mega-orders land on time, the risk-reward looks unfavourable at current levels.

## Recommendation — Sell

Sell, not Hold, because the valuation (~38-42x trailing) is priced for the full, on-schedule conversion of a still-PENDING order pipeline (P-75I, Scorpene follow-on) onto a shrinking, already-executed core order book (down 59% from FY21 peak, ~1.5x book-to-bill, roughly two years of visibility), and there is now a documented track record within this very research cycle of that conversion slipping: management's own 'sign P-75I before FY26-end' target has already been missed by 3+ months with only Finance Ministry (not CCS/final) approval secured, the Scorpene follow-on negotiations remain unstarted years after being first announced, and MDL has already lost one live mega-tender (NGC) to GRSE outright -- direct evidence the market's 'PSU incumbent wins everything' assumption embedded in the bull case is not automatic. FY26 core PAT growth was a modest 6.8%, partly flattered by a ~Rs 361cr favourable provisioning swing and other income running at ~38% of PBT, not a clean acceleration that would justify a re-rating premium on its own. This is not a call that the business is broken -- it is debt-free, cash-generative, dividend-paying, and holds a genuine two-year revenue runway plus real capacity investment underway -- but at this multiple, the risk/reward is asymmetric: further slippage on P-75I/Scorpene (consistent with the shipyard sector's own delivery-slippage history) has much further to fall than the stock has room to re-rate further if the orders land roughly on the timeline already priced in.

## Bull case

Mazagon Dock is a genuine strategic monopoly-adjacent asset -- India's primary conventional-submarine and stealth-frigate builder for the Navy, debt-free, consistently dividend-paying (Rs 18.12/share for FY26), with ROE/ROCE profile typical of the better-run defence PSUs and zero governance red flags. It carries a real, signed order book of Rs 20,535cr (as of 31-Mar-2026) providing roughly two years of revenue visibility, and is investing in genuine capacity expansion (a new floating dry dock at Nhava Sheva/Nhava Island, part of a Rs 5,000cr expansion, targeted operational by end-2026) that would let it execute larger future platforms if and when they are won. The pending pipeline is also genuinely large in absolute terms -- Project-75I alone (Rs 70,000-99,000cr) has already cleared Finance Ministry approval, the furthest-advanced stage such a mega-deal has reached, and a further Scorpene-class order and P-15C/next-gen-destroyer and P-17B/P-18 frigate/destroyer programs sit behind it. If P-75I and the Scorpene follow-on land in reasonably short order and at protected margins, the order book could multiply several-fold from here, which is exactly the kind of un-calendarable, gap-causing defence order-flow event this desk watches for.

## Bear case

The valuation already assumes that pending, unsigned order flow lands roughly on schedule, and the evidence so far this year cuts the other way: management's own guidance was to sign Project-75I 'before the end of FY26' (31-Mar-2026); as of this research it has cleared only Finance Ministry approval (29-May-2026) and still awaits final Cabinet Committee on Security sign-off, a slippage of three-plus months and counting on a self-set target. The Scorpene follow-on, first flagged in mid-2024, still has not entered formal negotiation as of early-2026 reporting. Mazagon Dock has already lost one live mega-tender outright -- the Next-Generation Corvette went to GRSE as L1 bidder -- directly undercutting the assumption that MDL wins every defence-shipbuilding project simply by being the incumbent PSU. Meanwhile the company's own already-signed order book has shrunk 59% from its FY21 peak as the P-17A frigate program it was building is now fully delivered, leaving roughly two years of confirmed revenue visibility and margins that already compressed in FY26 (PBT margin -190bps, PAT margin -120bps) on rising material costs. FY26's modest 6.8% core PAT growth was itself helped by a roughly Rs 361cr favourable swing in provisioning and by other income running near 38% of pre-tax profit -- earnings-quality features, not clean operating acceleration. At ~38-42x trailing earnings (peer GRSE trades similarly but is currently executing faster with expanding margins), and with a documented history of shipyard delivery and order-signing slippage in the very programs this valuation depends on, downside if P-75I or Scorpene slip again looks larger than the upside already reflected in the price.

## Detailed rationale

Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders (MDL) is India's premier warship/submarine PSU shipyard -- Kalvari-class (Scorpene) diesel-electric submarines, Nilgiri-class (P-17A) stealth frigates, Visakhapatnam-class (P-15B) destroyers -- built almost entirely for the Indian Navy. [DATA] FY26 (year ended 31-Mar-2026): total income Rs 14,145.71cr (+12.8% YoY), PAT Rs 2,578.36cr (+6.8% YoY, vs Rs 2,413.51cr FY25); Q4 FY26 alone showed PAT of Rs 674.16cr (+107.2% YoY) and revenue of Rs 3,850.39cr (+21.3% YoY), but that eye-catching quarterly YoY print is a low-base effect against a weak Q4 FY25 -- full-year core growth was a modest 6.8%, and Q4 FY26 PBT margin of 20.6% was actually the lowest of the four FY26 quarters (Q3 FY26 PBT margin was materially higher), driven by elevated other expenses. [DATA] Margins compressed FY26 vs FY25: PBT margin 24.9% (down 190bps from 26.8%), PAT margin 19.9% (down 120bps from 21.1%), material cost ratio up 670bps to 56.4% -- consistent with cost-side pressure as easier legacy-margin milestones roll off. [DATA] The order book is the central fact set: Rs 20,535cr as of 31-Mar-2026 (shipbuilding Rs 12,892cr + submarine/heavy-engineering Rs 7,643cr), down from Rs 23,758cr as of Dec-2025 and down 59% from an FY21 peak of ~Rs 49,700cr -- a five-year depletion trend, not a one-quarter blip, as the P-17A frigate program (MDL's own four-ship allocation of the seven-ship class) is now fully delivered: INS Mahendragiri, handed over 30-Apr-2026, was the fourth and last MDL-built P-17A hull, with GRSE building the remaining three for 2027 delivery. Book-to-bill on this order book is roughly 1.45-1.6x trailing revenue -- independent sell-side (ICICI Direct) itself frames this as providing only near-term (roughly two-year) visibility, materially thinner than the ~1.9x/2-2.5-year cushion the same order book offered as recently as Dec-2025.

[DATA] The bull case rests entirely on a large PENDING (not signed) pipeline, and the distinction matters: Project-75I (six AIP-fitted conventional submarines with Germany's TKMS, headline value cited variously at Rs 70,000cr-99,000cr) had contract negotiations completed and received Union Finance Ministry approval on 29-May-2026, but as of this research (19-Jul-2026) it is STILL awaiting final Cabinet Committee on Security (CCS) clearance and contract signature -- it is not signed. Management's own stated target, repeated across FY26 investor communication, was to sign P-75I 'before the end of FY26' (i.e. by 31-Mar-2026) and swell the order book to over Rs 1 lakh crore; that self-set deadline has already been missed by more than three months with no signing confirmed. The three-additional-Scorpene follow-on with France's Naval Group (~Rs 30,000-36,000cr), first publicly flagged in July 2024, was still described as pending renewed negotiation as of the Feb-2026 India-France Defence Dialogue and an Apr-2026 report -- also unsigned. The Next-Generation Corvette project (5 ships, ~Rs 13,500-33,000cr depending on source) has already been LOST: GRSE, not MDL, is the declared L1 bidder -- direct, recent evidence that MDL does not automatically win every mega-tender simply by incumbency, which undercuts the bull assumption that the full ~Rs 2.4-3 lakh crore 'pipeline' number converts to MDL by default. P-15C/next-gen-destroyer and P-17B/P-18 programs (~Rs 1,55,000cr combined) remain at an even earlier discussion stage. On the positive side of execution capacity, MDL is building a new floating dry dock at Nhava Sheva/Nhava Island (Rs 475cr dock within a broader Rs 5,000cr expansion, 37 additional acres, capacity for vessels to ~12,800 tonnes, targeted operational by end-2026) -- genuine capacity readiness for larger future platforms, though capacity without signed orders does not by itself generate revenue.

[DATA] Earnings quality also needs a discount: other income (largely interest on MDL's large cash/customer-advance balances) was ~8.8% of total income but ~37.7% of FY26 PBT -- a very large share of pre-tax profit is treasury income, not core shipbuilding margin. Separately, provisioning fell from Rs 717.42cr (FY25) to Rs 356.23cr (FY26), a roughly Rs 361cr favourable swing to PBT -- this lower-provisioning tailwind, not pure operating improvement, partly explains why profit grew at all despite the margin compression above; it is the kind of milestone/provision-driven PAT flattery this desk is charged to flag on shipyards specifically. Working-capital signals also moved the wrong way: trade receivables surged 144% YoY (Rs 1,067cr to Rs 2,606cr) even as inventory fell 44.5% and contract liabilities fell sharply -- consistent with the order book running down and collections lagging milestone billing. [DATA] Against this, the stock trades at Rs ~2,386-2,500 (market cap ~Rs 97,622cr as of 10-Jul-2026), a trailing PE of ~38-42x, near the upper end of the 30-40x band this desk already flags as characteristic of stretched defence-PSU re-ratings -- and independent commentary (Whalesbook) attributes a recent ~27% pullback from peak explicitly to 'timing of new contracts,' margin compression, and pipeline-conversion-risk concerns, not to any balance-sheet or governance issue. [INFERENCE] Peer GRSE trades at a similar ~37.5x multiple but is currently delivering materially faster core execution growth (Q3 FY26 revenue +49% YoY, margin +300bps, PAT +74%) versus MDL's Q3 FY26 revenue +15% YoY with margin -100bps -- meaning MDL is not even the best-executing name in its own sub-sector at a comparable multiple right now.
