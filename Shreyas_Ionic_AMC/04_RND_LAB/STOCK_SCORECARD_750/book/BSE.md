## Bull case

The Thursday expiry-day win is rule-driven, not a marketing win, so it is more durable than a typical share-gain story; BSE now runs a genuinely multi-engine model (Sensex/Bankex derivatives, StAR MF distribution, SME/mainboard listings, Asia Index data licensing, ICCL clearing) that diversifies it away from pure derivatives cyclicality. As a demutualized market-infrastructure institution it carries no promoter overhang and a clean, regulator-aligned governance structure, and exchanges are typically debt-light, high-margin, high-ROE businesses. NSE's approaching IPO (SEBI NOC granted Jan-26, board approval 6-Feb-26, global roadshow from 17-Jul-26, targeting a Sep-26 listing at a reported ~Rs.30,600 Cr raise) could re-rate the whole listed-exchange category rather than divert flows from BSE, since NSE's own listing does not itself take derivatives share from the Sensex/Bankex complex.

## Bear case

About 85% of revenue is transaction charges tied to derivatives volumes/volatility and to a regulator-allocated expiry slot that has already been reshuffled once in recent history and can be reshuffled again. The same SEBI that handed BSE the favorable Thursday slot has a demonstrated, multi-round track record of curbing F&O speculation (the Oct-2024 six-point package: higher lot sizes/contract value to Rs.15-20L, upfront premium collection, single weekly expiry per exchange, intraday position monitoring) explicitly aimed at the retail options turnover that is the primary input to BSE's re-rated earnings -- further tightening is a live, recurring event risk, not a one-time overhang, squarely in this desk's SEBI-derivative-regulation watch list. FY26's 59%/88% revenue/PAT growth is off a low base (FY25 derivatives revenue less than half of FY26's Rs.3,134 Cr) and is not a repeatable run-rate; next year's comps get materially harder. The trailing ~59-60x P/E already prices in continued rapid share/volume gains, and NSE's IPO could sharpen investor scrutiny of relative valuations once NSE trades publicly with full disclosure of its own economics.

## Detailed rationale

BSE Ltd (est. 1875) is India's oldest exchange, now a demutualized, listed market-infrastructure institution with no promoter group. Revenue is dominated by transaction charges (~85% of Q4FY26 revenue) from equity cash and Sensex/Bankex index-derivatives trading, plus listing fees, the BSE StAR MF distribution platform, data/index licensing (Asia Index JV with S&P) and treasury income. FY26 (year to Mar-26) was BSE's best-ever year: consolidated revenue Rs.5,148 Cr (+59% YoY) and PAT Rs.2,487 Cr (+88% YoY, vs Rs.1,322 Cr in FY25). Q4FY26 alone: revenue Rs.1,564 Cr (+85% YoY), PAT Rs.797 Cr (+61% YoY, +32-33% QoQ off Q3's Rs.602 Cr), transaction charges Rs.1,311 Cr (+114% YoY, +38% QoQ); full-year derivatives-trading revenue more than doubled to Rs.3,134 Cr. Board declared a Rs.10/share final dividend (record date 10-Jul-26). The step-change is largely structural: SEBI's single-weekly-expiry-per-exchange rule triggered a flip of expiry days around mid-2025 that handed BSE's Sensex/Bankex complex the Thursday slot vacated by NSE (which moved to Tuesday) -- Thursday was the historically highest-liquidity legacy expiry day.
