# TECHM — Tech Mahindra Ltd.

> [!summary]
> Tech Mahindra's June-quarter results confirmed its margin turnaround is real and on schedule - profitability keeps improving quarter after quarter, and the company continues to win new business at a healthy pace. But the part of the business that should be growing revenue, its telecom/communications work (its single largest client vertical), barely grew at all this quarter, and overall revenue growth remains the slowest among its large peers. The share price has already run up meaningfully in anticipation of the margin story finishing well, so at current levels an investor is paying close to full price for a turnaround that still has real execution risk left in it, with a core growth engine that has not yet turned around. We rate this a Sell - not because the company is doing badly, but because the good news already looks priced in while the main growth lever still isn't working.

## Recommendation — Sell

This is not a broken business - the margin delivery is real, repeated across 11 quarters, and deal TCV momentum is genuine revenue insurance. But the recommendation vocabulary here is Sell vs Hold, and the question that decides between them is: is there more upside left to be paid for, or has the market already paid for what remains? At ~27x trailing / ~19x forward earnings, with the single largest vertical (Communications, ~32% of revenue) barely growing and management itself flagging fresh margin headwinds (wage hikes, AI-productivity offsets, competitor pricing, a likely Q2 European-auto slowdown) right as the last ~60bps to the 15% target needs to be found, the risk/reward has turned asymmetric to the downside from here. A genuine coin-flip would have the bear and bull cases roughly balanced with no tie-breaker; here there are two tie-breakers pointing the same way - four of ten tracked brokerages (including an outright Sell and three targets 19-31% below CMP) explicitly cite valuation-normalisation risk, and our own independent quant composite already scores this 'Reduce' on both horizons. That combination, plus a core-vertical growth engine that is not delivering, tips this to Sell rather than Hold.

## Bull case

The turnaround is not a one-quarter story: margins have expanded for 11 straight quarters, hitting 14.4% EBIT margin this quarter (+330bps YoY), with management crediting disciplined delivery and cost optimisation under Project Fortius and reiterating a credible, quarter-by-quarter path to a 15% exit margin by Q4 FY27. New deal TCV of $1,078mn marks a third consecutive quarter above $1bn, giving genuine forward revenue visibility, and every industry vertical grew year-on-year this quarter, with Manufacturing (+9% QoQ on aerospace and European auto) and BFSI (+2.7% QoQ on compliance/AI-transformation spend) both contributing. The company is also positioning credibly in GenAI/agentic services through partnerships (Perplexity, Microsoft, Cisco) rather than just talking about it. At ~18.7x forward earnings with a ~3.2% dividend yield, if the last mile to 15% margin is delivered on the stated schedule, the multiple is not unreasonable for a company compounding EPS at a double-digit clip off margin recovery alone.

## Bear case

The uncomfortable fact is that Communications - historically Tech Mahindra's largest and most important vertical at roughly a third of revenue - actually shrank 1.3% sequentially this quarter and grew just 1.3% year-on-year, weighed down by Comviva seasonality and a client insourcing cloud spend; management's 'optimistic about communications as a growth driver later this year' is a promise, not a delivered result. Stripped of currency effects, overall growth was only 2.6% QoQ / 6.6% YoY in constant currency, continuing a multi-year pattern (FY26 revenue +7.22%, three-year revenue CAGR near 2%) that keeps Tech Mahindra behind large-cap IT peers on the one metric margin expansion cannot substitute for indefinitely. Management's own risk disclosure on the same call - competitor 'irrational pricing', wage-hike and AI-productivity margin pressure, and a flagged Q2 slowdown in the very European-auto book that drove this quarter's manufacturing beat - describes precisely the scenario that could stall the final stretch to 15%. The stock has already re-rated (+4.1% on results day alone, well off its 52-week low) largely in anticipation of that 15% delivery, which is why the forward P/E (~18.7x) looks so much cheaper than the trailing P/E (~27.2x): the EPS catch-up is substantially pre-priced. Four of ten brokerages tracked post-results - Goldman Sachs (outright Sell), Morgan Stanley and Jefferies (targets 19-20% below CMP, both explicitly citing valuation/de-rating risk once margins normalise) and Axis Capital (target ~31% below CMP) - read it the same way, and even a constructive house (Nuvama) argues the stock deserves a mid-tier, LTIMindtree-like multiple rather than a top-three-largecap one.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

The trailing 27.2x / forward 18.7x gap is the tell: the market has already substantially priced in the remaining ~60bps of margin gain to the 15% FY27 target flowing straight through to EPS - that is precisely why forward earnings look so much cheaper than trailing. To earn a HIGHER multiple from here over a 3-5 year view, Tech Mahindra needs a second act after Fortius completes: durable mid-to-high-single-digit constant-currency revenue growth led by its largest vertical (Communications), which is currently flat to shrinking. Peers with comparable or better trailing multiples (per Nuvama's own framing, TechM arguably merits a mid-tier/LTIMindtree-like multiple, not a top-three-largecap one) are demonstrating that growth, which TechM is not yet. Once the 15% margin is banked (management's own target: Q4 FY27), the incremental re-rating catalyst is largely spent, and the multiple has to be justified by growth alone - which is exactly the leg that's weakest today. Verdict: rich relative to what the underlying growth engine is proving out; priced for a clean finish to a turnaround whose largest vertical hasn't turned around yet.

## Detailed rationale

Tech Mahindra's Q1 FY27 (June-2026 quarter) print, reported 16 July 2026, was a genuine beat on the metric the whole thesis hinges on: margin. EBIT margin came in at 14.4% (EBIT Rs 2,264 cr), up 60bps QoQ and 330bps YoY, the 11th consecutive quarter of sequential margin expansion under the 'Project Fortius' turnaround, whose management-stated destination is 15% operating margin by Q4 FY27 (CFO Rohit Anand reiterated this cadence on the call). PAT rose 8.2% QoQ / 28.4% YoY to Rs 1,465 cr (PAT margin 9.3%, +80bps YoY), and new deal TCV was $1,078mn (+33.3% YoY), the third straight quarter above $1bn, which management is using as evidence of revenue visibility into H2. Revenue itself was the softer half of the story: $1,660mn, up only 2.2% QoQ / 6.1% YoY reported (2.6%/6.6% in constant currency); INR revenue growth of 17.7% YoY overstates organic momentum because a meaningful chunk is currency translation, not volume - FY26 full-year revenue growth of 7.22% YoY (matching our own quant-context figure) and a roughly 2% three-year revenue CAGR sit well behind large-cap peers over the same stretch.

The vertical mix explains why growth remains the weak leg. Communications (Telecom, ~32.3% of revenue, historically TechM's core and largest vertical) actually shrank 1.3% QoQ and grew a bare 1.3% YoY, dragged by Comviva seasonality and a client's cloud-spend insourcing - management professes optimism this turns into a 'growth driver' later in the year, but that is a forward promise, not a delivered number. Manufacturing was the standout (+9% QoQ) on aerospace and European auto programs, and BFSI grew a more modest 2.7% QoQ on compliance/wealth-platform/AI-transformation spend - but management itself flagged on the same call that the European auto book (the source of this quarter's manufacturing beat) may slow in Q2, and separately flagged 'irrational pricing' from competitors and margin pressure from wage hikes and AI-driven productivity shifts as live risks to the next legs of Fortius. Layered on top is the sector-wide backdrop: AI-led pricing compression and cautious discretionary IT spend are weighing on the whole tier-1 cohort this quarter, and the U.S. H-1B visa environment has tightened materially industry-wide (the six largest Indian IT employers' H-1B approvals fell ~40% YoY, from 18,469 in FY25 to 11,041 in FY26, with Tech Mahindra named among the worst-affected), against an Americas revenue exposure of roughly 40% of the book (FY26).

On valuation, the stock trades at ~27.2x trailing earnings (price ~Rs 1,573 post-result, +4.1% on the day; TTM EPS Rs 57.83) but only ~18.7x forward earnings - the wide trailing/forward gap is itself the market already pricing in the last leg of margin catch-up to 15%. Sell-side is unusually split for a beat-and-raise quarter: MOFSL (Rs 1,900), JPMorgan (Rs 1,700, Overweight), Investec (Rs 1,700), CLSA (Rs 1,634, Accumulate) and Nuvama (Buy, though explicitly capping TechM at 'LTIMindtree-like' rather than top-three-largecap multiples) are constructive, while Goldman Sachs (outright Sell), Morgan Stanley (Rs 1,270, explicit 'valuation de-rating once margins normalise' warning), Jefferies (Rs 1,260, calls the stock 'expensive at ~20x earnings') and Axis Capital (Rs 1,080, the lowest target in the set, ~31% below CMP) are not.
