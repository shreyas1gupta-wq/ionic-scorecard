# INDIGO — InterGlobe Aviation Ltd.

> [!summary]
> IndiGo remains India's dominant airline with roughly two-thirds of domestic passengers, and its underlying (adjusted) profitability held up better in the March 2026 quarter than the headline loss suggests. But the last three quarters show capacity growing faster than passenger demand, ticket yields falling, and load factors slipping, all while the overall domestic flying market itself is shrinking -- and the stock trades at a level that assumes this resolves smoothly. With engine-grounding relief only partial through FY27 and the international long-haul aircraft rollout running behind schedule, we see this as a Hold: a genuinely dominant franchise, but not one offering a margin of safety at the current price into an uncertain near-term print.

## Recommendation — Hold

This is not a Sell on moat grounds -- market share is stable-to-rising at 65.2% against a structurally weaker competitive set (Air India integration still working through its own issues, Akasa sub-scale), and the adjusted, ex-forex/exceptional profit trend (Rs.1,920.6 cr in Q4 FY26 alone) suggests the core business is not collapsing the way the headline FY26 net loss implies. But it is also not a case for fresh accumulation: three straight quarters of yield decline, capacity growing faster than demand into a domestic industry that is itself contracting (DGCA -2.9% to -4.2% YoY), an A321XLR delivery slip on the international thesis, and a market cap of ~Rs.1,97,500 cr against a GAAP loss year (trailing PE negative; ~25-26x even on the best recent adjusted-quarter annualized) leave the valuation offering little margin of safety for near-term execution risk. Absent a clear overvaluation trigger or clear moat erosion (neither is present -- share is stable, adjusted profitability is intact), Hold is the more defensible call than Sell; the July 23, 2026 Q1 FY27 print is the near-term swing factor.

## Bull case

IndiGo's domestic dominance is not eroding -- at 65.2% market share in July 2026 it is arguably more entrenched than at any point in recent years, helped by a competitive set that remains structurally weaker (Air India Group at 26.2% still mid-integration, Akasa at 5.5% sub-scale). The FY26 headline loss is substantially an artifact of one-off items -- new labour-code costs, operational disruption charges, and forex marks on dollar-denominated aircraft obligations -- rather than a core-margin collapse; strip those out and Q4 FY26 adjusted profit was Rs.1,920.6 cr for the quarter alone, a real sequential improvement from Q3's reported Rs.549 cr. Management guidance points to EBITDAR margins recovering to the mid-to-high 20s in a favorable fuel environment (low-20s through-cycle), continued Pratt & Whitney compensation cushioning the grounding drag, and a genuine multi-year growth lever in the A321XLR/international network (Delhi-Athens and Delhi-Istanbul already flying) working toward a ~40% international revenue mix. On a cash-margin (EV/EBITDAR-type) view rather than a GAAP-earnings view, the multiple looks far less stretched than the negative trailing PE implies.

## Bear case

The bear case is the operating trend, not a single bad quarter: yield has now fallen for at least three consecutive reported quarters (Rs.5.33 in Dec-2025, -1.8% YoY; Rs.5.20 in Mar-2026, -2.2% YoY), load factors have compressed from 86.9% to 84.6% to 85.8% over the same stretch, and capacity (ASK +11.2% in Q3 FY26) has been growing distinctly faster than demand (RPK +8.2%) -- textbook signs of an airline over-supplying its own market. That mismatch is happening precisely as the domestic industry pie itself contracts (DGCA: -2.9% YoY July 2026, -4.2% YoY April 2026), so IndiGo's rising share is share of a shrinking base, not proof of pricing power. The Pratt & Whitney grounding is not resolved, only guided to ease from the 40s to the 30s in aircraft-count terms by end-FY27, meaning a real slice of the fleet remains sidelined into next year. The international/widebody-adjacent growth story that underwrites much of the long-term earnings case has already slipped -- 9 of the 2026 A321XLR delivery batch are now delayed on Airbus supply-chain grounds. And all of this sits under a market cap of ~Rs.1,97,500 cr against a full-year GAAP net loss (Rs.2,393.6 cr in FY26) and a Gangwal-family promoter overhang still working through a pledged full exit by 2027 -- a valuation that is pricing in a clean recovery story precisely when the most recent operating data argues the recovery is not yet clean.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At a market cap of ~Rs.1,97,500 cr (share price ~Rs.5,220, mid-July 2026) against a full-year FY26 GAAP net loss, the market is valuing IndiGo almost entirely on a forward, normalized earnings basis -- even annualizing the best recent adjusted quarter (Q4 FY26 ex-forex/exceptional profit of Rs.1,920.6 cr, ~Rs.7,680 cr run-rate) implies roughly 25-26x adjusted forward earnings, a multiple that only a handful of global airlines sustain through a full fuel/FX/demand cycle. On an EV/EBITDAR view (using management's mid-to-high-20s EBITDAR margin guidance against ~Rs.85,000 cr revenue), the implied multiple looks closer to fair value for a company with IndiGo's market structure -- so the answer genuinely depends on which lens is used. What the current print does NOT yet show is proof that the airline can outgrow its own capacity additions: three quarters running of yield decline and load-factor compression, into a domestic market that is itself contracting, is the opposite of what a premium, duopoly-toll-booth multiple requires. Our judgment: the multiple is pricing in a smooth resolution of the grounding overhang and a successful international ramp; the near-term operating data (capacity-demand mismatch, yield erosion) argues for caution rather than confirmation, so we treat the stock as full-to-rich rather than cheap, without yet calling it a structural Sell given the still-intact market-share moat. Cycle-normalized, we anchor forward 3-5y earnings growth at 9% (below-nominal-GDP band) rather than a steady-compounder 13% -- consistent with FY26's actual 5.15% revenue growth, three straight quarters of yield erosion into a still-contracting domestic market, and only partial, multi-year relief from the P&W grounding and the delayed international ramp; this lower growth anchor is precisely why the ~25-26x adjusted multiple reads as full-to-rich rather than cheap.

## Detailed rationale

IndiGo remains India's dominant domestic carrier by a wide margin: DGCA data for July 2026 puts its domestic market share at 65.2% (82.15 lakh passengers), against Air India Group's 26.2% and Akasa's 5.5% [DATA]. Fleet stood at 441 aircraft as of March 31, 2026 (A320neo/A321neo/ATR/787/freighters) [DATA]. The moat is real and, if anything, widening even as the underlying pie shrinks -- domestic industry passenger traffic fell 2.9% YoY in July 2026 and 4.2% YoY in April 2026 per DGCA [DATA] -- meaning IndiGo is gaining share of a contracting market, a mixed signal rather than an unambiguous positive.

The last four reported quarters show a clear deceleration in earnings quality even as revenue keeps growing. Q1 FY26 (Jun-2025): PAT Rs.2,176 cr, down 20% YoY, EBITDAR margin 28.0% vs 29.7% YoY, passengers +12% YoY [DATA]. Q3 FY26 (Dec-2025): PAT collapsed 78% YoY to Rs.549 cr, hit by new labour-code costs (Rs.969.3 cr), operational disruptions (Rs.577.2 cr) and forex on dollar liabilities (Rs.1,035 cr); yield fell 1.8% YoY to Rs.5.33, load factor fell 2.4pp to 84.6%, and ASK grew 11.2% against RPK growth of only 8.2% -- capacity outrunning demand [DATA]. FY26 full year: revenue Rs.84,961.9 cr (+5.15% YoY) but a net LOSS of Rs.2,393.6 cr versus a Rs.7,258.4 cr profit in FY25 [DATA]. The pattern across three consecutive quarters is consistent: capacity (ASK) growing faster than demand (RPK), yields eroding, and load factors compressing -- a structural read, not a one-quarter blip, and it is happening while the overall domestic travel pie is itself contracting.
