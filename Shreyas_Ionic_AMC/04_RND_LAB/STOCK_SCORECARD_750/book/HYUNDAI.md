# HYUNDAI — Hyundai Motor India Ltd.

> [!summary]
> Hyundai Motor India's FY26 was a year of quiet deceleration: domestic volumes fell 2.3% and the company slipped from India's No.2 auto brand to No.4 as Mahindra and Tata's SUV-led pushes out-competed it, while full-year profit was flat-to-down (Rs.5,432 cr vs Rs.5,640 cr) with a sharp Q4 margin squeeze (EBITDA margin 10.4% vs 14.1% a year earlier). April and May 2026 opened FY27 on a genuinely stronger footing before a one-off supplier-fire disruption dented June's domestic numbers; management is responding with its highest-ever capex (Rs.7,500 crore), two new SUV launches, and continued ramp-up of the new Talegaon plant. Two structural watch-items temper the recovery case: the royalty paid to the Korean parent was permanently raised to 3.5% of revenue (from ~2.25-2.5%), a fixed drag on minority shareholders' economics, and Hyundai's electric-vehicle push (the Creta Electric) is running well below its own 15% EV-mix target at under 4% share. At roughly 29x trailing earnings -- in line with Maruti despite Maruti holding the market's top rank -- the stock is not pricing in either the competitive setback or these structural drags, so we rate it Hold: a name to monitor through the FY27 launch cycle rather than add to.

## Recommendation — Hold

We rate Hold, not Sell, because FY26 reads as deceleration-with-a-recovery-plan rather than structural collapse: April-May FY27 showed genuine double-digit-adjacent domestic growth before a one-off June fire disruption, exports are compounding at 21%+ YoY, and management has committed its highest-ever capex (Rs.7,500 cr) against two concrete FY27 SUV launches and a credible guide (8-10% volume, 11-14% margin) that would mark a real improvement over the Q4 FY26 trough. We stop short of any upgrade bias (moot given our Sell/Hold-only vocabulary) because at ~29.3x trailing earnings -- roughly at parity with Maruti's ~28.3x despite Maruti holding the #1 domestic rank while Hyundai was just demoted to #4 -- the multiple is not obviously discounting the competitive share loss, the EV-execution miss, or the now-structural 3.5% royalty drag on margins. This is a name to hold through the FY27 launch cycle and re-underwrite on the Q1 FY27 print and the two new-SUV reception, not one we would add to on the current setup.

## Bull case

Hyundai still has real, credible levers for FY27: April domestic sales hit a record 51,902 units (+17% YoY) and May added 9.1% growth, exports are compounding at over 20% YoY and now approach a quarter of total volume, the Creta reclaimed its crown as India's best-selling SUV with 200,000+ units sold in CY2025, and rural sales hit a record 23.6% share in Q2 FY26 -- genuine distribution deepening rather than a one-quarter blip. Management has committed Rs.7,500 crore of FY27 capex (its highest ever), split between two new SUV launches (a mid-size ICE model and a localized compact EV) and capacity expansion, on top of the newly operational Talegaon plant (170,000 units, expanding to 250,000 by 2028 on an upsized Rs.11,000 crore investment) that removes any capacity constraint on the 26-launch, 7-new-nameplate roadmap through FY30. Guidance of 8-10% volume growth and 11-14% EBITDA margin for FY27 would, if delivered, mark a real recovery from the Q4 FY26 trough, and the June sales dip has an identified, temporary cause (a supplier-plant fire) with normalcy already restored by June 22.

## Bear case

The harder-to-explain-away fact is that Hyundai has been structurally out-competed at home: it fell from India's No.2 to No.4 domestic PV brand in FY26 (12.49% share, down from 17.6% in FY20) as Mahindra's all-SUV portfolio (660,276 units, 14.1% share) and Tata Motors both passed it, and its own signature SUV mix slipped from 69% to 68% for the year -- falling sharply to 64% in Q4 FY26 alone, exactly when the competitive SUV mix was rising. Its EV strategy, meant to be the next leg of growth, is badly missing its own target: 3.9% EV share against a stated 15% goal, with Creta Electric monthly volumes actually declining through 2025 as its ~30% price premium pushes most EV shoppers back to the ICE model. Layered on top is a governance-linked structural drag: the royalty to the Korean parent was raised from 2.25-2.5% to a flat 3.5% of revenue (with SEBI headroom to go to 5%), following a Rs.10,782 crore pre-IPO special dividend that had already drained the company's cash balance by nearly half -- a sequence that converted Indian retained earnings into parent cash flow and now taxes every rupee of Indian revenue at a permanently higher rate, directly capping the margin recovery the FY27 guidance is counting on. Q4 FY26's 22% profit decline and ~370bps margin contraction show this is not a hypothetical risk; it is already visible in the P&L.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At ~29.3x trailing consolidated earnings, the market is pricing Hyundai as if it were a durable double-digit compounder -- yet FY26 consolidated PAT actually fell ~3.7% YoY, Q4 FY26 profit fell 22%, and the company's own FY27 guidance tops out at 8-10% volume growth with an 11-14% margin band that only gets back to roughly where FY26 started. For that multiple to be earned rather than merely tolerated, Hyundai needs to simultaneously reclaim SUV-mix momentum lost to Mahindra/Tata, convert its FY27 SUV/EV launch slate into real share gains against a now-entrenched competitive set, and grow margins despite a structurally higher 3.5% royalty outflow to Korea -- three things that must all go right together. Maruti Suzuki trades at a near-identical ~28.3x while holding the #1 domestic rank outright and improving its own SUV mix; on a relative basis we do not see why Hyundai should command parity, let alone the valuation premium some sell-side notes argued for back in 2024, before the FY26 share losses became visible. Our read: fair-to-slightly-rich, not attractively cheap and not egregiously overvalued -- consistent with a Hold rather than a Sell.

## Detailed rationale

Hyundai Motor India closed FY26 with domestic wholesales of 584,906 units, down 2.3% YoY, sliding to the No.4 spot in India's PV market (12.49% share, down from 17.6% in FY20) as Mahindra (660,276 units, 14.1% share, now officially India's No.2 brand) and Tata Motors both overtook it -- the first time in years Hyundai has fallen outside the domestic top three. SUV mix, the company's traditional strength, also slipped -- 68% in FY26 vs 69% in FY25, and more sharply in Q4 FY26 alone (64% vs 69% YoY) -- exactly as Mahindra's all-SUV portfolio and Tata's SUV push gained share. FY27 opened on a stronger note: April domestic sales hit a record 51,902 units (+17% YoY), May added 47,837 units (+9.1% YoY), but June domestic sales fell to 39,635 units (-9.97% YoY, -17.15% MoM) -- company-attributed to a ~13,900-unit production loss from a fire at a key supplier's plant, with normalcy restored by June 22 and the shortfall expected to be recovered within Q2 FY27. We read the June dip as supply-side and transient rather than a demand signal, consistent with April-May's genuine YoY strength. Exports (+21% YoY in Q3 FY26, now roughly a quarter of total volume) are the more reliable growth leg while domestic share is contested. Financially, full-year FY26 consolidated PAT was Rs.5,431.52 cr versus Rs.5,640.21 cr in FY25 (down ~3.7%) on revenue of Rs.70,763.33 cr (+2.3%) -- flat-to-down profit growth despite modest top-line growth. Q4 FY26 alone showed the compression clearly: PAT fell 22.2% YoY to Rs.1,255.63 cr, EBITDA fell 22% to Rs.1,966 cr, and EBITDA margin contracted ~370bps to 10.4% from 14.1%. This followed a stronger Q3 FY26 (Dec-25 quarter) in which PAT rose 6.35% YoY to Rs.1,234.4 cr, EBITDA rose 7.6%, domestic wholesales were up 5% sequentially on GST 2.0 and festive demand, and Creta reclaimed its crown as India's best-selling SUV (200,000+ units in CY2025) -- so the Q4 relapse is a genuine data point, not simply a one-quarter blip on a rising trend. FY27 guidance (8-10% volume growth, 11-14% EBITDA margin, ~Rs.7,500 cr capex -- the highest in recent years, ~45-50% on new products and the rest on capacity) is essentially guiding back toward the better end of the FY26 range, contingent on execution of two new launches (a mid-size ICE SUV and a localized compact EV) and the Talegaon ramp. The single most important minority-shareholder issue is the royalty paid to the Korean parent: raised from a negotiated 2.25-2.5% of revenue to a flat 3.5%, with SEBI-sanctioned headroom to 5% without further shareholder consent -- a change that, alongside the pre-IPO Rs.10,782 cr special dividend (March 2024, ~7.2x the FY22 payout, draining cash from Rs.17,741 cr to Rs.9,017 cr) and the IPO offer-for-sale (17.5% of the company sold at ~26x P/E against the parent's own ~5x listed multiple in Korea), permanently redirects a larger share of Indian retained earnings to Seoul. This is a structural governance drag that caps margin upside from any volume/mix recovery. Compounding this, Hyundai's own stated EV ambition (15% EV mix target) is badly lagging reality: EV market share sits at just 3.9%, and the Creta Electric's monthly run-rate has fallen (from ~850 units in March 2025 to ~512 by June 2025) as its ~30% price premium to mainstream competing EVs (Tata Nexon EV, MG Windsor) pushes an estimated 65-70% of EV inquiries back to the ICE Creta at the dealership counter. Talegaon (170,000-unit capacity, operational since October 1, 2025, expanding to 250,000 units by 2028 on an enhanced Rs.11,000 cr investment, up from Rs.7,000 cr originally) gives Hyundai capacity headroom to execute its FY27-30 roadmap (26 launches by FY30, 7 new nameplates) -- but capacity was never the binding constraint; competitive product cadence and pricing were.
