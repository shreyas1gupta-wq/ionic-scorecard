## Recommendation — Hold

This is an existing holding, and the fundamentals argue for continuing to hold rather than exiting, but without much margin of comfort. The deposit franchise, MFI credit-cost normalization and capital position (post Warburg/ADIA) are all genuinely improving and independently verifiable in the data, not just guided -- that rules out an active Sell on asset-quality or capital grounds. What keeps this from being a comfortable Hold is the combination of a still-elevated cost-to-income ratio (72.3%), a tighter funding position (95.5% CD ratio) than most peers, a genuinely messy earnings-quality quarter (fraud + tax-credit noise in Q4 FY26), and a meaningful gap between management's mid-teens ROE promise and the Street's much more conservative 9-12% base case -- a gap this bank has struggled to close on schedule before (MFI stress pushed out the FY25 recovery narrative; the Chandigarh fraud did the same to Q4 FY26). What would change my mind toward Sell: a Q1 FY27 print (due 25-Jul-2026) that shows credit cost re-accelerating rather than continuing its quarterly decline, or cost-to-income failing to move meaningfully below 70%, would suggest the ROE bridge is stalling rather than progressing.

## Bull case

The bull case: the deposit franchise keeps getting genuinely better, not just bigger -- CASA at 50.8% and rising, retail deposits at 79% of the base versus 27% at merger, and 32% CAGR deposit growth since 2018, which is a durable, structural asset for a bank of this size. The MFI/credit-cost drag that dominated FY25-26 is on a visible, verifiable downtrend (2.69% to 1.63% credit cost across the four quarters of FY26, MFI book down to 2.3% of loans) rather than a promise still to be delivered, and GNPA/NNPA are improving in the same direction. The balance sheet is now better capitalized than it has been in years: the completed Rs 7,500 crore Warburg Pincus/ADIA infusion lifts pro forma CRAR to ~18.9%, removing a capital-constraint overhang just as loan growth continues at a strong 20%+ YoY clip, and there is no near-term CEO-reappointment binary to add event risk (Vaidyanathan's RBI-approved term runs to Dec-2027).

## Bear case

The bear case: the deposit franchise has been expensive to build -- cost-to-income is stuck at 72.3%, roughly double what the large private banks run at, and UBS doesn't see it falling much below 67% even by FY27 -- so the operating-leverage half of the ROE bridge is the slower-moving half. Funding is tighter than most peers (CD ratio 95.5%, up from 93.4% YoY), leaving less headroom to keep funding loan growth from deposits alone. Earnings quality needs care: Q4 FY26's headline PAT was a net of a Rs 483 crore post-tax fraud hit and a Rs 130 crore tax credit, and pre-provision operating profit actually fell 41.6% YoY -- a reminder that a branch-level fraud event, even one confirmed isolated by KPMG, is exactly the kind of governance/control-environment flag that recurs as a bank scales branches fast. Most importantly, there is a real, quantifiable gap between management's guided mid-teens ROE and the Street's 9-12% consensus (UBS is at Sell, target Rs 75, below CMP) -- and this bank has a recent history of guided recoveries getting pushed out by exactly this kind of event (MFI stress, then the Chandigarh fraud).

## Detailed rationale

IDFC First's deposit-franchise build is genuinely one of the better retail-liability stories in the sector, and it shows up in numbers that keep improving quarter on quarter rather than plateauing. CASA ratio reached 50.8% as of the Q1 FY27 provisional update (30-Jun-2026), up from 49.8% the prior quarter and 48.0% a year earlier, on deposits of Rs 3,11,874 crore (+17.7% YoY, +5.9% QoQ) with CASA deposits themselves up 24.7% YoY. Retail deposits have gone from 27% of the book at the 2018 ICICI-Bank-style merger to roughly 79% now, and total customer deposits have compounded at a 32% CAGR since merger (CASA deposits at a 58% CAGR from a tiny Rs 5,274 crore base). But this franchise was not built for free: cost-to-income ratio for FY26 was 72.3% -- down a real 987bps from 82.2% at merger, but still far above the high-30s/low-40s% that HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank or Axis Bank run at, and UBS models cost-to-income staying elevated near 67% even by FY27. The other side of the funding equation is more stretched than SBIN or the large privates: credit-deposit ratio was 95.5% in Q1 FY27, up from 93.4% a year ago, as loan growth (20.6% YoY to Rs 3,05,488 crore) continues to slightly outrun even a fast-growing deposit base -- there is real funding tightness here, not SBIN-style comfortable headroom. On asset quality, the trend is improving but not yet best-in-class: GNPA eased to 1.61% in Q4 FY26 from 1.69% in Q3, NNPA to 0.48% from 0.53%. The more important number is the microfinance drag that dominated FY25-26 commentary: the JLG/MFI book has been worked down to about Rs 6,662 crore, roughly 2.3% of a ~Rs 2.9 lakh crore total loan book, and credit cost has fallen every single quarter through FY26 -- 2.69% (Q1), 2.24% (Q2), 2.05% (Q3), 1.63% (Q4), for a 2.13% full-year figure -- with a Rs 130 crore MFI contingency buffer still carried into FY27 as extra cushion. This is a genuine, verifiable normalization path, not just a management assurance -- management's own characterization that the industry-wide MFI stress of FY25-26 is 'behind them' is consistent with what the credit-cost trend line actually shows.

Earnings quality needs a careful read precisely because Q4 FY26 was unusually noisy in both directions. Reported PAT of Rs 319 crore (+4.9% YoY) looks like a modest, boring beat, but it is the net of two large, offsetting one-offs: a Rs 483 crore post-tax hit from the Chandigarh branch fraud (Rs 646 crore gross, involving employee collusion with government and school accounts; a KPMG forensic review confirmed it was isolated to one branch and not systemic), and a Rs 130 crore tax refund/credit that flattered the same quarter. Strip both out and normalized PAT was Rs 746 crore, up 145% YoY -- while pre-provision operating profit actually fell 41.6% YoY to Rs 1,059 crore from Rs 1,812 crore. None of the three numbers (319 / 746 / -41.6% PPOP) tells the whole story alone; the honest read is that underlying core profitability is recovering meaningfully as MFI credit costs normalize, but the reported headline is not a clean read-through and a one-off, branch-level fraud event is exactly the kind of governance overhang this sector-note flags for banks scaling branch networks fast. Separately, the balance sheet was de-risked on the capital side during FY26: Warburg Pincus (via Currant Sea Investments B.V., Rs 4,876 crore for ~9.48% post-money) and ADIA (via Platinum Invictus, Rs 2,624 crore for ~5.1%) completed a combined Rs 7,500 crore preferential CCPS infusion at Rs 60/share, approved by RBI and CCI, lifting pro forma CRAR from 16.1% to ~18.9%. Shareholders approved the raise itself overwhelmingly (99.18% in favour) but rejected Warburg's request for a board seat (64.1% versus the 75% threshold needed) -- a minor but real governance friction signal, not a capital problem. CEO V Vaidyanathan's RBI-approved reappointment runs to 18-Dec-2027, so there is no near-term CEO-approval binary stacking risk of the kind flagged for HDFCBANK.

On management's ROE promise: Vaidyanathan has publicly guided to a mid-teens ROE as achievable, with 15% framed as a 'first milestone' and 16-18% 'core ROE' once cost-to-income normalizes, positioning FY27-29 as the phase where the bank becomes 'stable and predictable.' The Street is considerably more conservative -- UBS models roughly 9% ROE / 1% RoA by FY27 (Sell rating, cost-income still ~67%), Nomura around 11.8% ROE by FY28, ICICI Direct about 10.8% for FY28E (downgraded to Hold in Oct-2025 after a quarter where PAT fell 58% YoY), and other estimates cluster around 10.7-11.2% three years out. Against a current ROE of roughly 3.8-6.5% (depending on the trailing window used across data sources) and a price near Rs 80.4 (about 1.4-1.5x book, ~42-43x trailing earnings), this is a real, quantifiable credibility gap between the management narrative and the consensus base case -- not a hidden problem, but a legitimate reason a reasonable analyst could land on either side of Hold versus Sell.
