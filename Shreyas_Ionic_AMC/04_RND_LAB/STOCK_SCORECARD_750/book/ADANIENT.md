# ADANIENT — Adani Enterprises Ltd.

> [!summary]
> Adani Enterprises is transforming from a trading/incubator conglomerate into an airports-roads-new energy-copper infrastructure platform, and that pivot is real -- about 80% of FY26 operating profit now comes from more mature, contracted businesses. But this year's headline 31% profit growth was almost entirely a one-time gain from exiting the Adani Wilmar edible-oils joint venture, not from the underlying business: the most recent quarter, stripped of that gain, actually posted a loss, hurt by rising depreciation on newly started assets and mining setbacks in Australia. The new growth engines -- airports, the copper smelter, roads, green hydrogen, data centers -- are still ramping up and have already shown delays (the copper plant, in particular, has run well below capacity). At a lofty valuation (a price-to-earnings ratio in the 40s-plus), the market is already pricing in a smooth, multi-year execution of all of these bets simultaneously, funded by continued heavy borrowing. Add a legal overhang that, while easing (the DOJ has moved to drop the US bribery case and settlements are proposed), has not fully cleared, and the risk-reward looks unfavourable at current prices. Recommendation: Sell.

## Recommendation — Sell

The valuation (trailing consolidated P/E in the low-to-high 40s by most sources, quant-context 43.58x, with some methodologies showing 50s-60s) is being paid for a business whose current-year 'growth' was overwhelmingly a one-off (Rs 9,215 cr AWL-exit + cement-transfer gain), whose most recent standalone quarter was loss-making, whose underlying quality metrics are weak (ROE/ROCE both ~8%, quality score 35/100, interest coverage a thin ~2.3x per quant context, balance-sheet flag AMBER, D/E 1.32x with another Rs 40,000 cr of FY27 capex to fund), and whose legacy revenue base (IRM/coal, still the largest segment) is structurally declining. The bull case -- five new infra verticals maturing into a re-rated, contracted-cash-flow platform -- is directionally real and partially visible in the FY26 EBITDA mix shift, but every one of those verticals is still early-stage or has already shown execution slippage (copper's 'technical setbacks' and sub-20% utilization; green hydrogen still pre-FID; data centers/PVC pre-revenue for years). Paying ~40x+ for that combination -- rather than for a business already compounding earnings cleanly -- is the wrong risk-reward, especially with a residual (if reduced) legal/governance overhang still working through US courts and SEBI. This is not a coin-flip: the valuation-versus-earnings-quality gap is wide enough, and it matches the quant context's own 'Reduce' call on both the 1-year and 3-year horizon.

## Bull case

The infrastructure pivot is genuine and measurable: FY26 EBITDA mix has shifted to roughly 80% mature/contracted businesses (core infrastructure platform plus contracted mining services) from a 50-50 incubator split a few years ago, and there are concrete, management-quantified EBITDA triggers over FY27-28 -- Navi Mumbai Airport (peak ~Rs 3,000 cr), Ganga Expressway (peak ~Rs 1,800 cr) and Kutch Copper (peak ~Rs 2,000+ cr), a combined 'three-asset unlock' management pegs at Rs 6,000-6,800 cr of incremental peak EBITDA by FY28. New energy manufacturing (solar modules, wind turbines) is growing at healthy double-digit clips with real customer offtake (e.g., the BPCL/Samsung C&T green-ammonia agreement), and marquee partners (Google, Microsoft, Jabil) lend credibility to the data-center ambition even if it is capital-hungry. On the balance sheet, group-wide borrowing costs have fallen to 7.8% from 9% two years ago on ratings upgrades, portfolio net-debt/EBITDA at 3.3x is inside the 3.5x guardrail, liquidity covers ~17 months of debt service, and the promoter group confirmed no fresh pledge was created in FY26. On governance, the most acute tail risk -- the US DOJ criminal indictment -- is subject to a DOJ dismissal motion (pending court approval) alongside negotiated SEC and Treasury settlements, easing what had been the single largest overhang on the stock.

## Bear case

The strongest bear point is earnings quality: the reported 31% FY26 PAT growth is not organic. Back out the disclosed Rs 9,215 cr exceptional gain from the Adani Wilmar exit and cement-asset transfer, and full-year core PBT was only ~Rs 4,310 cr -- and the most recently completed quarter (Q4 FY26, with no exceptional gain in it) was an outright net loss of Rs 221 cr, versus a Rs 3,845 cr profit a year earlier, driven by depreciation on the very assets (Navi Mumbai airport, Kutch copper) the bull case leans on, plus weather and mark-to-market hits in Australian mining. In other words, on a like-for-like basis the business is currently barely profitable to loss-making, not growing at 31%. Layered on that: ROE and ROCE both sit at just ~8%, a quality score of 35/100, a balance-sheet flag of AMBER, and interest coverage of only ~2.3x by the quant context -- weak metrics for a stock carrying a 40x-plus P/E. The legacy IRM/coal-trading business, still likely the single largest revenue line, is in structural decline (3-year revenue CAGR of -7.64%, with IRM revenue down as much as 45-59% YoY in soft quarters), meaning new-vertical growth has to work overtime just to hold consolidated revenue flat (FY26 total income grew only 3%). Execution risk in the new verticals is not hypothetical -- it has already shown up: the copper smelter has had 'technical setbacks' and ran at roughly 19% utilization in its first year against a 500,000-tonne nameplate, and management itself says the flagship green-hydrogen project has not reached a final investment decision. Finally, the governance overhang is reduced but not gone -- a US federal judge was still demanding sworn answers on the DOJ's dismissal motion as of early July 2026, SEBI reportedly still has over a dozen open matters from the original Hindenburg complaint, and fresh reporting in Feb-2026 raised new undisclosed-related-party allegations. None of that is priced for a stock trading at a growth multiple.

## Detailed rationale

On the surface this reads as a successful pivot story.
