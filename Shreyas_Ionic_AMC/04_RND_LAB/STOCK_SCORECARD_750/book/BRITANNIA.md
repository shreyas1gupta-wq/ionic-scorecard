# BRITANNIA — Britannia Industries Ltd.

> [!summary]
> Britannia had a strong FY26 - profit grew faster than sales as margins improved, helped by a September 2025 GST cut that made biscuits cheaper and lifted demand, plus continued growth in newer businesses like cakes, rusk, croissants and dairy. The company also just brought in a new CEO with a strong track record in building challenger brands. However, at the current share price the stock is priced for a lot of continued good news, some of this year's profit growth came from tax and margin effects rather than pure volume growth, input costs like fuel and packaging are rising, and there is a real competitive threat from new entrants adding biscuit-manufacturing capacity. We rate this a Hold: a well-run, quality business, but not attractively priced enough here to add with confidence.

## Recommendation — Hold

Hold, not Sell, because there is no franchise-level deterioration to justify an exit call - category leadership is intact and arguably widening (38% vs Parle's 32%), the volume recovery is real and broad-based across biscuits, adjacencies and e-commerce, the GST reset is a structural tailwind for the category rather than a one-off, and a new CEO with a strong distribution/challenger-brand pedigree is now in the seat. But Sell would also be too harsh given the balance-sheet quality and cash generation on display. At the same time this is not a name to add into at current levels: the ~50-56x multiple already discounts a lot of the good news, a meaningful share of this year's PAT growth traces to tax/margin effects rather than volume, the input-cost and Middle-East logistics disruptions are unresolved, and Patanjali's new capacity plus ITC's continued push mean the competitive tailwind cannot be taken for granted. Hold reflects 'a quality business, already priced for a lot of the good news, not cheap enough here to add with conviction.'

## Bull case

The bull case is straightforward: Britannia remains the biscuit category leader (~38% share) and its FY26 numbers show the growth is coming from real places - Q3's GST-transition dip reversed into ~12% Nov-Dec growth 'without channel stuffing' per management, adjacency categories (cake, rusk, croissant, wafers) are compounding double-digit with e-commerce growing even faster, EBITDA margin hit a fresh high of 18.54% in Q4, and the September 2025 GST cut on biscuits (18% to 5%) is a structural, not one-off, tailwind for the whole category's affordability and volume elasticity. The new CEO, Rakshit Hargave, brings a genuinely aggressive distribution/challenger-brand pedigree from Birla Opus and Beiersdorf/Lakme Lever - exactly the profile needed to defend share against Patanjali's and ITC's capacity pushes rather than cede it. Wheat and palm oil, the two biggest input lines, are already forward-covered, giving near-term margin visibility even as fuel/laminate/edible-oil costs run hot.

## Bear case

The honest bear case: at ~50-56x trailing earnings, the market is pricing sustained double-digit-plus earnings growth, but a meaningful share of FY26's 16-22% profit growth came from margin/tax effects rather than the ~5-8% volume growth that is the real underlying engine - strip out the GST base effect and this year's apparent tax-rate benefit, and the growth story looks much closer to a high-single-digit compounder than the multiple assumes. Margin is already near a record high with limited further operating-leverage room, the dairy/cheese sub-segment is openly under a 'strategic correction' (i.e., it has not been working), the West Asia conflict is an unresolved drag on the international business with no confirmed normalization date, and Patanjali Foods' Rs 750-crore biscuit-plant acquisition plus ITC's continued Sunfeast/Dark Fantasy push are a live capacity-and-share threat right as Britannia itself plans calibrated price hikes and grammage cuts for Q1 FY27 - a combination that could pressure volumes if timed badly. A brand-new CEO (in seat since mid-December 2025, barely two quarters of results) also carries genuine execution and strategy-continuity risk during the transition.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At ~50-51x trailing EPS (Rs 106.35, price ~Rs 5,361, market cap ~Rs 1.3 lakh crore), the market is pricing an earnings yield of ~2%, which for a mature, high-payout (~85%) FMCG staple implies an assumed sustained EPS growth path in the mid-to-high teens for an extended period. Britannia's actual demonstrated engine is 6-8% volume growth (per management's own FY27 framing) plus modest price/mix, against a margin base already near a record ~18.5% with limited further expansion room - a combination that more realistically supports high-single-digit-to-low-double-digit revenue growth and similar earnings growth once this year's GST base effect and tax-rate tailwind normalize out. The multiple is not extreme versus Britannia's own 10-year history (12% below its own median) or narrow FMCG-staple peers (~61.6x average), so this is not a bubble call - but it is fair-to-rich relative to what the underlying volume/margin math can genuinely deliver, i.e., priced for a cleaner and faster compounding path than the FY26 print actually shows once volume is separated from tax/margin effects.

## Detailed rationale

Britannia's FY26 delivered consolidated revenue of Rs 19,151.59 crore (+6.7% YoY) and PAT of Rs 2,537.01 crore (+16.3% YoY), with Q4 FY26 alone showing revenue of Rs 4,718.92 crore (+6.5-7.1% YoY) and PAT of Rs 679.68 crore (+21.6% YoY) as EBITDA margin expanded 48bps YoY to 18.54%. But the composition matters: Q4 PBT grew only ~4.4% to Rs 785.11 crore while PAT jumped 21.6% - a gap wide enough that a meaningful chunk of the quarter's reported profit growth likely came from a lower effective tax rate or a below-the-line item rather than pure operating improvement [INFERENCE from the PBT-vs-PAT divergence in the reported numbers]; that is not a scandal, but it means the 21.6% headline overstates underlying operating strength. Volume itself looks genuine, not manufactured: Q3 FY26 (the GST-transition quarter) printed 4.5-5% volume growth for the full quarter, but management said Nov-Dec accelerated to roughly 12% (volume+realization combined) 'without channel stuffing' as the industry normalized back to Rs 5/Rs 10 MRP packs after the September 22, 2025 GST cut on biscuits from 18% to 5%. That GST reset is a genuine structural tailwind for the category - a permanent tax cut passed through as lower shelf prices - not a one-quarter sugar high, though the YoY base-effect boost to reported growth fades once FY27 laps it.

Beyond core biscuits (~38% market share vs Parle's ~32%), the adjacency businesses - cake, rusk, croissant, wafers - are all compounding at double-digit rates, with e-commerce/quick-commerce growing even faster, and dairy runs through the Britannia Bel Foods JV where the company is the #2 cheese-slices player - though management itself flagged the cheese sub-category is under a 'strategic correction' under a new category head, i.e., that particular adjacency has been underperforming and needed a reset, a soft spot worth naming rather than glossing over. On inputs, wheat and palm oil are forward-covered, but fuel, laminate and edible-oil inflation are flagged as 'elevated', and management has announced calibrated price hikes plus grammage adjustments from Q1 FY27 to protect margin - a lever that, if pulled too hard right after a GST-driven affordability gain, risks giving back some of the volume momentum. Layered on top is a genuine, unresolved geopolitical disruption: the West Asia conflict hit vessel availability and logistics for the international business in Q4, forcing a shift of North-America-bound manufacturing from Oman back to the Mundra export unit, with normalization only expected 'in the coming quarters' - not yet cleared. On competitive structure, Patanjali Foods' Rs 750-crore biscuit-plant acquisition (approved March 14, 2026) is a capacity-led entrant threat, and ITC continues to press with Sunfeast/Dark Fantasy - the same entrant-capacity-share pattern this desk tracks in paints (Birla Opus vs Asian Paints). Notably, the new Britannia CEO, Rakshit Hargave (five-year term from December 15, 2025), was previously CEO of Birla Opus itself - the very disruptive entrant in that other category - which reads either as the incumbent hiring disruptor DNA to defend share, or simply as a strong distribution operator being recruited; either way it is a leadership discontinuity worth watching given he has been in seat for barely two quarters.

On valuation: at a share price of ~Rs 5,361 (market cap ~Rs 1.29-1.30 lakh crore, as of July 9, 2026) and TTM EPS of Rs 106.35, Britannia trades near 50-51x trailing earnings by one measure and as high as 56.5x by another (vs an FMCG-peer average of ~61.6x, a broader Indian food-industry average of just ~19.5x, and one bottom-up fair-value estimate of ~36.5x). The current multiple is ~12% below Britannia's own 10-year median of ~55.6x, so it is not rich versus its own history or narrow FMCG-staple peers - but a ~50x multiple (~2% earnings yield) on a franchise whose trailing 5-year revenue CAGR is ~8.2% (below the broad 'industry' average one data provider cites at ~14.9%, though that comparison set is wide and imperfect) is pricing in continued double-digit-plus EPS compounding for years, most of which this year came from margin/tax effects rather than volume. Margin is already near a record ~18.5%, capping further operating leverage; the reinvestment runway in a mature, high-payout FMCG staple (Rs 90.50/share FY26 dividend against Rs 106.35 EPS, roughly 85% payout) is limited. Genuine forward view: Britannia can likely compound revenue in the high-single-digits to ~11% (volume 6-8% per management's own FY27 framing, plus modest price/mix) with earnings growth in a similar low-double-digit band once the GST base effect and this year's apparent tax-rate tailwind normalize out - a steady compounder, not the higher-growth story the multiple implicitly assumes.
