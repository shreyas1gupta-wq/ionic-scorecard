## Bear case

The existential risk is structural and unresolved, not merely a sentiment overhang: transaction fees are ~78-80% of revenue, and a single Grid-India clearing price by design converts IEX from price-setter to bid-collector -- exactly the mechanism that compressed exchange margins in coupled European power markets. Second, fee compression is already happening pre-coupling: FY26 volume grew 17% but revenue grew only 13.6% (Q4 alone: volume +24.3% vs. revenue +12.5%), meaning IEX is already discounting per-unit realization to defend share against PXIL/HPX before the formal rules even bite -- the moat is eroding at the margin today, not just in a future scenario. Third, earnings quality carries a minor wrinkle: other/treasury income fell ~41% sequentially in Q4 FY26 (Rs 37.4cr to Rs 22.1cr), and the 2.67% FCF yield sits well below the ~4.4% earnings yield implied by the P/E, a gap consistent with settlement-guarantee-fund and working-capital absorption that makes headline PAT somewhat richer than the cash actually available to equity holders. Fourth, the repeated legal challenges, regulatory redesigns (round-robin scrapped for single-MCO in Apr-2026), and open consultation windows mean this stays a binary-event-risk headline name for another one to two years with no clean resolution date -- a 'controversy tax' on the multiple that can persist even if the eventual fee/share impact turns out moderate.
