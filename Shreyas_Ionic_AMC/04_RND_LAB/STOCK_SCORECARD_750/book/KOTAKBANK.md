# KOTAKBANK — Kotak Mahindra Bank Ltd.

> [!summary]
> Kotak Mahindra Bank continues to show a healthy underlying franchise -- loan growth around 15%, improving asset quality, and strong contributions from its asset-management, broking and life-insurance arms -- but the stock is not cheap at roughly 2.1 times book value, and the bank is heading into a leadership transition, with CEO Ashok Vaswani stepping down at year-end 2026 and a successor requiring the Reserve Bank of India's approval. We see no reason to sell a well-run, well-capitalised bank, but the current price already reflects most of the good news, so we rate it a Hold pending clarity on the new leadership and the margin trajectory.

## Recommendation — Hold

Hold, not Sell, because nothing in the current print signals franchise or asset-quality deterioration -- GNPA/NNPA are improving, credit costs are low and falling, capital is abundant, loan growth is healthy and diversified across retail and institutional books, and the previously-embargoed unsecured/digital engine is being re-grown with visible discipline rather than recklessness. It is not a high-conviction Hold either: consolidated P/B of ~2.14x against an 11.4% current ROE leaves little valuation cushion by a bank-only reverse-DCF lens, NIM has structurally compressed with management unwilling to guide it forward, and the CEO transition is a live, RBI-gated event running through H2 2026 that adds idiosyncratic uncertainty exactly when the multiple has no room for disappointment. A Sell call would need either a governance/succession misstep, a resumption of unsecured-book stress, or clear evidence the market's implied ROE-expansion assumption is wrong -- none of that is visible in the data today, so Hold is the honest call.

## Bull case

Asset quality is genuinely improving, not just stable -- GNPA down to 1.18% and NNPA to 0.27%, credit costs nearly halved YoY to 46bps, and provision coverage above 78%. Capital is abundant (CRAR 22.8%, CET1 22.4%), funding ~15% loan growth without any dilution pressure. The unsecured retail re-ramp (personal loans, cards, microfinance) paused after the RBI's digital-811/credit-card embargo is being restarted with visible discipline -- modest card-spend growth, government-guarantee-covered microfinance, double-digit-plus organic personal loans -- rather than a rush back to pre-embargo growth, exactly the behavior a forensic read wants to see post-regulatory-action. The subsidiary stable is compounding faster than the bank itself: AMC & Trustee PAT +22% YoY on 16% AUM growth, Life Insurance VNB +31.4% YoY at a rich 28.5% margin, Securities PAT of Rs 533 crore in the quarter -- a genuine capital-light earnings engine that a pure bank-book multiple can undervalue.

## Bear case

The strongest bear point is valuation-versus-delivered-ROE: at ~2.14x consolidated P/B, the market is paying for a return profile well above the 11.4% current consolidated ROE Kotak is actually delivering (3-year average ~14%), and a standard Gordon-growth check shows the bank-only multiple would need to sit well below 2x to be justified on ROE alone -- the premium survives only if the capital-light subsidiaries are valued separately via sum-of-the-parts, which is a judgment call, not a fact on the balance sheet. Layered on top is a real near-term event: CEO Ashok Vaswani exits at end-2026 and management has explicitly deferred to 'the RBI timeline' for a successor, meaning the market spends the next two-plus quarters pricing regulatory-approval risk on a leadership change at a bank that has prior form with regulators (the 2024 RBI digital-onboarding/credit-card embargo, and a Supreme-Court-upheld SEBI fine on the AMC over legacy Essel Group due-diligence failures) -- neither individually disqualifying, but together leaving little room for a bad quarter or a contested succession outcome. NIM compression (4.53% now versus ~4.9-5.0% two years ago) also looks structural rather than transient, and management's refusal to guide it forward should be read as genuine uncertainty rather than false modesty.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

Consolidated P/B of ~2.14x (BVPS Rs 182, price Rs 390) against a current consolidated ROE of ~11.4% (3-yr avg ~14%) implies a justified P/B of roughly 0.8-1.1x under a standard (ROE-g)/(COE-g) framework at COE~13%, g~5-6% -- i.e. the standalone bank alone does not obviously earn its current multiple. The gap is bridged only by ascribing separate, higher multiples to the capital-light subsidiaries (AMC, Securities, Life Insurance at a 28.5% VNB margin), consistent with sell-side sum-of-the-parts frameworks. Net judgment: fair-to-mildly-rich, not cheap -- the market is pricing sustained ROE expansion from the unsecured re-ramp and subsidiary compounding that has not yet fully shown up in the blended numbers, leaving no real margin of safety against a stumble in the CEO succession or a resumption of unsecured-book stress.

## Detailed rationale

Kotak Mahindra Bank's Q1 FY27 print (18-Jul-2026) confirms a franchise in good underlying health: standalone PAT of Rs 4,123 crore (+26% YoY) and consolidated PAT of Rs 5,480 crore (+~22-23% YoY), NII of Rs 7,928 crore (+9.2% YoY), net advances of Rs 5.12 lakh crore (+15% YoY; retail +12%, institutional/corporate +20%), and deposits of Rs 5.73 lakh crore (+12% YoY, average CASA +15.2% YoY). NIM has stabilized at 4.53% (Q1FY27) versus 4.67% (Q4FY26) versus a much richer ~4.9-5.0% two years prior -- the compression looks structural (rate-cut transmission plus deposit-cost competition) and management explicitly declined to give forward NIM guidance on the earnings call, which should be read as genuine uncertainty. Asset quality is the standout: GNPA improved to 1.18% (from 1.20% sequentially, 1.48% YoY), NNPA to 0.27% (from 0.34% YoY), provision coverage above 78%, and credit cost nearly halved YoY to 46bps (from 93bps) -- though an upcoming ECL provisioning-standard transition is flagged by the CFO as a one-time hit of under 2% of net worth with credit costs stepping up a structural 12-15bps once implemented, which is a standards change, not fresh stress. Capital remains abundant: CRAR 22.8%, CET1 22.4%.

The post-embargo unsecured re-ramp looks disciplined rather than reckless. Personal loans are growing double-digit-plus organically (excluding runoff of the acquired Standard Chartered portfolio); credit cards are being 'restacked' toward better-quality customers with only 4% QoQ spend growth; microfinance grew 10% YoY with credit costs declining on the back of Credit Guarantee Fund for Micro Units (CGFMU) coverage. Kotak 811 digital-savings balances are +32% YoY and now ~12.2-12.7% of CASA/savings balances -- a genuine recovery of the franchise the RBI had throttled, without a visible rush back to pre-embargo growth rates. Subsidiaries are compounding faster than the bank itself and are a real part of the investment case: Kotak Securities PAT of Rs 533 crore in the quarter, Kotak AMC & Trustee PAT of Rs 399 crore (+22% YoY on ~16% YoY average-AUM growth to Rs 6.09 lakh crore), and Kotak Life Insurance's FY26 VNB of Rs 1,260 crore (+31.4% YoY) at a rich 28.5% VNB margin (+350bps) with embedded value of Rs 19,224 crore (Operating RoEV 16.4%); overall subsidiary PAT grew ~20% YoY. Kotak General Insurance has been recast into a Zurich-Kotak JV (capital-recycling, not a red flag).

Valuation is full rather than cheap. At Rs 390/share (17-Jul-2026), consolidated P/B is ~2.14x on BVPS Rs 182 (+15% YoY) and P/E ~19.4x, against a current consolidated ROE of only ~11.4% (3-year average ~14%). A standard (ROE-g)/(COE-g) Gordon check at COE~13% and terminal g~5-6% implies a justified standalone-bank multiple closer to 0.8-1.1x book -- the bank alone does not obviously earn a 2.14x multiple on today's numbers; the premium is defensible only via sum-of-the-parts logic that ascribes separate, richer multiples to the capital-light subsidiaries (consistent with sell-side SOTP frameworks that value the bank plus subsidiaries separately). Layered on top of a full multiple is a genuine, dated event: CEO Ashok Vaswani will step down at the end of his three-year term (31-Dec-2026), has said he will not seek reappointment (stated reason: personal), and the board must submit a ranked successor shortlist to the RBI roughly four months ahead of term-end (i.e., by early August 2026) -- internal whole-time directors Paritosh Kashyap and Anup Saha are the named contenders, with RBI approval a hard regulatory gate on the outcome and timeline. Separately, India's Supreme Court in mid-July 2026 upheld a legacy (Essel Group-era) SEBI fine of Rs 2.1 crore on Kotak Mahindra AMC/Trustee and named individuals including MD Nilesh Shah -- financially immaterial but a reminder the group carries governance scar tissue.
