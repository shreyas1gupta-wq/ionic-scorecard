## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

[INFERENCE/OPINION] A cash-flow DCF is the wrong tool here for the same structural reason it is wrong for any growing NBFC (loan growth consumes operating cash by construction) -- the right lens is the residual-income/P-B identity, P/B = (ROE - g)/(Ke - g). At the current ~3.7x P/B, solving for the steady-state ROE the market requires across a defensible 13-14.5% cost-of-equity band and a 9-10% sustainable long-run growth rate (well below the current 35-40% AUM guidance, which is a near-term ramp, not a perpetuity) yields a required ROE of roughly 21-26% -- a genuinely demanding ask. What makes this more than an arbitrary-parameter exercise is that two independent, credible anchors both land well short of that number: management's own official target (3.5% ROA by Jun-2028, translating to ~16-17% ROE at the current ~3.82x D/E) and Jefferies' bull-case initiation model (ROE expanding from 6% in FY26 to 16% by FY29) triangulate almost exactly to each other -- and both sit 5-10 points below what the price requires. There is also a genuine two-stage nuance that argues the other way: because ROE is on a rising trajectory (not static), a full multi-year residual-income build (rather than the naive single-stage perpetuity formula) would value the interim high-growth years separately from the terminal value, potentially supporting a higher fair multiple than the single-stage math implies. But that cuts both ways here: with trailing ROE still ~5-6%, below the 13-14% cost of equity, the interim years are actually residual-income-NEGATIVE (value-destructive per rupee of book added) until the ROE ramp clears Ke -- which, on management's own Jun-2028 target, may not happen for roughly another 18-24 months. Net judgment: this is not a case of a rich-but-defensible quality premium (the Bajaj Finance-style read) -- it is a case where the price requires outperforming the company's own official guidance and the most bullish independent analyst simultaneously, while the underlying return on capital is still sub-Ke today. That is a real overvaluation flag, not a modeling artifact of one aggressive discount-rate choice.
