## Recommendation — Hold

Hold, not Sell. The quant model's Sell verdict is driven by a weak technical/momentum score, not by deteriorating fundamentals -- and on fundamentals this review finds a record order book (2.6x book-to-bill), improving core-business leverage and working capital, no balance-sheet or governance red flags, and a stock that has materially outperformed a badly de-rated capital-goods sector over the past year. That argues against selling into weakness. But it stops short of a high-conviction Hold: FY26 revenue growth missed guidance, FY27 growth/margin guidance was cut, sell-side EPS estimates are down 8-10% post-Q4, a real (if company-guided-transient) Middle East-linked execution air-pocket runs through H1 FY27, and at ~32x trailing earnings / ~5.6x book the stock has limited margin of safety heading into the July 28 Q1 FY27 print -- even the constructive sell-side case (PL Capital, BUY) models multiple compression from here, not expansion. Net: hold the existing position through the results event and the guided soft patch; this is not a name we would add fresh capital to at this multiple, nor one where the fundamental case has broken enough to justify exiting.

## Bull case

Order book at an all-time high of ~Rs 7.40 lakh crore (+28% YoY) against FY26 revenue of Rs 2.86 lakh crore -- roughly 2.6x book-to-bill -- gives several years of visible revenue, on the back of the best order-inflow year (+22% YoY) in the company's history. Core balance-sheet and cash quality are genuinely improving: parent-level gross D/E improved to 0.95x (from 1.12x) and net D/E to 0.35x (from 0.60x), cash plus investments rose to ~Rs 80,400 crore from ~Rs 66,300 crore, and FY26 operating cash flow grew 87% YoY as net working capital fell to a multi-year-low 4.1% of revenue. Capital discipline is visible in portfolio actions, not just words -- Nabha Power and Hyderabad Metro are being divested (closing Q1 FY27) as sub-scale/low-return concession assets, freeing capital for the Lakshya'31 growth vectors (data centers, green hydrogen, semiconductor design, industrial electronics) -- and the year's reported-PAT softness traces to one identifiable one-off (a Rs 1,155 crore labour-code provision), not core operating deterioration, with recurring PAT still up 18% YoY. Over the past twelve months L&T has meaningfully outperformed its own sector (roughly +9% vs a sector mean near -22%), evidence its own fundamentals -- not just sentiment -- have held up better than peers through a broad capital-goods drawdown. The company also carries a live, credentialed defence-manufacturing franchise (L&T Precision Engineering & Systems -- K9 Vajra-T artillery, repeat/second-batch order delivered ahead of schedule on the first tranche), though this remains a minority slice of group order book (Hi-Tech Manufacturing is the smallest of the three main segments at ~Rs 35,000 crore) rather than a primary swing factor the way it would be for a pure-play defence name.

## Bear case

FY26 revenue growth of 12% fell short of the company's own 15% guidance, and FY27 guidance was cut to a more modest 10-12% order-inflow/revenue growth with explicitly flattish margins -- management itself is signaling deceleration. Execution risk is real and corroborated across multiple independent sources, not one analyst's outlier view: Middle East supply-chain disruption is expected to keep H1 FY27 execution soft, Infrastructure Projects showed 'subdued' Q4 progress, Energy Projects margins are pressured by legacy project cost overruns, recovery is only guided for 'a couple of quarters' out, and ~78% of the international order book (~Rs 3 lakh crore) sits in Middle East geographies -- a real geographic/counterparty concentration. Sell-side has already responded: PL Capital cut FY27E/FY28E EPS by 8.0%/10.4% post-Q4 and now models multiple compression to 22x (from a trailing ~32x) even while retaining a BUY, and Nomura has separately flagged margin pressure into the July 28 Q1 FY27 print -- a live, ~10-day-out event-risk window. Layered on a decelerating growth/margin outlook, the stock's ~32x trailing P/E and ~5.6x book leave little valuation cushion if the guided soft patch runs longer or deeper than management expects, and FY27's cash-flow comparison is guided to be structurally tougher as the exceptional FY26 working-capital tailwind (4.1% of revenue) partially reverses toward a guided ~10%.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At a current price of roughly Rs 3,850 (~32x trailing consolidated EPS, ~5.6x book, against a ~17% ROE), the market is pricing sustained mid-to-high-teens earnings compounding for several years -- well above what management's own FY27 guidance (10-12% revenue growth, flattish margins) supports over the next one to two years. Tellingly, even the constructive sell-side case does not assume the multiple holds: PL Capital's own BUY-rated target of Rs 4,632 (cut from Rs 4,806) is built on just 22x Mar'28E earnings, i.e., the bull case is an EPS-growth-and-roll-forward story at a lower multiple, not a re-rating story. Our own reverse-DCF read: a business compounding revenue at 11-13% with flat-to-modestly-improving margins and a 15-17% ROE/ROCE profile would normally warrant something closer to 22-26x forward earnings in India's capital-goods complex, not 30x+. The premium L&T currently holds looks like it is being carried by scarcity value (the only large-cap, liquid, diversified quality EPC/capex play), the optionality embedded in a 2.6x book-to-bill order backlog, and early, not-yet-monetized optionality in the Lakshya'31 push into data centers/green hydrogen/semiconductors -- a real but fragile set of reasons to pay up, leaving little margin of safety if H1 FY27 execution disappoints as guided. Net judgment: valuation is fair-to-modestly-rich, not cheap, and not yet a value opportunity -- but also not stretched enough, against a genuinely improving core balance sheet and record order book, to justify a Sell on valuation grounds alone.

## Detailed rationale

L&T enters FY27 with an all-time-high consolidated order book of roughly Rs 7.40 lakh crore (+28% YoY), built on FY26 order inflows of Rs 4.36 lakh crore (+22% YoY, international 58% of inflows). Against FY26 revenue of Rs 2.86 lakh crore (+12% YoY), that is a book-to-bill of ~2.6x -- genuine multi-year revenue visibility, and not a single-order artifact: segment-level order book (Infrastructure Rs 4.23 lakh crore, Energy Rs 2.58 lakh crore, Hi-Tech Manufacturing Rs 0.35 lakh crore) broadly reconciles with the group figure. But FY26 revenue growth of 12% missed the company's own 15% guidance, and FY27 order-inflow and revenue growth guidance has been cut to a more modest 10-12% with explicitly flattish margins -- management itself is guiding for deceleration, not just the market pricing it in. The proximate cause is Middle East-linked supply-chain disruption (logistics/insurance cost inflation, project delays) expected to keep H1 FY27 execution soft, plus 'subdued' domestic Infrastructure progress in Q4; recovery is guided for H2 FY27 as conditions normalize. Roughly 78% of the international order book (~Rs 3 lakh crore) sits in Middle East geographies -- a real geographic/counterparty concentration risk specific to L&T's EPC exposure, distinct from the India-facing risk vectors that apply to pure-play defence names.

On earnings quality, one one-off is clearly identifiable and fully explains the gap between recurring and reported profit: a Rs 1,155 crore (net of tax and minority interest) exceptional provision for employee benefits tied to the new labour code, almost exactly bridging FY26 recurring PAT (Rs 17,238 crore, +18% YoY) down to reported consolidated PAT (Rs 16,084 crore). Underlying profit growth is therefore healthier than the headline PAT print suggests. A second, cash-side nuance cuts the other way: FY26 operating cash flow rose 87% YoY (~Rs 35,490 crore vs ~Rs 18,960 crore) as net working capital fell to a multi-year-low 4.1% of revenue -- management itself flags this as exceptional and guides working capital back toward ~10% of revenue in FY27, meaning next year's cash-flow comparison is guided to be structurally tougher, independent of the P&L. Margin softness (Q4 EBITDA margin -62bps YoY to 10.4%) is attributed by the company and sell-side to legacy Energy-project cost overruns/close-outs on contracts now concluding, expected to ease within a couple of quarters -- plausible and consistent with the order-book-quality read that new bookings are not (yet) shown to carry the same problem, but this is company/sell-side framing, not independently verified by this review. Headline consolidated leverage (D/E 1.15x, interest coverage 3.5x) looks unremarkable for an investment-grade industrial, but this blended figure is dragged down by L&T's captive NBFC (L&T Finance), which runs leveraged as a lender by design; parent/core-EPC leverage is healthier and improving (gross D/E 0.95x vs 1.12x prior year, net D/E 0.35x vs 0.60x) -- the consolidated ratio overstates core-business balance-sheet risk.

On the price weakness itself: our own cross-sectional data shows the broader listed construction/capital-goods sector down roughly 22% over the trailing year while L&T is up modestly (+9%) over the same window -- L&T has been the relative safe-haven within a sector-wide de-rating, not its source, over a 12-month horizon. The more recent leg of weakness (trailing ~3 months) tracks the sector almost exactly and coincides with real new information: a 'mixed' Q4 print, both PL Capital and (per headline) Nomura flagging margin pressure into the July 28 Q1 FY27 print, and sell-side EPS estimates being cut 8-10% post-results. So the recent weakness reads as a blend -- mostly sector-wide multiple normalization off a rich starting valuation, plus a real, company-guided (hence likely transient) execution/margin air-pocket that the market is not wrong to be pricing in, rather than a signal of a broken business.
