# IOC — Indian Oil Corporation Ltd.

> [!summary]
> Indian Oil looks statistically cheap (about 4.7x trailing earnings, below book value) but FY26's record Rs 36,802 crore profit was a peak-cycle number - boosted by a refining-margin spike, inventory gains, and a one-off government compensation catch-up that together will not repeat. In fact the reversal has already started: industry marketing margins on petrol and diesel have swung sharply negative in the current (Apr-Jun 2026) quarter because global crude jumped on Middle East tensions while domestic pump prices stayed flat, a repeat of the same government-pricing pattern that hurt IOC's earnings in FY23 and FY25. Debt is genuinely coming down and the dividend is generous, but with no real revenue growth (down slightly over the last three years) and a structural risk of the government capping retail prices in bad crude quarters, we don't think the current valuation is the bargain it appears to be. We rate it a Sell.

## Recommendation — Sell

Sell over Hold because three things point the same direction rather than offsetting: (i) the earnings base the cheap-looking multiple is built on is a peak-cycle print (core GRM ~2.5x YoY, inventory gains, one-off LPG catch-up) that a reverse-DCF has to strip out before judging value; (ii) the government-pricing-interference risk that periodically wrecks OMC earnings (FY23, FY25) is not hypothetical here - it is showing up right now in the Q1 FY27 quarter about to be reported (retail margins -Rs 6/-Rs 18.9 per litre on petrol/diesel); and (iii) there is no underlying growth story to fall back on (3y revenue CAGR -2.3%) - petrochemical capacity additions are real but small, multi-year-away contributors. The offsetting positives (falling debt, ~5-6% dividend yield, below-book valuation, no balance-sheet red flag) are real but describe a well-run, ex-growth, cyclical PSU utility, not a reason to hold through a quarter where the specific risk this analysis is built to catch is actively firing. The quant model's own 'Reduce' call at both the 1y and 3y horizon is consistent with this read.

## Bull case

IOC delivered record refining throughput (75.5 MMT, 107.4% capacity utilisation) and ~5% marketing-volume growth in FY26, funded ~Rs 34,000 crore of capex while still cutting gross debt to Rs 131,822 crore from Rs 152,271 crore a year earlier, and paid out a strong Rs 8.25/share in dividends for the year - a real, cash-backed shareholder return from a company trading below book value (~0.91x) at ~4.7x trailing earnings. The Panipat, Barauni and Gujarat refinery/petrochemical expansions (adding 20-25% refining capacity and diversifying into petrochemicals) are genuinely on the runway for commissioning through Aug-Dec 2026, and the Project SPRINT cost programme is delivering real, repeatable savings (Rs 2,200 crore in FY26, Rs 2,500 crore targeted FY27) independent of the commodity cycle. The government has also shown willingness to eventually compensate OMCs for LPG under-recoveries via budget allocation (~Rs 30,000 crore FY26 RE/FY27 BE, industry-wide), meaning the pricing-interference risk is not entirely uncompensated over time.

## Bear case

The strongest bear point is that the 'cheap' multiple is measured against an earnings base that is not repeatable: core GRM alone rose roughly 2.5x YoY in FY26 (about $9.86/bbl vs $3.69/bbl in FY25), stacked with inventory gains and a one-off LPG under-recovery compensation catch-up - none of which are run-rate items. That this cycle turns was not a one-off risk to flag for the future; it is already happening in the quarter IOC is about to report (results 31-Jul-2026): retail marketing margins have gone to roughly -Rs 6/ltr on petrol and -Rs 18.9/ltr on diesel as crude spiked on Middle East tensions while the government held domestic pump prices flat, echoing the exact mechanism that crushed OMC earnings in FY23 and FY25. IOC has essentially no structural growth to offset this cyclicality (3y revenue CAGR -2.3%), and its business model gives the government a standing option to capture the upside (frozen prices when crude falls) while OMCs absorb the downside (frozen prices when crude spikes) - a recurring, uncompensated-in-real-time asymmetry that the current quarter is illustrating again.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At ~4.7-4.8x trailing PE and ~0.91x price-to-book, the market appears to be pricing IOC as if its record FY26 earnings (core GRM ~$9.86/bbl, plus inventory gains, plus a one-off LPG compensation catch-up) were close to sustainable. They are not: core GRM alone was ~2.5x its FY25 level, and the marketing-margin engine that also boosted FY26 has already swung to a loss of roughly Rs 6-19/ltr on petrol/diesel in the very next quarter on a fresh crude spike. Normalising the earnings base for the cycle (stripping the peak GRM, the inventory swing, and the one-off compensation item) implies a materially lower sustainable EPS than the FY26 print, which pushes the effective/normalized multiple well above the 4.7x headline - into fair-value territory for a zero-structural-growth (3y revenue CAGR -2.3%), government-price-controlled utility, rather than a demonstrable bargain. The current multiple does not look cheap enough to compensate for the recurring pricing-interference risk once the earnings base is properly cycle-adjusted.

## Detailed rationale

IOC's FY26 (Mar-2026) standalone PAT of Rs 36,802 crore (+184% YoY vs Rs 12,962 crore in FY25; consolidated PAT Rs 43,677 crore vs Rs 13,789 crore) is a genuine peak-cycle print, not a run-rate. Three tailwinds stacked in the same year: (1) core GRM (ex-inventory) recovered to roughly $9.86/bbl from $3.69/bbl in FY25 - a >2.5x swing; (2) rising crude through the year generated inventory gains that flip to losses whenever crude falls, so a meaningful chunk of the headline beat is a mark-to-market timing item, not operating margin; (3) government approval of LPG under-recovery compensation released a cumulative catch-up payment that boosted revenue/cash in the year it was booked, not an annually-recurring item at that size. Refining throughput was a record 75.5 MMT at 107.4% capacity utilisation and marketing volumes grew ~5% YoY to ~105 MMT - genuinely good operating execution - but the earnings multiple attached to this print has to be judged against what happens when the cycle normalises, and IOC has shown exactly that pattern in three of the last four years (FY23 and FY25 were both weak on the same government-pricing mechanism working in reverse).

That reversal is already visible in real time. Q4 FY26 blended retail marketing margin was already negative (-Rs 3.4/ltr: +Rs 2.9/ltr on petrol, -Rs 6/ltr on diesel on a capped-RTP basis), and it has since worsened sharply: industry data for Q1 FY27 (Apr-Jun 2026, IOC results due 31-Jul-2026) show retail margins of -Rs 6/ltr on petrol and -Rs 18.9/ltr on diesel, a swing of roughly Rs 16-27/ltr YoY, driven by a Strait-of-Hormuz-linked crude spike and a weaker rupee while domestic pump prices stayed flat. Petroleum Minister Hardeep Singh Puri has put industry-wide (IOC+BPCL+HPCL) under-recovery from selling fuel below market cost at roughly Rs 75,000 crore, and analysts peg the Mar-Jun 2026 marketing-loss hit at ~Rs 35,000 crore industry-wide - expected to be partly offset by concurrently strong refining margins and recoverable over 1-2 quarters, but it is a live negative catalyst sitting directly in front of the print this stock is about to deliver. On the balance sheet, gross borrowings actually fell to Rs 131,822 crore (Mar-2026) from Rs 152,271 crore (Mar-2025) despite ~Rs 34,000 crore of FY26 capex - a genuine positive, funded by the peak-year cash flow. FY26 total dividend was a strong Rs 8.25/share (Rs 5 first interim + Rs 2 second interim + Rs 1.25 final, record date 14-Aug-2026), consistent with the PSU's high-payout policy. Capacity growth is real but slow to monetise: the Panipat refinery expansion (15 to 25 MMTPA, Rs 38,000 crore approved cost, ~Rs 27,000 crore spent) targets December 2026 commissioning after multiple slippages from an original mid-2024 date (COVID, scope additions, equipment supply-chain delays post the Ukraine war); Barauni and Gujarat expansions are targeted for August and November 2026. FY27 capex guidance is Rs 32,700 crore (mostly refining/pipelines, ~Rs 5,000 crore renewables), and the Project SPRINT cost-efficiency programme delivered Rs 2,200 crore of savings in FY26 (targeting Rs 2,500 crore in FY27) - a genuine, non-cyclical margin lever, but small relative to the swings the marketing/GRM cycle produces.

On valuation: the stock trades at ~4.7-4.8x trailing PE and ~0.91x price-to-book (CMP ~Rs 142, market cap ~Rs 2,00,000 crore) - superficially very cheap. But that multiple is being applied to an EPS base inflated by peak GRM, inventory gains, and a one-off compensation catch-up, all three of which are already unwinding in the very next quarter. Revenue has not grown structurally (3y revenue CAGR -2.3%, per the quant context), so there is no volume-growth offset to the earnings-quality issue. Cycle-adjusting the earnings base down (a reasonable estimate given core GRM alone was ~2.5x its FY25 level, before adding the inventory and LPG-catch-up boosts) puts the normalized multiple meaningfully higher than the 4.7x headline - closer to a fair-to-full multiple for a business with no growth and a structural, recurring government-pricing overhang that just reasserted itself. This is not a case of an irrationally cheap stock; the market is arguably still being generous given the negative catalyst already in the pipe.
