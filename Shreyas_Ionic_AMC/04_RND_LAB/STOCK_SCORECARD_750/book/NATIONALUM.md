## Bull case

The balance sheet is as clean as they come: D/E of 0.0 and interest coverage of 79.3x mean the entire multi-year capacity program (Pottangi bauxite mine, Angul smelter/power expansion, ~Rs 30,000cr) can be funded without meaningful leverage, and ROE (26.8%) and ROCE (36.6%) are both excellent even after adjusting for the currently favorable aluminium price. NALCO remains a globally low-cost integrated producer (historically ~$190-220/t alumina production cost against a global benchmark of $220-230/t or higher), so it stays profitable through commodity troughs that would hurt higher-cost peers -- evidenced by operating margins holding above 46% even as alumina crashed through FY26. The dividend is a genuine support: Rs 10.50/share paid across FY26 (three interim payments) is a ~2.9% yield at CMP, a real cash return independent of the multiple re-rating debate. Government promoter holding sits at 51.3%, very close to the practical floor for retaining PSU/majority-control status, which caps how much further stake the government can realistically sell down via OFS -- no NALCO-specific disinvestment was found in current research (only an unrelated NLC India 3% OFS in June 2026), reducing near-term supply-overhang risk relative to PSUs with more room to divest. The capacity pipeline is credible, not aspirational: Pottangi's 50-year mining lease is already granted and mining was targeted to start around mid-2026, securing future bauxite supply as legacy sources deplete, while the Angul smelter/power expansion, though late-dated (2030 commissioning), is a real, EOI-issued, land-acquisition-stage project rather than a slide-deck ambition. Aluminium's structural supply backdrop (China's 45mt smelter cap already binding, a forecast 2026 global deficit) gives real support to the metal price NALCO is currently riding, and management's own FY27 guidance (Rs 19,000cr revenue, 40-45% EBITDA margin) implies continued near-term earnings growth that two independent brokerages (Motilal Oswal, Systematix) have both recently upgraded estimates for.

## Bear case

The core risk is earnings-quality, not balance-sheet: NALCO has now posted two consecutive 'record' profit years driven by two different, largely uncontrollable commodity spikes (alumina in FY25, aluminium in FY26), and a PE of 11.2x on that base looks cheaper than it cycle-adjusts to. LME aluminium at $3,100-3,200/t (having spiked to $3,685/t in April 2026) sits well above pre-2024 historical averages, and a meaningful part of the current premium is tied to a transient Middle East supply disruption (EGA/ALBA) that is not guaranteed to persist for three years -- if it resolves and China's cap enforcement loosens even partially (which industry commentary flags as a live possibility, including carve-outs for renewable-power smelters), aluminium could mean-revert toward $2,600-2,800/t, pulling normalized EPS down from the current ~Rs 31 TTM to something closer to Rs 24-28 by this analyst's estimate -- a 15-25% downside to the earnings base the current multiple is anchored on. Alumina, the other half of the swing story, is currently depressed at ~$305-310/t (down from FY25's $650-695/t peak), so there is not much room for a second alumina windfall to offset an aluminium reversal in the near term. The big capacity additions (Pottangi bauxite, Angul smelter/power JV) are FY29-30 dated and will not contribute to earnings inside a 3-year window, so there is no near-term volume-growth offset to a price-driven earnings dip. Execution risk is real, if modest: Pottangi has already seen its start-date pushed multiple times and faced local social-license friction requiring public company clarification, and the Angul expansion is still at DPR-revision stage with no firm capex-approval date. Two independent brokerages (Motilal Oswal, Systematix) both rate the stock Neutral with only 10-22% upside to target, and the stock's own 95.5% 12-month run has already priced in much of the metals-cycle re-rating, leaving less margin of safety for a name whose earnings base is more cyclically elevated than the headline multiple suggests.

## Detailed rationale

NALCO is a Navratna CPSE (Ministry of Mines, Government of India holds 51.3% as of June 2026) running a fully integrated bauxite-alumina-aluminium-captive power chain out of Odisha: Panchpatmali bauxite mines, a 2.275 MTPA calcined-alumina refinery at Damanjodi (a 5th stream is being added to take this to ~3.275 MTPA), an Angul aluminium smelter (~4.6 lakh TPA) backed by ~1,200 MW of captive power. The commercial swing factor in this business is not the smelter -- it is third-party alumina sales. In FY26 NALCO dispatched 14.46 lakh tonnes of alumina but sold only 1.38 lakh tonnes domestically, meaning the bulk is sold into the global spot/contract alumina market, and that price has been extraordinarily volatile: it spiked to roughly $650-695/t in Q4 CY2024/Q1 CY2025 (the peak that inflated FY25's back-half earnings), fell to an NSR of ~$416/t by Q1 FY26, and has since crashed further to spot levels of roughly $305-310/t as of the Q4 FY26 print (April 2026) -- more than a 50% round-trip decline from peak. NALCO's own cost of alumina production has historically run around $190-220/t (Wood Mackenzie-rated lowest-cost bauxite-and-alumina producer globally as recently as FY19), so the segment is still profitable at today's depressed alumina price, just nowhere near the windfall margin it captured in FY25.

The quarterly PAT trail, cross-checked against the internal PIT dataset (unified_quarterly_pit.parquet, 26 rows for NATIONALUM, available_date-stamped), tells the real cyclicality story better than the annual headline does. FY25 built through the year on the alumina spike: Rs 588cr (Q1) -> Rs 1,046cr (Q2) -> Rs 1,566cr (Q3) -> Rs 2,067cr (Q4, the actual cycle peak) for an FY25 total of Rs 5,267cr. FY26 opened with a sharp reset as alumina crashed -- Rs 1,049cr in Q1FY26, barely half the prior quarter -- then rebuilt through the year to Rs 1,430cr, Rs 1,595cr, and Rs 1,722cr in Q4FY26, for an FY26 total of Rs 5,816cr (a record, +10.4% YoY), even though Q4FY26 was down 16.7% YoY against the Q4FY25 alumina-spike comparator. The rebuild through FY26 was not alumina-led -- it was LME aluminium-led: aluminium spiked to $3,685/t on 24 April 2026 on Middle East supply disruptions (EGA/ALBA), and mid-July 2026 spot is still $3,100-3,200/t, a level supported structurally by China's self-imposed 45 million-tonne smelter cap (domestic output already at ~45.02 Mt in 2025) and a forecast 2026 global supply deficit (estimates range from ~200kt to Macquarie's ~930kt shortfall call). So NALCO has now posted two consecutive 'record' years off two different commodity tailwinds -- alumina in FY25, aluminium in FY26 -- which is exactly the setup that makes a bare trailing PE look more reassuring than it should for a cyclical.

The growth pipeline is real but late-dated and will not move the 3-year earnings needle much. Pottangi bauxite mine (3.5 MTPA capacity, a 50-year lease over 697.98 hectares in Koraput) was targeted by the CMD to start mining around mid-2026 -- it replaces depleting legacy ore sources and feeds the 5th alumina-refinery stream, a genuine long-term cost/supply-security positive, though local social-license friction (the company has had to publicly clarify 'no displacement') is worth watching as an execution risk rather than dismissing. The bigger project -- an Angul smelter brownfield expansion (+0.5 MTPA) plus a 1,080 MW captive power plant in a 50:50 JV with NLC India, together roughly Rs 30,000cr of capex (smelter alone ~Rs 17,000cr) -- is still at the DPR-revision/land-acquisition stage as of mid-2026, with commissioning pointed at 2030. NALCO can fund this comfortably (D/E 0.0, interest coverage 79.3x, ROE 26.8%, ROCE 36.6%), but it is a FY29-30 earnings event, not a FY27-29 one. Management's own FY27 guidance is for revenue of ~Rs 19,000cr (~13% above FY26's ~Rs 16,787cr standalone), with EBITDA margin held at 40-45% as 'production costs return to last year's levels' -- i.e., cost normalization offsetting alumina weakness, not a step-change in volumes. Motilal Oswal (Neutral, TP Rs 400, valuing the stock at 7.5x EV/EBITDA on FY28E after raising FY27 EBITDA/PAT estimates 9%/12% on the LME price benefit) and Systematix (Neutral, TP Rs 442) both read this the same way -- modest upside, fairly priced, not a re-rating candidate.
