# LENSKART

> [!summary]
> Lenskart is executing well operationally -- India same-store sales growth of ~24% and continued international expansion are real, volume-led progress, not accounting tricks. But the stock's valuation (a P/E variously cited between ~136x and ~277x across sources, all far above global peer EssilorLuxottica's ~50x) demands years of flawless execution, and the most recent quarter (Q4 FY26) actually saw profit fall year-on-year even as revenue grew over 40%, on the back of aggressive new-store costs. We are holding per the quant model's call, but flagging this as a valuation-vs-fundamentals tension worth the desk's attention rather than a comfortable Hold.

## Bull case

Lenskart is the clear category leader in a structurally underpenetrated Indian organized-eyewear market, and it is compounding through a genuine mix of new-store additions (603 net adds in FY26) AND same-store growth (~24% in Q4 FY26 across metro, Tier-1 and Tier-2+ markets alike) -- that combination is the strongest possible signal a consumer analyst can ask for, since it means the demand is real and not just distribution-led sugar. International operations (Japan/SEA/Middle East, 718 stores) are still sub-scale on margin (~7% EBITDA margin pre-IndAS116) but are improving (+335bps YoY) and represent a long growth runway once they mature toward India-segment profitability. EBITDA margin at the group level is reported expanding (FY26 EBITDA +55.3% YoY to Rs 1,790 cr, ~19.9% margin), and interest coverage (9.8x) and D/E (0.35) show a balance sheet that is not under stress.

## Bear case

The valuation is the whole ballgame here and it is stretched by any lens: every P/E figure I could source (135.8x on the quant sheet, ~187.9x on screener.in, ~277x per marketsmojo) sits multiples above EssilorLuxottica's ~50x, and the IPO itself was priced at ~238x with several credible houses (Ambit: Sell; Cholamandalam: 'way too high') on record calling it overvalued even before the stock re-rated further post-listing. Reported ROE is only 2.35% and ROCE 9.7% -- thin returns on capital for a business commanding a growth/tech-like multiple, and one analyst's framing that investors are 'paying premium tech valuations for a retail business with single-digit margins' rings true on the numbers. Most concretely: Q4 FY26 PAT fell ~7.5% YoY (Rs 203-204 cr vs Rs 220 cr) even as revenue rose >40% YoY, meaning the capex/opex of aggressive store rollout is currently outrunning the profit the model is supposed to be scaling into -- a real near-term earnings-quality wrinkle, not a one-off item to wave away. The business is also capital-intensive (owned retail + manufacturing + international rollout), so sustaining 20%+ growth without either margin inflection or further capital draw is the open question.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At ~Rs 94,140 cr market cap against FY26 PAT of ~Rs 500-530 cr, the stock trades at roughly 178-188x trailing consolidated earnings (consistent with the 135x-277x range cited across sources depending on EPS basis), versus global listed optical peer EssilorLuxottica at ~50x. To 'earn into' even a 50x normalized multiple over a 5-year horizon from today's ~180x starting point requires either (a) ~29% sustained annual PAT CAGR for 5 years with the multiple holding, or more realistically (b) a combination of ~20-25% PAT growth plus a meaningful de-rating as growth normalizes -- i.e. the current price already assumes Lenskart executes a rare, sustained hyper-growth-with-margin-expansion path for the rest of this decade. Judgment: RICH. The operating story (same-store growth, international margin inflection) is genuinely good, but not good enough on the evidence to date (2.35% ROE, a YoY profit decline last quarter) to comfortably clear what the multiple is asking for. This is not a >=25% growth call, so no ROE/dilution exceptional-tier disclosure is triggered, but it is exactly because ROE is only 2.3% that I am NOT willing to underwrite the higher end of the growth range the bulls would need for the price to make sense.
