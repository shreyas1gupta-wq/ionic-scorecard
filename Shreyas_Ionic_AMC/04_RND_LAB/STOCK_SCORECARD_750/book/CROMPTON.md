# CROMPTON

> [!summary]
> Crompton's FY26 headline loss (Rs.230.76cr) looks alarming but is a one-off: a Rs.716.04cr non-cash writedown on the Butterfly Gandhimathi kitchen-appliances unit, the SECOND such impairment on that 2022 acquisition (the first was ~Rs.692cr in FY24). Strip that out and the underlying business improved through the year - Q4 delivered the highest quarterly operating profit on record, helped by the GST rate cuts on durables (effective Sep-2025) and price hikes recovering margin from commodity cost pressure. The balance sheet is clean (near-zero debt, strong interest cover) and the dividend was maintained. The concern is less about this quarter and more about a pattern: management has now destroyed roughly Rs.1,400cr of capital on one acquisition twice, growth has been mid-single-digit on a trailing basis, and the stock's forward valuation (~27.5x) already assumes a good deal of the hoped-for recovery holds up. We are holding, not selling, and the next real test is the Q1 FY27 result due around 6-Aug-2026.

## Recommendation — Hold

Independently, I agree with Hold on the merits: the P&L headline (FY26 net loss) is fully explained by a disclosed, audited, non-cash impairment rather than a demand or margin problem - underlying Q4 operating profit was a record and the GST-2.0 tailwind plus price actions are genuine, verifiable positives - so there is no operating case for a Sell. ## Bull case

Underlying operations are healthier than the headline loss suggests: Q4 FY26 revenue grew 10.79% YoY with the highest-ever quarterly operating profit (PBDIT ex-other-income Rs.270.72cr), aided by the GST 2.0 durables rate cuts (28%->18% on ACs/large TVs, effective 22-Sep-2025) which the industry expects to support an ~11% durables-sector CAGR into FY29. Price actions (Jan-2026, more planned) are restoring margin after a weak H1 FY26. The core categories hold real competitive position: fan leadership retained through the BEE 2.0 transition (a compliance hurdle likely to squeeze smaller rivals more than Crompton), water heaters at #2 by GT share, and Butterfly appliances showing genuine premiumization (double-digit growth in gas stoves/cookers) even after the writedowns. Balance sheet is a clear strength - debt/equity near zero, interest coverage 18.8x, ROCE in the low-20s%, and the FY26 dividend (Rs.3/share) was maintained despite the reported loss. New TAM entries (residential wires, solar rooftop with a Rs.500cr order book) offer optionality beyond the mature core, and the stock is already down ~25% over the past year, pricing in a fair amount of the bad news.

## Bear case

The honest bear case is not the headline loss itself but what it reveals: this is the SECOND impairment (Rs.716.04cr, on top of ~Rs.692cr in FY24) against the same 2022 Butterfly Gandhimathi acquisition - cumulative writeoffs of roughly Rs.1,408cr against a subsidiary management chose to buy and has twice had to mark down for 'underperformance/integration difficulties.' That is a genuine, recurring capital-allocation and execution problem, not stale data or noise, and it is the strongest reason not to get more constructive here even though the quant model correctly can't 'see' the pattern across two separate fiscal years. Separately, trailing growth is unimpressive - 3yr revenue CAGR of 5.63% and FY26 revenue growth of just 2.96% - and Q4's 10.8% YoY jump needs to be checked against how much of it was GST-driven demand pulled forward into the festive/Q4 window versus a genuine step-change in run-rate; Q1 FY27 (due 6-Aug-2026) is the test. Meanwhile the stock trades at a rich forward P/E (~27.5x) for that growth profile, and the new wires business is a late, small entry into a market Polycab/Havells/KEI/Finolex already dominate - optionality, but also a fresh execution test for a management team whose M&A track record (Butterfly) argues for caution, not credit in advance.
