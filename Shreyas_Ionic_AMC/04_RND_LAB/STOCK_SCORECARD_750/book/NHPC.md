## Bull case

NHPC is a rare-scale, GoI-backed hydro platform with 9,333 MW operating and, more importantly, 9,204 MW (17 projects) already under construction - a near-doubling of capacity with funding largely secured, plus a further ~20,000 MW across clearance/survey stages and a large pumped-storage MoU pipeline that positions the company well for India's dispatchable clean-power buildout through 2030-2032. Regulated cost-plus tariffs under CERC norms give revenue visibility once projects are commissioned and rated, leverage is moderate (D/E 1.26x, interest coverage 3.68x) for the capex load being carried, and the balance sheet/GoI backing gives it privileged access to long-tenor, low-cost project finance that IPP peers do not have.

## Bear case

The core operating engine is weaker than the headline growth suggests: Q4 FY26 other income alone (Rs 305 cr) exceeded profit before tax, and a tax reversal did much of the heavy lifting on the reported 68-71% PAT jump - strip those out and the underlying generation business grew far more modestly. A meaningful slice of FY26 revenue (Rs 1,278 cr) is a provisional CERC tariff estimate on two newly-commissioned projects (Parbati-II, Subansiri Lower) that remains subject to a final tariff order, carrying real clawback risk. ROE (~10%) and ROCE (~7%) are subpar for a ~21x earnings multiple that is already above the stock's own 5-year average, and the growth pipeline the market is implicitly paying for is concentrated in Himalayan/Northeast hydro sites with a well-documented history of multi-year execution delays (geology, R&R, forest clearance, law-and-order) - so the 9,204 MW under-construction book should not be underwritten on a straight-line timeline. Momentum is negative across every window we track (3m/6m/12m/24m all down), consistent with the market re-rating the stock down from its earlier PSU-power froth.

## Detailed rationale

NHPC is India's largest hydropower generator (GoI Navratna PSU, ~70% government-owned), operating 9,333 MW across 31 stations (incl. subsidiaries/JVs) as of FY26-end, predominantly hydro with a growing solar/PSP tail. Revenue is earned mostly under CERC cost-plus regulated tariffs via long-term PPAs to state discoms/bulk buyers, so the business is closer to a rate-regulated utility than a merchant power play [DATA]. FY26 was the company's highest-ever capacity-addition year: 1,850 MW commissioned (750 MW Subansiri Lower, 800 MW Parbati-II, 300 MW Karnisar solar). Full-year consolidated PAT rose to Rs 4,220 cr (FY25: Rs 3,412 cr, +24.6%); Q4 FY26 PAT jumped ~68-71% YoY to a figure reported as Rs 1,549 cr by one wire and Rs 1,460 cr by another (source discrepancy noted, both consolidated) [DATA]. The headline growth needs discounting: in Q4 FY26, other income of Rs 305 cr equalled 106.85% of PBT (per Marketsmojo's result read, titled 'Tax Reversal Masks Underlying Operational Weakness'), meaning core generation economics were roughly breakeven-to-negative at the PBT line and the reported jump was carried by treasury/other income plus a tax reversal, not operating leverage [DATA/INFERENCE]. At the full-year level other income was Rs 1,349 cr (30% of PBT) - a smaller but still structurally large share of the profit base. Separately, NHPC recognized Rs 1,278.44 cr of PROVISIONAL sales revenue in FY26 on the two newly-commissioned large projects (Parbati-II, Subansiri Lower) pending final CERC tariff orders - a standard-but-real regulatory-lag risk: if the final tariff comes in below the provisional rate, a future-period revenue/EPS clawback follows. This is a recognized feature of Indian hydro-utility accounting, not a one-off scandal, but it means a chunk of FY26's 'growth' is a rate estimate, not a settled number [DATA/INFERENCE]. On returns, ROE is 10.2% and ROCE 7.2% [DATA] - mediocre for a stock trading near 21x trailing earnings (vs its own 5-yr average PE of ~19.5x per third-party trackers), and structurally capped because CERC's approved RoE on the regulated-equity component of hydro tariffs runs well above what flows through to consolidated ROE once construction-phase assets, JV drag and large idle-cash balances are blended in. The bull case is real but is a multi-year execution story, not a near-term one: 17 projects (9,204 MW) are under construction - a fully-funded, near-100%-of-current-base pipeline that would roughly double capacity by ~FY29-30 if delivered on time - plus 9 projects (10,263 MW) at clearance stage and 9 more (9,830 MW) at survey/investigation stage, and a long-dated aspiration of 16,700 MW renewables by 2030 and 50+ GW by 2047, including a 20.8 GW pumped-storage MoU pipeline (~Rs 62,400 cr) that is still at JV/DPR stage with no committed capex schedule [DATA]. Indian hydro execution history (Subansiri itself took ~2 decades from clearance to commissioning; Northeast/Himalayan sites carry geological, R&R and law-and-order delay risk) argues for discounting the pipeline's near-term contribution meaningfully rather than underwriting the full MW count on a straight timeline [INFERENCE]. Balance sheet leverage (D/E 1.26x, interest coverage 3.68x) is unremarkable for a utility funding a large committed capex program via GoI-linked/taxable bond access - not a distress signal, but not a reason to pay up either.
