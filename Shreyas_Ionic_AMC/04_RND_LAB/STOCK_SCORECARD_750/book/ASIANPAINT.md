# ASIANPAINT — Asian Paints Ltd.

> [!summary]
> Asian Paints' Q4FY26 recovery (PAT +69% YoY, double-digit decorative volume growth, margin expansion to 19.4% consolidated PBDIT) is real in magnitude but substantially explained by an easy year-ago comp (Q4FY25 had a Rs 183cr exceptional impairment and a genuine demand trough) and raw-material cost deflation that management says has more room to run into H2FY27 - not clear proof the two-year Birla Opus share war has been won back. Decorative volume still grew faster than value in the same 'recovery' quarter, tier-2/3 share loss to Birla Opus continued (Opus +7pp, AP -5pp), a newly Akzo-acquired JSW Dulux adds a fourth well-funded competitor from December 2025, and the CCI's abuse-of-dominance investigation (opened July 2025, Supreme Court declined to stay it in October 2025) remains unresolved. At ~59x trailing earnings - essentially unchanged from AP's pre-disruption historical premium of 55-75x - a reverse-DCF implies the market is pricing ~9-10% perpetual growth against 5.1% delivered in FY26 and a 1.0% trailing 3-year CAGR. That gap, layered on a structurally tougher four-player market and an open regulatory overhang, is not adequately priced in. Sell, with the honest caveat that this is a real judgment call: Q4 was a genuine beat and sell-side targets are split, not uniformly bearish.

## Recommendation — Sell

Sell, on balance, though I want to be honest this is closer to a genuine judgment call than a clean-cut case. The quant score (Hold, composite 43.9/45.4, quality 80.8th percentile but value only 30.6th percentile) is reading the same tension I am: balance-sheet and profitability quality remain excellent (ROE 23.9%, ROCE 31.2%, D/E 0.18, zero red flags, GREEN bs_flag) but the stock is priced for a growth rate the underlying franchise is no longer delivering. My own reverse-DCF puts the market's implied perpetual growth assumption at ~9-10% against a trailing 3-year CAGR of just 1.0%, FY26 revenue growth of 5.1%, and my own forward estimate of 6-8% - a real and, in my view, insufficiently priced gap, made worse by three factors the Q4FY26 'beat' narrative glosses over: (1) the quarter's ease is substantially base-effect (Q4FY25 was a genuine trough with an exceptional impairment) and raw-material-deflation driven, not proof of regained pricing power; (2) decorative volume growth still outran value growth in the same quarter, meaning AP is still buying volume with discounting even as raw-material costs partially reverse; and (3) the competitive structure has gotten harder, not easier, with Birla Opus still taking share in tier-2/3 towns and a newly-capitalized JSW Dulux now a fourth serious player, while the CCI's abuse-of-dominance investigation remains open and unresolved. None of this is a balance-sheet or governance concern - it is a valuation-versus-structural-growth mismatch at a multiple (~59x) that has barely moved from AP's old near-monopoly premium despite the monopoly demonstrably no longer existing.

## Bull case

The fundamentals that are not in dispute: Asian Paints remains the largest organised decorative-paint player in India by a wide margin (still roughly 50%+ share even after two years of disruption), carries essentially no balance-sheet risk (D/E 0.18, interest cover 34.4x, GREEN bs_flag, zero red flags), and generates best-in-class returns (ROE 23.9%, ROCE 31.2%). Q4FY26 was a genuinely broad-based beat - consolidated PAT up 69.3% YoY to Rs 1,172 crore, revenue up 11%, decorative volume growth of 12.4%, consolidated PBDIT margin up 220bps to 19.4% - and management has maintained rather than cut its 18-20% EBITDA margin guidance. Diversification away from the core decorative battleground is working: international business grew net sales 8.9% to Rs 3,339.7 crore in FY26 with PBT nearly doubling to Rs 266.4 crore (Sri Lanka, Egypt, UAE strength), and both industrial-coatings joint ventures (PPG Asian Paints, Asian Paints PPG) delivered double-digit Q4 growth at healthy margins, an orthogonal growth engine largely insulated from the decorative price war. The premium PreLux range is reportedly performing well, supporting a genuine premiumization lever alongside new-product innovation. Sell-side opinion is not uniformly bearish either - Nomura (Rs 3,600), Systematix (Rs 3,105) and ICICI Direct (Rs 3,055) all carry price targets meaningfully above the current ~Rs 2,690-2,700, and the stock has already rallied roughly 25% off its 52-week low on the back of the Q4 print, indicating real institutional conviction that the worst of the share-loss story is behind it.

## Bear case

The core decorative franchise is facing a structurally harder competitive environment than at any point in its listed history, and the Q4FY26 'recovery' does not, on close inspection, prove that has changed. The comp was unusually easy - Q4FY25 PAT had fallen 45% YoY on a genuine demand trough (decorative volume growth of only 1.8% against a 5.2% revenue decline) plus a Rs 183 crore one-off impairment on White Teak/Causeway Paints and an Indonesia disposal loss - and a large share of the Q4FY26 margin expansion is attributed by management itself to raw-material deflation (crude at $75-80/bbl, ~1.4% material cost deflation) that is explicitly guided to continue benefiting H1-H2FY27, a cost tailwind rather than proof of regained pricing power. Decorative volume growth (12.4%) still outpaced value growth (10.2%) in the same quarter - AP is still discounting/mixing down to defend volume even as the cost backdrop eased, and in tier-2/3 towns specifically Birla Opus continued taking share (+7pp) while Asian Paints gave up ~5pp, meaning the bleed has not stopped, only relocated to a less-visible part of the market. Birla Opus itself shows no sign of having peaked - it has built out 21% of India's organised decorative manufacturing capacity in under two years, reached 30,000+ outlets across 10,400+ towns, and is growing at roughly 3x the industry rate on better dealer margins, 20-day credit terms (versus AP's 5-day) and 10%-free-paint promotions funded by Rs 10,000 crore of committed Aditya Birla Group capital. JSW Paints' completed ~$1.64 billion acquisition of Akzo Nobel India (December 2025, rebranded JSW Dulux in March 2026) adds a fourth well-capitalized competitor to what used to be a comfortable AP-led oligopoly. The CCI's investigation into alleged dealer-coercion tactics by Asian Paints (opened July 2025 on a Grasim/Birla Opus complaint; Supreme Court declined to stay the probe in October 2025) remains open with no concluded DG report found, an unresolved legal and reputational tail risk. And despite all of this playing out over more than two years, the stock trades at ~59x trailing earnings - essentially within Asian Paints' own pre-disruption historical premium band of 55-75x - meaning almost none of the valuation de-rating that a genuinely broken near-monopoly might warrant has actually occurred.

## Detailed rationale

Asian Paints' Q4FY26 print (declared 29 May 2026) is genuinely strong on the surface - consolidated PAT up 69.3% YoY to Rs 1,172 crore, revenue up 11%, domestic decorative volume growth of 12.4% with value growth of 10.2%, standalone PBDIT margin at 18.9% (vs 17.8%) and consolidated at 19.4% (vs 17.2%), gross margin up 87bps to 44.8% - and the Street has read it as proof the two-year Birla Opus share-loss story has stabilized, with the stock bouncing roughly 25% off its 52-week low of ~Rs 2,115 into the current ~Rs 2,690-2,700 (17 July 2026 close, mcap ~Rs 2,56,000-2,60,700 crore). I want to be careful about how much of that recovery is organic versus mechanical. First, the comp is unusually easy: Q4FY25 was a genuine trough quarter - PAT down 45% YoY to Rs 692 crore, revenue down 4.3%, decorative volume growth of just 1.8% against a 5.2% revenue decline (severe downtrading as customers shifted to cheaper brands), margins down 210-220bps, plus a Rs 183 crore exceptional impairment charge on the White Teak/Causeway Paints goodwill and an Indonesia-disposal loss. A 69% PAT jump off that base is arithmetic as much as recovery. Second, management's own commentary attributes a large share of the Q4FY26 margin expansion to raw-material deflation (crude stabilizing in the $75-80/bbl range, ~1.4% material cost deflation) rather than pricing power regained, and guidance is for the benefit to continue into H1FY27 with the 'larger part' landing in H2FY27 - i.e., a cost tailwind still working through the P&L, not evidence the competitive dynamic has reversed. Third, and most tellingly for my volume-vs-price framework: decorative volume growth (12.4%) still outran value growth (10.2%) in the very quarter being read as the turnaround - a ~2pp negative price/mix drag that persisted even as full-year FY26 revenue grew a modest 5.1% to Rs 35,583.5 crore (decorative contributing Rs 30,830.4 crore, 86.8% of sales). That is consistent with AP still buying volume with discounting/mix-down to defend share against a much better-funded new entrant, not a sign pricing power has stabilized.

The competitive structure itself has gotten harder, not easier, over the review period. Birla Opus (Grasim/Aditya Birla Group) has gone from a standing start to an estimated 10%+ decorative revenue share and high-single-digit volume share in under two years, built out 21% of India's organised decorative-paint manufacturing capacity, reached 30,000+ retail outlets and 10,400+ towns, and is growing at roughly 3x the industry rate - none of which reads like a disruptor that has plateaued. Its playbook is structural, not promotional: dealer margins that outdo incumbents, extended 20-day credit versus Asian Paints' 5-day terms, a 10%-free-paint offer, and Rs 10,000 crore of initial committed capital. In tier-2/3 towns specifically, Opus has taken roughly 7 points of share while Asian Paints has given up about 5, even as AP holds up better in premium urban markets - the bleed has not stopped, it has just moved to a part of the market that reports less visibly. Layered on top, JSW Paints completed its ~$1.64 billion (€1.4bn) acquisition of a 61.2% stake in Akzo Nobel India in December 2025 and rebranded it JSW Dulux in March 2026, instantly creating a well-capitalized fourth major player - the industry has gone from a comfortable AP-led oligopoly to a genuinely contested four-way fight between two incumbents (AP, Berger) and two deep-pocketed conglomerate entrants (Birla, JSW) within an 18-month window. And the CCI's investigation into Asian Paints - opened 1 July 2025 on a Grasim/Birla Opus complaint alleging AP pressured dealers into exclusivity via foreign trips, discounts, reduced credit and delayed service for anyone stocking Birla Opus - remains open; the Supreme Court declined to stay the Director-General's probe in October 2025 and I found no evidence the DG report has since been concluded or its findings published. That is a live, unresolved regulatory tail risk, and the alleged conduct itself (fighting for shelf space through dealer coercion rather than economics) is a tell that the distribution moat is being defended by muscle in places where Opus's better dealer economics are winning on the merits. On the diversification side, the picture is genuinely better: international business grew net sales 8.9% to Rs 3,339.7 crore with PBT more than doubling to Rs 266.4 crore (Sri Lanka, Egypt, UAE strong), and both industrial coatings JVs grew double-digit in Q4 (PPG Asian Paints +20.9% to Rs 586.1 crore at a 12.2% PBT margin; Asian Paints PPG +15% to Rs 399.1 crore) - real bright spots, but together still under 10% of consolidated revenue and not large enough to offset the core decorative dynamic.

On valuation, the part that tips my judgment: at ~59x trailing consolidated earnings, Asian Paints has barely de-rated at all from its own 2015-2022 historical premium band of roughly 55-75x, despite more than two years of proven, capacity-backed structural share loss to entrants that did not exist in that earlier era. Running a simple Gordon-style reverse-DCF - P/E of ~59x, an estimated ~70-80% payout ratio (AP is a mature, high-payout franchise with ROE of 23.9%, so a low retention/low-reinvestment assumption is reasonable), and an ~11.5% cost of equity - solves to the market pricing something like 9-10% perpetual growth. That is a much larger ask than what has actually been delivered: 5.1% FY26 revenue growth, a 1.0% trailing 3-year revenue CAGR per the quant snapshot (the worst multi-year stretch in the company's listed history), and my own forward estimate of roughly 6-8% annualized over the next three years (see below). Unlike HUL, where a comparable exercise showed the stock had already absorbed most of its de-rating (33x versus a historical 55-65x) and the implied-versus-delivered gap was moderate, Asian Paints is being asked to prove a much bigger growth story at a multiple that has not moved, against a competitive threat that is structurally more dangerous than anything HUL faces in D2C beauty (two industrial conglomerates building physical capacity, not venture-funded digital upstarts HUL can simply acquire). I recognize the counter-case is real and not fringe - Q4 was a genuinely broad-based beat, guidance of 18-20% EBITDA margin is intact, and the sell-side is split rather than uniformly bearish, with several targets (Nomura Rs 3,600, Systematix Rs 3,105, ICICI Direct Rs 3,055) well above CMP against others (JM Financial Rs 2,500 Reduce, Kotak Rs 2,900 Neutral) below or near it. But on my own read of the volume/price decomposition, the unresolved CCI overhang, and a multiple that has not repriced for a fourth well-funded competitor now in the market, I come down on the side that this is priced for a recovery to the old monopoly-era growth algorithm that the new market structure does not support - which is a Sell on an existing holding, not a portfolio-level panic call.
