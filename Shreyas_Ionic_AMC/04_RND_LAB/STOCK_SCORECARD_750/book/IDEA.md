# IDEA

> [!summary]
> Vodafone Idea's headline profit this year came from a one-time accounting gain tied to the government's AGR dues relief, not from the underlying business turning profitable -- excluding that gain, the company has posted a net loss every quarter, still carries negative net worth, and needs far more capital than it has raised so far to fund its network build-out. Subscriber and ARPU trends are improving, which is worth watching, but the balance-sheet risk keeps this a Sell.

## Recommendation — Sell

The quant score is Sell and nothing in the fundamentals argues for a rescue to Hold. The headline-cheap PE is entirely a function of a non-recurring AGR liability write-back, not real earnings power; strip it out and Vi has posted a net loss every single quarter for years, sits on negative net worth (~Rs 87,744 crore as of Dec-2025, materially improved but likely still negative even after the Q4 AGR gain), and faces a resumption of large spectrum payment obligations from FY27 with only token promoter capital raised so far against a stated Rs 50,000+ crore capex need. Operating metrics (ARPU, EBITDA margin, subscriber stabilisation) are genuinely improving, but that is a reason to monitor for a future rescue, not to override a balance-sheet-driven Sell today.

## Bull case

Vi is showing its first genuine operating green shoots in years: ARPU has risen for 19 consecutive quarters to Rs 190 (Q4 FY26), EBITDA margin has expanded to 43.1%, and net subscriber additions turned positive from February 2026 after a long period of attrition to Jio and Airtel. The April-2026 AGR reassessment (dues cut from Rs 87,695 crore to Rs 64,046 crore, with the residual NPV obligation deferred largely to FY32-FY41) is a real and durable reduction in near-term cash liability, not just an accounting entry, and the Government of India's continued equity backing (48.99% stake) signals a policy-level intent to keep the third private operator alive for competitive-market reasons.

## Bear case

None of the operating improvement changes the solvency math yet. Total debt (~Rs 2,09,000 crore as of Dec-2025) and negative net worth (~Rs 87,744 crore same date) dwarf the promoter's Rs 1,182 crore recent capital raise, and spectrum payment obligations step back up sharply from FY27 (Rs 7,000 crore) through FY29 (Rs 28,000 crore) just as the company also needs Rs 50,000-55,000 crore of 4G/5G capex to remain competitive. Free cash on hand was only Rs 3,715 crore at Mar-2026. The government has explicitly said it will not convert further dues to equity unless Vi exhausts other options -- there is no assured second backstop -- and the Q4 FY26 profit that re-rated the stock was ~99% attributable to a non-recurring AGR liability write-back rather than operations. This is still a company that loses Rs 5,000-6,600 crore of cash every ordinary quarter.

## Detailed rationale

Vodafone Idea is the weakest of India's three private wireless operators, still ceding share to Reliance Jio and Bharti Airtel while running a subscriber base of ~192.8mn (128.9mn 4G/5G) at end-Q4 FY26 (Mar-2026), per the company's own investor presentation. Reported ARPU has climbed for 19 straight quarters to Rs 190 in Q4 FY26 (from Rs 175 a year earlier) on the back of industry tariff hikes, and net subscriber adds turned positive from Feb-2026 for the first time in years -- a genuine, verifiable operating green shoot. But the P&L is dominated by a one-off: Q4 FY26 consolidated PAT of Rs 51,970 crore includes an exceptional gain of ~Rs 57,491-58,116 crore (sources vary slightly) from the DoT's April-2026 AGR reassessment, which cut Vi's AGR dues from Rs 87,695 crore to Rs 64,046 crore and let the company derecognise ~Rs 80,500 crore of liability against a revised NPV obligation of ~Rs 24,900 crore (payable in a deferred schedule from FY32-FY41). Strip that one-off out and the underlying business remains loss-making: Q1 FY26 net loss Rs 6,608 crore, Q2 FY26 Rs 5,524 crore, and a 9M-FY26 cumulative loss of Rs 17,418 crore against a negative net worth of Rs 87,744 crore as of Dec-2025. EBITDA is genuinely positive and improving (Q4 FY26: Rs 4,889 crore, margin 43.1%), but depreciation and finance costs on a still-enormous liability stack (total debt ~Rs 2,09,000 crore as of Dec-2025, before the AGR write-down; bank debt alone Rs 4,127 crore at Mar-2026) continue to overwhelm operating cash flow every quarter except the one-off quarter itself.

The balance sheet is the crux. Even after the AGR relief, spectrum payment obligations ramp back up (Rs 7,000 crore FY27, Rs 15,000 crore FY28, Rs 28,000 crore FY29) and 4G/5G capex commitments of Rs 50,000-55,000 billion (management's own guided range) are still largely unfunded -- the company's own recent capital raise was a promoter infusion of just Rs 1,182 crore, immaterial next to the need. The Government of India converted Rs 36,950 crore of dues into equity in April-2025 (raising its stake to ~48.99%), but has explicitly signalled it will not do so again unless Vi exhausts other options first -- there is no assured further backstop. Free cash on the balance sheet was only Rs 3,715 crore at Mar-2026. The quant score's near-zero PE (4.35 in the scorecard) is a pure earnings-quality artifact of the AGR one-off, not a signal the stock is cheap; ROE is blank/not meaningful because net worth is negative, and D/E of -5.38 in the data reflects that same negative-net-worth condition (a genuine solvency flag for a non-financial name, not a business-model feature to be waved through the way it would be for a bank/NBFC).

On recurring operations, revenue growth is real but modest: Q4 FY26 revenue Rs 11,332 crore (+2.88% YoY), Q1 FY26 Rs 11,008 crore (+5% YoY), consistent with the quant sheet's revenue_growth_1y (~3.0%) and revenue_cagr_3y (~2.1%) -- a company whose ARPU-led growth has barely offset years of subscriber attrition, only now stabilising. The bull case (tariff hikes continuing, subscriber losses arrested, EBITDA margin expansion) is credible and improving quarter over quarter, but it has to run for several more years, fully funded, before it dents a liability base this large. At the current market cap (~Rs 1.5 lakh crore per the scorecard), the equity is best understood as a leveraged, government-subsidised turnaround call option -- not a statistically cheap compounder -- and the quant Sell (final_score_3y/1y = 39, bs_flag RED) is well supported by the underlying data, not just a scoring artifact.
