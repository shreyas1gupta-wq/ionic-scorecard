# DLF — DLF Ltd.

> [!summary]
> DLF has the best balance sheet it has had in a decade — net cash of over Rs 14,000 crore, growing rental income from its Cyber City/DLF-GIC rental business, and a rising dividend. But its own management guided flat home-sales bookings for FY27 (around Rs 20,000 crore, same as FY26, which itself was down 5% from the prior year's record), and said plainly it will not chase higher volumes the way competitors are. The stock, however, trades at a rich ~40x earnings and ~3.6x book value, with the market pricing in growth or a rental-business listing (REIT) that management has not committed to a timeline for. Delhi-NCR's home-buying market overall was the weakest of any major Indian city in the first half of 2026, though DLF's own Gurugram corridor is holding up better than most. Good company, but the price is asking for more than the guidance promises — Sell.

## Recommendation — Sell

The call comes down to paying a growth/scarcity multiple (39-45x P/E, 3.6x P/B, 'Strong Buy' consensus with ~20% implied upside) for a business whose own management guidance is flat bookings for a second straight year, on a 3-year average ROE of only 9.3%. The balance sheet is genuinely excellent (net cash, rising dividend, capital discipline) and the rental annuity (DCCDL, +16% YoY) is a real, growing, high-quality asset — that is the case for Hold, not for a distressed call. But nothing in the current data supports paying today's multiple for tomorrow's earnings: the REIT catalyst that could justify a re-rating has no confirmed date, the residential upcycle that drove the FY24-25 re-rating has itself decelerated (FY26 bookings -5% YoY), and the broader NCR market is the weakest major metro in the country even if Gurugram specifically is holding up. On a Sell/Hold-only vocabulary, that valuation-vs-guidance gap is a Sell: this is a 'don't pay this multiple for what the company itself says it will deliver' call, not a 'the business is broken' call.

## Bull case

DLF ended FY26 with net cash of Rs 14,155 crore, its strongest balance sheet position in ten years, backed by record collections (Rs 13,500 crore, +15% YoY) and net cash generation of Rs 7,746 crore (+25% YoY). The rental annuity business, DCCDL (66.67% DLF-owned, GIC the balance), is a genuinely high-quality, growing asset: rental income rose 16% YoY to Rs 5,525 crore on 95%+ occupancy, with PAT up 38% YoY to Rs 2,726 crore, giving DLF a recurring cash stream that is largely insulated from residential-cycle swings. Management has explicitly chosen capital discipline over chasing volume (flat FY27 bookings guidance versus peers pushing higher), which, combined with the debt-free balance sheet and a 33%-higher dividend, is a defensible, shareholder-friendly posture. Its home geography, Gurugram's premium corridors, has also outperformed the rest of NCR, with prices up 13% YoY even as broader NCR sales declined, and the luxury (>Rs 2 crore) segment continues to be supported by resilient HNI/NRI demand. The long-discussed DCCDL REIT listing, prepped with Morgan Stanley/KPMG since 2020, remains a real (if undated) source of potential value unlock on top of all this.

## Bear case

The bear case is simple: the multiple has run ahead of the guidance. DLF trades at ~39-45x earnings and ~3.6x book value against a 3-year average ROE of just 9.3% and ROCE of 6.3% — multiples of a compounding growth story, not a company that just told the Street its FY27 pre-sales target is flat at ~Rs 20,000 crore, the same number it (barely) hit in FY26 after bookings actually fell 5% YoY off a record FY25 base that was itself driven by a single one-week, Rs 11,000 crore sellout (Privana North) unlikely to recur every year. Delhi-NCR overall was the weakest major residential market in India in H1 2026 (sales -7% YoY, Noida/Greater Noida launches down 72%), a real signal the post-pandemic luxury supercycle is maturing, even though DLF's specific Gurugram corridors have fared better. The FY27 pipeline carries real execution risk: the Goa ultra-luxury villa launch (part of the ~Rs 14,000-15,000 crore of new-launch sales guided) is stalled behind a public-interest litigation with no confirmed launch date, and The Dahlias, expected to contribute Rs 5,000-6,000 crore of FY27 sales, is reported only half sold through. The REIT catalyst that could justify today's multiple by crystallizing DCCDL's annuity value has been discussed since 2020 with no confirmed listing date — it is optionality the market is pricing as a near-certainty. And the Q4 FY26 print, where reported revenue missed Street estimates by roughly a third purely on completion-accounting timing even as collections and EPS beat, is a reminder that the P&L cannot be used to verify the growth the stock is priced for in real time.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At ~39-45x trailing P/E and ~3.6x book value, the market is pricing DLF for sustained double-digit growth in either residential bookings, rental annuity income, or both, plus a reasonable probability of a near-term DCCDL REIT monetization event. The business as management itself has guided it cannot clear that bar over the next 1-2 years: FY27 pre-sales guidance is flat at ~Rs 20,000 crore (0% guided growth), the FY26 print that got there was actually a 5% YoY decline off a one-off-inflated FY25 record, ROE (9.3% 3-yr avg) and ROCE (6.3%) are unremarkable for the multiple being paid, and the REIT listing that could justify a structural re-rating has no confirmed date. The only piece of the business genuinely compounding at a rate consistent with the multiple is the DCCDL rental annuity (+16% YoY), but that alone is not large enough within consolidated earnings to carry a 40x+ multiple on the whole company. Verdict: rich. The price implies more than the current guidance and cycle position can deliver.
