# SHREECEM — Shree Cement Ltd.

> [!summary]
> Shree Cement is a financially disciplined, debt-free cement maker that has grown capacity to around 70 million tonnes and posted healthy volume growth (+11%) in its just-reported March-2026 quarter. But profit for that quarter actually fell versus a year ago, as fuel, power and wage costs rose faster than pricing, and the company's per-tonne profitability - long its calling card - has slipped behind larger rival UltraTech for the first time in this comparison. At roughly 55-57 times trailing earnings, the stock trades well above peers, a premium that its current ~7.5% return on equity does not obviously justify, especially with cement pricing expected to stay flat through the monsoon and two much larger rivals (UltraTech, and the Adani-owned Ambuja/ACC group) continuing to add capacity faster than Shree can match. We rate this a Sell on valuation grounds, not a business-quality concern - the balance sheet and dividend track record remain genuinely strong.

## Recommendation — Sell

Sell over Hold because the valuation premium (~72% to peer-median PE, ~55-57x trailing earnings, ~18.5x EV/EBITDA) is not supported by the return profile (ROE ~7.5%, ROCE ~10.3%, both roughly half of Shree's own FY23 levels), and the traditional justification for that premium - cost leadership - has visibly eroded: Shree's Q4 FY26 EBITDA/tonne of Rs 1,125 now trails UltraTech's Rs 1,253 in the same quarter, a reversal of a gap that used to run the other way. Layer on a demand/pricing environment that dealer-channel checks describe as flat-to-soft through the monsoon quarter, and an industry structurally being squeezed into two megaplayers (UltraTech past 200 MTPA, the Adani-owned Ambuja/ACC platform consolidating toward ~140 MTPA by FY28) expanding capacity far faster than Shree's deliberately self-funded ~Rs 3,000 crore/year capex program - and the multiple is pricing in a margin/return recovery the last several quarters have not delivered (Q4 FY26 net profit fell 8.5% YoY despite 11% volume growth). The offsetting positives - net-cash balance sheet, rising dividend, genuine premiumization mix-shift, strong full-year profit growth off a weak FY25 base - are real and describe a well-run, conservatively capitalized business, but they describe Hold-quality operations at a Sell-level price. A Hold call would be more defensible if the multiple were closer to peer levels or if ROE/ROCE were recovering faster; neither condition holds today.

## Bull case

Shree remains one of the most conservatively run large-cap industrials in India - net cash of roughly Rs 6,000 crore, debt/EBITDA of just 0.55x, and zero promoter pledging, giving it full optionality to keep expanding without diluting shareholders or raising leverage. Full-year FY26 profit grew a strong 42.6% YoY to Rs 1,706 crore and the board raised total dividend 36% to Rs 150/share, evidence that cash generation is real even in a tough pricing year. Its "value-over-volume" premiumization strategy is showing up in the numbers - premium-branded cement rose to 22% of trade sales in Q3 FY26 from 15% a year earlier - a genuine, measurable step toward defending margin rather than chasing share in a price war it would struggle to win against much larger rivals. Green power now covers 61% of electricity needs (up from 59%) and every new kiln is being built with waste-heat recovery, a real structural cost tailwind as it compounds over the next several years of capacity additions toward ~72 MTPA by FY27.

## Bear case

The hardest number to explain away is per-tonne economics: Shree's Q4 FY26 EBITDA/tonne of Rs 1,125 is now below UltraTech's Rs 1,253 in the same quarter - a reversal of the cost-leadership gap that has historically been the entire justification for Shree's premium rating. Layer on ROE of just 7.5-7.7% and ROCE of 10.3% for FY26 (versus 13.5%/16.3% as recently as FY23), and the stock's ~55-57x trailing P/E and 18.5x EV/EBITDA - a ~72% premium to peer-median PE - looks like it is pricing in a margin and return recovery the last several quarters simply have not delivered; Q4 FY26 net profit actually declined 8.5% YoY despite 11% volume growth, as coal, pet-coke, power, freight and wage costs outran realizations. The company also faces a structural squeeze it is not trying to out-invest: UltraTech has already crossed 200 MTPA and the Adani-owned Ambuja/ACC platform is consolidating Orient, Sanghi and Penna cement toward ~140 MTPA by FY28, while Shree's own capex has been deliberately "scaled back" to a self-funded ~Rs 3,000 crore/year pace targeting just ~72 MTPA by FY27 - conceding the capacity race to two national players with far deeper pockets, right as near-term cement pricing looks flat-to-soft through the monsoon quarter.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At ~55-57x trailing earnings and ~18.5x EV/EBITDA (a ~72% premium to peer-median PE of roughly 30x), the market is implicitly underwriting a return to Shree's historical mid-teens ROE/ROCE (13.5%/16.3% in FY23) and a restoration of its cost-leadership gap versus UltraTech, sustained for years. What the business is actually delivering is ROE near 7.5-8%, ROCE near 10%, an EBITDA/tonne that now trails UltraTech's, and a capex program deliberately paced below the two consolidating megaplayers. Our own forward view is volume growth in the high-single-to-low-double digits (management's own FY27 guidance is ~10%, capacity growing ~65.8 to ~72 MTPA), with EBITDA/tonne likely range-bound (roughly Rs 1,100-1,250) rather than expanding, given industry overcapacity risk from Adani's and UltraTech's aggressive additions. That supports earnings growth in the low-double-digits at best over 3-5 years - well short of what a 55-57x multiple typically requires to compound into a reasonable forward return. Verdict: rich. The multiple looks like a legacy scarcity/quality premium that current operating data (declining Q4 profit, sub-UltraTech EBITDA/tonne, soft near-term pricing) is not yet re-earning.

## Detailed rationale

Shree Cement's operational picture as of the latest reported quarter (Q4 FY26, quarter ended March 2026) is a mix of genuine volume strength and quiet margin erosion. Capacity has reached roughly 70 MTPA following full commissioning of the integrated Kodla (Karnataka) plant in Feb-Mar 2026, on a path to ~72 MTPA by FY27 and a longer-dated, now-softening ~80 MTPA FY28-29E ambition that management itself is hedging pending demand and utilization (capacity utilization was only 66% in Q4 FY26, up from 56% in Q3 FY26). Q4 FY26 domestic cement sales rose 11% YoY to 10.56 MT, realizations improved to ~Rs 4,725/tonne, and EBITDA/tonne actually improved YoY (Rs 1,125 vs Rs 1,032 in Q4 FY25; Rs 1,161 for full FY26 vs Rs 1,071 in FY25). Yet Q4 FY26 net profit still fell YoY (~Rs 528-532 crore vs ~Rs 556 crore), because coal/pet-coke/power/freight/packaging cost inflation, rising employee costs (Rs 314.2 crore vs Rs 298.4 crore YoY) and elevated depreciation (Rs 762-788 crore/quarter, reflecting capacity built ahead of utilization) outran the realization and EBITDA/tonne gains. Full-year FY26 optics are stronger in isolation - PAT of Rs 1,706 crore vs Rs 1,196 crore in FY25 (+42.6%), total dividend raised 36% to Rs 150/share - but that comparison flatters against a weak FY25 base; FY26 ROE (~7.5-7.7%) and ROCE (~10.3%) remain well below Shree's own FY23 levels (13.5%/16.3%).

The more consequential change is competitive: Shree's premium rating was historically earned on genuine cost leadership (lowest-cost-producer reputation, WHRS-first capex, rising green-power mix - 61% of electricity in Q4 FY26, up from 59% YoY, with every new kiln paired to waste-heat recovery). That edge looks less durable now - UltraTech posted consolidated EBITDA/tonne of Rs 1,253 in the same Q4 FY26 (Rs 1,296 ex-acquired-asset drag), meaningfully ahead of Shree's Rs 1,125. Industry structure is also consolidating around two megaplayers at a pace Shree is not trying to match: the Adani-owned Ambuja/ACC platform (absorbing Orient Cement, with Sanghi and Penna cement lined up next) is headed toward ~118 MTPA now and a stated ~140 MTPA by FY28, while UltraTech crossed a 200 MTPA milestone in Q4 FY26 en route to a guided ~240 MTPA. Shree's own capex has been deliberately "scaled back" per its Q3 FY26 concall commentary (~Rs 3,000 crore/year, internally funded, no debt raise) - a prudent, balance-sheet-first stance (net cash ~Rs 6,000 crore, debt/EBITDA 0.55x, no promoter pledge) that nonetheless cedes scale-growth pace to the two national platforms. Management's stated response is a "value over volume" premiumization pivot - premium-branded products rose to 22% of trade sales in Q3 FY26 from 15% a year earlier - a sensible defensive strategy, but one that by construction trades volume share for margin defense, in a demand backdrop where dealer-channel checks through July 2026 point to flat-to-soft pricing through the monsoon (weak construction activity, labour shortages, slower government project execution, heatwaves).

On valuation: at a CMP of roughly Rs 26,500-26,600 (early-to-mid July 2026), market cap ~Rs 95,000-96,000 crore, Shree trades near 55-57x trailing P/E and ~18.5x EV/EBITDA - a reported ~72% premium to peer-median PE of roughly 30x. That premium sits awkwardly against an ROE of just ~7.5-7.7% and ROCE of ~10.3% for FY26. Sell-side coverage is genuinely split and clustered tightly around CMP: Citi retains Buy but cut its target to Rs 29,200 from Rs 31,650; ICICI Direct is Buy at Rs 33,140 (18.5x average FY27-28E EV/EBITDA); but Morgan Stanley is Underweight at Rs 25,500 (below CMP), ICICI Securities Hold at Rs 26,200 (essentially at CMP), and Axis Direct Hold at Rs 27,020 - signalling limited perceived margin of safety on either side of the current price among professional coverage. Q1 FY27 results are not yet out; the board meeting to consider them is scheduled for July 31, 2026.
