# VIPIND

> [!summary]
> VIP Industries just posted a Rs.338cr net loss for FY26 on revenue down 15%, with roughly Rs.130cr of that tied to a deliberate inventory clean-up by its new private-equity owner (Multiples Group took control from the Piramal family in mid-2025) - but even adjusting for that one-off, the core business is still losing money. The bigger issue is competitive: VIP's share of the organized luggage market has fallen from about 47% five years ago to roughly 29% today, while rival Safari Industries has overtaken it at ~32% share and is growing profitably. The balance sheet is stretched (debt nearly three times reserves) and next quarter's results (due by mid-August) are still pending. New management has guided to a revenue recovery in FY27, but volume is guided to grow faster than revenue, which itself signals continued discounting rather than a clean, margin-accretive turnaround. We rate this a Sell.

## Recommendation — Sell

The quant Sell is not an artifact of a single bad print or a one-off distortion I can credibly rescue: even stripping out the FY26 inventory-provision one-off, the underlying business remains loss-making, and the more important problem - organized market share collapsing from ~47% to ~29% while Safari overtakes at ~32% with a profitable, growing model - is a multi-year structural trend, not a one-time item. The balance sheet (D/E ~2.8x, negative interest coverage, reserves smaller than debt) adds real solvency-adjacent risk, and at ~2.3-2.7x EV/Sales the stock is not obviously cheap enough to compensate for an unproven, one-year-old PE-backed management team executing a turnaround against a stronger competitor. I affirm Sell rather than rescue to Hold.

## Bull case

There is a genuine, if early, turnaround setup: new professional ownership (Multiples PE) replaced a long-tenured promoter family in mid-2025, brought in a new CEO, and used the transition to front-load an aggressive inventory/channel clean-up (channel days down from 90+ to sub-60) that resets the base rather than dragging losses out over several quarters. Motilal Oswal has a Buy rating and Rs.475 target on the thesis that fresh capital, focus, and premiumization (Carlton, Skybags) can restore share and margins, and the stock already trades near its 52-week low, pricing in a good deal of the bad news. If the new management executes, FY27's guided high-single-digit revenue growth plus operating leverage off the FY26 trough could produce a sharp swing back to profitability.

## Bear case

The strongest bear point is that VIP is not just having a bad year, it is structurally losing the share war it used to lead: from ~47% of organized luggage share in CY20 to ~29% by Dec-2025, overtaken by Safari at ~32% - and Safari's growth (+15.7% revenue, +20% volume, ~14% EBITDA margin) shows the category is fine, VIP specifically is losing. Management's own FY27 guidance (double-digit volume growth but only high-single-digit revenue growth) is an implicit admission that recovering volume still requires discounting, which caps the margin-recovery story analysts are underwriting. Layer on a D/E of ~2.8x with negative interest coverage, ROE of -92%, an unproven management team barely a year into the job, and Q1 FY27 numbers not yet out (estimates already point to continued YoY decline) - the risk that this is a value-trap rerating rather than a genuine turnaround is real, and the current ~2.3-2.7x EV/Sales multiple is not cheap enough to make that risk worth taking.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At mcap ~Rs.4,300cr against FY26 sales of Rs.1,858cr, VIP trades at ~2.3x EV/Sales (~2.7x including net debt of ~Rs.738cr) for a business that is currently loss-making at both EBITDA and PAT level and structurally losing category share. That multiple only clears if the new PE-backed management restores VIP to something close to its historical high-single-digit-to-low-teens EBITDA margin within 2-3 years while simultaneously arresting the share slide against Safari - a double execution ask from a management team with under 12 months' track record. This is priced for a smooth turnaround the evidence does not yet support; growth estimate below is well under the 25% hypergrowth threshold so no ROE/dilution disclosure is triggered (ROE is deeply negative in any case, -92% LTM).

Q4 FY26 alone: sales ~Rs.436-437cr (down ~12% YoY), EBITDA loss ~Rs.82cr, net loss ~Rs.129cr - derived by differencing FY26 total vs 9M, consistent with the reported Q4 figures (revenue Rs.436.2cr, PAT -Rs.128.9cr) [DATA].

The structural story is a share war VIP is losing, not winning: organized-sector luggage share fell from ~47% in CY20 to ~29% by Dec-2025, while Safari Industries overtook VIP to ~32% share, growing revenue +15.7% YoY on +20% volume growth with EBITDA margins near 14% and positive cash generation [DATA, whalesbook.com Safari-vs-VIP comparison].
