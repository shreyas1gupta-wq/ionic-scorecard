# LTF

> [!summary]
> L&T Finance, the L&T-promoted retail NBFC, delivered another strong quarter (Q1 FY27, Jun-2026): consolidated profit up ~29-31% YoY, loan book up 27% YoY, and continued improvement in asset quality including in the microfinance book where industry-wide stress has been a concern. Capital and liquidity buffers are comfortable and the balance sheet shows no red flags. The main caveat is that the stock already trades at a rich valuation (roughly 23x earnings, ~2.6-2.8x book) that assumes management's 2031 targets (higher return on equity, lower credit costs) get delivered over the next few years - a reasonable but not yet proven bet. We rate it a Hold: good business, well-priced-to-perfection stock.

## Recommendation — Hold

Quant score (final_score_3y 52.0, final_score_1y 48.5) = Hold, and under the V1 asymmetric-override rule a Hold cannot be moved to Sell by analyst judgment - only Sell-to-Hold rescues are permitted. On the fundamentals, this is not remotely a Sell case: asset quality is improving on both gross and net Stage-3 metrics, the microfinance book (the industry's stress point) is outperforming peers by a wide margin, capital and liquidity are ample (CRAR 17.89%, LCR 325%), the promoter (L&T, 66% holding) is stable, and management is executing (imperfectly, but sequentially improving) against a credible, quantified 2031 roadmap. The genuine tension is valuation richness (23.5x PE / ~2.6-2.8x P/B against a 12.7% current ROE) versus a fundamentally sound but not-yet-fully-re-rated lender - that is a Hold, not an escalation-worthy Hold-vs-Sell coin flip.

## Bull case

Retail-led, 98%-retailised book growing 27% YoY with disbursement momentum (retail disbursements +36% YoY in Q1FY27, +39% for FY26) well ahead of the 20%+ Lakshya-2031 target. Asset quality is improving, not deteriorating - Gross Stage-3 down to 2.86% from 3.31% YoY, Net Stage-3 down to 0.90% from 0.99% YoY - and the rural JLG/microfinance segment specifically is outperforming the industry (96.5% 0-DPD vs 77.9% industry average, delinquency at ~21% of the industry average) at a time when microfinance stress has hurt several peers. Capital adequacy (CRAR 17.89%) and liquidity (LCR 325%) are both comfortable, and the promoter (Larsen & Toubro, 66.03% holding) provides parentage stability with no pledge or governance overhang identified. Management has laid out a specific, numbers-backed 2031 roadmap (20%+ book CAGR, <2% credit cost, 3.0-3.2% ROA, 16-18% ROE) and Q1 FY27 progress, while incomplete, is directionally consistent with it (NIM+fees 10.47% within guided band, credit cost improving sequentially).

## Bear case

The stock is not cheap: ~23.5x trailing PE and roughly 2.6-2.8x book against a current ROE of only 12.71% and ROA of 2.48% - both still below the Lakshya-2031 targets (16-18% ROE, 3.0-3.2% ROA) management is guiding toward. That gap has to close via credit-cost normalization (currently 2.54% vs a sub-2% target, guided to 2.0-2.2% only by Q4FY27) and further margin/opex leverage - none of which is guaranteed, and cost of funds is actually guided slightly higher for FY27 (7.35-7.40% vs 7.20% in Q1), a modest margin headwind. If credit costs plateau rather than fall, or if book growth decelerates from the current elevated 27% pace as the denominator grows, the current multiple - which is effectively pricing in successful execution of a multi-year re-rating story - would have real compression risk. The reported PAT growth also benefits from a still-favorable credit-cost base versus the FY24-25 microfinance-sector stress cycle; some of the YoY profit growth is normalization rather than pure structural improvement, and that tailwind fades as the base normalizes.

## Valuation (reverse-DCF judgment)

At ~23.5x trailing PE and ~2.6-2.8x P/B against a current ROE of 12.71% (below most estimates of NBFC cost of equity), the market is pricing meaningful execution of the Lakshya-2031 re-rating (ROE to 16-18%, credit cost to sub-2%) over the next 2-3+ years, not today's return profile. This is rich-to-fair rather than cheap: justified only if credit-cost normalization and margin/opex leverage actually deliver the guided ROA/ROE step-up; if execution stalls at current ROE levels the multiple has room to compress toward more NBFC-typical 1.5-2x book. Not flagging the >=25% growth disclosure requirement since our forward estimate below is in the 'strong' band, not hypergrowth.

Asset quality continues to improve: Gross Stage-3 2.86% (vs 3.31% a year ago), Net Stage-3 0.90% (vs 0.99% a year ago). CRAR a comfortable 17.89%, liquidity coverage ratio 325% - no balance-sheet-stress signal. FY26 (Mar-2026) full year: consolidated PAT Rs 3,003 cr (+14% YoY, stated 'before effect of Labour Code considered in Q3FY26' - that one-time provision was a modest ~Rs 21 cr hit at the LTF level in Q3FY26, i.e. NOT a material earnings-quality distortion despite the parent L&T itself taking a much larger Rs 1,191 cr Labour-Code hit - the two are separate line items, checked and cleared). FY26 retail book Rs 1,19,508 cr (+26% YoY), retail disbursements Rs 83,213 cr (+39% YoY, highest ever), retailisation 98% of book, GS3 improved to 2.88% from 3.29% YoY.

Management unveiled the 'Lakshya 2031' strategy (Apr-2026): an AI-led retail-lending roadmap targeting a ~Rs 3 lakh crore book by 2031, 20%+ compounded book growth, credit cost below 2%, ROA of 3.0-3.2%, ROE of 16-18%. Q1 FY27 progress is mixed-but-directionally-right: book growth (27% YoY) already exceeds the 20%+ target; but credit cost (2.54%) remains above the sub-2% target (management guides this down to 2.0-2.2% by Q4FY27), and ROA (2.48%) / ROE (12.71%) both remain below the eventual 3.0-3.2%/16-18% targets even though both improved sequentially. Cost of funds guided up slightly to 7.35-7.40% for FY27 from 7.20% in Q1 - a mild margin headwind that NIM guidance (10-10.5%) has already absorbed. The standout asset-quality data point is the rural JLG/microfinance book, where LTF posted 96.5% 0-DPD vs a 77.9% industry average and delinquency running at just ~21% of the industry average during a period when microfinance-sector stress has been a live sector-wide worry - this is a genuine underwriting-discipline signal, not noise (customer indebtedness caps of Rs 2 lakh, max-3-lenders association norm, 0-DPD-history requirement for fresh sourcing).

Valuation: share price Rs 305.85 (20-Jul-2026), market cap ~Rs 77,688 cr (matches the quant sheet's Rs 77,596 cr), trailing PE ~23.5x, P/B in the ~2.6-2.8x range per standard broker/screener multiples (one aggregator showed an outlier P/B of 1.11 on the same date, which is inconsistent with peer-comparable sources and is treated as data noise, not used). At current ROE of ~12.7%, a 2.6-2.8x P/B is a materially forward-priced multiple - it only makes sense if Lakshya-2031 execution (mid-teens+ ROE, sub-2% credit cost) actually lands over the next 2-3 years. The stock is thus pricing meaningful execution success already; a stall in credit-cost normalization or ROE re-rating is the main multiple-compression risk, not a solvency or asset-quality risk given CRAR/liquidity/promoter backing are all solid.
