## (a) THE MANAGER / FRAMEWORK SECTIONS

### WESTERN

#### W1 — Terry Smith / Fundsmith
- **Rule:** ROCE ≥15% cycle-average, **unleveraged** (ROOCE variant); cash conversion ≥95% of operating profit; high,
  stable gross margin (~62% actual); interest cover — avoid <10×. Hard sector exclusions: banks/financials/insurance/
  real-estate/commodities/utilities/airlines/heavy-capex/heavy-M&A-growth.
- **Sizing:** 20–30 holdings; historically ~2–3%/yr turnover ("do nothing").
- **Sell discipline:** thesis break / valuation indefensible / distinctly better opportunity — **no numeric rule.**
- **Best regime:** falling-rate decade / quality-outperformance. **Worst:** rate-rise / quality-underperformance.
- **[HONESTY FLAG]** 5 straight years lagging MSCI World (2021–2026). **July 2026: Smith publicly capitulated** — will
  now "account for momentum"; 6-month turnover spiked to **51.8%** vs historic 2–3%; swapped 13 holdings for 12 new
  (AppLovin/Mastercard/Netflix/TSMC/Uber IN; Novo Nordisk/Nike/LVMH/Unilever OUT). The "quality + do nothing" doctrine
  is **regime-dependent** — built in a falling-rate decade, broke under a rate-rise regime.

#### W2 — Nick Sleep / Nomad
- **Rule:** "Scale Economics Shared" flywheel (scale savings passed to customers → reciprocation → more scale);
  "destination analysis" — underwrite end-state economics, not the current multiple.
- **Sizing:** radically concentrated (~10 or fewer positions); near-zero turnover (~260-week avg hold vs ~20-week market).
- **Sell discipline:** almost none — thesis-break only.
- **Best regime:** long secular-compounder runways. **Worst:** untested — closed before any adverse regime.
- **[HONESTY FLAG]** 921% vs MSCI World 117% (2001–2014), but **closed voluntarily at a market peak in 2014** — no
  post-2014 drawdown test; record is path-dependent on 2–3 winners (Amazon especially). "Fast rabbit/slow rabbit" term
  could NOT be verified in primary sources — flag as unconfirmed.

#### W3 — Mohnish Pabrai / Dhandho
- **Rule:** 9 tenets — existing businesses, slow-change simple industries, distressed businesses/industries, durable
  moat, bet heavy when odds favor you, arbitrage, margin of safety, low-risk/high-uncertainty asymmetry, clone don't innovate.
- **Sizing:** fractional/quarter-Kelly in practice, ~10% max per position, 5–10 core ideas.
- **Sell discipline — MOST EXPLICIT OF ALL SIX WESTERN:** won't sell at a loss within 2–3 years of buying UNLESS
  conviction that intrinsic value is now below price; **the "3-year rule"** — still underwater after 3 years = you
  misjudged intrinsic value, take the loss without hesitating; sell when price ≥ intrinsic value.
- **Best regime:** distressed/mean-reversion. **Worst:** correlated crashes (concentration + distressed tilt).
- **[HONESTY FLAG]** Pabrai Funds fell ~60%+ in 2008–09; many investors capitulated near the bottom.

#### W4 — Li Lu / Himalaya
- **Rule:** circle of competence built narrow/slow; "long-lost uncle died and left you the company" ownership-mindset
  test; Graham margin-of-safety + Munger/Buffett quality synthesis.
- **Sizing:** highly concentrated (~5 core holdings; direction not stated).
- **Sell discipline:** thesis break or price >> intrinsic value — **least documented of all six.**
- **Best regime:** long-horizon quality-value. **Worst:** China-concentration risk not fully stress-tested in public record.
- **[HONESTY FLAG]** lightly stress-tested public record; China concentration under-examined.

#### W5 — Philip Fisher (*Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits*)
- **Rule:** 15-point qualitative growth checklist (revenue runway, management renewal, R&D effectiveness, sales org,
  margins, labor/exec relations, management depth, cost controls, moat, long-range profit view, dilution, candor in bad
  times, integrity). Scuttlebutt method (competitors/customers/suppliers/ex-employees).
- **Sizing:** concentrated growth (not numerically specified in batch).
- **Sell discipline — THE 3 AND ONLY 3 REASONS TO SELL:** (1) original purchase was a mistake; (2) company no longer
  passes the 15 points (usually management deterioration or growth runway matured); (3) distinctly better opportunity
  (high bar). **Explicitly NOT reasons to sell:** stock went up a lot; fear of a market decline; high P/E alone.
- **Best regime:** durable-growth compounders. **Worst:** management/runway deterioration (which triggers the sell).
- **[HONESTY FLAG]** relies heavily on qualitative scuttlebutt not reproducible from data.

#### W6 — Charlie Munger (*Poor Charlie's Almanack*)
- **Rule:** latticework of ~80–100 mental models; written/mental pre-investment checklist (counters ~25 standard causes
  of misjudgment); "invert, always invert" (ask how to guarantee failure, avoid that); two-track analysis (rational +
  psychological/incentive factors).
- **Sizing:** extreme low turnover ("sit on your ass" investing).
- **Sell discipline:** thesis-break or genuinely superior redeployment only.
- **Best regime:** high-quality compounders held for decades. **Worst:** none disclosed (doctrine, not a fund record).
- **[HONESTY FLAG]** a doctrine, not an audited return series — no drawdown record to stress.

### INDIAN

#### I1 — Raamdeo Agrawal / MOSL (QGLP)
- **Rule:** QGLP is **MULTIPLICATIVE** (any factor = 0 → output = 0). Quality (business + management), Growth (earnings
  growth × valuation re-rating; explicit warning vs "quality traps" = great business / sluggish growth), Longevity (CAP
  = Competitive Advantage Period, delays mean reversion), Price (comes LAST — "price covers a multitude of mistakes"),
  Size (added later — small under-covered names).
- **Sizing:** ~20–25 names; no published hard cap.
- **Sell discipline — WEAKEST documented for him:** implied thesis-break only (Q, G, or L turns off); no explicit checklist.
- **Best regime:** trending bull / quality-growth over 10–12yr holds. **Worst:** deep-value/mean-reversion regimes, and
  when you overpay ("P comes last" is his own documented risk — great businesses at 40–80× can produce a lost decade).
- **[HONESTY FLAG]** the 47 "enduring 100×" winners (avg ₹300cr rev / ₹250cr mkt-cap at entry, 1994–2014, 12yr avg hold,
  47% CAGR) are **ex-post-selected = survivorship**; he concedes "transitory 100×" fads (Satyam, Unitech) in the same study.

#### I2 — Prashant Jain (ex-HDFC AMC)
- **Rule:** buy cheap-relative-AND-absolute, reasonable-quality/reasonable-management businesses that are out of favor;
  willing to be VERY concentrated against the benchmark (PSU banks/financials 4.8% of Nifty but 18–22% fund weight, Dec-2014).
- **Sizing:** high benchmark-relative concentration in out-of-favor sectors.
- **Sell discipline — BEST-DOCUMENTED OF ANY MANAGER IN EITHER BATCH:** bought consumer stocks 2007 at <20×, STARTED
  SELLING as they hit ~40× — an explicit **valuation-ceiling round-trip (buy cheap, sell rich)**; the mirror image of Maheshwari.
- **Best regime:** value / mean-reversion + post-capitulation. **Worst:** sustained narrow quality/growth-led bulls.
- **[HONESTY FLAG]** 2018–2020 his PSU/corporate-bank/value bets badly lagged for 2+ years under sustained criticism
  before working. Long-run ~17.9% CAGR (1994–2022) vs Sensex ~9.6%, but the beat was uneven/concentrated in windows.

#### I3 — Basant Maheshwari
- **Rule:** growth-at-any-reasonable-price, AGGRESSIVELY growth-tilted — buys high growth "irrespective of valuation"
  because high growth always carries a premium P/E. Criteria: ROE >30%, regular dividend, sales growth 25–30% sustained
  5–6 years. Explicit "10× mandate" (won't buy unless 10× possible).
- **Key mechanism — THE OPPOSITE OF JAIN:** pays UP for growth and profits from P/E **re-rating UP** as growth continues
  (buy a 40%-grower at 40×; if growth persists P/E expands to 70–90× — "that's where the money is made").
- **Sizing:** concentrated; comfortable with **leverage** conditional on risk management.
- **Sell discipline:** weak/qualitative — "move out at first sign of trouble"; implied sell = growth decelerating (which
  reverses the re-rating engine).
- **Best regime:** growth-momentum bulls. **Worst:** de-rating/bear markets.
- **[HONESTY FLAG]** STRUCTURALLY FRAGILE in de-rating markets: concentration + leverage + high-P/E growth is a
  triple-negative when multiples contract.

#### I4 — Porinju Veliyath (Equity Intelligence PMS)
- **Rule:** bottom-up value + turnaround/special-situations in small/micro-caps — undervalued + under-tracked +
  improving-management. Independent rater's caveat: label says "quality at a discount," revealed behavior is "aggressive
  investor willing to take higher risk" — **believe revealed behavior, not the label.**
- **Sizing:** concentrated small/micro-cap; no per-name rule (illiquidity IS the concentration risk).
- **Sell discipline — EFFECTIVELY ABSENT:** held through the 2018–2020 collapse with no evident stop/de-risk rule;
  explanation was regime-based ("challenging phase"), not a triggered exit.
- **Best regime:** small-cap bull only. **Worst:** small/mid-cap busts (structurally fragile).
- **[HONESTY FLAG] THE CLEANEST CAUTIONARY CASE IN THE STUDY:** ~45% CAGR at peak → "worst performer" within months;
  Q1FY19 −18.54% while indices positive; Sept-2018 −20% month; losses in 10 of 14 months (Jan-2018→Feb-2019); related
  EQ India AIF −25.22% in FY19 vs Nifty +14.93%. Driver = 2018–19 small/mid crash hit his exact tier with **zero de-risking.**

## (b) UNIFIED CROSS-CUTTING RULES (convergent evidence = highest confidence)

Merged from both batches' synthesis paragraphs. **[MY CALL]** on the AGREE/DIFFER split.

### Where Western and Indian sets AGREE (robust, transferable — size your confidence here)

1. **Quality anchored on high returns on capital + cash conversion.** Smith (ROCE≥15% unlevered, cash conv ≥95%),
   Munger/Fisher (quality + margins + moat), Agrawal (Q in QGLP), Maheshwari (ROE>30%). Convergent across ≥5 independent
   managers in two markets — the single most-agreed entry anchor. **Western adds the sharper spec: UNLEVERED returns +
   cash conversion** (guards against leverage-flattered ROE and accrual-flattered profit — exactly our `quality_cfo_pat` logic).
2. **Radical concentration paired with a NARROW circle of competence.** Sleep (~10), Li Lu (~5), Pabrai (5–10),
   Munger, Agrawal (20–25), Porinju (concentrated). Both sets reject diversification-for-its-own-sake. Corollary both
   sets share: concentration is only safe *inside a narrow circle* — Porinju is the cautionary case of concentration
   OUTSIDE a de-riskable circle (illiquid micro-caps).
3. **Very low turnover with THESIS-BREAK (not price-or-fear) selling as the DEFAULT.** Smith (2–3%/yr historic), Sleep
   (near-zero), Munger ("sit on your ass"), Fisher (sell only for 3 reasons, explicitly NOT because price rose or you
   fear a decline), Agrawal (12yr holds). The default exit in BOTH sets is "sell only when the thesis breaks."
4. **Explicit pre-mortem / inversion / checklist to kill bad ideas BEFORE sizing.** Munger (inversion + 25-misjudgment
   checklist), Pabrai (Dhandho tenets as a gate), Fisher (15-point checklist). A structured *disqualifier* precedes the
   position — you earn the right to size by first trying to kill the idea.

### Where they genuinely DIFFER (regime-dependent — do NOT treat as universal)

5. **The valuation engine splits HARD — and the split is sharper in the Indian set.** Two opposite, both-profitable engines:
   - **Buy-cheap-SELL-RICH (sell INTO P/E expansion):** Jain (buy ~20× → sell ~40×) is the cleanest exemplar in either
     batch. Pabrai (sell at price ≥ intrinsic value) is the Western cousin.
   - **Buy-FOR-P/E-expansion (ride the re-rating UP):** Maheshwari (buy 40× → ride to 70–90×). Sleep's "destination
     analysis" (pay up for end-state economics) is the nearest Western relative, but no Western manager here is a
     Maheshwari-style leveraged high-P/E rider.
   - Agrawal sits between (growth × re-rating, but "P comes last"); Porinju has the loosest valuation discipline of all.
   - **The Western set is mostly Buffett/quality-school** (pay a fair price for durable quality and hold), so it does NOT
     display the Indian set's sharp buy-cheap-vs-buy-momentum schism. **The two engines win in OPPOSITE regimes** — Jain
     in value/mean-reversion + post-capitulation, Maheshwari/Agrawal in growth-momentum bulls. A doctrine that is silent
     about which regime it is in will be blindsided (see §c gap 3, Smith 2026).
6. **Sell-discipline EXPLICITNESS is the rarest, highest-value differentiator.** Ranking best→worst across BOTH batches:
   **Jain (explicit valuation-ceiling round-trip) ≈ Pabrai (numeric 3-year rule) ≈ Fisher (3 reasons) > Smith/Munger/
   Sleep/Li Lu/Agrawal/Maheshwari (implied thesis-break only) > Porinju (effectively none).** Only 3 of 10 managers give
   an operational sell rule; the failures (Marcellus quality-trap per R2; Porinju's un-de-risked −25% year) cluster on
   the no-explicit-sell-rule end. **This is the single most valuable element to FORMALIZE** — it is also the biggest
   ALPHA_RANKER gap (§c).
7. **Leverage:** embraced only by Maheshwari (Indian, and flagged structurally fragile); universally avoided by the
   Western quality-school. Not a convergent rule — a regime/style-specific bet with an explicit downside.

### Methodology lesson both batches raised independently
8. **Every one of these records benefits from survivorship / endpoint-selection bias.** Nomad closed at a peak;
   Fundsmith's record was built in a falling-rate decade and broke in 2026; Pabrai took a 60% GFC drawdown; Agrawal's
   100× list is ex-post-selected; Li Lu is lightly stress-tested; Porinju is the un-cited −25% counter-example. **The
   drawdown half of each record is the more honest, less-quoted half** — carry it forward, never the marketing CAGR alone.
   (Directly consistent with this desk's own house rule: never quote a strategy's edge pre-cost, pre-drawdown, or off a
   selected sample.)

### GENUINE GAPS (not in ALPHA_RANKER in any form)

**GAP 1 — HEADLINE: no SELL / deceleration / valuation-ceiling EXIT trigger. ALPHA_RANKER scores ENTRY attractiveness
only; it never models an exit.** This is the convergence point of the entire study:
- Jain's valuation-ceiling round-trip (sell INTO P/E expansion), Fisher's "3 reasons to sell," and Pabrai's numeric
  3-year rule are the three strongest sell disciplines in either batch — and R2 independently found the SAME gap from the
  PMS side (SageOne, which HAS a deceleration/valuation exit → 25.1% CAGR/13.8yr, the best-verified number in that study;
  vs Marcellus, which does NOT → negative alpha, textbook quality-trap). **Three independent lines of evidence — Western
  greats, Indian greats, and the domestic PMS record — all point to the exit rule as the single highest-value codable
  piece, and it is absent from both scorecards.**
- **Building on R2 (not just repeating):** the sell trigger is *cheaply buildable with data we already have.* It needs
  no new source — PIT fundamentals (`_w6fg2_scored` growth deceleration → the Fisher/Maheshwari "growth runway matured /
  first sign of trouble" signal) + `stock_valuation_pit` PE + the richness band (the Jain "name has round-tripped from
  cheap to rich" signal). Concretely: an exit fires when (i) the name's own valuation crosses from cheap-relative to
  rich-relative (Jain), OR (ii) confirmed growth decelerates below the threshold that justified entry (Fisher/Maheshwari),
  OR (iii) a forensic hard-veto trips (Munger/Fisher governance). This is `codable strategy #1` in `PMS_STUDY_20260712/
  SYNTHESIS.md` — a ready spec. **Recommendation: the next scorecard-reset research item should be an EXIT scorecard, not
  a third entry horizon.** It also directly addresses the S4-absolute finding from S7 (that model has no drawdown control
  because it has no exit logic — worse max-DD than a random placebo at every horizon).

**GAP 2 — no name-level position-size LADDERING on confirming conviction.** Pabrai (fractional/quarter-Kelly, ~10% cap),
Solidarity (3%→5%→8%→10–15% as the thesis confirms, per R2). ALPHA_RANKER's B3 is *regime-level gross-exposure* sizing
only — there is no per-name conviction ladder. Lower priority than GAP 1 (codability medium; needs a live conviction
signal), but it is the mechanism that lets a concentrated book (rule §b-2) add to winners without over-committing on entry.

**GAP 3 — no explicit doctrine-fragility / regime-drift monitor (the Smith-2026 lesson).** Smith's July-2026 capitulation
is a methodology lesson in itself: **any static "quality/value/growth" doctrine can break under regime change** — his was
built in a falling-rate decade and inverted turnover from 2–3% to 51.8% when quality stopped working. The survivorship/
endpoint-selection skepticism both batches raised is the same lesson from the sample-construction side.
- **The connection to note (and a genuine ALPHA_RANKER strength):** ALPHA_RANKER's regime-conditional gating is a
  *partial structural answer* to exactly this failure mode — it does NOT run one static doctrine. It suppresses momentum
  in BEAR_OVERSOLD, flips to the certified `rev5d` switch in breadth washouts, gates momentum off at both valuation tails,
  and (precautionarily) routes to gold/cash at richness ≥160 (REGIME_SPEC_V2 layers A/B/C/F). A Fundsmith that had
  momentum-gating-by-regime wired in would not have needed a public capitulation. **What's still missing:** ALPHA_RANKER
  gates by *market* regime, but has no monitor for "our own composite is underperforming its own regime expectation →
  down-weight / investigate" — i.e., a self-decay tripwire on the scorecard itself. The firm's `edge-decay`/`decay-check`
  cadence is the process-level version; a codified in-scorecard version would be the full answer. Flag as a design item,
  not a gap to build today.
