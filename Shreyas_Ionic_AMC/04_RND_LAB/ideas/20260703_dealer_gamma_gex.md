- **Name:** `dealer_gamma_gex_gate` — net dealer-gamma-exposure regime gate for the short-vol book (S-01..S-04).
- **One-line edge:** This is **not a standalone alpha signal**. It is a proposed REGIME GATE: when net dealer gamma is deeply negative (dealers structurally short gamma), intraday moves mechanically amplify and short-vol sleeves (S-01 IV/RV straddle, S-02 earnings straddle, S-03 FF calendar, S-04 short strangle) should be de-sized or paused; when dealer gamma is positive, dealer hedging flow dampens moves toward the max-pain/pinning strike and mean-reversion / short-vol harvesting is favored. Output is a discrete/continuous multiplier on existing sleeve sizing, not a new entry signal.

- **Economic WHY (who loses money to us, why do they keep doing it — forced/behavioral/structural?):**
  **Forced, mechanical flow — not behavioral.** Market makers who are net long options must sell into rallies and buy into selloffs to stay delta-hedged (long gamma → stabilizing). Market makers who are net **short** options (negative dealer gamma) must do the opposite — buy into rallies, sell into selloffs — which is **destabilizing** and amplifies realized moves. Nobody "loses money to us" in the classic anomaly sense; this is a **market-structure/liquidity-provision** effect. The people on the other side are option buyers whose aggregate positioning forces dealers into a hedging posture that the dealers themselves do not choose freely — they must hedge regardless of view. We are not exploiting a mispricing; we are exploiting a **predictable flow constraint** that changes the conditional distribution of realized range/pinning. This is why it belongs as a **regime gate on existing edges**, not a new stated edge: the OI surface tells us when our SHORT-VOL sleeves are hedging-flow-tailwind vs hedging-flow-headwind days.

- **GEX computation sketch (sign convention stated LOUDLY per instructions):**
  1. For each `trade_date`, for each `(strike, option_type)`: compute Black-Scholes gamma Γ(S, K, T, σ, r) using spot S (proxy TBD — see caveat above), T = `dte`/365, and an IV estimate (NOT on disk in this surface — would need to be back-solved from `close`/premium via Newton-Raphson, or sourced from an existing IV file if one exists elsewhere in `datasets/derived/`). **IV back-solving MUST apply the firm's IV<100% sanity cap from the Lessons Learned entry (2026-07 INFY IV=133% blowup) before any derived Γ enters this feature.**
  2. Dollar gamma per strike/type: `GEX_i = OI_i × Γ_i × contract_multiplier × S²  × 0.01` (the ×0.01 is the standard "$ change in dealer hedge notional per 1% move in spot" scaling convention used by SqueezeMetrics/SpotGamma-style GEX; must be re-derived, not copy-pasted, since contract multiplier and convention vary by source).
  3. **SIGN CONVENTION — STATED LOUDLY, UNVERIFIED, MUST BE PRE-REGISTERED BEFORE ANY BACKTEST:** The standard US-market convention (SpotGamma et al.) assumes **dealers are net LONG calls and net SHORT puts** from customer order flow (retail/institutional buy calls for upside, buy puts for hedging, dealers take the other side) — under that convention, GEX = `Σ(call_OI × Γ_call) − Σ(put_OI × Γ_put)`, scaled by S². **This assumption has NOT been verified for NIFTY/BANKNIFTY.** Indian index options flow is dominated by retail/prop **option SELLING** (India has one of the highest retail short-options-writing bases globally, especially post-STT/lot-size changes), which could plausibly mean dealers are net **short calls AND short puts** relative to the US convention, or the sign could even flip by strike/moneyness. **Do not assume the US sign convention transfers.** The cheap-test below is explicitly designed to let the data reveal the correct sign empirically (bucket by raw GEX value under BOTH sign conventions and see which one produces the theoretically-expected relationship: very negative "dealer-short-gamma" days should show HIGHER next-day realized range, not lower).
  4. Aggregate to net GEX per `trade_date` (sum across strikes and expiries, or restrict to near-week — decision to make at cheap-test since only 31%-coverage sparse dates exist for the full aggregate).
  5. Regime bucket: quintile or sign-split on daily GEX z-scored over a trailing window (window length is a free parameter — count against the ≤5 free parameter SOP limit).
