Something that took me a while to unlearn: the correlation number sitting in a risk report is really an average of two very different markets, pretending to be one number.

In calm periods, assets wander somewhat independently and correlation looks low, sometimes flatteringly low. In real stress, the same assets start moving for the same reason, usually liquidity, and correlation jumps toward one almost overnight. Measure it across the whole cycle and you get a figure that describes neither regime honestly.

The mistake I used to make was trusting the calm-period number as if it were the risk. It's closer to the opposite. A low long-run correlation can just mean the portfolio hasn't been tested yet by the regime where it actually matters.

I've stopped asking how correlated a portfolio is. I ask how correlated it becomes on its worst days, and size for that answer instead.
