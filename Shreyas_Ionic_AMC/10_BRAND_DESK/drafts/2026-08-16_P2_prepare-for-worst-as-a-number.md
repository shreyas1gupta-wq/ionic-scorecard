## Draft text (LinkedIn)

"Hope for the best, prepare for the worst" is the kind of line that sounds wise and does nothing. It's a mood. I've been trying to turn it into something with a number attached.

For me, preparing for the worst isn't pessimism about the market. It's a set of decisions made in advance, while I'm calm, about what I'll do when I'm not. How big a single position can get before it can hurt me regardless of how right I am. How much of the portfolio is secretly the same bet wearing different tickers. What drawdown I'll tolerate before I cut, decided before the drawdown, not during it.

The reason to fix these in advance is simple: the moment you actually need the rule is the exact moment you'll be least able to think clearly enough to write one. Fear and a losing position are a bad committee.

None of this is about predicting the crash. I don't think I can. The point is to build something that survives being wrong — because I will be wrong, repeatedly, and the job is to still be standing when I'm right.
