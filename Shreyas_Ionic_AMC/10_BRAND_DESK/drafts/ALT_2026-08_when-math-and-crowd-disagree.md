There's a specific discomfort I've learned to sit with: when my own read on something and the market's read on it point in different directions.

The easy move is to assume the crowd knows something I don't, and fall in line. Consensus prices in a huge amount of information, and most of the time it isn't obviously wrong. But every mispricing I've ever studied started exactly there — a crowd that was very confident and very wrong, for reasons that only looked obvious afterward.

So the rule I hold myself to is narrower than "be contrarian." It's: disagree with the market only when the numbers and the gut agree with each other first. Being contrarian for its own sake is just consensus wearing a different costume.

Most days I end up agreeing with the market anyway. The days I don't are the ones that matter.

Views are my own.
