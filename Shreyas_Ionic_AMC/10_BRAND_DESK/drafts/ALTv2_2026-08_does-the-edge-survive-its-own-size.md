A question I've started asking about every good result I come across, mine or anyone else's: would this still work if the money trying to do it were ten times larger.

Most edges, published or personal, are found at a size small enough that the market never notices the discovering. The return is real, at that size. The mistake is assuming the number travels, that something tested on modest capital simply scales up with a bigger cheque attached.

It rarely scales that cleanly. The same trade that was invisible at small size starts moving the price it's trying to capture once enough capital chases it, and an edge that looked structural turns out to have had a ceiling nobody measured, because nobody had reached it yet.

It's changed how I read every good backtest and every good track record, including the ones I'm most proud of. Not did this work, but how much of it was room in the market that a little more capital would have used up.

Views are my own.
