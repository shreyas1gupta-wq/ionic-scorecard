Spent enough time now on both sides of this argument, pure numbers versus pure judgment, to have a strong opinion on it.

A model will tell you a business is statistically cheap. It won't tell you a promoter is quietly diluting shareholders, or that an entire industry is one policy change away from a different set of economics. A gut call will sometimes catch exactly that. It will also, just as often, talk you into a story that only exists because you wanted to believe it.

What I keep coming back to is using each to check the other. Let the data narrow the universe down to what's actually statistically interesting, so you're not falling for a narrative nobody asked for. Then let judgment ask the one question no dataset can answer on its own: does this still make sense as a business, not just as a number on a screen.

Neither one alone has ever been enough for me, and I don't expect that to change.

Views are my own.
