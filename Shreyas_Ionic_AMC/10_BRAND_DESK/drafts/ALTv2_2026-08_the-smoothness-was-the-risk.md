There's a specific week in market history I still think about. February 2018. A product that had spent years compounding steadily, that looked by every conventional measure like one of the best risk-adjusted trades around, lost almost all of its value in a single session when volatility finally moved.

What got me, studying it later, was that nothing about the strategy had actually changed that week. The same position that built the smooth track record was the one that erased it. The steadiness was never separate from the risk. It was the same trade wearing a calm face for as long as calm happened to last.

It's made me suspicious of any return stream that looks too clean for too long. Smoothness isn't evidence of safety. Sometimes it's just evidence that the bad draw hasn't shown up in the sample yet.

I ask a version of that question about everything I look at now, not only the obvious short-volatility trades: what am I actually short here, that I simply haven't been charged for yet.

Views are my own.
