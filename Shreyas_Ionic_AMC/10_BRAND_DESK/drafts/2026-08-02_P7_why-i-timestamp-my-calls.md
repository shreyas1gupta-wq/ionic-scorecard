## Draft text (LinkedIn)

I've started writing my market views down before I act on them — with a timestamp I can't go back and edit.

Here's the problem it solves. Opinions about markets are free. Everyone has them, and everyone remembers their good ones. Ask around after a big move and you'll find a hundred people who "saw it coming." Almost none of them wrote it down first.

So I decided to hold myself to a harder standard than memory. Before a view goes public, I log it: the claim, the horizon, and the date, in a form that can only be scored one way later — right, wrong, or unresolved. Then I leave it alone. No quiet edits, no reframing a miss into "technically I was early."

Two things happen when you work like this.

The first is that you get honest with yourself fast. It's very hard to fool the version of you that already committed the empty scorecard. The prediction stops being a story you tell and becomes a thing that either happened or didn't.

The second is slower, and it's the one I actually care about. A few years from now, I won't need anyone to take my judgment on faith. The record will be there to read — including the calls that aged badly, which I'll publish too, because a track record you only show when it flatters you isn't a track record.
