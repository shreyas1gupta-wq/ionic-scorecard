Something I keep coming back to after a strong market run.

The instinct after a rally is to go hunting for the next big winner. I've been doing the opposite — spending more time on what would hurt if I'm wrong than on what might work if I'm right.

It sounds defensive. I think it's just honest accounting. The market rarely hands you obvious bargains; more often it's a few fair prices buried in a lot of froth dressed up as opportunity. Telling those two apart is most of the work.

My lean right now: hold fewer things, understand them better, pay prices that leave room to be wrong. Chasing the story everyone's already paid up for has never been where I've made money.

Views are my own.
