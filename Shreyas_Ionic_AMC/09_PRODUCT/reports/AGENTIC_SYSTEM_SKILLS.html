Self-improvement loop — after every task, a short review captures the lesson into the responsible agent's own memory, a session scorecard ranks output per token spent, and weak methods get rewritten. The system gets better at its own methods over time, and the learning survives even when an agent is replaced.
Why it holds up

Cheap by default. Skeptical by default. Human where it counts.

◆ Cheap by default

  • →Only research what changed. Full run, quick delta, or cached carry — most of the universe costs almost nothing most weeks. ~89% fewer tokens.
  • →Cheapest capable model. Benchmarked blind: review work runs one tier down at ~1/15 the cost, same quality.
  • →Convert before reading. Documents become lean text first — roughly 35× less to read the same file.

▚ Skeptical by default

  • ·A ten-class checklist for the quiet ways a backtest lies.
  • ·A one-day-lag test: a real edge degrades gently, a leak collapses.
  • ·Code frozen the moment it goes to paper — no quiet tuning to flatter itself.
  • ·Every headline number traces to a script, or it is tagged unverified.

◆ Human where it counts

  • →The question. Every hypothesis originates with a person; the system has never handed over a genuinely new idea.
  • →The final call. The capital gate never auto-advances — judgement about what is real enough to act on stays human.

Peak efficiency isn't the model doing more. It's a system that is cheap, skeptical and self-improving by default, wrapped around a human who owns the question and the final call.
