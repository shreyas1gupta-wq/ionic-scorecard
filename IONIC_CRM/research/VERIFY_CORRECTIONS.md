### 1. [INFERENCE] DPDP s.12(1)/s.7(i) analysis concluding an employee cannot demand erasure, 'But s.8(7) still bites independently: erase as soon as it is reasonable to assume that the specified purpose is no longer being served'
- **Wrong because:** Stated as a live, currently-operative obligation ('still bites'). It is not. The MeitY commencement notification of 13 November 2025 brought the DPDP Act into force in phases: only s.1(2), s.2, ss.18-26, 35, 38-43 and s.44(1)&(3) commenced on Gazette publication. Sections 3-17 -- which contain ALL THREE sections the researcher relies on (s.7 legitimate uses, s.8 data fiduciary obligations incl. s.8(7) erasure, s.12 right to erasure) -- commence eighteen months after publication, i.e. ~12-14 May 2027. As of today (3 Aug 2026) none of s.7, s.8 or s.12 is in force. No employee can exercise a DPDP erasure right today and no s.8(7) deletion duty attaches today. This is exactly the 'legal obligation stated as live when the relevant provisions are not yet enforced' failure mode. The substantive reasoning is sound but the tense is wrong, and it materially misstates urgency -- the researcher also cited only dpdpa.com convenience reproductions, which carry the section text but NOT the commencement schedule, so fetching their own cited source could never have caught this.
- **Correct:** Re-state as forward-looking: 'From ~12 May 2027, DPDP ss.7/8/12 will apply. Until then no DPDP right or obligation is exercisable against this app.' Then note what IS binding today (see 'missing': IT Act s.43A + SPDI Rules 2011, and SEBI CSCRF). Design to the May-2027 standard by choice, not because law compels it now.
- **Source:** AZB & Partners, 'India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act: Phased Rollout and Key Compliance Milestones' (https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/) -- states ss.7, 8 and 12 come into force 12 May 2027; corroborated by Shardul Amarchand Mangaldas, 'Enforcement of the DPDP Act and notification of the DPDP rules' (https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/) -- 'Rights of Data Principals' land 14 May 2027; and TeamLease RegTech summary of the MeitY commencement notification (https://www.teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/49364/meity-issued-a-notification-regarding-the-commencement-of-the-dpdp-act/). Verified 2026-08-03.

### 2. Gotcha: git clone events being unprovable on GitHub Free is 'directly against DPDP Rule 6's 1-year access-log expectation' -- used to argue the app must keep its own access log now
- **Wrong because:** Rule 6 of the DPDP Rules 2025 is not in force. Under Rule 1 of the Rules (G.S.R. 846(E), Gazette 13/14 November 2025), only Rules 1, 2 and 17-21 commenced on publication; Rule 4 commences at 12 months; and Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23 -- which includes Rule 6 (reasonable security safeguards, and the sub-clause (e) one-year log retention) -- commence eighteen months after publication, ~May 2027. So there is no live 1-year access-log obligation to be 'directly against' today. Presenting it as a current compliance failure inflates a 2027 obligation into a present-day one.
- **Correct:** The one-year retention text is real and correctly quoted -- Rule 6(e): 'retain such logs and personal data for a period of one year, unless compliance with any law for the time being in force requires otherwise' -- but it binds from ~May 2027. Correct framing: build the app's own access log now because you cannot retrofit logs you never captured, and because ~May 2027 is inside this app's expected life -- not because Rule 6 applies today.
- **Source:** Rule 6 text: https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule6.html (verbatim sub-clauses (c) and (e) confirmed). Commencement schedule: Rule 1 commencement (https://dpdpaedu.org/docs/DPDPA%20Rules/Title%20and%20Commencement/) and AZB & Partners (https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/), which places Rule 6 in the third phase effective 12 May 2027. ### 3. [DATA] SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations 2020 five-year preservation requirement 'lands squarely in DPDP s.8(7)'s unless retention is necessary for compliance with any law carve-out' -- i.e. it justifies retaining the CRM's employee task history
- **Wrong because:** The regulation and the five-year period are correct (and the regulation number 29 is confirmed, so that flag resolves in the researcher's favour). But the application to this app is over-broad -- the classic 'applies to the firm' vs 'applies to this internal tool' conflation. Reg 29 preserves 'the books of account and other records and documents mentioned under this chapter'. The enumerated set sits in Reg 27(1): balance sheet, profit and loss account, auditor's report, statement of financial position, and 'records in support of every investment transaction or recommendation which will indicate the data, facts and opinion leading to that investment decision'. An internal employee ticket ('chase KYC doc', 'prepare review deck') is none of those. Only the subset of tickets that actually evidence investment rationale would be caught. As written, the claim would let the firm assert a SEBI mandate over its entire employee task history -- which is both unsupported and, once DPDP commences, the wrong side of s.8(7).
- **Correct:** Narrow it: SEBI Reg 29 + Reg 27(1) compel five-year retention only of ticket content that documents an investment transaction or recommendation rationale. Everything else (admin/ops/HR-flavoured tickets) has NO statutory retention anchor and should get an explicit business-purpose retention period set by the firm. Practically: add a per-ticket flag for 'investment-decision record' and retain on two different clocks. Do not blanket-assert SEBI cover.
- **Source:** Reg 29 and Reg 27(1) text via taxguru reproduction of SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations 2020 (https://taxguru.in/sebi/sebi-portfolio-managers-regulations-2020.html); regulation numbering corroborated by compliancecalendar (https://blogs.compliancecalendar.in/sebi-portfolio-managers-regulations-2020-5590). ### 5. Gotcha: D1 exports 'must be continually polled or they auto-cancel -- so run backups out of hours and not from a fire-and-forget 10 ms-CPU Worker'
- **Wrong because:** Conflates CPU time with wall-clock time. The 10 ms Free-plan figure is 'CPU time per HTTP request' -- time spent executing, not time spent awaiting a fetch. Polling a D1 export consumes almost no CPU while awaiting. More importantly, Cloudflare's own limits table gives Cron Triggers a 15-minute DURATION limit on the Free plan, which is exactly the budget a polling loop needs. Keep the sound half of the advice: run it out of hours, because 'a running export will block other database requests.' If the subrequest budget is tight, drive the export from GitHub Actions instead -- but for capability reasons, not because of the 10 ms figure.

## india-dpdp-act  (5 refuted)

### 1. Claim 6 + gotcha: 'Client personal data falls entirely OUTSIDE s.7(i)... Client PII in a ticket would have no lawful ground at all' / 'One client name typed into a ticket note breaks the entire legal architecture'
- **Wrong because:** The premise (s.7(i) covers employees only) is correct, but the conclusion does not follow. s.4(1) offers TWO alternative grounds and s.7 offers NINE legitimate uses. A SEBI-registered wealth manager processing its own clients' personal data has a lawful ground under s.4(1)(a) (consent obtained at onboarding under the PMS/advisory agreement) and, independently, very likely under s.7(a) — verbatim: 'for the specified purpose for which the Data Principal has voluntarily provided her personal data to the Data Fiduciary, and in respect of which she has not indicated to the Data Fiduciary that she does not consent to the use of her personal data.' Clients voluntarily hand over their data to the firm. The researcher has silently assumed s.7(i) is the app's ONLY available ground, when the ground attaches to the FIRM's processing of that data principal, not to the individual database it sits in. DPDP contains no free-standing 'purpose limitation' ground test that a client-servicing ticket would fail; the specified purpose in the client's onboarding notice (providing wealth-management services) plausibly covers internal task tracking for that client's work.
- **Correct:** Client PII in a ticket is a purpose-limitation, notice-scope, data-minimisation and SEBI-CSCRF-scope problem — NOT an absence-of-lawful-ground problem, and it does not 'break the entire legal architecture'. Keeping client PII out remains the right build decision (it minimises breach exposure, keeps the app off the client-PII register, and avoids s.8(3) accuracy duties on client records), but present it as a risk-reduction design choice, not as a legal impossibility. Do not let the server-side validator be justified on a legal theory that would collapse under a lawyer's first question.
- **Source:** DPDP Act 2023 ss.4(1) and 7(a) verbatim, official MeitY gazette text of Act No. 22 of 2023, https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf (downloaded and text-extracted 2026-08-03)

### 2. Claim 11 / gotcha: CERT-In requires Annexure-I incidents including 'Unauthorised access of IT systems/data', 'Data Breach' and 'Data Leak' to be reported within six hours 'with no threshold'
- **Wrong because:** The verbatim quotes of the Direction are accurate, but 'no threshold' is contradicted by CERT-In's OWN official FAQ (May 2022), Q30, which imposes a severity/scale filter on the 6-hour clock: 'Any incident as stated in Annexure-I of the Cyber Security Directions of 28.04.2022 and meeting the following criteria should be reported within the stipulated 6 hour time: • cyber incidents and cyber security incidents of severe nature (such as denial of service, distributed denial of service, intrusion, spread of computer contaminant including Ransomware) on any part of the public information infrastructure including backbone network infrastructure • Data Breaches or Data Leaks • large-scale or most frequent incidents such as intrusion into computer resource, websites etc. • cyber incidents impacting safety of human beings'. Annexure-I item (iii) 'Unauthorised access of IT systems/data' is therefore in the 6-hour bucket only where it is 'large-scale or most frequent' intrusion — it is not unconditional. FAQ Q23 also says the s.70B(7) penal power 'will be exercised reasonably and on occasions when the non-compliance is deliberate'. FAQ Q30 additionally permits partial filing: 'The entities may provide information to the extent available at the time of reporting.'
- **Correct:** Data Breach and Data Leak ARE unconditionally 6-hour reportable — keep that in the runbook. But drop 'no threshold' as a general statement: a single failed login attempt or one employee seeing another's ticket is not a 6-hour filing. Write the runbook against FAQ Q30's four criteria and note that a partial report inside 6 hours followed by detail later is expressly sanctioned.
- **Source:** CERT-In, 'FAQs on Cyber Security Directions of 28.04.2022' (May 2022), Q23, Q24 and Q30, https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/FAQs_on_CyberSecurityDirections_May2022.pdf (downloaded and text-extracted 2026-08-03)

### 4. Gotcha: keeping client PII out of the ticket DB keeps the app out of SEBI CSCRF scope ('it simultaneously drags the app into SEBI CSCRF scope')
- **Wrong because:** CSCRF scope is set by WHAT THE SYSTEM IS USED FOR and by segregation, not by whether client PII sits in it. SEBI's June-2025 CSCRF FAQ Q8 (the very quote the researcher cites in claim 16) reads: 'The controls shall apply only to IT infrastructure, network, application, software, etc. being used for SEBI RE related activities.' An internal ticketing app whose tickets track NDPMS/advisory deliverables IS being used for SEBI-RE-related activities, with or without client names. FAQ Q27 closes the escape route: audit coverage is limited to systems under SEBI purview 'However, this shall be applicable only if infrastructure/ software/ applications are properly segregated. If there are any ancillary/ connected systems used for accessing/ communicating with systems under SEBI purview, those systems should also be covered under audit against CSCRF.' So the client_ref-only design does NOT buy CSCRF exemption, and the implied converse ('no client name = out of scope') is wrong.
- **Correct:** Assume the app is in CSCRF scope as an RE application and argue about its criticality classification instead — FAQ Q10 says internet-facing but business-non-critical tools ('survey forms, loan calculators, etc.') may be classified non-critical by the RE on a risk assessment, with the Board/Partners/Proprietor approving the critical-systems list. Target: in scope, classified non-critical, listed in the IT asset inventory (FAQ Q9 permits a manual/Excel inventory for lean REs). The client_ref design still earns its keep on breach-impact grounds, not on scope grounds.
- **Source:** SEBI, 'FAQs on Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience Framework (CSCRF) for SEBI REs and Framework for Adoption of Cloud Services by SEBI REs' (June 2025), Q8, Q9, Q10, Q26, Q27, https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/jun-2025/1749647139924.pdf (downloaded and text-extracted 2026-08-03)

### 5. Gotcha stated in the present tense: 'You cannot lawfully hard-delete on request inside one year. Rule 8(3) (all Data Fiduciaries) plus Rule 6(1)(e) impose a one-year retention floor... A naive DELETE-on-request button is itself a Rules breach.'

### 2. Claim 1 / headline: 'The preserved set is the closed enumeration in Reg 27(1)' — hence SEBI's record-retention regime 'is a closed enumeration that an employee-only task tracker escapes entirely.'
- **Wrong because:** The researcher quotes Reg 29 correctly ('records and documents mentioned under this chapter') and then silently narrows 'this chapter' to Reg 27(1). I read the gazette text: Reg 27 and Reg 29 sit in CHAPTER IV — GENERAL OBLIGATIONS AND RESPONSIBILITIES (Regs 21-34), which begins at 'CHAPTER IV ... Code of Conduct. 21.' and runs to Reg 34, ending before 'CHAPTER V INSPECTION AND DISCIPLINARY PROCEEDINGS. 35.' (The researcher's phrasing also implies the chapter is Chapter III; Chapter III is 'ELIGIBLE FUND MANAGERS', Regs 16-20.) Chapter IV additionally contains Reg 22 (client agreement + disclosure document), Reg 24 (16 sub-clauses of general responsibilities, incl. 24(10) 'proper and timely handling of complaints from his clients'), Reg 30 (separate client-wise accounts, audits), Reg 31 (periodic reports to the client), Reg 33 (information to the Board, incl. 'the names of the clients whose portfolio it has managed') and Reg 34 (compliance officer). Reg 29 by its own terms preserves all of those for 5 years.
- **Correct:** The 5-year preservation duty is NOT a 5-item closed list; it is 'every record or document mentioned in Chapter IV'. That is an open-textured set defined by SUBJECT MATTER, which is exactly the failure mode the design is trying to avoid. It is not yet, substantively: the DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified by MeitY on 14-Nov-2025 with staged commencement dates of 14-Nov-2025, 14-Nov-2026 and 14-May-2027, the substantive obligations (notice, consent, security safeguards, breach notification, retention/erasure) commencing 14-May-2027. Decision-relevant both ways: today the binding constraints are CSCRF + CERT-In, so DPDP is not a reason to stop; but employee personal data (name, work email, task history — admittedly in scope per the brief) lands inside enforceable DPDP obligations roughly nine months after this app would ship, so consent/notice, purpose limitation, erasure-on-exit and breach notification should be designed in now rather than retrofitted.
